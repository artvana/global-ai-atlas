Introduced by Representative Navarro Suárez
Referred to the Committee on Consumer Affairs
ACT
To amend Articles 2, 3, and 4 of Act No. 185-2024, known as the "Cyber Privacy Protection Act for Children and Youth", for the purpose of establishing a digital duty of care for online platforms; providing for impact assessments on minors, parental control mechanisms, age verification, protection against content manipulated through artificial intelligence, economic rights of minor digital content creators, and for other related purposes.
STATEMENT OF MOTIVES
Puerto Rican children and youth grow up today in an omnipresent digital environment. Social networks, mobile applications and virtual interaction spaces offer educational and expression opportunities, but also present significant risks for the mental health, safety and dignity of our minors. Numerous studies have linked certain digital design practices, prolonged exposure to harmful content and the use of recommendation algorithms with the increase of anxiety, depression, cyberbullying and eating disorders among adolescents.
Act No. 185-2024 established a pioneering cyber privacy protection framework in Puerto Rico. However, the accelerated development of technology, including generative artificial intelligence, synthetic content and platforms based on adaptive algorithms, demands strengthening that legislation with a comprehensive approach to digital security and child welfare. It is the duty of the State to protect minors from manipulation, commercial exploitation and exposure to digital environments that may affect their emotional development or violate their right to privacy.
Through this amendment, a general digital duty of care is incorporated, applicable to technology companies and platforms with minor users. They are required to adopt reasonable measures to prevent foreseeable harms, avoid addictive or manipulative design practices and guarantee adequate privacy settings from the outset. In addition, it is provided that companies conduct impact assessments on minors before introducing new functions or significant redesigns, and the prohibition of using minors' personal data for advertising or marketing purposes is reinforced.
This legislation also addresses emerging phenomena such as the creation and dissemination of false or manipulated images of minors through artificial intelligence, imposing criminal liability and prevention duties. Likewise, it recognizes the economic and privacy rights of minors who generate income as content creators, ensuring that part of those income are reserved for their future benefit. Finally, an educational public policy of digital literacy is integrated into the school system, in charge of the Department of Education, to foster the responsible, critical and safe use of technology.
BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY OF PUERTO RICO:
Section 1. – New subsections (i), (j) and (k) and (l) are added to Article 2 of Act 185-2024, to read as follows:
	"Article 2. – Definitions
	The terms used in this Act shall have the following meaning:
	(a)…
	…
	(i) "Compulsive Use" – means a persistent and repetitive use of a Social Media Internet Site or Application that significantly impacts one or more important activities in the life of a minor, including socializing, sleeping, eating, learning, reading, concentrating, communicating or working."
	(j) "Design Feature" – means any feature or component of a Social Media Site or Application that fosters or increases the frequency of use, time spent or activity generated by minors on the Social Media Site or Application. Design features include, among others:
(i) infinite scrolling or auto play;
(ii) rewards or incentives based on the frequency of use, time spent or activity generated by minors on the Social Media Site or Application;
(iii) "push" notifications and alerts;
(iv) badges or other visual symbols of awards based on the frequency of use, time spent or activity generated by minors on the Social Media Site or Application;
(v) personalized design features for minor users;
(vi) in-game purchases or in-app purchases, or;
(vii) filters that alter the appearance of the minor user.
	(k) "Personalized Design Feature"—means a totally or partially automated design feature system, which includes a recommendation system, that responds to or is based on the collection of personal data of users and that fosters, incites or increases the frequency of use, time spent or activity generated by minors on the Social Media Site or Application."
	(l) "Personalized Recommendation System" – means a totally or partially automated system that is used to suggest, promote or classify content, including other users, hashtags or publications, in function of and based on the personal data of users. A recommendation system that suggests, promotes or classifies content based solely on the language, city, or age of the user shall not be considered a personalized recommendation system."
Section 2. – New subsections (i), (j) and (k) and (l) are added to Article 4 of Act 185-2024, to read as follows:
	(a)…
	…
	(i) Every Social Media Internet Site or Application, as defined in this Act, shall establish and exercise a reasonable duty of care in the creation and implementation of a design feature to prevent and mitigate the following harms to minors, for which a prudent and reasonable person would agree that such harms were foreseeable by the Social Media Internet Site or Application and would also agree that the design feature implemented by the Social Media Internet Site or Application is a contributing factor to such harms:
(1) Eating disorders, substance use disorders and suicidal behaviors;
(2) Depressive disorders and anxiety disorders when such conditions are objectively verifiable and clinically diagnosable symptoms and are related to the compulsive use of the Social Media Internet Site or Application;
(3) Patterns of use indicating compulsive use of the Social Media Internet Site or Application;
(4) Physical violence or virtual harassment that is so severe, pervasive and objectively offensive that it impacts the life of a minor;
(5) Sexual exploitation and abuse of minors;
(6) Distribution, sale or use of narcotics, tobacco products, cannabis products, gambling or alcohol;
(7) Financial harms caused by unfair or deceptive practices or harmful practices (as defined by Section 5(a)(4) of the Federal Trade Commission Act (15 U.S.C. 45(a)(4))).
(j) Rule of Interpretation – Nothing in Article 4(i) of this Act shall be construed as requiring a Social Media Internet Site or Application to prevent or impede any minor from---
(1) deliberately and independently searching for or specifically requesting content; or
(2) accessing resources and information on the prevention or mitigation of the harms described in Article 4(i) of this Act;
	Nothing in this section shall be construed as a limitation on the viewpoint expressed by users through any speech, expression or information protected by the First Amendment of the Constitution of the United States of America.
(k) Every Social Media Internet Site or Application, as defined in this Act, shall provide to the minor user safeguards of easy access and use that—
(1) limit the ability of other users to communicate directly with the minor;
(2) prevent other users from having access to personal information of minor users;
(3) automatically limit the use of Design Features that foster or increase the frequency of use, time spent or activity generated by minors on the Social Media Site or Application, including infinite scrolling or auto play; rewards or incentives based on the frequency of use, time spent or activity generated by minors on the Social Media Site or Application; "push" notifications and alerts; badges or other visual symbols of awards based on the frequency of use, time spent or activity generated by minors on the Social Media Site or Application; personalized design features for minor users; in-game purchases or in-app purchases, or; filters that alter the appearance of the minor user.
(4) provide an immediately, automatically and easy-to-use available mechanism that limits the time the minor user spends on the Social Media Site or Application.
Section 3. -- Severability Clause
If any part of this Act is declared null or unconstitutional by any competent court, said declaration shall not affect, impair or invalidate the other provisions.
Section 4. -- Effective Date
This Act shall take effect immediately ninety (90) days after its approval.
