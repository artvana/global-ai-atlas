GOVERNMENT OF PUERTO RICO
    20th Assembly								           3rd Session
	    Legislative 								      Ordinary
HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES
H. B. 1098
FEBRUARY 2, 2026
                                         Introduced by Representative Pérez Cordero
Referred to the Committee on Judiciary
ACT
To amend Rules 9.1 and 9.2 of the Rules of Civil Procedure of 2009, as amended, for the purpose of removing the obligation to notify the fax number as a means of communication between the parties and other technical amendments; to amend Rule 37.4 of the Rules of Civil Procedure of 2009, as amended, for the purpose of requiring that the preliminary report between attorneys include controversies regarding the authenticity and admissibility of evidence created through generative artificial intelligence; to amend Rule 47 of the Rules of Civil Procedure of 2009, as amended, for the purpose of preventing reconsideration from being used to present evidence that could or should have been presented in prior judicial stages, and that was not evaluated by the court.
STATEMENT OF MOTIVES
In this measure, technical corrections are made to Rules 9.1 and 9.2 to remove the obligation to include any fax number as a method of notification. At least in this jurisdiction, that method of notification has lost relevance and is becoming less and less common. This does not prevent the parties from including it, if they so consider it appropriate.
The advancement of generative artificial intelligence has caused questioning regarding the viability of the legal system—both at the federal level and at the state and territorial level—to address the authentication and admissibility of evidence that could have been created or manipulated through generative artificial intelligence. On August 1, 2025, a Louisiana state law took effect, which amended its rules of civil procedure to address this. Such state law is more comprehensive than what is done in this measure, in which it is clarified that controversies over evidence manipulated with generative artificial intelligence must be addressed before trial—in the preliminary report between attorneys—except for just cause. For the moment, we understand that the rest of our rules are drafted to deal with this technological controversy without requiring the other amendments that took effect in the state of Louisiana.
Some attorneys use Rule 47 to bring for the first time on reconsideration evidence that they unjustifiably and untimely omitted to present. This practice is more common in suits resolved summarily or by some dispositive ruling. It is unacceptable that the court may reconsider a determination through evidence that it did not have before its consideration. Worse still, that the court be charged with having erred for not evaluating evidence that was not presented to it in its proper time. Thus, the need is reaffirmed for attorneys to be zealous and careful with the evidence they have and that they intend to use.
In short, this Legislative Assembly has the power to amend the Rules of Civil Procedure of 2009. Thus, the wording of the rules is improved to help the parties and the courts to elucidate the truth of judicial controversies in a fair manner. The rules are also adapted to the inevitable changes brought by technological advances.
      BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY OF PUERTO RICO:
Section 1. Rule 9.1 of the Rules of Civil Procedure of 2009, as amended, is hereby amended to read as follows:
"Rule 9.1. Signature and information of the writs.
When the party in the suit has legal representation, every writ shall be signed by at least one attorney of record, who shall include in the writ his name, his attorney number before the Supreme Court of Puerto Rico, telephone number [and fax number, and his] postal address and [electronic] email address, as they appear in the registry of the Supreme Court of Puerto Rico.
In addition, in the first writ presented by the attorney, he shall notify the physical and postal address, the email address, and the telephone number of the party he represents. When a natural person is a party in the suit and is not represented by an attorney, he shall sign his writ and state his telephone number, [fax number, and] his postal address and [electronic] email address, if any.
The attorney or the party shall immediately notify the court, through a motion under the case caption, of any change in his telephone number, [fax number, and in his] postal address or [electronic] email address.
...
If it is determined that a writ has been filed with false, simulated, defamatory or indecorous information or offensive or coarse language is used, the court shall impose any sanction in accordance with the provisions of Rule 9.3."
Section 2. Rule 9.2 of the Rules of Civil Procedure of 2009, as amended, is hereby amended to read as follows:
"Rule 9.2. Legal representation.
The attorney who assumes the professional representation of a party in a proceeding pending before the court, shall present a motion to that effect[, in which he shall include]. He shall include his attorney number before the Supreme Court of Puerto Rico, telephone number, [fax number, and] postal address and [electronic] email address.
When an attorney who has appeared before a court representing a client requests to renounce that representation, he shall present a written motion to that effect. The attorney shall set forth the reasons why his renunciation should be permitted and shall report the telephone number, the email address, and the postal address of the person represented. He shall also state that he has notified the client of the renunciation and that he has complied with the requirements of [the canons of the Code of Professional Ethics] the Rules of Professional Conduct. The court shall have the power to reject the renunciation requested in those exceptional cases in which it deems that the rights of a party could be seriously injured or that the proceeding would be unduly delayed."
Section 3. Rule 37.4 of the Rules of Civil Procedure of 2009, as amended, is hereby amended to read as follows:
"Rule 37.4. Meeting in preparation for the pretrial conference.
In cases set for pretrial conference, the attorneys for the parties shall meet informally with each other at least fifteen (15) days before the date set for the conference. This, with the purpose of preparing, in accordance with the Case Management Report, the agreements in the scheduling order and subsequent incidents thereto, a Preliminary Report between Attorneys that includes the following:
(a) Names, addresses, telephone numbers, [fax] and email of the attorneys who will intervene in representation of the parties at the trial on the merits of the case.
...
Unless just cause is shown, the court shall not allow the presentation at trial of those documents, witnesses or controversies not identified in accordance with this rule, and shall consider as waived those objections and defenses that have not been specified in the report. This includes challenges to the authenticity or admissibility of computer-generated evidence or other electronic evidence, manipulated or fabricated, in whole or in part, with generative artificial intelligence."
Section 4. Rule 47 of the Rules of Civil Procedure of 2009, as amended, is hereby amended to read as follows:
"Rule 47. Reconsideration.
...
The motion for reconsideration must set forth with sufficient particularity and specificity the facts and the law that the moving party considers should be reconsidered and shall be founded on substantial issues related to the pertinent findings of fact or material conclusions of law. This motion may not be used to present evidence that could or should have been presented in prior judicial stages.
...
The motion for reconsideration shall be notified to the other parties in the suit within the fifteen (15) days established by this rule to present it before the court simultaneously. The term to notify shall be of strict compliance."
Section 5. Severability.
If any clause, paragraph, article, section, subsection or part of this act is declared unconstitutional by a competent court, the judgment to that effect shall not affect, prejudice or invalidate the rest of this act. The effect of said judgment shall be limited to the clause, paragraph, article, section, subsection or part of this act so declared unconstitutional.
Section 6. Effective Date.
This Act shall take effect immediately upon its approval.
