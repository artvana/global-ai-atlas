# Colombia: The Government of Colombia Introduces a New Bill to Regulate Artificial Intelligence

Source: Baker McKenzie Connect on Tech Blog
Published: August 5, 2025
Authors: Juan Pablo Concha Delgado, Yuliana Salamanca Jaramillo, Pedro Londoño, Julian David Ruiz Rondan

## Current Status

On July 28, 2025, Colombia's government submitted legislation to the Senate designed to "regulate AI to foster its ethical, responsible, competitive, and innovative development." The overarching objective involves positioning the nation as a regional governance leader.

## Risk Classification Framework

This framework categorizes AI systems according to four risk classifications:

1. Critical risk
2. High risk
3. Systems with specific transparency obligations
4. Systems with minimal or no risk

Understanding which category applies to a company's AI systems determines whether usage faces prohibition, conditional requirements, or adherence to conduct codes and best practice standards.

## Intellectual Property Implications

The legislation mandates that "development of AI systems must be based on the explicit consent of individuals when using their works, voices, or images." This provision settles longstanding disputes regarding authorization necessity.

Concerning copyright protections, the Bill establishes a potential avenue for usage without authorization if legitimate legal grounds exist, though case-specific risk evaluation remains essential.

## Enforcement and Penalties

The Ministry of Science, Technology, and Innovation assumes enforcement responsibilities, issuing binding directives. A penalties structure imposes institutional and personal fines reaching 3,000 monthly minimum wages, alongside potential activity suspension for twenty-four months or permanent operational closure involving AI systems.

## Regulatory Sandboxes

The bill provides for the implementation of regulatory "sandboxes" to allow controlled experimentation with AI systems under regulatory oversight.

## Key Principles

The draft bill, inspired by international standards from the OECD, UNESCO, and the European Union, establishes key principles to boost AI development and investment, including:
- Ethical and responsible development
- Innovation promotion
- Protection of fundamental rights
- Risk-proportionate regulation

## Background and Context

Colombia has introduced multiple draft bills to regulate artificial intelligence. The July 2025 bill represents the government's official legislative initiative, distinct from earlier parliamentary proposals. The bill consolidates approaches from previous legislative efforts including a parliamentary bill (Boletín No. 422 of 2025).

Key aspects of earlier draft bills covered by the original Baker McKenzie article (pre-2025):
- Colombia's exploration of AI governance frameworks
- Risk-based approaches to AI regulation
- Data protection considerations for AI systems
- Government's positioning as a regional AI governance leader

The complete draft bill is accessible through the Colombian Senate portal. Baker McKenzie offers support for policy creation and implementation regarding artificial intelligence matters.

Additional context from other secondary sources:

## Bill No. 43 (Draft Law 043-25)

The Draft Law No. 043 of 2025 aims to establish a comprehensive regulatory framework for the development, use, and governance of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Colombia.

## Bill No. 422 of 2025

The Senate of the Republic of Colombia published Draft Bill of Law 422 of 2025, a legislative initiative aimed at establishing a regulatory framework for the development, use, and oversight of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in the country. This bill was presented by the Ministry of Science, Technology and Innovation, in conjunction with the Ministry of Information and Communications Technologies.

## Main Pillars

The main pillars include:
- Classification of AI systems according to their risk level (ranging from low to prohibited)
- Implementation of regulatory "sandboxes"
- Incentives for research and development
- Mechanisms for the protection of fundamental rights

As of the date of this record, neither bill has been approved, and it remains to be seen if these initiatives from the Colombian authorities will change the course of previous bills.
