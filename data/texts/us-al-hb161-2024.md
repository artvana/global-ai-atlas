# Alabama House Bill 161 — Prohibition on Nonconsensual AI-Generated Intimate Images

**HB 161 | Alabama Legislature, 2024 Regular Session**
*Alabama — Enacted April 24, 2024 | Effective October 1, 2024*

---

## Overview

Alabama House Bill 161 extends Alabama's existing nonconsensual intimate image (NCII) laws to explicitly cover images, videos, and other media that are generated or substantially altered using artificial intelligence. The law prohibits the creation and distribution of sexually explicit AI-generated depictions of identifiable real persons without their consent, and provides both criminal penalties for violators and a civil cause of action for victims.

The law is part of a national trend of state legislation targeting AI-generated nonconsensual intimate imagery — sometimes referred to as "AI deepfake porn" — which has proliferated with the advent of widely accessible generative AI image tools. Alabama's law applies to both fully AI-generated images (where the depicted person never posed) and AI-manipulated images (where real images of a person are altered to appear sexually explicit).

## Key Provisions

### Prohibition on Nonconsensual AI-Generated Intimate Images

- Prohibits the knowing creation, possession with intent to distribute, or distribution of sexually explicit AI-generated depictions of an identifiable real person without that person's consent.
- Covers images, videos, audio recordings, and multimedia content.
- Applies to: (1) images generated entirely by AI without any underlying real intimate image; (2) images where real, non-intimate photographs of a person are manipulated by AI to appear sexually explicit; and (3) images where real intimate photographs are altered to change the depicted person's identity or appearance.
- "Identifiable" means a reasonable person who knows the depicted individual would recognize them from the content.

### Consent Requirement

- Content is prohibited unless the depicted person has provided explicit, knowing consent to the creation and/or distribution of such content.
- Consent to creation does not imply consent to distribution; separate consent is required for each.
- Consent may be revoked; distribution after revocation of consent is a separate violation.

### Criminal Penalties

- Creation or distribution without consent constitutes a Class A misdemeanor for a first offense.
- Penalty: up to one year in county jail and/or a fine up to $6,000.
- Enhanced penalties may apply where the victim is a minor (classified as a more serious offense under Alabama's separate child exploitation statutes).

### Civil Cause of Action

- Victims may bring a private civil lawsuit against any person who violates the statute.
- Available damages include: actual damages (including emotional distress), statutory damages, punitive damages in cases of willful or malicious conduct, and attorney's fees and costs.
- The civil remedy exists independently of any criminal prosecution and does not require a prior criminal conviction.

### Scope and Exemptions

- Applies to all persons who create or distribute covered content, including individuals, websites, and platforms that actively solicit or curate such content (as distinguished from mere passive hosting, which may implicate federal Section 230 questions).
- Exemptions for bona fide educational, journalistic, or law enforcement purposes where content is used as evidence or for public awareness.
- Does not apply to content that is clearly satirical and not reasonably mistaken for a depiction of the real person.
