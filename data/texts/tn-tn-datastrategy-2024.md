# Tunisia National Artificial Intelligence Strategy 2023–2025 and Digital Economy Governance Framework

**Issued by:** Ministry of Communication Technologies and Digital Economy (MTCEN); Council of Ministers of Tunisia  
**Adoption Date:** July 2023  
**Framework:** Digital Economy Act No. 2019-47; National Digital Economy Strategy; Presidential Initiative on Digital Transformation  

---

## Part I: National AI Strategy 2023–2025 — Background and Vision

### 1.1 Introduction and Context

Tunisia's National Artificial Intelligence Strategy 2023–2025 represents the government's commitment to positioning Tunisia as a leading digital economy and AI innovation hub in North Africa and the Arab world. The Strategy builds upon Tunisia's established strengths in information and communication technology (ICT) education, a well-educated technical workforce, and a growing ecosystem of technology start-ups and digital enterprises.

The Strategy was developed by the Ministry of Communication Technologies and Digital Economy (MTCEN) in consultation with public institutions, private sector actors, civil society organizations, academic institutions, and international partners. It reflects both the significant opportunities that AI presents for Tunisia's economic development and social progress, and the governance challenges that responsible AI deployment requires.

Tunisia's AI Strategy is aligned with broader regional and international frameworks, including the African Union Data Policy Framework, the UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence (2021), the Global Partnership on Artificial Intelligence (GPAI), and Tunisia's commitments under the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) digital trade provisions.

### 1.2 Strategic Vision

Tunisia's vision is to become by 2025:

- A regional reference center for AI research, talent development, and innovation;
- A country in which AI is deployed responsibly in key economic sectors to drive productivity growth, improve public services, and reduce inequalities;
- A country with a robust AI governance framework that protects fundamental rights, builds public trust, and promotes ethical AI practices;
- An active participant in regional and global AI governance and standard-setting.

### 1.3 Diagnosis of Tunisia's AI Ecosystem

Tunisia has a number of assets that provide a foundation for AI development:

**Human capital**: Tunisia has a relatively high level of education in technical fields, with a significant number of engineering and computer science graduates annually. Several Tunisian universities, including the University of Tunis El Manar, the University of Sfax, and the National Engineering School of Tunis, have established research programs in AI-related fields.

**ICT sector**: Tunisia has a significant ICT export sector, with established IT services and outsourcing industries providing services to European markets. The ICT sector accounts for approximately 7% of GDP.

**Start-up ecosystem**: Tunisia has an active start-up ecosystem, supported by organizations such as Startup Tunisia, Smart Capital, and the Carthage Entrepreneurship Institute. A growing number of Tunisian start-ups are developing AI-based products and services.

**Governance and regulatory environment**: Tunisia has a relatively sophisticated legal framework for data protection, cybersecurity, and digital commerce, including Law No. 63-2004 on Personal Data Protection, Law No. 5-2004 on Cybersecurity, and Law No. 47-2019 on the Digital Economy.

**Challenges**: Tunisia faces significant challenges including limited domestic investment in AI research, gaps in advanced computing infrastructure, a brain drain of technical talent to Europe and North America, and limited adoption of AI in the public sector.

## Part II: Strategic Pillars and Objectives

### Pillar 1: AI Research and Technology Development

**Objective 1.1: Build National AI Research Capacity**

The Strategy commits to establishing Tunisia as a recognized center for AI research, with the following measures:

(a) Establishing a National Center for Artificial Intelligence Research (Centre National de Recherche en Intelligence Artificielle — CNRIA) as a dedicated national research institution with a mandate covering basic research, applied research, and technology transfer;

(b) Funding at least ten AI research chairs at Tunisian universities by 2025, in partnership with the private sector and international research institutions;

(c) Establishing three AI research excellence clusters, focusing respectively on AI for health, AI for agriculture, and AI for financial technology;

(d) Increasing national research funding for AI-related projects, with a target of doubling annual public funding for AI research by 2025.

**Objective 1.2: Promote Open Innovation**

(a) Establishing an AI open innovation platform to share datasets, models, tools, and research findings among Tunisian researchers, enterprises, and public institutions;

(b) Supporting Tunisian participation in international AI research collaborations, including with the European Research Area, African Union research networks, and Arab League technology initiatives;

(c) Encouraging the development and adoption of open-source AI tools and promoting Tunisian contributions to the global open-source AI community.

**Objective 1.3: Build AI Infrastructure**

(a) Establishing a national AI computing infrastructure, including high-performance computing facilities accessible to researchers and enterprises;

(b) Developing a national AI data lake aggregating high-value public datasets for AI training and research purposes, subject to data protection requirements;

(c) Establishing AI testing and validation environments to support enterprises in testing AI products before deployment.

### Pillar 2: AI Talent and Education

**Objective 2.1: Develop AI Talent**

(a) Reforming ICT and engineering curricula at Tunisian universities and higher education institutions to incorporate AI training, covering both technical AI skills and AI ethics and governance;

(b) Establishing vocational training programs in AI applications for technical and non-technical workers in key sectors;

(c) Implementing a national AI scholarship program to support Tunisian students in pursuing advanced AI studies at domestic and international institutions;

(d) Establishing a national AI skills certification framework.

**Objective 2.2: Retain and Attract AI Talent**

(a) Implementing policies to reduce the brain drain of AI talent, including improved career development opportunities in the public sector and incentives for the private sector;

(b) Establishing a reverse brain drain program to attract Tunisian AI professionals currently working abroad back to Tunisia;

(c) Creating attractive conditions for international AI talent to work in Tunisia, including facilitated work permit procedures.

**Objective 2.3: Promote AI Literacy**

(a) Incorporating AI literacy into secondary school curricula by 2025;

(b) Implementing a national campaign to promote public understanding of AI, its benefits, and its risks;

(c) Developing AI literacy programs for public officials and government employees.

### Pillar 3: AI Applications in Key Sectors

**Objective 3.1: AI in Agriculture**

Tunisia's agriculture sector, which faces significant challenges from climate change and water scarcity, is identified as a priority sector for AI application, including:

(a) AI applications for precision agriculture, including crop yield prediction, automated irrigation management, and pest and disease detection;

(b) AI applications for supply chain management, reducing post-harvest losses and improving market access for smallholder farmers;

(c) AI tools for rural advisory services, providing AI-powered agricultural advice through mobile applications accessible to smallholder farmers.

**Objective 3.2: AI in Healthcare**

(a) AI-assisted diagnostic tools for prevalent diseases in Tunisia, including tuberculosis, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease;

(b) AI applications for epidemic surveillance and public health monitoring;

(c) AI-enhanced health information systems to improve hospital management and patient care;

(d) AI applications for telemedicine and remote healthcare in underserved rural areas.

**Objective 3.3: AI in Financial Services**

(a) AI applications in digital banking, including credit assessment, fraud detection, and intelligent customer service;

(b) AI applications to extend financial inclusion, providing AI-powered financial services to the unbanked population;

(c) AI applications in insurance, including claims processing, risk assessment, and fraud detection.

**Objective 3.4: AI in Public Administration**

(a) AI-enhanced e-government platforms to improve the quality and accessibility of public services;

(b) AI tools for administrative process automation to reduce processing times and costs;

(c) AI applications in tax administration to improve compliance and reduce evasion;

(d) AI applications in customs and border management to facilitate legitimate trade and improve security;

(e) AI applications in judicial administration to support case management and reduce backlogs.

**Objective 3.5: AI in Tourism and Cultural Heritage**

Tunisia's significant cultural heritage and tourism sector is a focus for AI applications including:

(a) AI-powered personalized tourism recommendation systems;

(b) AI applications for cultural heritage digitization and preservation;

(c) AI-enabled smart tourism management systems.

### Pillar 4: AI Governance and Ethics

**Objective 4.1: Develop an AI Governance Framework**

(a) Developing a National AI Ethics Charter aligned with UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of AI and other international frameworks;

(b) Establishing an AI Governance Council, comprising representatives of government, private sector, civil society, and academia, to advise on AI policy and oversee the implementation of the AI Ethics Charter;

(c) Developing sectoral AI governance guidelines for priority sectors, including healthcare, financial services, and public administration;

(d) Reviewing and updating existing legislation — including personal data protection, cybersecurity, and digital economy laws — to ensure they are fit for purpose in the AI era.

**Objective 4.2: Ensure Responsible and Fair AI**

(a) Establishing requirements for transparency and explainability in AI systems used in high-stakes public and private sector contexts;

(b) Promoting the adoption of algorithmic impact assessment practices for AI systems that affect individuals' rights or access to services;

(c) Establishing a framework to identify and address algorithmic bias and discrimination;

(d) Protecting digital rights, including the right to explanation and the right to human review of consequential automated decisions.

**Objective 4.3: Promote Data Privacy and Security**

(a) Reviewing Law No. 63-2004 on Personal Data Protection to assess whether updates are needed to address AI-specific privacy challenges, including automated profiling, biometric data processing, and cross-border AI data flows;

(b) Strengthening the capacity of the Instance Nationale de Protection des Données Personnelles (INPDP) to enforce data protection rules in AI contexts;

(c) Promoting privacy-by-design and data minimization principles in AI development;

(d) Developing cybersecurity standards for AI systems, particularly for critical infrastructure AI applications.

### Pillar 5: AI Ecosystem and Industry Development

**Objective 5.1: Support AI Start-ups and Enterprises**

(a) Establishing a National AI Fund with dedicated funding for AI start-ups and scale-ups, co-invested with the private sector;

(b) Establishing AI-focused regulatory sandboxes to allow AI enterprises to test innovative products with appropriate regulatory flexibility;

(c) Providing technical assistance and business development support to AI enterprises;

(d) Facilitating access to AI infrastructure, including cloud computing and datasets, for AI start-ups.

**Objective 5.2: Attract Foreign AI Investment**

(a) Creating a favorable investment climate for foreign AI investors, including transparent investment regulations, IP protection, and investor support services;

(b) Promoting Tunisia as an AI near-shoring destination for European enterprises;

(c) Establishing AI-focused free zones or special investment zones with attractive fiscal and regulatory conditions.

**Objective 5.3: International Cooperation**

(a) Actively participating in international AI governance forums, including the GPAI, UNESCO's AI governance initiatives, the ITU AI for Good platform, and African Union AI initiatives;

(b) Establishing bilateral AI cooperation frameworks with key partner countries, prioritizing France, Germany, the European Union, and Gulf Cooperation Council countries;

(c) Positioning Tunisia to play an active role in the development of international and African AI standards.

## Part III: Governance of the National AI Strategy

### Implementation Structure

**The Ministry of Communication Technologies and Digital Economy (MTCEN)** is designated as the lead ministry for implementing the National AI Strategy, with responsibility for:

(a) Overall coordination and monitoring of strategy implementation;

(b) Management of the National AI Fund;

(c) Operation of the National AI Governance Council;

(d) International representation on AI governance matters.

**The National Center for Artificial Intelligence Research (CNRIA)** shall, upon its establishment:

(a) Conduct and coordinate national AI research;

(b) Manage the National AI Computing Infrastructure and Data Lake;

(c) Support AI talent development;

(d) Provide technical expertise to the MTCEN and other ministries.

**Sectoral Ministries** shall be responsible for implementing AI applications within their respective sectors in accordance with the sectoral objectives set out in Pillar 3.

**The National AI Governance Council** shall advise on AI policy, promote ethical AI, and monitor the implementation of the AI Ethics Charter.

### Monitoring and Evaluation

The implementation of the National AI Strategy shall be monitored against key performance indicators (KPIs), including:

(a) Number of AI research publications by Tunisian researchers;

(b) Number of AI start-ups established;

(c) Volume of foreign investment attracted to the AI sector;

(d) Number of AI talent trained through national programs;

(e) Level of AI adoption in key sectors;

(f) Public trust in AI as measured by periodic surveys.

Progress reports shall be published annually by the MTCEN.

## Part IV: AI Ethics Charter

### 1. Preamble

Tunisia affirms its commitment to the UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence (2021) and to the responsible, ethical, and human-centered development of artificial intelligence.

### 2. Core Ethical Principles

**Proportionality and Do No Harm**: AI systems shall be used only where necessary and appropriate to achieve legitimate goals, and shall incorporate safeguards to prevent foreseeable harm to individuals, communities, and the environment.

**Safety and Security**: AI systems shall be safe and secure, tested and validated before deployment, and monitored during operation.

**Fairness and Non-Discrimination**: AI systems shall not discriminate on the basis of race, gender, age, disability, religion, political opinion, socioeconomic status, or other protected characteristics, and developers shall actively identify and address algorithmic bias.

**Sustainability**: AI development shall respect environmental limits and contribute to Tunisia's sustainable development goals.

**Privacy**: AI systems shall respect privacy rights and comply with applicable data protection legislation.

**Human Oversight and Determination**: Significant decisions affecting individuals shall involve meaningful human oversight; humans shall retain responsibility and accountability for AI-assisted decisions.

**Transparency and Explainability**: AI systems shall be developed and deployed with a commitment to transparency and explainability appropriate to the context and the sensitivity of the decisions they inform.

**Responsibility and Accountability**: Organizations developing and deploying AI systems bear responsibility for ensuring ethical compliance and shall be accountable for the impacts of their systems.

**Education and Awareness**: Developers, deployers, and users of AI shall receive appropriate education on AI ethics and its practical implications.

**Multi-stakeholder Governance**: The governance of AI shall involve a diverse range of stakeholders, including civil society, affected communities, and international partners.

### 3. Application of the Charter

The AI Ethics Charter applies to all public authorities and is recommended as a voluntary standard for private sector entities operating in Tunisia. The National AI Governance Council shall develop practical guidance for applying the Charter in specific sectors and contexts.
