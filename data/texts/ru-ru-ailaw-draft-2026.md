Russia Advances Draft AI Regulation Framework

Source: Digital Watch Observatory — https://dig.watch/updates/russia-advances-draft-ai-regulation-framework
Published: 16 April 2026

DRAFT FEDERAL LAW ON THE FUNDAMENTALS OF STATE REGULATION OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE TECHNOLOGIES
RUSSIA | 2026 (Draft)

Russia has moved forward with a draft law outlining the fundamentals of state regulation of AI technologies, with the public consultation closed on 15 April 2026. The proposal outlines a structured compliance framework to tighten oversight of AI system development and deployment nationwide.

Key Provisions of the Draft Law:

1. Mandatory Testing for AI Operators
Under the draft, AI system operators would be required to test their systems to identify potential uses that could violate Russian legislation. This introduces a proactive compliance obligation on AI deployers to assess their systems for regulatory risk.

2. Classification of Trusted AI Models
The framework introduces a classification of trusted AI models, which would be subject to formal security verification by authorised federal bodies responsible for technical intelligence countermeasures and information security. This creates a two-tier system distinguishing ordinary AI systems from those that have undergone formal state certification.

3. Certification Process for Quality Compliance
The proposed rules establish a certification process for quality compliance, to be carried out in accordance with procedures defined by the Russian government. These measures aim to create a multi-layered oversight system for AI security and performance in regulated environments.

4. Security Checks by Federal Authorities
Trusted AI models would undergo formal security checks by federal authorities overseeing information protection and technical intelligence. This ensures that state security agencies have visibility into high-risk AI systems.

5. Compliance Framework Scope
These measures aim to create a multi-layered oversight system for AI security and performance in regulated environments.

Significance and Policy Implications:

The proposed framework signals a shift towards tighter state control over how AI is tested, classified, and deployed, particularly in sensitive or high-risk environments. By introducing mandatory testing, security certification and government-defined quality standards, it increases regulatory scrutiny across the AI lifecycle.

The broader implication is a move towards more centralised governance of AI systems, where compliance and risk management become embedded requirements rather than optional best practices.

Timeline:
- Public consultation closed: 15 April 2026
- The draft law is under review by the State Duma following the consultation period
