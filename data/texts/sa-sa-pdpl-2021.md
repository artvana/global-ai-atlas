## Summary and Analysis

### Purpose and Scope

Saudi Arabia's Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL), issued by Royal Decree M/19 of 9 Sha'ban 1442H (corresponding to 16 March 2021) and amended by Royal Decree M/148 of 5 Muharram 1444H (corresponding to 3 August 2022, with the amendments effective 14 September 2023), is Saudi Arabia's first comprehensive, standalone data protection statute. The Law and its implementing Regulations issued by the Saudi Data and AI Authority (SDAIA) establish a modern framework for the protection of personal data in the Kingdom, balancing privacy rights with the Kingdom's Vision 2030 digital transformation objectives and its aspiration to develop Saudi Arabia as a leading data economy in the region.

The Law applies to any processing of personal data of individuals residing in Saudi Arabia, where: the processing is carried out in Saudi Arabia; or the processing is carried out outside Saudi Arabia by a controller established in Saudi Arabia or by a controller processing data about individuals residing in Saudi Arabia. The Law covers automated and non-automated processing of personal data in structured filing systems.

Exemptions include: processing for personal or household purposes; processing by competent authorities for the prevention, investigation, or prosecution of crimes, or security purposes, under applicable legislation; processing for journalistic, literary, or artistic purposes within professional ethical standards where the public interest overrides the privacy interest; and processing of fully anonymized data.

The **Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority (SDAIA)** is the regulatory authority primarily responsible for the PDPL during an initial period, with subsequent transfer of certain responsibilities to the **National Data Management Office (NDMO)** under SDAIA and coordination with the **National Cybersecurity Authority (NCA)** on security-related aspects. The Law contemplates a transition from SDAIA's initial oversight to a longer-term regulatory structure. SDAIA issues implementing regulations, receives and investigates complaints, and administers enforcement. The NCA is separately responsible for cybersecurity standards applicable to personal data.

### Key Definitions

**Personal data** means any data — regardless of its source or form — that leads to the identification of an individual specifically, or that makes it possible to identify them directly or indirectly. This definition is broad and includes online identifiers, location data, biometric data, and combinations of data elements that could identify a person.

**Sensitive data** is given a specific definition in Saudi Arabia's PDPL encompassing: data that reveals racial or ethnic origin; political opinions; religious beliefs; security data (criminal or criminal-related records); health data; biometric data; genetic data; financial data beyond what appears in official documents; data relating to those under legal care (such as children or persons with disabilities); and any other data designated as sensitive by SDAIA.

**Controller** means the party that determines the purposes and means of processing personal data, whether a natural or legal person, public or private.

**Processor** means any party processing personal data on behalf of and under the instruction of the controller.

**Data subject** means the natural person to whose personal data processing relates.

**Consent** must be explicit, specific, and informed. For sensitive data, consent must be in writing. The amended Law (2023) clarifies that consent obtained through deception, coercion, or excessive incentivization is void.

### Lawful Bases and Core Principles

**Lawful Bases.** The PDPL specifies that personal data may be processed only where one of the following grounds applies:

- The data subject's consent;
- The necessity of processing for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party;
- Processing required to protect vital interests of the data subject or others;
- Processing for compliance with regulatory, judicial, or security requirements;
- Processing for scientific, research, or statistical purposes under appropriate safeguards; or
- Processing necessary for achieving the legitimate interest of the controller or a third party, provided this does not prejudice the interests or rights of the data subject.

For **sensitive data**, only explicit written consent, vital interests, regulatory or judicial requirements, and health or medical purposes are recognized as valid bases, reflecting the heightened risk associated with such data.

**Data Quality Principles.** The PDPL requires that personal data be: collected for a legitimate, specific purpose; processed in a manner compatible with that purpose; accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date; adequate and not excessive for the stated purpose; and retained no longer than is necessary.

**Security.** Controllers must implement appropriate technical and organizational security measures commensurate with the risks of the processing. The NCA publishes cybersecurity standards applicable to personal data that controllers must implement. Controllers must ensure that processors implement equivalent security measures.

**Data Breach Notification.** Controllers are required to notify SDAIA of personal data breaches that may affect the rights and interests of data subjects, without undue delay and within a period specified by SDAIA in its implementing regulations (set as 72 hours in SDAIA's 2023 guidance). High-risk breaches must also be communicated to affected data subjects. Breach notifications must describe the nature of the breach, the categories and numbers of data subjects affected, the likely consequences, and the measures taken or proposed.

**Data Minimization and Purpose Limitation.** Controllers must not collect personal data beyond what is necessary for the specified purpose and must not process data for incompatible purposes without new authorization.

**Data Protection Officer.** The 2023 amendments to the PDPL and SDAIA's implementing regulations require certain controllers to appoint a Data Protection Officer: specifically, controllers that process sensitive data at large scale, that engage in systematic and large-scale monitoring of data subjects, or that are public authorities. The DPO must be a qualified professional registered with SDAIA and must be afforded operational independence within the organization.

**Records of Processing.** Controllers must maintain records of their processing activities as required by SDAIA's regulations, documenting purposes, categories, recipients, transfers, retention periods, and security measures.

**Children's Data.** Processing personal data of minors requires parental or guardian consent. Additional protections apply to the collection of children's data through digital services.

### Rights of Data Subjects

The PDPL grants data subjects the following rights:

**Right to be informed.** The right to be informed of the collection and processing of personal data before or at the time of collection, including information about the controller, the purpose, the categories of data, and the data subject's rights.

**Right of access.** The right to obtain from the controller confirmation of whether personal data is being processed and, if so, access to that data along with information about the processing.

**Right to correction.** The right to have inaccurate or incomplete personal data corrected or completed.

**Right to erasure.** The right to have personal data deleted when it is no longer necessary for the original purpose, consent is withdrawn, processing is unlawful, or legal obligations require deletion.

**Right to restriction.** The right to request that processing be restricted where accuracy is contested, an objection is pending, or the data subject needs the data for legal claims.

**Right to portability.** The right to receive a copy of personal data in a readable electronic format for use in other contexts, where technically feasible.

**Right to object.** The right to object to processing based on legitimate interests and to processing for direct marketing.

**Right to withdraw consent.** The right to withdraw consent at any time.

**Right to contest automated decisions.** The right not to be subject to solely automated decisions with significant effects, and to obtain human review and explanation.

**Right to complain.** The right to file a complaint with SDAIA.

Controllers must respond to data subject requests within 30 days, extendable by a further 30 days. Unjustified refusals may be challenged before SDAIA.

### Cross-Border Transfer Rules

The PDPL establishes a comprehensive and, in some respects, stringent framework for international data transfers. Personal data may not be transferred outside Saudi Arabia except where one of the following conditions is satisfied:

- The recipient country or organization has been approved by SDAIA as providing an adequate level of protection;
- The controller has entered into a data transfer agreement approved by SDAIA providing equivalent protection;
- The transfer serves Saudi national interests;
- The transfer is necessary for performance of a contract with the data subject or for pre-contractual steps at the data subject's request;
- The data subject has given consent with full knowledge of the risks;
- The transfer is necessary to protect vital interests; or
- The transfer serves a compelling public interest recognized under Saudi law.

For **sensitive data**, cross-border transfers require SDAIA approval, and the transfer must not conflict with Saudi national interests or regulatory requirements. SDAIA maintains and publishes a list of approved recipient countries and approved transfer mechanisms.

A notable feature of the Saudi framework is its emphasis on national interests as a consideration in cross-border transfers, reflecting the Kingdom's policy approach to data sovereignty and its concern about sensitive data flowing to jurisdictions with adversarial relationships with Saudi Arabia.

### Automated Decision-Making and AI Provisions

The PDPL as amended in 2023 contains provisions addressing automated decision-making and profiling. Data subjects have the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing — including profiling — that produces legal effects or similarly significant effects on their personal, professional, financial, or social situation.

Exceptions to this right apply where:

- The automated decision is necessary for the performance of a contract between the data subject and the controller;
- The decision is required by applicable law and provides appropriate safeguards; or
- The data subject has given explicit consent.

Where an automated decision is made, the controller must: inform the data subject that the decision was made by automated means; provide a meaningful explanation of the factors taken into account and their significance; allow the data subject to express their point of view; enable the data subject to contest the decision; and ensure that a qualified human with appropriate authority can review and, if appropriate, override the automated decision.

SDAIA's implementing regulations elaborate on these obligations, requiring controllers that use AI or machine learning systems for decision-making affecting data subjects to: document the logic and parameters of the automated system; conduct periodic reviews of the system for accuracy and bias; maintain audit logs sufficient to reconstruct automated decisions; and provide data subjects with case-specific explanations of decisions affecting them.

Given Saudi Arabia's significant focus on AI as part of its Vision 2030 and the National Strategy for Data and AI (NSDAI), SDAIA has a particular interest in ensuring that AI-driven decisions in high-impact sectors — including banking, insurance, public services, and employment — are subject to appropriate accountability and transparency mechanisms. SDAIA has issued supplementary guidance specifically addressing the use of AI in credit assessment and public sector decision-making.

### Enforcement and Penalties

SDAIA is the primary enforcement authority, with coordination with the NCA on cybersecurity-related violations. SDAIA is empowered to conduct inspections, require production of information, issue compliance orders, suspend processing, and impose administrative fines. Enforcement decisions are subject to an appeal process before SDAIA, with ultimate judicial review before the administrative courts.

**Administrative fines** under the PDPL are structured as follows:

- For violations of the Law's general obligations — including processing without lawful basis, failure to honor data subject rights, inadequate privacy notices, and failure to appoint a DPO where required: fines up to **SAR 5,000,000** (approximately USD 1.33 million).
- For violations involving sensitive data, unauthorized cross-border transfers, serious security breaches, and violations of specific prohibitions in the Law: fines up to **SAR 10,000,000** (approximately USD 2.67 million).
- For repeat violations (violations committed again within three years of a final enforcement decision): fines doubled, up to **SAR 20,000,000** (approximately USD 5.33 million) and in some circumstances up to **SAR 50,000,000** (approximately USD 13.3 million) for the most serious repeat violations.

**Criminal penalties** apply to willful violations for personal gain or to cause harm: imprisonment and/or fines determined by the competent criminal court. Legal persons may also be held criminally liable through their officers.

In determining the appropriate penalty, SDAIA considers: the nature and gravity of the violation; the number of affected data subjects; the duration of the violation; whether the violation was willful or negligent; the harm caused or potential harm; the financial size of the violating entity; and any remedial measures taken.

### Implementation Trajectory

The PDPL entered into force for the original 2021 text after a one-year grace period, with the 2023 amendments (some of which introduced new obligations) coming into effect from September 2023. SDAIA has issued comprehensive implementing regulations covering consent, cross-border transfers, sensitive data, breach notification, and data subject rights. SDAIA has also published sector-specific guidance for financial services, healthcare, and e-commerce, and has launched a data protection compliance certification program for controllers. Saudi Arabia's data protection framework is developing rapidly in response to the Kingdom's AI ambitions, and SDAIA's dual mandate — promoting AI and data use while protecting personal data — reflects the government's approach of enabling innovation through clear, predictable regulation.
