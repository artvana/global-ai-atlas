# Delaware House Bill 333 — Delaware Artificial Intelligence Commission Act

**HB 333 | Delaware General Assembly, 153rd Session**
*Delaware — Enacted July 17, 2024 | Effective July 17, 2024*

---

## Overview

Delaware House Bill 333, the Delaware Artificial Intelligence Commission Act, establishes the Delaware Artificial Intelligence Commission within the Office of the State Auditor. The Commission is a study and advisory body tasked with assessing the risks and benefits of generative artificial intelligence across all three branches of Delaware's state government, developing recommendations for AI governance and procurement, and reporting annually to the General Assembly. The Commission has no independent enforcement authority and imposes no direct regulatory obligations on the private sector.

The law reflects Delaware's cautious, deliberative approach to AI governance — choosing to study the technology and develop evidence-based recommendations rather than immediately enacting prescriptive regulations. The Commission is scheduled to automatically terminate after 10 years unless reauthorized.

## Key Provisions

### Establishment of the Commission

- Creates the Delaware Artificial Intelligence Commission as a formal body within the Office of the State Auditor.
- Commission membership includes representatives from the legislative, executive, and judicial branches of state government, as well as members with expertise in technology, law, civil rights, and business.
- Members are appointed through a combination of legislative leadership appointments and gubernatorial appointments.
- Commission members serve without additional compensation beyond their existing government salaries (for government officials) or as unpaid public servants (for private sector members).

### Mandate and Responsibilities

- Assess the risks and benefits of generative AI technology across state government operations, including use in public benefits administration, law enforcement, courts, and education.
- Review national and international policy developments in AI governance, including federal agency guidance, other states' legislation, and international frameworks.
- Develop recommendations for AI governance standards applicable to Delaware state agencies.
- Develop recommendations for AI procurement policies to ensure the state acquires AI systems that meet safety, transparency, and civil rights standards.
- Identify uses of AI in state government that pose unacceptable risks and recommend prohibitions or restrictions.
- Recommend legislation to the General Assembly addressing AI governance for both the public and private sectors.

### Reporting Requirements

- Commission must submit annual reports to the General Assembly and the Governor.
- Reports must include: a summary of AI uses identified in state government, an assessment of associated risks, recommendations for governance and procurement standards, and any proposed legislation.
- Reports are public records and must be published on the Commission's official website.

### Sunset Provision

- The Commission automatically terminates 10 years after the effective date of the Act unless the General Assembly reauthorizes it.
- This sunset reflects legislative intent to reassess the Commission's utility as the AI regulatory landscape matures.

### Limitations

- The Commission has no power to promulgate binding regulations, issue enforcement actions, or impose penalties.
- Its recommendations are advisory only and require separate legislative or executive action to become binding.
- The Act imposes no direct requirements on private-sector AI developers or deployers operating in Delaware.
