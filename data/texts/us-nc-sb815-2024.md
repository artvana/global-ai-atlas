# North Carolina Protecting Elections from Deceptive AI Act (SB 815)

**Enacted October 3, 2024 | Codified at G.S. Chapter 163, Article 22F**

---

## Purpose

The Act prohibits the knowing use of artificial intelligence to generate or distribute deceptive political advertisements that falsely depict a candidate or elected official doing or saying something they did not do or say, without required disclosures.

---

## Definitions

**"Deceptive AI-generated media"** means images, audio, or video that:
(a) depicts a candidate, elected official, or political party chair doing or saying something they did not actually do or say; and
(b) was created or substantially altered using AI or other digital technology in a manner designed to mislead a reasonable person.

**"Clearly false content"** means synthetic media that a reasonable person would believe to be authentic but is fabricated or materially altered.

**"Candidate"** has the same meaning as in G.S. 163-278.6.

**"Election communication"** means any message distributed for the purpose of influencing North Carolina voters in connection with any election.

---

## Section 1 — Prohibition on Deceptive AI Political Advertisements (G.S. 163-274.4)

**(a) Prohibition.** It is unlawful for any person to:
(1) Create, distribute, publish, or cause to be distributed or published a deceptive AI-generated advertisement concerning any candidate or elected official within 90 days of an election, unless the advertisement includes a required disclaimer;
(2) Create or distribute a materially deceptive AI-generated depiction of a candidate casting a ballot, operating a voting machine, or performing any act related to the casting of a ballot.

**(b) Required disclaimer.** Any election communication that includes AI-generated content depicting a real candidate or elected official must include a clear and conspicuous disclosure stating: "This [image/audio/video] has been generated or substantially altered using artificial intelligence."

The disclosure must be:
- In the same language as the advertisement
- Displayed for the duration of any video content
- Spoken aloud in any audio content
- Of sufficient size and contrast to be clearly readable in any visual content

**(c) Exemption.** This section does not apply to:
(1) A bona fide news broadcast or news story
(2) Satire or parody that is clearly labeled as such and that a reasonable person would understand is not factual
(3) Archival, documentary, or informational content
(4) Advertisements depicting fictional scenarios that no reasonable person would mistake for fact

---

## Section 2 — Civil Enforcement

**(a) Private right of action.** Any candidate, elected official, or political party depicted in a deceptive AI-generated election communication in violation of this Article may bring a civil action against the responsible person for:
(1) Injunctive relief to prevent further distribution
(2) Actual damages
(3) Statutory damages of not less than $1,000 and not more than $25,000 per violation
(4) Reasonable attorneys' fees and costs

**(b) Expedited relief.** Given the time-sensitive nature of electoral harm, courts shall give priority to motions for preliminary or temporary injunctive relief in actions brought under this Article. Courts may grant injunctive relief within 48 hours of a properly filed motion if the court finds likely violation and irreparable harm.

---

## Section 3 — Criminal Penalties

A person who knowingly violates this Article with the intent to influence the outcome of an election commits a Class 1 misdemeanor. A second or subsequent violation within four years of a prior conviction is a Class I felony.

---

## Section 4 — Responsibility of Platforms

Any online platform that receives actual notice of a deceptive AI-generated election communication in violation of this Article and fails to remove it within 48 hours of receiving notice from the depicted candidate, elected official, or political party may be subject to civil liability as set out in Section 2.

Platforms that establish and enforce policies prohibiting deceptive AI political content, including takedown procedures, are entitled to a safe harbor from liability under this section.

---

## Effective Date

This Act became effective upon ratification and applies to election communications distributed on or after October 3, 2024.
