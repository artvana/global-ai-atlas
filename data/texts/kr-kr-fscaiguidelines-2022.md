              GUIDELINES ON THE OPERATION OF AI IN THE FINANCIAL SECTOR


1. Purpose and Scope of Application

A. The Guidelines aim to enhance trustworthiness throughout the entire process of planning/design, evaluation/verification, introduction/operation and monitoring related to the development, commercialization and use of artificial intelligence (hereinafter "AI") systems in the financial sector, thereby promoting the activation of AI and contributing to securing customer trust in financial services.

B. The Guidelines apply to financial companies that directly or indirectly use, or intend to use, AI systems for the business of providing financial services and financial products (excluding cases where the use of AI systems has no impact on customers, such as internal employee management within financial companies or the simple efficiency improvement of routine work), as well as to non-financial companies that directly or indirectly use, or intend to use, AI systems in the business of providing finance-related services such as product recommendation and credit evaluation (collectively hereinafter "financial companies, etc.").

C. An "AI system" means a software or hardware system designed by humans which, given a specific objective, operates in the physical or digital dimension by acquiring data and recognizing the environment, interpreting the acquired data, inferring knowledge or processing information, and determining the best action to achieve that objective.

D. Financial companies, etc., taking comprehensive account of factors such as the characteristics of the service in which the AI system is used, customer characteristics, and the number of customers of the service in which the AI system is used, may adjust the scope of application of the Guidelines, etc., within a range that does not undermine the purpose of the Guidelines, namely the activation of AI and the securing of customer trust in financial services.


2. Establishment of Governance

A. Financial companies, etc. shall establish ethical principles and standards for the use of AI, taking into account the values pursued by the organization and the principal contexts in which AI is used.

B. Financial companies, etc. shall, in order to assess and manage the potential risks of AI systems, specifically define the roles, responsibilities and authority of organizational members across the entire AI system life cycle. Financial companies, etc. may separately establish an AI Ethics Committee for organizational management consistent with the AI ethics principles and standards.

C. Financial companies, etc. shall identify and assess the potential risks that may arise from AI use throughout the entire AI system life cycle, and shall prepare a risk management policy necessary for managing potential risks arising from AI use, including by reviewing measures to manage and minimize such risks. The risk management policy shall include matters concerning the operation of systems for the protection of consumer rights, the management of AI models and training data, communication with the supervisory authorities in the event of problems related to the AI system, and the promotion of an in-company culture of accountability for AI, among other operational matters.

D. Where a financial company, etc. uses an AI system for services that may pose significant risks to individual rights, safety and freedom — such as unjust discrimination against individuals (hereinafter "high-risk services") — it shall establish appropriate internal control activities and approval procedures and designate a person responsible for approval. The person responsible for approval shall hold a position capable of carrying out responsible duties, but may concurrently hold similar duties such as Chief Risk Officer, Credit Information Protection and Management Officer, or Chief Information Security Officer.


3. Planning and Design Stage of the AI System

A. Financial companies, etc. shall examine whether the purposes for which the AI system is used are consistent with the ethical principles and shall assess the social, economic and cultural impacts and potential damage that may arise from the use of AI, taking into account the context of use.

B. Financial companies, etc. may flexibly use AI systems taking into account the purposes and characteristics of the AI system and the characteristics of customers. However, where the AI system entirely replaces human decision-making, or replaces important decisions, financial companies, etc. shall design the AI system so that they can effectively supervise and control the AI system and maintain accountability.


4. Development Stage of the AI System

A. Financial companies, etc. shall, for proper AI training, investigate and verify the source, quality and bias of data, and shall examine methods for improving data quality, including periodic data updates.

B. Where an AI system uses sensitive information under Article 23(1) of the Personal Information Protection Act and Article 18 of its Enforcement Decree, or other similar privacy-related information, financial companies, etc. shall take sufficient measures for the safe use of information, such as obtaining prior consent or applying de-identification measures; shall assess the necessity of using such information; and shall ensure that there is no possibility of re-identification, leakage or misuse of such information during data processing.

C. Where financial companies, etc. use AI systems in financial services, etc., for which there is a duty to explain to customers under the relevant laws and regulations, or where AI systems are used in high-risk services, they shall consider explainability from the development stage, identify available explainable AI techniques, and make efforts to introduce them.


5. Evaluation and Verification Stage of the AI System

A. Financial companies, etc. shall select and manage appropriate performance target levels and performance measurement indicators for the AI system, taking comprehensive account of the AI ethical principles, the purpose of the AI system, the impact on customers and the degree of potential damage caused by error cases, and any trade-offs among AI performance measurement indicators.

B. Financial companies, etc. shall select and manage appropriate fairness target levels and fairness assessment indicators for the AI system, taking comprehensive account of the AI ethical principles, the purpose of the AI system, the impact on customers and the degree of potential damage by fairness assessment indicator, and any trade-offs among AI fairness assessment indicators. Where imbalances are found according to the selected fairness assessment indicators, technical and managerial efforts shall be made to improve fairness.

C. Where financial companies, etc. use AI systems in financial services, etc., for which there is a duty to explain to customers under the relevant laws and regulations, or where AI systems are used in high-risk services, they shall transparently apply appropriate AI techniques such as explainable AI, verify whether context-appropriate explanations are produced, and shall make efforts to improve explainability to a reasonable level within a range that does not undermine the stability and reliability of the AI system.


6. Introduction, Operation and Monitoring Stage of the AI System

A. When operating AI systems facing customers, financial companies, etc. shall, in line with the nature of the AI system, notify customers of appropriate remedies, including whether AI is being used, the consumer's rights under the relevant laws and regulations such as the right to explanation and the right to object, and the procedures for filing objections or complaints.

B. Financial companies, etc. shall periodically monitor the performance of the introduced AI system and verify the possibility of performance improvement, including by examining the necessity of data re-training.

C. Financial companies, etc. shall verify whether the AI system is exposed to possibilities of misuse and abuse, such as data poisoning attacks or adversarial attacks by customers or third parties, and shall make efforts to introduce, within the scope of available technologies, measures that can minimize cases of misuse and abuse. Financial companies, etc. shall also reflect procedures by which they can be continuously notified of security vulnerabilities in AI development environments — such as open-source-based AI development frameworks — and shall make efforts to build the best possible security systems.


7. Special Provisions on Outsourcing of AI System Tasks

A. Where financial companies, etc. intend to outsource the development and operation of AI systems to external institutions, they shall prepare risk management directives so that the trustee institution can develop and operate the AI system in compliance with these Guidelines and the AI ethical principles and risk management policies established on the basis of the Guidelines, and shall ensure that the risks involved in operating the AI system are not enlarged compared with the case where the financial company directly develops and operates the AI system.

B. Financial companies, etc. shall establish and operate periodic reporting and inspection systems to verify whether the development and operation of AI systems by external institutions are carried out in accordance with the risk management directives, and shall ensure that strict ex-ante checks are performed for high-risk services, including prior verification by the financial company, etc. of plans for AI development and operation, and the establishment of measures and reporting procedures in the event of consumer harm.

C. Where consumer harm arises in connection with the development and operation of AI systems, the financial company, etc. and the external institution shall prepare clear liability provisions and procedures for handling damages, in order to prevent delays in compensation, etc.
