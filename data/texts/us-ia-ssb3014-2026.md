 A BILL FOR 
 An Act relating to the use of artificial intelligence systems and related software by state agencies for employment and other purposes. 
 
 
 
 BE IT ENACTED BY THE GENERAL ASSEMBLY OF THE STATE OF IOWA: 
 
 Section 1. NEW SECTION. 8.94 Inventory of artificial intelligence systems. 
 1. The department shall maintain an inventory of artificial intelligence systems, as defined in section 19A.1, used by state agencies, as defined in section 19A.1. The department shall issue guidance to state agencies identifying the data elements to be collected and submitted to the department for such inventory, including but not limited to the purpose and uses of the systems. 
 2. The department shall post the inventory on the department&rsquo;s internet site as soon as practicable. The department shall submit a report with an updated annual inventory from the previous year to the general assembly by January 15. 
 3. The department may withhold information from the inventory if it determines disclosure of the information would jeopardize the security of state information technology assets, or as otherwise prescribed by state law. 
 Sec. 2. NEW SECTION. 19A.1 Definitions. 
 As used in this chapter, unless the context otherwise requires: 
 1. a. &ldquo;Artificial intelligence system&rdquo; means a machine-based system to which all of the following apply: 
 (1) The system can, for a given set of human-defined objectives, make predictions, recommendations, or decisions influencing real or virtual environments that, when used, may directly impact the public. 
 (2) The system uses machine-based and human-based inputs to perceive real and virtual environments, abstract such perceptions into models through analysis in an automated manner, and use model inference to formulate options for information or action. 
 b. &ldquo;Artificial intelligence system&rdquo; includes but is not limited to systems that use machine learning, large language models, natural language processing, and computer vision technologies, including generative artificial intelligence. 
 2. &ldquo;Automated employment decision-making tool&rdquo; means any software that uses algorithms, computational models, or artificial intelligence techniques, or a combination thereof, to materially automate or replace human decision making regarding employment, including but not limited to wages and other compensation, hiring, selection for recruitment, discipline, promotion, and termination. &ldquo;Automated employment decision-making tool&rdquo; does not include any software used primarily for basic computerized processes, such as calculators, spell-checking tools, autocorrect functions, spreadsheets, electronic communications, or any tool that relates only to internal management affairs such as ordering office supplies or processing payments, and that does not materially affect the rights, liberties, benefits, safety, or welfare of any individual in the state. 
 3. &ldquo;Directly impact the public&rdquo; means the use of an artificial intelligence system that would control, have a material impact on, or meaningfully influence the outcome of activities that impact the safety, welfare, or rights of the public. Such activities include but are not limited to assessments or decisions about individuals, including in law enforcement, housing, hiring and employment, financial, educational, or health care contexts; decisions regarding access to or eligibility for government benefits; decisions about child welfare; or decisions about the functioning of emergency services or critical infrastructure. 
 4. &ldquo;State agency&rdquo; means any unit or official of state government in the executive, legislative, or judicial branch, including but not limited to an authority, board, commission, council, department, or elected or appointed official. 
 Sec. 3. NEW SECTION. 19A.2 Disclosure of automated employment decision-making tools. 
 1. A state agency that uses an automated employment decision-making tool shall publish a list of such tools on the state agency&rsquo;s internet site no later than ninety days after each tool is used. The state agency shall submit an annual report on such usage in the previous year to the general assembly by January 15 each year. If the department of administrative services uses such tools on behalf of another state agency, the department of administrative services shall comply with this section on the state agency&rsquo;s behalf. 
 2. The list and annual report required by subsection 1 shall include all of the following information: 
 a. A description of the automated employment decision-making tool used by the state agency. 
 b. The date that the state agency began use of the tool. 
 c. A summary of the purpose and use of the tool. 
 d. Any other information deemed necessary and relevant by the state agency. 
 Sec. 4. NEW SECTION. 19A.3 Artificial intelligence systems &mdash; prohibited uses. 
 1. A state agency shall not use artificial intelligence systems in a manner that affects any of the following: 
 a. The existing rights, benefits, or privileges of employees pursuant to an existing collective bargaining agreement under chapter 20. 
 b. The existing representational relationships among employee organizations or the collective bargaining relationships between an employer and an employee organization. 
 2. A state agency shall not use artificial intelligence systems in a manner that alters the rights, benefits, or privileges of state agency employees, including but not limited to terms and conditions of employment, civil service status, and collective bargaining rights pursuant to chapter 20. 
 3. A state agency shall not use artificial intelligence systems in a manner that results in the discharge; transfer or loss of position, including involuntary temporary transfer; or reduction in wages, employment benefits, or hours of nonovertime work of state agency employees. 
 4. A state agency shall not transfer existing duties or functions performed by state agency employees to an artificial intelligence system. 
 
 
 EXPLANATION 
 The inclusion of this explanation does not constitute agreement withthe explanation&rsquo;s substance by the members of the general assembly. 
 This bill relates to the use of artificial intelligence systems and related software by state agencies for employment and other purposes. 
 The bill defines &ldquo;state agency&rdquo; as any unit or official of state government in the executive, legislative, or judicial branches, including but not limited to an authority, board, commission, council, department, or elected or appointed official. 
 The bill requires the department of management (DOM) to maintain an inventory of artificial intelligence systems, as defined in the bill, used by state agencies. The bill requires DOM to issue guidance to state agencies regarding the information to be included in the inventory, post the inventory on DOM&rsquo;s internet site, and submit an annual report on the inventory to the general assembly. The bill authorizes DOM to withhold information from the inventory in specified circumstances. 
 The bill requires a state agency that uses an automated employment decision-making tool, as defined in the bill, for employment-related purposes to publish a list of such tools on its internet site no later than 90 days after each tool is used and submit an annual report to the general assembly on its use of such tools. If the department of administrative services (DAS) uses such tools on behalf of another state agency, DAS shall comply with these requirements on the state agency&rsquo;s behalf. The bill specifies content of the list and report. 
 The bill prohibits a state agency from specified uses of artificial intelligence systems that affect collective bargaining rights or other employment rights, benefits, or privileges of state agency employees or that result in discharge, transfer or loss of position, or reduction in wages, employment benefits, or hours of nonovertime work of state agency employees. The bill prohibits a state agency from transferring existing duties or functions performed by state agency employees to an artificial intelligence system.
