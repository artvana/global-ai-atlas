# New York Senate Bill 8391 — Digital Replicas of Deceased Performers Act Amendment

**Senate Bill No. 8391**

*New York — December 11, 2025*

---

## Key Provisions

### Purpose and Scope

SB 8391 amends New York's post-mortem right of publicity law as it applies to digital replicas and AI-generated likenesses of deceased performers, significantly strengthening protections by closing two major loopholes.

### Removal of the "Reasonable Observer" Standard

Prior law required that a digital replica of a deceased performer be realistic enough to deceive a reasonable observer in order to be actionable. SB 8391 removes this standard, meaning that digital replicas need not be indistinguishable from the real person to be covered by the law. Even stylized or partially synthetic digital recreations that are recognizable as depicting the deceased performer may be actionable.

### Elimination of the Disclaimer Carve-Out

Under prior law, use of a deceased performer's digital replica could be permitted if accompanied by a sufficient disclaimer informing the audience that the content uses a digital replica. SB 8391 eliminates this disclaimer carve-out, meaning disclaimers alone are no longer sufficient to authorize use of a deceased performer's digital replica without the consent of the estate or authorized party.

### Expansion to Sound Recordings and Live Musical Performances

SB 8391 expands the categories of actionable uses to include:
- Sound recordings featuring a deceased performer's digital voice replica
- Live performances of musical works that use a deceased performer's digital likeness or voice

Previously, the post-mortem right of publicity statute applied primarily to audiovisual works; the amendment explicitly extends protection to sound-only recordings and live music performance contexts.

### Civil Remedy

The estate or authorized representative of a deceased performer may bring a civil action for:
- Injunctive relief
- Actual damages (including lost licensing revenue)
- Disgorgement of profits attributable to the unauthorized use

### Effective Date

The act takes effect immediately upon the Governor's signature on December 11, 2025.
