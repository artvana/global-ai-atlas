ELECTRONIC MARKED-UP VERSION
GOVERNMENT OF PUERTO RICO
SENATE OF PUERTO RICO
S.B. 769
October 7, 2025
Presented by Mr. Rivera Schatz
Co-author Mr. Rosa Ramos
Referred to the Committee on Science, Technology and Artificial Intelligence
ACT
To create the "Puerto Rico Artificial Intelligence Development and Innovation Institute Act," for the purposes of establishing the public policy of the Government of Puerto Rico on the proactive and responsible management of Artificial Intelligence (AI) to foster the sustainable and resilient development of our economy, society, infrastructure, and governmental institutions; to make Puerto Rico a regional and global hub for the development and application of Artificial Intelligence technologies; to define and establish its nature, purposes, functions, governance structure, and operation that promote collaboration between the public, private, and academic sectors; to establish provisions and principles of transparency, ethics, evaluation, and accountability in all processes and stages of development, innovation, and adoption of Artificial Intelligence technologies; to encourage integration with open data policies to maximize the value of governmental data in Artificial Intelligence projects; to create and capitalize the Puerto Rico Artificial Intelligence Development and Innovation Fund; to amend Sections 2023.01, 2061.01, and 5010.01 of Act 60-2019, as amended, known as the "Puerto Rico Incentives Code"; to amend Article 6 of Act 75-2019, known as the "Puerto Rico Innovation and Technology Service Act"; to amend Article 2 of Act 13-2017, as amended, known as the "Act to Authorize the Creation of a Non-Profit Corporation, which shall Adopt the Concept of Enterprise Puerto Rico"; to amend Article 3 of Act 214-2004, as amended, known as the "Puerto Rico Science, Technology and Research Trust Act"; and for other related purposes.
STATEMENT OF MOTIVES
Artificial Intelligence (AI) constitutes one of the most important industries in the world in the 21st Century, generating annually trillions of dollars in economic impact according to international organizations such as the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD) and the World Economic Forum. According to projections, exponential growth is estimated that could reach $15.7 trillion by 2030, driven by advances in machine learning, big data processing, and applications in sectors such as health, education, infrastructure, manufacturing, and public services. The United Nations (UN) and the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) have highlighted how Artificial Intelligence has transformed the global landscape, with a 300% increase in investments during the past decade, positioning nations such as the United States, China, and the European Union at the forefront of this technological revolution. In the Americas, countries such as Mexico and Brazil have begun to integrate it into their national strategies, although many face lags in competitiveness due to the lack of adequate digital infrastructure and coherent public policies.
In 2020, the federal Congress approved the "National Artificial Intelligence Initiative Act of 2020," which establishes a coordinated program throughout the federal government to accelerate research and application of Artificial Intelligence with the objective of promoting economic prosperity and national security. The mission of the "National Artificial Intelligence Initiative" is to ensure United States leadership in research and development of this technology, as well as to prepare the present and future workforce for its integration.
The "National Artificial Intelligence Initiative" provides a general legal framework to strengthen and coordinate the activities of research, development, demonstration, and education of Artificial Intelligence in all instrumentalities of the United States government, in cooperation with academic, industrial, non-profit organizations and civil and social organizations. The work of this initiative is organized in six (6) strategic pillars: innovation, advancement of trustworthy Artificial Intelligence, education, training, infrastructure, and international cooperation, which provide us with a guide for the legislation needed in this new field of technology and thus stay at the forefront of the times.
For the past two decades, at the governmental level public policies have been formulated and executed aimed at diversifying our economy; by way of example, we can mention: the creation of the Science, Technology and Research Trust (Act 214-2004), promoting the export of services (Act 20-2012), attracting resident investor individuals (Act 22-2012), the creation of the DMO (Act 17-2017), the new Incentives Code (Act 60-2019), among others. In Puerto Rico, the manufacturing sector continues to be a significant economic pillar. Data from the Fiscal Oversight Board indicate that manufacturing represented $48.8 billion in economic activity in the year 2022, or 43% of the island's Gross Domestic Product (GDP). It is followed by the sectors of Real Estate ($21.1 billion), Retail Sales ($7.2 billion), Finance and Insurance ($5.3 billion), Government ($5.1 billion), and Health and Social Services ($4.7 billion).
However, the development and novel application of Artificial Intelligence emerges as a viable option to strengthen and diversify the economy, modernize our infrastructure, advance business competitiveness, improve education, health, and public safety in keeping with the times. Likewise, this tool fosters the transformation of governmental services so that they are efficient and cost-effective. Equally, the new public policy for the adoption of Artificial Intelligence must avoid digital inequalities, privacy gaps, or algorithmic discrimination, and promote a responsible approach that preserves ethical values and generates inclusive benefits.
To materialize this potential, various jurisdictions have established research and development (R&D) institutes and centers with public-private governance, operational autonomy, and a strong results orientation, thereby achieving the acceleration of applied research, technology transfer, and the formation and attraction of companies and talent. Among the relevant examples in the United States is the "Georgia Research Alliance" (GRA), a 501(c)(3) organization that articulates research universities, government, and industry in the state of Georgia. The GRA has demonstrated that an independent entity, with a voice from academia and industry and governmental visibility, can attract substantial investment, consolidate eminent chairs, and catalyze the commercialization of intellectual property.
Other successful models of leading jurisdictions are the "Massachusetts Technology Collaborative" (MassTech) and the "Virginia Innovation Partnership Corporation" (VIPC). Both integrate quasi-public governance with operational agility and specialized divisions in talent, business and academic ecosystems, and innovation. According to a recent analysis published by Forbes magazine, states such as California, Massachusetts, and Virginia lead the Artificial Intelligence revolution through key metrics such as specialized education, technology hubs, technology jobs, business adoption, and investment, with California hosting more than 450 Artificial Intelligence companies and robust research programs at universities. MassTech operates with clear goals to build strategies, strengthen connections, and assist companies, including an AI Hub that fosters public-private collaborations with $400 million in investments. This is complemented by the "Massachusetts Green High-Performance Computing Center" (MGHPCC), a non-profit consortium of universities with state backing for advanced computing infrastructure; combining public oversight with agile execution. On the other hand, the VIPC emphasizes investment in the creation of new companies ("startups") and commercialization to attract external capital.
In addition, models such as the "Empire AI Consortium" of New York and the Research Triangle Park of North Carolina offer valuable principles to adapt in Puerto Rico. Empire AI, a public-private consortium of leading universities, focuses on responsible Artificial Intelligence innovation through a cutting-edge computing center temporarily housed at the University of Buffalo (SUNY), promoting state collaboration for research and development, with emphasis on ethical practices and shared access to high-performance infrastructure. This model highlights the importance of multi-sector university alliances to accelerate AI adoption, avoiding duplication and maximizing economic impact through collaborative innovation. For its part, the "Research Triangle Park," the largest research park in North America in a 14-county region, operates under principles of collaboration between universities, industry, and government, attracting massive investments (such as $700 million from Genentech for 420 jobs and more than $2 billion from Johnson & Johnson for 500 jobs), fostering an innovation ecosystem with a high concentration of specialized professionals. In fact, according to CNBC, North Carolina was selected as the best State for business in 2025.
On the other hand, the National Science Foundation (NSF), in collaboration with the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) and other federal agencies, created in 2020 the AI Institutes network that is funded through competitive multi-million dollar investments (exceeding $500 million) intended to consolidate Artificial Intelligence research centers with national impact. These institutes seek to apply AI to strategic challenges such as sustainable agriculture, health, STEM education, food security, and human-machine collaboration, while fostering the formation of specialized talent and cooperation between academia, industry, and the public sector. This initiative offers a valuable model for structuring research and development consortia that drive local innovation, reinforce agricultural and climate resilience, and strengthen the knowledge economy.
In Puerto Rico there are precedents that validate similar structures. Act 214-2004, as amended, known as the "Puerto Rico Science, Technology and Research Trust Act" created the Puerto Rico Science, Technology and Research Trust, a private non-profit entity with public mandate, capable of administering state, federal, and private funds, developing programs and projects, and collaborating with the government, academia, and industry. On the other hand, Invest Puerto Rico, a private non-profit organization, was created by Act 13-2017, as amended, known as the "Act to Authorize the Creation of a Non-Profit Corporation, which shall Adopt the Concept 'Enterprise Puerto Rico'", in order to promote Puerto Rico as a competitive investment jurisdiction to attract new businesses and capital investments to the island. In strategic sectors such as advanced manufacturing, biosciences, technology, and aerospace. Similarly, Act 17-2017, known as the "Act for the Promotion of Puerto Rico as a Destination" created the Destination Marketing Organization (DMO), another non-profit organization to make Puerto Rico a global tourism destination, promoting the visitor economy and the events and conventions industry. At the municipal level, the Municipality of Bayamón has been an anchor of innovation by collaborating with the private non-profit entity Engine-4 Foundation in laboratories and smart city pilots, operating an urban living lab that integrates data, infrastructure, and intelligent solutions for the benefit of its citizens. Today, Engine-4 and the Municipality of Bayamón have a technology creation space that is a benchmark in the Caribbean, which includes a laboratory for Artificial Intelligence, 5G, and NB-IoT, unique on the island.
In order to position Puerto Rico as a regional and global model in responsible Artificial Intelligence, this Act creates the Puerto Rico Artificial Intelligence Development and Innovation Institute with its own legal personality and recognition as an entity of public interest. The Institute will have the responsibility of promoting the commercialization of intellectual property and the application of Artificial Intelligence through competitive conditions for businesses. Likewise, it will foster the development of talent for sustainable and resilient growth, based on principles of transparency and accountability that can guide the creation of Artificial Intelligence ecosystems in Puerto Rico, aimed at generating 21st Century jobs, forming and attracting capital, transforming traditional industries and governmental structures at the state and municipal levels.
The consolidation of resources, processes, and specialized talent through the creation of this new Artificial Intelligence Institute will increase the critical mass to produce excellent science (peer-reviewed publications, patents, standards, and reference data), capture external competitive funds, and accelerate innovation with impact. This capacity will allow addressing the challenges of the 21st Century: resilience to disasters, health, education, infrastructure, security and privacy, and the development of Artificial Intelligence for local, regional, and global markets.
The relationship between the Artificial Intelligence Institute and the Government of Puerto Rico will focus on three main areas: (i) economic development strategies and management, especially the commercialization of intellectual property, technology licensing, the creation and expansion of technology-based companies, and the development and attraction of talent trained in the latest trends and best practices of Artificial Intelligence; (ii) providing technical solutions for agencies and public corporations (advanced analytics, applied Artificial Intelligence, digital modernization, and cybersecurity); and (iii) the co-development of public innovation to address social challenges and foster prosperity and well-being at the individual and community level.
The Act proposes that Engine-4 Foundation select an entity through an open, transparent, and competitive mechanism as a partner and operational arm of the new Artificial Intelligence Institute, in direct and close collaboration with the government, academia, and the private sector, and the Municipality of Bayamón as headquarters, including existing institutions with public policy mandates of high relevance to Artificial Intelligence, such as: Invest Puerto Rico, the Science, Technology and Research Trust, and research and development centers of the University of Puerto Rico. This proposal is justified due to the scope, resources, programs, and capabilities of Engine-4 Foundation: (1) end-to-end technical advantage difficult to match on the island (hyperscale infrastructure suitable for experimentation, research, and development in AI; (2) a production-grade 5G/NB-IoT laboratory for wireless experimentation and device testing; (3) a facility of more than 41,000 square feet in the smart city corridor of Bayamón, connecting the Urban Train with university centers such as Universidad Ana G. Méndez, Universidad Politécnica, Universidad de Puerto Rico, and Universidad Sagrado Corazón, and a makerspace with more than 19 industrial 3D printers and precision instrumentation to take AI ideas to instrumented prototypes and pilots.
This Act ensures and consolidates the integration of a non-governmental Institute, but of public interest, with diverse governance, diversified funds, and transparency mechanisms inspired by proven models. This offers clarity, continuity, and speed of implementation, with safeguards of transparency, ethics, and performance evaluation.
Compliance with standards of transparency and open governments, as established in Act 122-2019, as amended, known as the "Puerto Rico Government Open Data Act," recognizing that governmental data is essential for innovation, continuous improvement, and tangible results, balance is fundamental in proactive disclosure with privacy and security protections. Artificial Intelligence, by depending on big data, must integrate with these principles to avoid ethical risks, discrimination, or cybersecurity gaps, while generating economic value through the reuse of data in open formats.
Therefore, with the creation of this Institute, we position Puerto Rico on the path to be a competitive and resilient destination, where every public dollar mobilizes external resources, accelerates the responsible adoption of Artificial Intelligence, and converts knowledge into socioeconomic impact, aligned with the global evolution of data as a strategic asset, preserving our values and natural resources for future generations.
BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY OF PUERTO RICO:
Article 1.— Title
This Act shall be known as the "Puerto Rico Artificial Intelligence Development and Innovation Institute Act."
Article 2.— Declaration of Public Policy
It shall be the public policy of the Government of Puerto Rico to promote competitiveness, research, innovation, and activities that increase the long-term sustainable economic growth of Puerto Rico through the development and application of technologies related to Artificial Intelligence. This new Institute seeks to provide the appropriate environment, opportunities, and tools to improve governmental efficiency, health, education, and public safety, as well as to modernize our infrastructure and foster the development and attraction of companies and talent, in order to offer a better quality of life and achieve community well-being.
This Institute seeks to guarantee a collaborative relationship between the private sector, academia, and the Government of Puerto Rico for the implementation of a public policy of responsible Artificial Intelligence, based on stability, certainty, and credibility. Likewise, it seeks to promote and exercise controls in order to achieve processes based on transparency and accountability, focused on agility and measurable results: generation of intellectual property, modernization of our infrastructure, attracting foreign direct investment, fostering local capital investment, the attraction and establishment of new businesses, talent development and job creation, as well as strengthening high-impact activities, the supply and value chain, and the creation of conglomerates in strategic sectors.
At the governmental level, it shall be public policy that Artificial Intelligence be administered in such a way that an optimal level of efficiency is achieved in public agencies, the availability and access to essential services is expanded in a cost-effective manner, and public and private initiatives are fostered that aim to eliminate the digital divide in our society. The public policy that is adopted and promulgated is consistent with the objective of having technology and its use further inserted into the daily life of our citizens. In addition, the development and application of Artificial Intelligence-based technologies is of vital importance to meet the expectations of citizens regarding education, health, housing, and public safety, as well as modern governance standards.
Article 3.— Definitions
For purposes of this Act, the following words or phrases shall have the meaning expressed below:
(a) "Operational Agreement" – Means the binding instrument that regulates the operational relationship between the Institute and Engine-4 Foundation operational partner, as well as with collaborating entities, as provided by this Act.
(b) "Agency" — Means any board, body, examining tribunal, commission, public corporation, office, division, administration, bureau, department, authority, official, employee, person, entity, or any instrumentality of the Executive Branch of the Government of Puerto Rico.
(c) "Divisions or Centers of Excellence" – Means the programmatic units of the Institute oriented to applied research, technology transfer, and talent formation in prioritized domains.
(d) "Code" — Refers to the "Puerto Rico Incentives Code," created under Act 60-2019.
(e) "Code of Ethics" – Means the internal policy of ethics, integrity, conflict of interest management, and transparency adopted by the Institute in compliance with this Act.
(f) "Consortium" – Means the alliance led by the University of Puerto Rico, between public and private universities for the establishment and operation of the AI computing center.
(g) "Open Data" – Means data made publicly available in open, reusable, and redistributable formats, in accordance with Act 122-2019, known as the "Puerto Rico Government Open Data Act."
(h) DDEC – Means the Department of Economic Development and Commerce, in accordance with Act 141-2018, known as the "Act for the Implementation of the 2018 Reorganization Plan of the Department of Economic Development and Commerce."
(i) "Engine-4" – Means Engine-4 Foundation, Operational Partner – non-profit corporation organized under the laws of Puerto Rico and Section 501(c)(3) of the United States Internal Revenue Code selected through a competitive Request for Proposal (RFP) process, responsible for serving as headquarters and operational arm of the Institute.
(j) "Beneficiary Entity or Beneficiary Entities" — Shall mean any natural or juridical person, including any governmental entity, to which the assistance of the Institute is provided.
(k) "FCTIPR" – Means the Puerto Rico Science, Technology and Research Trust, created in accordance with Act 214-2004, as amended, known as the "Puerto Rico Science, Technology and Research Trust Act."
(l) "Fund" – Means the Puerto Rico AI Development and Innovation Fund.
(m) "Governor" — Means the Governor of Puerto Rico.
(n) "Government" — Means the Government of Puerto Rico.
(o) "Responsible Artificial Intelligence" – Means the development and application of Artificial Intelligence with principles of ethics, non-discrimination, applicability, privacy, and data governance.
(p) Innovation — Means a collaborative, creative, and applied process of discussion and analysis of ideas and solutions to challenges. Innovation presumes the creation of something new or improved through services, processes, technologies, products, or models.
(q) "Institute" – Means the Puerto Rico Artificial Intelligence Development and Innovation Institute created through this Act.
(r) "Invest PR" – Means Invest Puerto Rico, created under Act 13-2017, as amended.
(s) "Board" – Means the Board of Directors of the AI Institute.
(t) "Municipality" — Means the municipalities of the Government of Puerto Rico.
(u) "Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs)" — Are businesses as defined in Act 60-2019 (Puerto Rico Incentives Code).
(v) "Person" — Means any natural or juridical person; provided that, in the case of juridical persons, they may be of public or private nature, and be organized or existing under the laws of the Government of Puerto Rico, of the United States of America, or of any State of the United States of America.
(w) "Strategic Plan" — Means the "Puerto Rico Artificial Intelligence Development and Innovation Strategic Plan," that the Institute shall establish.
(x) "PRITS" - Means the Puerto Rico Innovation and Technology Service, as created by Act 75-2019, as amended, known as the "Puerto Rico Innovation and Technology Service Act."
(y) "Intellectual Property" — Means patents, inventions, formulas, processes, designs, patterns, know-how, copyrights, business secrets, literary, musical or artistic compositions, trademarks, factory seals, trade names, brand names, franchises, licenses, contracts, methods, programs, systems, procedures, goodwill, campaigns, surveys, studies, trials, projections, estimates, customer lists, technical data, or any other similar property.
(z) "Proposer or Proposers" — Means the person who requests assistance from the Institute.
(aa) "Institute Projects" — Means those Artificial Intelligence development and innovation projects that the Board determines qualify to be promoted by the Institute.
(bb) "Strategic Projects" — Means those projects as provided in the Incentives Regulation in accordance with the provisions of Section 2014.01 of Act 60-2019.
(cc) "Business Secrets" — The definition of this term shall be made as provided in the Puerto Rico Rules of Evidence.
(dd) "University" – Means the University of Puerto Rico.
(ee) "Private University" – Means any licensed private university.
Article 4.— Creation and Nature
The Institute is created as a non-profit corporation, with its own legal personality and patrimony, of perpetual succession, independent of the Government of Puerto Rico, although recognized in law as an entity of public interest. It shall not constitute a governmental agency, corporation, or instrumentality; it shall have public purposes and shall operate in an open, transparent, and accessible manner.
(a) The DDEC is designated as transitional organizing entity. The Institute shall have its main headquarters and operational partner selected through an open and competitive Request for Proposal (RFP) process conducted by the Board of Directors. through an agreement at Engine-4 in the Municipality of Bayamón. The Operational Agreement between the Institute and Engine-4 and the operational partner shall have an initial term of no less than five (5) and no more than ten (10) years, renewable for equal terms, subject to performance metrics and reports defined in the Agreement itself.
(b) The DDEC, in collaboration with Engine-4 the entity selected through the competitive process, shall be responsible for incorporating and organizing the Institute in accordance with Act 164-2009, as amended, known as the "General Corporations Act."
(c) The Secretary of the DDEC, in collaboration with Engine-4 the entity selected through the competitive process, shall be responsible for convening and coordinating a Nominations Committee to recommend the initial members of the Board according to Article 6 of this Act.
(d) The DDEC shall be responsible for safeguarding and administering the initial funds for organization expenses until the Board is constituted.
(e) The DDEC, in collaboration with Engine-4 and the Municipality of Bayamón the entity selected through the competitive process, shall be responsible for preparing the Operational Agreement, convening the first meeting of the Board for its swearing-in and approval of the Operational Agreement, which shall include an administrative and operational implementation plan in accordance with the best practices for similar organizations, and applicable state laws.
(f) The Operational Agreement shall define the scope of services and responsibilities to be delegated to Engine-4 the entity selected through the competitive process, including, but not limited to, implementing the governance structure, coordinating programmatic execution, administering personnel and resources, and requirements for periodic reports, compliance and termination clauses for cause, and annual performance review processes, established within the agreement.
(g) The personnel of the Institute shall not be considered public employees and shall be subject to policies of merit, equal opportunity, integrity, and performance, in accordance with regulations approved by the Board.
(h) The transitional role of the DDEC shall cease upon the swearing-in of the Board and approval of the Operational Agreement, at which time all operational authority shall be transferred to the Institute in accordance with this Act.
(i) The operational and decision-making autonomy of the Institute is guaranteed. No agency or instrumentality of the Government of Puerto Rico may invalidate the public policy or managerial determinations adopted by the Institute in accordance with this Act, nor exercise control over its actions beyond what is provided herein. The Institute shall be subject only to the supervision expressly established by the laws applicable to non-profit corporations of public interest and the terms of this Act.
Article 5.— Purposes, Functions and Duties of the Institute
In order to comply with the objectives of this Act, the Institute shall carry out the following tasks, among others:
Develop and implement a strategic plan with short, medium, and long-term goals, in order to achieve the public policy defined in this Act. The strategic plan shall avoid duplication of tasks and efforts carried out by other entities, nor shall it replace or compete with state programs whose principal mission is different from the research and development of Artificial Intelligence. On the contrary, it shall collaborate and coordinate with said entities through agreements, competitive subcontracting, calls, and the purchase of services.
(a-1) Execute and/or co-execute fundamental scientific research in Artificial Intelligence and applied sciences, oriented to discovery, publications, and advanced talent, as a basis for new intellectual property and enabling capabilities of the ecosystem.
(a-2) Develop translational research that converts findings into prototypes, patents, models, and commercializable products, and makes possible technology transfer and science-based ventures, without prejudice to alliances with universities and companies.
(b) Lead Puerto Rico's insertion into the main international evaluations of Artificial Intelligence capabilities and readiness, generating periodic diagnostics to identify and close institutional, regulatory, scientific, and infrastructure gaps, in order to sustainably raise the economic competitiveness of Puerto Rico.
(c) Promote research and innovation activities in Artificial Intelligence and related disciplines, focused on high-impact challenges and opportunities for Puerto Rico in strategic sectors, infrastructure, and governmental efficiency, as well as priority topics such as education, economic development, governmental efficiency, health, and public safety.
(d) Develop and coordinate training programs, workshops, scholarships, and certification in Artificial Intelligence for students, professionals, public servants, and communities.
(e) In coordination with the University, FCTIPR, and private universities, facilitate the transfer of technology, the responsible commercialization of innovation results, and the creation of spin-off companies and industrial conglomerates related to Artificial Intelligence.
(f) In coordination with the DDEC, the University, private universities, and the FCTIPR, support the formation and growth of companies belonging to the Artificial Intelligence sector, resulting in the creation of new jobs and the development, attraction, and retention of talent, through entrepreneurship, acceleration, growth, and commercialization efforts, expanding regional industries and innovation ecosystems by leveraging local companies with academia and multinational companies.
(g) In coordination with PRITS, provide technical advice and support to agencies of the Government of Puerto Rico in the adoption of Artificial Intelligence solutions.
(h) Provide technical advice and support to municipalities in the adoption of AI solutions.
(i) Establish and manage Divisions or Centers of Excellence, including municipal alliances, with academic organizations and private entities.
(j) Establish nodes and facilities in other regions of Puerto Rico inspired by models such as the Research Triangle Park in North Carolina to foster collaborative ecosystems of innovation and entrepreneurship at a regional scale.
(k) Develop and establish a consortium between the principal public and private universities of Puerto Rico, without limitation to other duly accredited universities in other jurisdictions, and the private sector, in order to establish a cutting-edge AI computing center as part of the Institute's infrastructure, located at the facilities of Engine-4 the entity selected through the competitive process, and with capacity to expand to other nodes. Said center shall provide access to high-performance computing power to support research, development, and innovation projects in Artificial Intelligence, encompassing the entire life cycle, from basic research to practical application and commercialization.
(l) Coordinate with PRITS, DDEC, Invest PR, and the FCTIPR the execution of strategic projects and Institute projects aligned with the Artificial Intelligence public policy outlined in this Act, as well as strengthen the economic competitiveness of Puerto Rico through the adoption of Artificial Intelligence technologies in the strategic sectors defined by Invest PR and the DDEC.
(m) Annually prepare and publish a Puerto Rico AI Capabilities Map, identifying actors and strengths, and a Complementarity Plan that details coordination mechanisms to avoid duplication and maximize synergies using globally recognized models and tools.
(n) Adopt and execute a Regulation for the administration of the Puerto Rico Artificial Intelligence Development and Innovation Fund, in accordance with Article 10 of this Act.
(o) Establish competitive, open, and transparent calls so that universities, consortia, and non-profit organizations or companies may propose Artificial Intelligence projects aligned with the strategic plan.
(p) Adopt and execute a Responsible Artificial Intelligence Policy that incorporates, among others, principles of security, non-discrimination, applicability, privacy, and data governance.
(q) Adopt and execute a Code of Ethics.
(r) Adopt and execute an Intellectual Property Policy, which must include developing and administering intellectual property, as well as entering into licensing and royalty participation agreements.
(s) Integrate principles of open data in accordance with Act 122-2019, ensuring that data generated or used in Artificial Intelligence projects is proactively disclosed in open formats, except for privacy or security exceptions, and collaborating with PRITS for data governance.
(t) Establish mechanisms for evaluation, measurement of results, and public reports on its execution and performance.
(u) Adopt internal regulations, policies, and procedures for its proper operation.
(v) Enter into contracts, agreements, memoranda of understanding, and subcontracts with public and private entities, local and international.
(w) Acquire, lease, administer, and dispose of movable and immovable property; and manage necessary permits and authorizations.
(x) Accept donations, legacies, and grants; and receive income from services and activities of its mission.
(y) Hire personnel and consultants, fix their compensation in accordance with market practices for specialized talent, and establish merit, evaluation, and development systems.
(z) Establish subsidiaries or affiliated entities necessary for operational or compliance purposes, provided that all profit or net income is reinvested in the fulfillment of its mission.
(aa) Establish scholarship and recognition programs for students and professionals in the area of Artificial Intelligence, including the granting of aid for specialized graduate studies and the creation of residencies or internships at the Institute, in order to develop and retain local talent.
(bb) Implement collaboration initiatives with experts and organizations in other jurisdictions of the United States and at the international level, such as visiting researcher programs or exchanges, that allow the transfer of knowledge to Puerto Rico and elevate the global exposure of the local Artificial Intelligence ecosystem.
Article 6.— Composition, Rights, Powers and Functions of the Board
The Board of Directors shall be the governing body of the Institute and shall establish its public policy to comply with the objectives of this Act.
The Board shall be constituted by nine (9) eleven (11) members:
(a) The Secretary of the DDEC or his designee (ex officio).
(b) The Chief Innovation and Information Officer of the Government (PEII) or his designee (ex officio).
(c) The Mayor of the Municipality of Bayamón, or his designee.
(d) (c) The Executive Director of the FCTIPR or his designee.
(e) (d) The President of the University or his designee Four (4) representatives of the University of Puerto Rico designated by its President: (1) the President of the University of Puerto Rico or his designee; (2) a representative from the Office of Information Systems of the Central Administration of the University (OIS) (3) an academic with technical expertise in computer science or AI, engineering, data science, and cybersecurity; and (4) an academic with expertise in social sciences, humanities, or ethics.
(f) (e) One (1) representative of the private sector with experience in Artificial Intelligence development, innovation, and projects to be appointed by the Governor.
(g) (f) Three (3) representatives of the private sector with experience in Artificial Intelligence development, innovation, and projects to be appointed by the Board.
The members appointed by the Governor and by the Board (except the ex officio) shall have staggered terms of three (3), four (4), and five (5) years, respectively; vacancies shall be filled for the remaining term through the corresponding appointment mechanism. They may be re-nominated for additional non-consecutive terms by the Board, in accordance with the regulations.
Vacancies shall be filled for the remaining term through the corresponding appointment mechanism. Members who are not officials shall exercise their functions ad honorem, receiving only reimbursement of reasonable expenses incurred in the performance of their duties, as determined by the Board through regulations.
The private citizens of the private sector designated as members of the Board shall not be considered officials for all purposes, including the provisions of Act 1-2012, as amended, known as the "Organic Act of the Office of Governmental Ethics of Puerto Rico."
Both the Board of Directors and the executive and operational personnel of the Institute shall be subject to strict regulations on conflict of interest control and the applicable provisions of the Organic Act of the Office of Governmental Ethics to protect intellectual property financed with State funds.
The Board shall select from among its members a President and a Vice-President, who shall replace the President in his absence, as well as a Secretary, who shall be elected from among those considered officials.
A majority of the members of the Board shall constitute quorum for all purposes and agreements shall be taken by the majority of those present. Provided that, any action necessary or permitted at any meeting of the Board, shall be authorized without a meeting, provided that all members of the Board give their written consent to said action. In such case, the written document shall be entered in the minutes of the Board. It is also provided that the members of the Board may participate, respectively, in any meeting of the Board or any committee thereof, by telephone conference, or other means of communication, through which all persons participating in the meeting can hear each other simultaneously. The participation of any member of the Board in the manner described above shall constitute attendance at said meeting. Provided that, when participation in any meeting is by telephone conference, the members may not collect per diems.
The members of the Board shall not be liable in their personal capacity in cases of monetary claims for damages derived from their actions, or from the breach of their obligations as members of the Board, except for acts or omissions that are not in good faith or that constitute intentional improper conduct or knowing violations of the law, or for any transaction where the member receives an undue personal benefit. The Institute may indemnify any person who is or has been a member of the Board, officer, employee, or agent of the Institute under the same parameters that a corporation may indemnify its directors, officers, employees, or agents under Act 164-2009, as amended, known as the "General Corporations Act."
The Board shall have, among others, the following powers, duties, and faculties:
(a) Establish the public policy and administrative and operational mechanisms that ensure compliance with the objectives of this Act.
(b) Approve the strategic plan and budgets.
(c) Supervise compliance with the Operational Agreement.
(d) Appoint an Executive Director aligned with the operational agreement, establish his duties and powers in harmony with what is provided in this Act, and fix the compensation based on salary competitiveness studies for similar positions in Puerto Rico, and in other jurisdictions comparable with Puerto Rico. The Executive Director shall occupy a position of free selection, free removal, and shall serve in accordance with the parameters established by the Board. He must be recruited based on his experience, knowledge, administrative and managerial capacity in the area of management of institutions with similar purposes to the Institute.
(e) Appoint other officials as required to achieve efficient operation and compliance with the objectives of this Act.
(f) Create and supervise the performance of the university consortium for the AI computing center, in accordance with Article 9 of this Act.
(g) Approve the establishment of Divisions or Centers of Excellence in addition to those defined in Article 8 of this Act.
(h) Establish Advisory Committees and Working Committees.
(i) Approve nodes and facilities in other regions of Puerto Rico.
(j) Approve the Fund Regulation, in accordance with Article 10 of this Act, as well as all those regulations that are necessary to advance the purposes of this legislation.
(k) Approve the AI Capabilities Map and the Complementarity Plan.
(l) Ensure the integration of open data principles in accordance with Act 122-2019.
(m) Approve policies, procedures, guidelines, including, but not limited to, the Responsible AI Policy, a Code of Ethics, and the Intellectual Property Policy, as well as those administrative determinations that ensure sound governance of the Institute.
(n) Hold at least four (4) annual meetings and one (1) public hearing for input from interested parties.
(ñ) Any other action and determination that is consistent with the purposes of this legislation.
Article 7.— Duties and Responsibilities of the Executive Director
The Executive Director shall execute the strategic plan of the Institute, and shall direct and supervise the administrative affairs of the Institute, the consortium, the divisions or centers of excellence, and any committee, regional node or facility, subsidiary, or entity created by the Board, and in accordance with the Collaborative Agreement. In addition, he shall manage alliances, projects, and funds, and shall be responsible for compliance with the policies, procedures, and guidelines approved by the Board, including the Responsible AI Policy, the Code of Ethics, and the Intellectual Property Policy, among others, in accordance with Articles 13 and 14 of this Act. He shall also be responsible for complying with Article 15 (Annual Report and Audits) and Article 10 (Capitalization of the Institute and Funds) of this Act.
The Board may delegate to the Executive Director those powers and responsibilities that it considers appropriate. The Executive Director shall have the authority to hire, administer, and sanction the employees of the Institute, and to determine their compensation, within the approved budgetary limits and applicable internal regulations. The Executive Director shall establish personnel policies, participate in the preparation of the strategic plan and the annual budget, contract the services of external professionals, attend to the day-to-day operation of the Institute, and execute the instructions of the Board. He may also recommend the creation of executive positions, subject to approval by the Board. In addition, he shall prepare the annual report provided in Article 15 of this Act and any other reports required by the Governor, the Legislative Assembly, or the Board.
Article 8.— Divisions or Centers of Excellence, and Advisory Committees of the Institute
The following Divisions or Centers of Excellence shall be created within the Institute, each with an Advisory Committee whose members shall be designated by the Board:
(a) Research and Development Division, focused on fundamental and translational projects, intellectual property development, prototypes, and technological validation, with access to advanced computing and data and experimentation infrastructure.
(b) Business Ecosystems Division, focused on supporting startups and regional expansion.
(c) Investments Division, focused on providing seed financing and venture capital.
(d) Commercialization Division, focused on technology transfer and licensing.
(e) Talent Development Division, focused on the training and retention of the workforce and on attracting specialized talent.
(f) Strategic Alliances Division, focused on multi-sector agreements between government, academia, and the private sector.
(g) Responsible Artificial Intelligence and Ethics Advisory Committee, composed of up to five (5) members who are experts in technological ethics, law, data protection, and representatives of citizens, designated by the Board. This Committee shall advise the Board and the Executive Director on the elaboration and implementation of the Responsible AI Policy, the Code of Ethics, and on the evaluation of possible risks in the projects of the Institute.
(h) Ethics and Social Sciences in AI Division: Focused on the oversight and investigation of the ethical, social, labor, and ecological impact of Artificial Intelligence systems. This division shall have binding authority to require environmental impact evaluations (including water and energy consumption of servers), conduct algorithmic bias audits, and halt projects that represent unreasonable risks to civil rights or the natural resources of Puerto Rico.
The Advisory Committees shall advise on specific goals, ensuring alignment with the strategic plan. The Board may authorize other divisions or centers of excellence, and advisory and working committees, in order to advance the public policy of this Act, within parameters of sound administration.
Article 9.— Consortium for the Artificial Intelligence Computing Center
The Board University of Puerto Rico shall coordinate the initial governance to create the shall create a consortium between public and private universities to establish a cutting-edge AI computing center as part of the Institute's infrastructure, inspired by the Empire AI model to promote shared and responsible access. The Board shall approve the policies, procedures, and guidelines necessary to allow the consortium to establish collaborative agreements, contribute and receive resources, and facilitate shared access to facilities, data, and talent, integrating open data governance.
Article 10.– Capitalization of the Institute and Puerto Rico Artificial Intelligence Development and Innovation Fund
The Puerto Rico Artificial Intelligence Development and Innovation Fund is created. The Fund shall be nourished by:
(a) An annual amount of ten million dollars ($10,000,000) from the income collected annually by the Department of Treasury and intended to nourish the Economic Incentives Fund ("FIE") administered by the DDEC, income collected annually under Section 5010.01 of Act 60-2019, as amended.
(b) Minimum annual contribution of five thousand dollars ($5,000) from Resident Investor Individuals with active decrees under Act 60-2019 (or Act 22-2012), in good standing.
(c) Companies shall receive a supplementary tax credit of 10% (increasing from 50% to 60%) if they contract their Research & Development activities through the Institute subject to a fiscal cap established by the DDEC and the Secretary of Treasury. The effectiveness of the increases to the tax credits contemplated in the amendments to Section 3030.01 of Act 60-2019, which raise the cap from 50% to 60%, shall be strictly conditioned on the evaluation, certification, and compliance with the Principle of Fiscal Neutrality and the Certified Fiscal Plan.
(d) The Board shall identify and manage allocation money for the Artificial Intelligence Computing Center.
(e) Federal grants related to AI, requested by the Institute.
(f) Private grants, sponsorships, donations, and contributions from philanthropic organizations, companies, and academic institutions in and outside of Puerto Rico.
(g) Annual contributions from academic institutions participating in the consortium, established by agreements and regulations.
Nothing shall limit income from services, reinvested in the mission of the Institute, nor the attraction of massive investments of economic impact as in referenced models.
The Board shall establish, through policies and regulations, competitive processes, and transparent criteria based on best practices and the best public policy value, to receive and evaluate applications and disburse grant and venture capital funds, prioritizing projects aligned with the strategic plan, measurable impact, and ethical compliance. It shall include open calls for eligible persons and proposers, such as universities, consortia, municipalities, and private companies.
Article 11.— Relationship with the University
The University of Puerto Rico shall be a strategic academic partner of the Institute and shall have representation on the Board in accordance with Article 6 of this Act. It may contribute researchers, faculty, students, laboratories, data, and other capabilities, and shall benefit from access to facilities, programs, and resources of the Institute.
The Institute and the University shall enter into agreements for sponsored research, shared use of facilities and equipment, co-direction of Divisions or Centers of Excellence, and academic personnel training and exchange programs, respecting university autonomy.
The ownership and exploitation of intellectual property resulting from collaborative projects shall be governed by specific agreements, which may contemplate co-ownership and distribution of benefits in accordance with contributions and applicable regulations, as approved by the Board.
Article 12.— Intergovernmental and Municipal Coordination
The Institute shall coordinate with PRITS the identification of challenges, opportunities, projects, initiatives, and AI pilots that contribute to governmental modernization. PRITS shall serve as a liaison between the Institute and the government and its agencies.
The Institute shall support, through a collaboration agreement or professional services contracts, the efforts that the DDEC carries out to advance the eligible research and development activities carried out in Puerto Rico that are related to AI incentivized through Section 3030.01 of Act 60-2019, as amended, (Tax Credit for Science and Technology), efforts to promote Innovative SMEs in Puerto Rico, incentivized by the DDEC through Regulation MO-DEC-015 (Programs and Economic Incentives at the DDEC), as well as efforts to promote business incubators, in accordance with Section 2100.03 of Act 60-2019, as amended, (Business Incubator Program).
In addition, the Institute may enter into agreements and contracts with municipalities for implementations, urban laboratories, talent training, and validation of solutions in real environments.
Article 13.— Intellectual Property and Commercialization
The intellectual property generated in projects of the Institute shall be governed by its Intellectual Property Policy and by specific agreements with collaborators, which may contemplate co-ownership and revenue participation schemes, as approved by the Board. The Institute may participate in spin-off companies or receive royalties or other returns, always in compliance with its non-profit nature and reinvesting all net income in its mission, as approved by the Board.
Article 14.— Policies on Ethics, Conflicts of Interest, Data, Privacy and Compliance
The Institute shall adopt a Code of Ethics applicable to members of the Board, officials, and personnel, that includes, at a minimum: annual disclosure of interests; mandatory recusal in matters that present conflict; prohibition of undue gifts; and standards for contracting with related parties through safeguards and evaluation by independent third parties. The Board shall ensure the implementation of the Code and the presentation of annual reports. Contracts between the Institute and other related entities must be evaluated and approved by the Board without participation of members with potential conflict of interest.
The Institute shall adopt a Responsible Artificial Intelligence Policy that establishes principles, safeguards, and risk evaluation processes for internal projects and collaborations. Guidelines on data governance, privacy, and information security shall be adopted, with access controls, data minimization, and cybersecurity measures. Personal data processed by the Institute shall be handled in accordance with applicable laws and good practices, and internal policies approved by the Board.
Article 15.— Metrics, Reports and Audits
The Institute shall present an Annual Report to the Governor and the Legislative Assembly that shall include, among other things, the following:
(a) Audited financial statements.
(b) Complete report of activities, contracts, and transactions.
(c) Status and progress of programs, divisions or centers of excellence, regional nodes or facilities, the university consortium, initiatives and projects, with quantifiable achievements vs. goals (metrics, projects executed, agreements signed, and use of public funds, among others).
(d) Information on new projects and educational activities.
The Board shall appoint an Auditor General for semi-annual audits of funds. The Institute shall be subject to the jurisdiction of the Office of the Comptroller and faculties of the Office of Management and Budget (OGP) for performance audits and use of public funds.
Article 16.— Transitional Provisions
The DDEC, in collaboration with Engine-4 the University of Puerto Rico and PRITS, shall have one hundred and eighty (180) days from the effective date of this Act to incorporate and organize the Institute in accordance with Act 164-2009, convene and coordinate a Nominations Committee (in collaboration with Engine-4, the University of Puerto Rico, and PRITS) to recommend the initial members of the Board, safeguard and administer the initial funds for organization expenses until the Board is constituted, prepare the Operational Agreement, convene the first meeting of the Board, and approve the Collaborative Agreement with Engine-4 the entity selected through the competitive process.
Within one hundred and eighty (180) days of the constitution of the Board, a transparent and competitive process shall be carried out to select the Executive Director and the operational partner, the strategic plan shall be prepared, approved, and published, and the policies, codes, procedures, and guidelines required by this Act shall be prepared and approved.
Within one hundred and eighty (180) days of the constitution of the Board, the Institute shall execute the agreements or covenants with the Government to implement the provisions established in this Act related to the transfer of public funds for the operation, administration, and execution of the Institute's public policy.

Article 23.- Non-Existence of Proper Right
Nothing provided in this Act shall be interpreted as the creation of a private right of action in favor of third parties against the Institute, its Board, executives, or personnel, except as provided by other applicable laws.
Article 24.— Supremacy
The provisions of this Act shall prevail over any rule or regulation that is not in harmony with them.
