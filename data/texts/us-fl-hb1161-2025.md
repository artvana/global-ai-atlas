# Florida House Bill 1161 (Brooke's Law) — Platform Obligations for Deepfake Intimate Image Removal

**House Bill No. 1161**

*Florida — June 1, 2025*

---

## Key Provisions

### Purpose and Scope

Named after Brooke Shields, who publicly advocated for the legislation, HB 1161 (Brooke's Law) imposes obligations on online platforms to establish victim-reporting mechanisms and remove confirmed nonconsensual deepfake intimate images within 48 hours.

### Platform Reporting Mechanism Requirement

Online platforms must establish and maintain an accessible reporting mechanism that allows victims to report nonconsensual AI-generated or digitally altered intimate images. The reporting mechanism must be prominently accessible — not buried in terms of service — and must allow victims to submit reports without undue burden.

### 48-Hour Removal Obligation

Upon receiving a valid report, the platform must:
1. Conduct an internal review to confirm the reported content constitutes nonconsensual deepfake intimate imagery
2. Remove confirmed content within 48 hours of the valid report

### Civil Liability

Failure to maintain the required reporting mechanism, or failure to remove confirmed nonconsensual deepfake intimate imagery within the 48-hour window, creates civil liability for the platform. Victims may bring civil actions for damages, injunctive relief, and attorney's fees.

### Coverage

The law applies to AI-generated and digitally altered intimate images, not only to content created with actual photographs. It covers both wholly synthetic imagery and AI-manipulated content derived from real images.

### Effective Date

The act takes effect October 1, 2025.
