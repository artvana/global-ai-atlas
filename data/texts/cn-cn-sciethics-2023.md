# Measures for Scientific and Technological Ethics Review

**Promulgating Entities:** Ministry of Science and Technology; Ministry of Education; Ministry of Industry and Information Technology; and other relevant State Council departments  
**Promulgation Date:** 2023-06-13  
**Effective Date:** 2023-12-01  
**Basis:** National Mid- and Long-Term Plan for Science and Technology Development; Opinions of the General Office of the CPC Central Committee and the General Office of the State Council on Strengthening the Ethics Governance of Science and Technology  

---

## Chapter I: General Provisions

**Article 1:** These Measures are formulated in order to regulate scientific and technological ethics review activities, strengthen the ethics governance of science and technology, prevent and control ethics risks of scientific and technological activities, and promote the healthy development of science and technology, in accordance with the relevant laws and regulations and the Opinions of the General Office of the CPC Central Committee and the General Office of the State Council on Strengthening the Ethics Governance of Science and Technology.

**Article 2:** These Measures apply to the ethical review of scientific and technological activities, including basic research, applied research, technology development, and transformation of scientific and technological achievements, carried out by scientific and technological activities entities (hereinafter "entities") that engage in activities in the territory of the People's Republic of China and that touch upon ethical risks in the areas of life sciences, medicine, artificial intelligence, and other fields as prescribed in these Measures.

"Scientific and technological activities entities" referred to in these Measures include universities, research institutes, medical and healthcare institutions, enterprises, and other organizations that carry out scientific and technological activities.

**Article 3:** The ethics review of scientific and technological activities shall follow the principles of:

(1) **Ethics first** — scientific and technological activities shall place the well-being of humanity, the protection of the dignity and rights of research subjects, and the maintenance of public order as paramount, and shall not pursue the advancement of science and technology at the expense of ethics;

(2) **Lawfulness and compliance** — scientific and technological activities shall comply with the requirements of laws, regulations, and relevant ethics standards;

(3) **Risk management** — the potential ethics risks of scientific and technological activities shall be identified, assessed, and managed, and corresponding risk prevention and control measures shall be adopted;

(4) **Independent and objective review** — ethics review shall be conducted independently, objectively, and fairly, free from undue influence from the research proponents, funders, or other parties with a material interest in the outcome of the review;

(5) **Informed consent** — where scientific and technological activities involve human subjects or research participants, their informed consent shall be obtained in a manner that is genuine, voluntary, and free from coercion.

**Article 4:** The Ministry of Science and Technology, in conjunction with relevant departments, is responsible for coordinating and guiding the ethics review work for scientific and technological activities nationwide. Relevant departments of the State Council are responsible for the organization and management of ethics review work within their respective systems and fields in accordance with the law. Local science and technology departments shall be responsible for guiding and supervising the ethics review work within their respective regions.

**Article 5:** Scientific and technological activities entities shall establish or maintain ethics review committees (hereinafter "ethics committees") to be responsible for the ethics review of the scientific and technological activities conducted by the entity.

---

## Chapter II: Scope of Ethics Review

**Article 6:** Scientific and technological activities that must undergo ethics review include the following:

(1) Research involving human subjects, including biomedical research, psychological research, social science research, and behavioral research involving human participants;

(2) Research involving human biological specimens, human genetic resources, or human embryonic stem cells;

(3) Research on, development of, or application of artificial intelligence that may have a material impact on the rights, dignity, or safety of individuals or groups, or that involves significant social risks;

(4) Research on neurotechnology that may affect human cognition, consciousness, emotion, or behavior;

(5) Research involving the use of new biological technologies, including gene editing and synthetic biology, applied to humans or in ways that may affect human health or ecology;

(6) Any other scientific and technological activities prescribed by the state as requiring ethics review.

**Article 7:** For artificial intelligence research and technology development activities, ethics review is specifically required for:

(1) AI systems that are used or intended for use in high-stakes decision-making affecting individuals' fundamental rights, including employment decisions, judicial decisions, medical diagnosis and treatment, and financial credit assessments;

(2) AI systems used for social monitoring, tracking, profiling, or behavioral analysis of individuals or groups;

(3) AI systems that generate or manipulate content in ways that may seriously mislead users or undermine the authenticity of information in public discourse;

(4) AI systems that interact directly with humans in sensitive domains, including mental health support, elderly care, and interactions with minors;

(5) AI systems involving the use of biometric data, including facial recognition, gait recognition, and other behavioral biometrics;

(6) AI systems with autonomous decision-making or action capabilities deployed in physical environments, including autonomous vehicles, robotic systems, and unmanned aerial vehicles;

(7) Large-scale AI models trained on data involving sensitive personal information or data obtained from populations that may have been collected without adequate consent.

**Article 8:** The following scientific and technological activities are subject to special review and may require approval from ethics review committees at higher levels or relevant competent authorities:

(1) Research that involves major national security implications;

(2) Research that involves the creation of novel organisms, pathogenic microorganisms, or other potentially dangerous biological agents;

(3) Research on AI systems with potential for large-scale autonomous decision-making or action in critical infrastructure or public safety domains;

(4) Research that involves the collection or use of data from particularly vulnerable populations, including incarcerated persons, persons with disabilities, children, and the economically disadvantaged.

## Chapter III: Ethics Committees

**Article 9:** Scientific and technological activities entities that engage in activities requiring ethics review under Article 6 shall establish ethics committees. Entities that do not have sufficient scale or capacity to independently establish ethics committees may apply to participate in ethics committees established jointly by two or more entities, or may commission qualified ethics committees to conduct reviews on their behalf.

**Article 10:** Ethics committees shall be composed of members with diverse professional backgrounds, including:

(1) Experts with relevant scientific and technological professional expertise;

(2) Experts in ethics, law, philosophy, sociology, or related humanities and social sciences disciplines;

(3) Members representing the public interest who are not engaged in scientific research;

(4) Where the committee reviews medical or life science research, medical professionals and at least one member with a clinical background are required.

Ethics committees shall have not fewer than seven members. The proportion of humanities and social science experts and public members shall not be less than one quarter of the total membership.

**Article 11:** Ethics committees shall operate independently. Members of ethics committees shall not participate in the review of projects in which they have a personal or financial interest. Entities shall not exert undue pressure on ethics committees or interfere with their independent review functions.

**Article 12:** Ethics committee members shall be appointed for fixed terms of no more than five years and may be reappointed for up to two consecutive terms. Ethics committees shall regularly renew their membership to maintain diversity and independence.

**Article 13:** Ethics committees shall establish and maintain written operating procedures covering at least:

(1) The scope of activities subject to ethics review;

(2) The procedures and timelines for submitting, processing, and deciding on review applications;

(3) Procedures for expedited review of low-risk activities;

(4) Procedures for continuous monitoring of approved activities;

(5) Procedures for handling violations and adverse events;

(6) Procedures for managing conflicts of interest;

(7) Procedures for appealing review decisions.

## Chapter IV: Ethics Review Procedures

**Article 14:** Entities or researchers proposing to carry out scientific and technological activities that require ethics review shall submit an ethics review application to the relevant ethics committee before commencing the activities. The application shall include:

(1) A description of the proposed activity, including its purpose, method, expected outcomes, and timeline;

(2) An analysis of potential ethics risks and the measures proposed to prevent or mitigate such risks;

(3) A plan for obtaining informed consent from participants, where applicable;

(4) The qualifications and relevant experience of the principal researcher;

(5) Any other materials required by the ethics committee.

**Article 15:** Ethics committees shall conduct reviews through one of the following procedures depending on the nature and risk level of the proposed activity:

(1) **Expedited review** — for scientific and technological activities that involve minimal risk to participants and are of a type that has previously been approved without significant modifications; the review may be conducted by the chair of the committee or a small panel designated by the chair;

(2) **Full committee review** — for scientific and technological activities that involve more than minimal risk to participants, activities involving vulnerable populations, activities with significant social implications, or activities that do not qualify for expedited review; the review shall be conducted at a convened meeting of the full committee with at least a majority of members present;

(3) **Multi-level review** — for activities that fall within the categories described in Article 8, the review shall be conducted at the entity level and shall also be submitted to the relevant competent authority at the national or sectoral level.

**Article 16:** In conducting ethics reviews, ethics committees shall evaluate:

(1) Whether the scientific and technological activity complies with applicable laws, regulations, and ethics standards;

(2) Whether the potential benefits of the activity outweigh its potential risks;

(3) Whether the methodology is appropriate, including whether the use of human subjects or vulnerable populations is justified and whether the number of research subjects is minimized;

(4) Whether the informed consent process is adequate, voluntary, and respects the autonomy of participants;

(5) Whether the privacy and confidentiality of participants are adequately protected;

(6) Whether the interests of vulnerable populations are adequately safeguarded;

(7) Whether the distribution of benefits and burdens of the research is fair and equitable;

(8) Whether adequate safety monitoring and adverse event reporting mechanisms are in place.

**Article 17:** For AI-related scientific and technological activities, ethics committees shall additionally evaluate:

(1) Whether the AI system's design reflects core values including human dignity, non-discrimination, transparency, and accountability;

(2) Whether the collection and use of training data are lawful and comply with personal information protection requirements;

(3) Whether the AI system's decision-making processes are sufficiently transparent and explainable, or whether the opacity is justified by a legitimate and proportionate purpose;

(4) Whether adequate mechanisms exist for human oversight and intervention in the AI system's operation;

(5) Whether the system's potential for bias or discriminatory outputs has been assessed and mitigated;

(6) Whether the social and economic impacts of the AI system have been considered.

**Article 18:** Ethics committees shall reach their decisions through deliberation among the members present. Decisions approving scientific and technological activities require the agreement of not less than two-thirds of members present. Ethics committees may:

(1) Approve the activity as submitted;

(2) Approve the activity subject to specific modifications or conditions;

(3) Require revision of the application and resubmission for further review;

(4) Reject the application.

**Article 19:** Ethics committees shall communicate their decision to the applicant in writing within the period prescribed by the committee's operating procedures. Where an application is rejected or subject to conditions, the written decision shall set out the reasons.

**Article 20:** Applicants who disagree with the decision of an ethics committee may apply for reconsideration or appeal in accordance with the entity's appeal procedures or, where applicable, to the relevant competent authority.

## Chapter V: Continuous Ethics Monitoring

**Article 21:** Entities and researchers that receive ethics approval shall conduct scientific and technological activities in strict accordance with the approved plans and conditions. Where it becomes necessary to make material modifications to the approved activity, the entity or researcher shall apply for a modification review before implementing the changes.

**Article 22:** Ethics committees shall establish mechanisms for the continuous monitoring of approved activities, including:

(1) Periodic progress reports from researchers on the implementation of approved activities;

(2) Procedures for reporting and responding to adverse events, unanticipated risks, or ethics concerns that arise during the conduct of the activity;

(3) Site visits or audits of approved activities where deemed necessary.

**Article 23:** Where an ethics committee determines that an approved activity is being conducted in a manner that involves ethics violations or unanticipated serious risks, the committee shall have the authority to suspend or withdraw approval for the activity and require the researcher to cease the activity immediately.

---

## Chapter VI: Obligations of Entities

**Article 24:** Entities shall fulfill the following obligations with respect to ethics review:

(1) Establish and maintain a functioning ethics committee with adequate resources, staffing, and operational independence;

(2) Ensure that researchers are informed of ethics review requirements and requirements for continuing compliance;

(3) Ensure that scientific and technological activities that require ethics review do not commence until ethics approval has been obtained;

(4) Establish internal complaint mechanisms for reporting ethics violations;

(5) Cooperate with inspections and audits conducted by competent regulatory authorities;

(6) Report to the relevant competent authority any serious ethics violations or incidents involving the research activities of the entity.

**Article 25:** Entities shall register their ethics committees with the relevant competent authority and provide information including the committee's composition, terms of reference, and operating procedures. Updates to ethics committee composition and operating procedures shall be reported to the relevant authority in a timely manner.

---

## Chapter VII: Supervision and Legal Liability

**Article 26:** Science and technology departments and other relevant competent authorities shall conduct supervision and inspection of the ethics review work of entities within their respective jurisdictions and fields, including:

(1) Review and verification of the establishment and operation of entities' ethics committees;

(2) Inspection of ethics review records and documentation;

(3) Investigation of reported ethics violations;

(4) Assessment of the overall quality and effectiveness of entities' ethics review systems.

**Article 27:** Where entities fail to establish an ethics committee as required, or where an ethics committee fails to operate in accordance with these Measures, the relevant competent authority shall order correction within a specified period. Where entities fail to correct the situation within the prescribed period, the relevant authority may suspend the entity's eligibility to receive public research funding or apply other sanctions as provided by applicable regulations.

**Article 28:** Where scientific and technological activities are carried out without obtaining required ethics approval, or where the activities deviate from the approved plan in material ways, the relevant entity shall be ordered to suspend the activities and make corrections. Depending on the severity of the violation, the entity may be subject to:

(1) Revocation of the ethics approval for the relevant activities;

(2) Suspension of new ethics review applications for a prescribed period;

(3) Public notification of the violation;

(4) Suspension of eligibility for public research funding;

(5) Other penalties as provided by applicable laws and regulations.

**Article 29:** Where researchers commit ethics violations in the conduct of scientific and technological activities, the entity shall initiate disciplinary procedures against the researcher. The researcher may also be subject to restrictions on holding leadership positions, debarment from public research funding applications, and where criminal liability is constituted, criminal prosecution in accordance with law.

---

## Chapter VIII: Supplementary Provisions

**Article 30:** For the purposes of these Measures:

(1) "Scientific and technological ethics review" refers to the process of assessing, managing, and supervising the ethics of scientific and technological activities;

(2) "Ethics committee" refers to the institutional body established by an entity to conduct ethics review of scientific and technological activities;

(3) "Minimal risk" means that the probability and magnitude of harm anticipated in the proposed activity are not greater in and of themselves than those ordinarily encountered in daily life or during the performance of routine activities;

(4) "Vulnerable populations" means individuals and groups that are less able to protect their own interests in the context of research participation, including minors, the elderly, persons with cognitive impairments, prisoners, patients, pregnant women, and persons in economically disadvantaged circumstances.

**Article 31:** For scientific and technological activities involving both Chinese and foreign entities, the ethics review requirements of both countries shall apply. Where a foreign ethics review has been conducted, the entity shall assess whether the foreign review meets the requirements of these Measures.

**Article 32:** These Measures take effect on December 1, 2023.
