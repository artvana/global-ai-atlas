# Guidance for AI Adoption

## Overview

Published: October 2025
Issuing body: Department of Industry, Science and Resources / National AI Centre (NAIC)
Status: Non-binding voluntary guidance

The Guidance for AI Adoption was published in October 2025 as the first update of the Voluntary AI Safety Standard (VAISS). It streamlines the previous 10 guardrails into 6 essential practices while maintaining alignment with Australia's AI Ethics Principles and international standards.

The guidance is non-binding. For organisations already aligned with the VAISS, the Implementation Practices version integrates and builds on the VAISS principles without requiring a restart.

## The Six Essential Practices

### Practice 1: Decide Who Is Accountable

Organisations must establish clear ownership of AI systems. This involves:
- Naming an executive responsible for AI governance
- Defining roles for approval and monitoring
- Clarifying decision rights across departments
- Embedding accountability into policies and job descriptions

### Practice 2: Understand Impacts and Plan Accordingly

Before deployment, organisations should:
- Identify affected stakeholders
- Consider consequences across privacy, safety, fairness, security, employment, and reputation
- Classify systems by risk level with mitigation plans
- Establish fallback processes for system failures

### Practice 3: Measure and Manage Risks

AI risks should be incorporated into enterprise risk management:
- Incorporate AI risks into enterprise risk registers using existing frameworks (privacy management, cybersecurity, workplace health)
- Define risk appetite
- Document controls for regular review
- Address threats such as data poisoning, supply chain vulnerabilities, and data drift

### Practice 4: Share Essential Information

Transparency requirements include:
- Disclosing AI use to affected parties
- Providing accessible explanations of system capabilities and limitations
- Maintaining an AI system register
- Communicating expectations to suppliers

### Practice 5: Test and Monitor

Systems require:
- Pre-deployment testing for accuracy, robustness, bias, and security using realistic scenarios
- Ongoing monitoring with defined incident thresholds and escalation procedures
- Prevention of "set and forget" approaches through continuous monitoring

### Practice 6: Maintain Human Control

- Decision authority remains with people
- Staff require training to question or override AI outputs, particularly in high-stakes domains
- Appeal mechanisms must be accessible to customers and employees

## International Alignment

The implementation practices align with:
- ISO standard on AI management systems, AS ISO/IEC 42001:2023
- US NIST AI Risk Management Framework (RMF) 1.0 and its Generative AI Profile
- Australia's AI Ethics Principles

## Implementation Support

The NAIC released a suite of practical tools:
- AI screening tool
- Policy guide and template
- AI register template
- Glossary of terms and definitions

The National AI Centre will phase in further complementary tools and resources over the following 12 months.

## AI-Generated Content Guidance

A separate guidance on AI-generated content was released by the National AI Centre in November 2025, setting out best-practice approaches for transparency through mechanisms such as labelling, watermarking, and metadata recording.
