## Summary and Analysis

### Purpose and Scope

Mexico's Ley Federal de Protección de Datos Personales en Posesión de los Particulares (LFPDPPP), originally enacted in 2010, was substantially amended in 2025 to introduce AI-specific provisions, modernize the consent framework, expand data subject rights in the context of automated processing, and align the Law more closely with contemporary international standards including the GDPR and the Latin American data protection modernization trends exemplified by Chile's Ley 21.719. The 2025 amendments (published in the Diario Oficial de la Federación in March 2025) represent the most significant reform to the LFPDPPP since its enactment and respond to technological developments — particularly the widespread deployment of machine learning, automated decision-making, and profiling systems in Mexico's commercial, financial, and digital economy sectors.

The LFPDPPP as amended in 2025 applies to the processing of personal data by private individuals and entities (personas físicas o morales de carácter privado) that carry out processing in Mexican territory. Processing by public sector bodies continues to be governed separately under the Ley General de Protección de Datos Personales en Posesión de Sujetos Obligados (2017). The 2025 amendments do not change the fundamental scope of the LFPDPPP in terms of who it covers, but they significantly expand what obligations apply to covered entities, particularly in the context of AI-driven processing.

The **Instituto Nacional de Transparencia, Acceso a la Información y Protección de Datos Personales (INAI)** continues as the primary regulatory and enforcement authority under the LFPDPPP. The 2025 amendments expand INAI's powers to include authority over AI-specific compliance obligations, algorithmic transparency enforcement, and cross-border transfer monitoring. INAI may issue binding guidelines (lineamientos) and recommendations (recomendaciones) on all aspects of the Law, including the new AI provisions.

### Key Definitions (as Amended 2025)

**Personal data** (datos personales) means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person.

**Sensitive personal data** (datos personales sensibles) means data that may affect the most intimate sphere of the data owner or whose misuse may cause discrimination or serious risk, including data about racial or ethnic origin, present and future health status, genetic information, religious, philosophical, and moral beliefs, trade union membership, political opinions, and sexual preference.

**Data owner** (titular) is the natural person to whose data the processing relates.

**Responsible party** (responsable) is the private natural or legal person that decides on the processing of personal data.

**Processor** (encargado) is the natural or legal person that processes personal data on behalf of and under the instructions of the responsible party.

**Automated decision** (decisión automatizada) — a new definition introduced by the 2025 amendments — means any decision made with respect to a data owner that is based solely or primarily on the automated processing of personal data, including profiling, without meaningful human evaluation.

**Profiling** (elaboración de perfiles) means any form of automated processing of personal data used to evaluate certain personal aspects relating to a natural person, in particular to analyze or predict that person's work performance, economic situation, health, personal preferences, interests, reliability, behavior, location, or movements.

**Algorithmic system** (sistema algorítmico) means any system that, based on computational rules, mathematical models, or machine learning techniques, processes personal data to generate recommendations, predictions, classifications, or decisions that may affect a data owner.

### Amendments on Automated Decision-Making and Profiling

The 2025 amendments insert a new chapter into the LFPDPPP specifically addressing automated decision-making and profiling. This represents the most significant substantive innovation of the reform.

**Right to Not Be Subject to Solely Automated Decisions.** Data owners have the right not to be subject to a decision that produces legal effects or similarly significant effects based solely on automated processing of their personal data, including profiling. This right applies in the context of decisions about access to credit, insurance, employment, education, housing, health services, and access to digital platforms and services.

Exceptions to this right apply where the automated decision: is necessary for the performance of a contract between the data owner and the responsible party; is authorized by applicable law; or is based on the data owner's explicit consent. Where an exception applies, the responsible party must implement appropriate safeguards.

**Transparency Obligations for Algorithmic Systems.** Responsible parties that use algorithmic systems to process personal data must inform data owners, in the privacy notice (aviso de privacidad), of: the existence of automated decision-making or profiling; the logic underlying the automated processing; the significance of the processing for the data owner; and the likely consequences of such processing for the data owner. This information must be provided in clear, plain, and accessible language.

**Right to Explanation.** Data owners subject to an automated decision that affects their interests have the right to request an explanation of the automated decision-making process, including the factors taken into account and their relative weight. Responsible parties must respond to such requests within 20 business days.

**Right to Human Review.** Where an automated decision has been made on one of the permitted exception bases, data owners have the right to request that the decision be reviewed by a competent natural person with the authority and practical ability to assess and, if appropriate, override the automated outcome. Responsible parties must establish procedures for this review and must communicate the outcome within 20 business days.

**Right to Contest.** Data owners may contest automated decisions and present their point of view. Responsible parties must provide accessible mechanisms for contesting decisions and must notify the data owner of the outcome.

**Prohibition on Certain Automated Processing.** The 2025 amendments introduce a prohibition on automated processing — including profiling — that produces discriminatory effects based on racial or ethnic origin, gender, sexual preference, disability, religious beliefs, or political opinions, unless explicitly required or authorized by law. Responsible parties using algorithmic systems must periodically audit those systems for discriminatory impacts and must take corrective action when discrimination is identified.

**Algorithmic Impact Assessments.** For high-risk automated processing — defined as automated decision-making affecting large numbers of data owners, involving sensitive personal data, or likely to produce significant effects — responsible parties must conduct an **Algorithmic Impact Assessment** (Evaluación de Impacto Algorítmico) prior to deployment. The assessment must document the purpose and logic of the system, the personal data processed, the potential risks to data owners, and the safeguards implemented. INAI may require responsible parties to submit algorithmic impact assessments for review.

### Amendments on Consent for Profiling

The 2025 amendments clarify and strengthen the consent requirements for profiling. Where a responsible party processes personal data for profiling purposes — particularly behavioral profiling for commercial advertising, marketing, and audience targeting — explicit consent (consentimiento expreso) is required. This is a higher standard than the general LFPDPPP consent framework, which in some contexts permits implied consent.

The amendments specify that consent for profiling must: be separate from consent for other processing purposes; clearly describe the profiling activities and their likely impact on the data owner; and be obtainable and revocable through simple, accessible mechanisms. Pre-ticked boxes, bundled consent, and consent obtained as a condition of accessing a service are not valid as the basis for profiling where an alternative means of providing the service without profiling exists.

### Expanded Data Subject Rights

The 2025 amendments supplement the existing ARCO rights (Access, Rectification, Cancellation, Opposition) with new rights specific to automated processing:

**Enhanced Right of Access.** In addition to the existing right of access, data owners may request specific information about the automated processing of their data, including the categories of data used in profiling, the sources of that data, the logic of the automated processing, and the output produced.

**Right to Data Portability.** The 2025 amendments formally introduce a data portability right, allowing data owners to obtain their personal data in a structured, commonly used, and machine-readable format and to request its transmission to another responsible party where technically feasible.

**Right to Restrict Automated Processing.** Data owners may object to the processing of their personal data for profiling purposes where no compelling legitimate interest overrides their objection. Responsible parties must cease profiling within 20 business days of receipt of a valid objection, unless a compelling basis for continued processing is established.

**Posthumous Data Rights.** The amendments clarify that data owners may designate a representative to exercise their data rights, including in the context of automated processing, and that certain rights may be exercised on behalf of deceased persons by their heirs.

### Privacy Notice Requirements

The 2025 amendments significantly expand the information that must be included in a privacy notice (aviso de privacidad) where the responsible party uses automated decision-making or profiling. The notice must now specifically state: whether automated decision-making or profiling will be used; the categories of personal data involved; the logic of the automated processing; the significance and likely consequences for the data owner; and the data owner's rights in relation to automated processing. Where automated processing involves sensitive personal data, the notice must be particularly clear and must obtain explicit consent.

### Cross-Border Transfer Rules

The LFPDPPP's cross-border transfer regime, established in the original 2010 Law, requires that international transfers of personal data comply with requirements ensuring equivalent protection in the recipient jurisdiction. The 2025 amendments add specific provisions for transfers involving algorithmic processing: where personal data is transferred to a foreign entity for the purpose of automated processing or profiling, the responsible party must ensure that the foreign recipient is bound by obligations equivalent to the 2025 amendments' AI-specific requirements.

INAI may issue guidance identifying countries or territories that provide adequate protection for transferred personal data in the context of automated processing, and may approve model contractual clauses for transfers involving algorithmic processing.

### Obligations of Processors

The 2025 amendments expand the obligations of processors in the context of AI. Where a responsible party engages a processor to operate or maintain an algorithmic system, the processor agreement must specifically address: the scope of automated decision-making or profiling conducted by the processor on behalf of the responsible party; the processor's obligations to maintain the algorithmic system within approved parameters; the processor's obligation to notify the responsible party of any identified bias, inaccuracy, or malfunction; and the processor's obligation not to use the personal data for training AI models for third parties without the responsible party's explicit authorization.

### Enforcement and Penalties

INAI continues to be the primary enforcement authority. The 2025 amendments expand INAI's enforcement toolkit in relation to AI-specific violations:

- INAI may require responsible parties to suspend the use of an algorithmic system while an investigation into a potential violation is pending.
- INAI may order responsible parties to conduct an algorithmic audit by an accredited third party and to share the results with INAI.
- INAI may issue binding remediation orders specifying the changes a responsible party must make to an algorithmic system to achieve compliance.

Penalties under the LFPDPPP (as updated by inflation adjustments and the 2025 amendments) range from fines of MXN 100 to MXN 320,000 daily wages (días de salario mínimo general vigente en el Distrito Federal) for general violations, with elevated fines for violations involving sensitive personal data, violations of automated decision-making rights, and repeat violations. The 2025 amendments introduce a specific aggravated penalty category for violations of the algorithmic transparency and human review rights, with fines at the upper end of the scale. Corporate officers may be held personally liable for willful violations.

Data owners may file complaints with INAI and may also seek civil compensation for damages through the ordinary courts system. INAI may also publish the details of enforcement actions involving AI-related violations, serving as an additional deterrent and providing public transparency about the Law's application.
