OVERVIEW

The Ministry of Transport, Communications and Information Technology (MTCIT) issued Oman's National Artificial Intelligence Policy in August 2024 following a public consultation period. The consultation was open through August 28, 2024, with both Arabic and English versions available.

This policy is a non-legally binding document that does not impose concrete obligations on providers or deployers of AI systems. It establishes high-level principles and guidelines for responsible AI development and use in Oman, aligned with Oman Vision 2040.

POLICY STATUS AND LEGAL CHARACTER

The National AI Policy is issued as tertiary legislation (a non-binding policy or circular) under Oman's legislative structure, not as primary legislation (royal decrees/laws) or secondary legislation (ministerial decisions with enforcement mechanisms). This means:
- No direct fines or enforcement mechanisms attached
- No concrete legal obligations on providers or deployers
- Intended as guidance to inform practice and future regulation
- Regulatory bodies called upon to align internal systems with policy content

SCOPE

The policy applies to both public and private organizations operating in the Sultanate of Oman. Individual persons are excluded from the scope.

KEY PRINCIPLES

The draft policy centers on four high-level principles:
1. Privacy — AI systems must respect individual privacy rights and comply with Oman's Personal Data Protection Law
2. Equity — AI must promote fair and non-discriminatory outcomes
3. Transparency — AI systems and their decision-making processes should be understandable
4. Accountability — Clear responsibility must be assigned for AI system outcomes

PROVIDER OBLIGATIONS

For AI providers (developers and creators of AI systems), the policy establishes that they should:
- Conduct impact assessments before deployment
- Perform periodic evaluations of deployed systems
- Establish safeguards for AI system use
- Adopt cybersecurity provisions
- Document system capabilities and limitations
- Engage with deployers to ensure appropriate use

DEPLOYER OBLIGATIONS

For AI deployers (organizations using AI systems), the policy establishes that they should:
- Comply with the Personal Data Protection Law
- Properly destroy data after use when no longer needed
- Ensure data quality and reliability for AI training and operation
- Provide human supervision mechanisms over AI decisions
- Take responsibility for AI decisions affecting individuals

GOVERNANCE APPROACH

Unlike the EU AI Act's risk-based framework with legally binding requirements and financial penalties, Oman's policy:
- Does not classify AI systems by risk level with corresponding obligations
- Makes no distinction between high-risk and low-risk AI for regulatory purposes
- Relies on voluntary adoption and alignment by government entities
- Depends on future regulatory development for specific sectors

The policy distinguishes between providers (developers) and deployers (users) of AI systems, assigning different responsibilities to each category.

RELATIONSHIP TO OTHER OMAN FRAMEWORKS

- Oman's Personal Data Protection Law — the key binding framework referenced for data protection
- National Cybersecurity Strategy — cybersecurity obligations referenced
- Oman Vision 2040 — overall national development framework
- National Program for Artificial Intelligence and Advanced Digital Technologies (September 2024)

COMMENTARY

Legal commentators note that the non-binding approach may be appropriate given AI's rapid development, avoiding over-regulation that could inhibit innovation. However, they identify one area requiring attention: Oman's Personal Data Protection Law requires amendment to mandate consent for profiling and automated decision-making, aligning with practices in Bahrain, UAE, and other neighboring jurisdictions.

The policy signals Oman's intent to establish a governance framework for AI while leaving space for the technology to develop. Regulatory bodies were called upon to align their internal systems with the policy and circulate it among affiliated entities.
