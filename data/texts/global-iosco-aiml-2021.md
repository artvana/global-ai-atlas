The Use of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning by Market Intermediaries and Asset Managers

OICU-IOSCO, FR06/2021
September 2021

International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO)

NOTE: The PDF at iosco.org returned 403 Forbidden. Content below is extracted from official IOSCO news releases, the IOSCO publication page, and secondary legal commentary.

OVERVIEW

The IOSCO Board published this Final Report on September 7, 2021, examining the use of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) by market intermediaries and asset managers. This report is the culmination of IOSCO's multiyear effort engaging with market intermediaries and asset managers to identify real-life AI and ML use cases and their associated risks.

The IOSCO Board established a mandate in April 2019 for two committees—Committee 3 on Regulation of Market Intermediaries and Committee 5 on Investment Management—to examine best practices arising from the supervision of AI and ML. The Final Report is based on responses received to the Consultation Report issued in June 2020.

BACKGROUND

Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning are increasingly used in financial services, due to a combination of increased data availability and computing power. The use of these technologies may be altering firms' business models within the financial services sector.

AI and ML use cases by market intermediaries and asset managers include:
- Trading and investment decision support
- Risk management
- Regulatory compliance
- Customer service and interaction
- Fraud detection and cybersecurity
- Portfolio construction and management
- Credit risk assessment

KEY AI-RELATED CHALLENGES IDENTIFIED

The report identifies key AI-related challenges that IOSCO members may face, including:

1. Governance and oversight: Ensuring appropriate oversight mechanisms exist for AI/ML systems, with clear accountability lines and board-level awareness.

2. Algorithm development, testing, and ongoing monitoring: Robust processes for the development, validation, testing, deployment, and ongoing monitoring of AI/ML models.

3. Data quality and bias: Ensuring the quality, appropriateness, and reliability of data used to train and run AI/ML models; addressing potential biases in training data.

4. Transparency and explainability: The ability to understand, explain, and audit AI/ML-driven decisions, particularly important for regulatory oversight and client relations.

5. Outsourcing: Governance challenges when AI/ML is developed or operated by third parties; maintaining accountability despite outsourcing.

6. Ethical concerns: Ensuring AI/ML systems operate within legal, regulatory, and ethical boundaries; preventing discriminatory or otherwise harmful outcomes.

SIX MEASURES — EXPECTED STANDARDS OF CONDUCT

IOSCO published six measures that reflect expected standards of conduct by market intermediaries and asset managers using AI/ML:

Measure 1 — Governance and Accountability
Intermediaries and asset managers using AI/ML should have in place appropriate governance frameworks and internal controls, and clearly defined accountability for AI/ML decisions. Oversight should be commensurate with the materiality and complexity of the AI/ML use case.

Measure 2 — Human Oversight and Controls
Intermediaries and asset managers should have sufficient human oversight and controls, with staff having appropriate knowledge and expertise to understand, oversee, and challenge AI/ML systems. There should be the ability to override or intervene when necessary.

Measure 3 — Robustness, Reliability and Accuracy
Intermediaries and asset managers should ensure that AI/ML models are robust, reliable, and accurate. This includes processes for model development, testing, validation, and ongoing monitoring, including stress testing and performance monitoring.

Measure 4 — Data Governance and Bias Identification
Intermediaries and asset managers should have appropriate data governance processes, including procedures to identify, assess, and address risks of bias. Training data should be of sufficient quality, quantity, and diversity.

Measure 5 — Transparency and Explainability
Intermediaries and asset managers should be able to explain AI/ML-driven decisions and processes to regulators and, where appropriate, to clients. The degree of explainability required should be commensurate with the materiality of the decision.

Measure 6 — Cyber Risk Management
Intermediaries and asset managers should identify, assess, and manage cyber risks arising from AI/ML use, including risks from data breaches, adversarial attacks, and vulnerabilities in third-party AI/ML services.

REGULATORY OBSERVATIONS

IOSCO found that most jurisdictions do not have AI/ML-specific regulation, instead applying existing frameworks (conduct, prudential, market integrity). Some jurisdictions are considering more targeted guidance.

Key supervisory observations:
- AI/ML development is concentrated among a small number of technology providers, creating concentration risk
- Smaller firms may rely more heavily on third-party AI/ML without adequate oversight
- Explainability requirements vary significantly across use cases
- Regulators are investing in their own AI/ML capabilities for supervisory purposes

RECOMMENDATIONS FOR IOSCO MEMBERS

The Final Report includes guidance to assist IOSCO members in supervising their regulated entities over the use of AI and ML:

- Review and assess whether current regulatory frameworks adequately address AI/ML risks
- Consider issuing guidance or regulations addressing the six measures where not already covered
- Develop supervisory capacity and expertise to oversee AI/ML use
- Engage in international cooperation given the cross-border nature of AI/ML deployment
- Monitor emerging AI/ML developments and update frameworks as needed
