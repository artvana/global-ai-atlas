## Elections (Integrity of Online Advertising) (Amendment) Act 2024
### Singapore Act 34 of 2024

**Parliament of Singapore**
**Enacted:** October 15, 2024 | **Effective:** January 22, 2025 (Polling Day for 2025 General Election provisions; ongoing for general provisions)

---

## Overview

The Elections (Integrity of Online Advertising) (Amendment) Act 2024 (Act 34 of 2024) amends Singapore's Presidential Elections Act 1991 and Parliamentary Elections Act 1954 to prohibit AI-generated or AI-manipulated political content in online election advertising. It is the **first Singapore legislation to explicitly mention and regulate artificial intelligence**, specifically targeting deepfakes and synthetic media depicting electoral candidates.

The Act bans the publication of digitally generated or digitally manipulated election advertising — including AI-synthesized images, audio recordings, and video depicting candidates — without mandatory disclosure that the content is AI-generated. It extends Singapore's existing Online Election Advertising (OEA) regulatory framework to the AI era, closing a gap that could otherwise be exploited for election interference.

---

## Legislative Background

### Existing Online Election Advertising Framework

Singapore enacted its Online Election Advertising regime in 2020, requiring persons who publish online election advertising to register with the Returning Officer and submit declared campaign expenditure reports. The regime applied to text, images, audio, and video published online during the election period.

However, the 2020 framework did not explicitly address AI-generated or AI-manipulated content. The rapid advancement of generative AI technology — enabling convincing deepfake videos, AI voice clones, and AI-synthesized candidate images — created a specific risk: fabricated content depicting candidates saying or doing things they never said or did could be published as election advertising without any disclosure obligation under the existing law.

### Context: Singapore's POFMA and Online Misinformation Framework

Singapore already had one of the world's most robust legal frameworks for addressing online misinformation, including the Protection from Online Falsehoods and Manipulation Act 2019 (POFMA). However, POFMA was designed for false statements of fact and was not calibrated specifically for AI-generated synthetic media in election contexts. The 2024 Amendment fills this gap with targeted, elections-specific AI content rules.

### Parliament's Intent

During the second reading of the Bill in Parliament (October 2024), the Minister for Digital Development and Information stated that the Amendment was aimed at "ensuring that the integrity of Singapore elections is not undermined by artificial intelligence tools that can create convincing but false depictions of candidates." The Amendment was passed with broad cross-party support.

## Key Definitions

**Digitally Generated Content** — Content created in whole or in substantial part using digital technology, including artificial intelligence, machine learning, generative adversarial networks (GANs), or similar computational methods, that depicts, represents, or portrays an individual in a manner in which that individual did not actually appear, speak, or act.

**Digitally Manipulated Content** — Content in which an individual's appearance, voice, words, or actions have been altered, synthesized, or fabricated using digital technology, including artificial intelligence, in a manner that misrepresents what the individual actually said, did, or appeared.

**Candidate** — A candidate standing for election in a general election, by-election, or presidential election in Singapore, during the election period as defined by the Parliamentary Elections Act or Presidential Elections Act.

**Online Election Advertising (OEA)** — Any matter or arrangement in electronic form that is published to promote or procure the electoral success of, or to prejudice the electoral prospects of, one or more candidates at an election.

**Election Period** — The period between Nomination Day and Polling Day (inclusive) for any election conducted under the Parliamentary Elections Act or Presidential Elections Act.

## Core Prohibitions

### Section 1 — Prohibition on Undisclosed AI-Generated Election Advertising

No person may publish, cause to be published, or knowingly permit to be published, any online election advertising that:

1. Contains digitally generated or digitally manipulated depictions of a candidate's appearance, voice, words, or actions; **and**
2. Does not include a clear and prominent disclosure that the content has been digitally generated or manipulated.

This prohibition applies regardless of:
- Whether the content is published by the candidate, by supporters, or by any third party;
- Whether the content is published on a platform registered for OEA purposes;
- Whether the content was created with or without the consent of the depicted candidate.

### Section 2 — Prohibition on Fabricated Candidate Depictions

Separate from the disclosure requirement, the Act creates a specific prohibition on online election advertising that:
1. Depicts a candidate doing, saying, or appearing in a manner that is entirely fabricated or substantially false; **and**
2. Is likely to deceive a reasonable person into believing that the depiction is real.

This prohibition applies regardless of disclosure — i.e., even a disclosed deepfake may be prohibited if it is so deceptive as to be likely to mislead voters about a candidate's actual conduct, words, or character.

### Section 3 — Prohibition on AI Impersonation of Candidates

No person may use AI-generated voice synthesis, facial synthesis, or any other AI-generated representation to impersonate a candidate in online election advertising without the candidate's prior written consent.

---

## Mandatory Disclosure Requirements

### Section 4 — AI Disclosure Label

Online election advertising that contains digitally generated or digitally manipulated content must include a disclosure label that:

1. Is clearly and prominently displayed on the content itself (not merely in a caption or metadata);
2. States, in plain English (and where applicable, in other official languages): *"This image/video/audio contains digitally generated or AI-manipulated content"* or equivalent words approved by the Elections Department;
3. Is visible at all times during the display of the advertising, and is not removable or obscurable by the viewer;
4. For video content, appears as a persistent on-screen label throughout the duration of the advertisement.

The Elections Department may prescribe technical standards for the format, placement, and size of the disclosure label.

---

## Scope and Application

### Election Period

The Act's prohibitions apply during the election period (Nomination Day to Polling Day). However:
- The disclosure obligation for AI-generated content applies to content published during the election period, regardless of when it was created;
- Content created before the election period that depicts a candidate may be required to include the AI disclosure label if it is re-published or shared during the election period.

### Geographic Scope

The Act applies to content published online that:
1. Is published from within Singapore; **or**
2. Is published from outside Singapore but targeted at Singapore voters or accessible to Singapore residents, where the publisher is a Singapore citizen or permanent resident or a Singapore-registered entity.

### Platform Obligations

Online platforms that host user-generated content used as election advertising must:
1. Implement mechanisms to enable users to flag AI-generated election advertising;
2. Remove or apply the required disclosure label to AI-generated election advertising within 24 hours of receiving a notice from the Returning Officer;
3. Cooperate with the Elections Department's investigations.

---

## Enforcement

### Section 5 — Criminal Penalties

A person who publishes online election advertising in violation of this Act commits an offence and is liable on conviction:
- For an individual: a fine not exceeding **SGD 1,000** and/or imprisonment for a term not exceeding **12 months**;
- For a body corporate: a fine not exceeding **SGD 5,000**.

Penalties are consistent with Singapore's existing election advertising offences framework and reflect the criminal law approach to election integrity in Singapore.

### Section 6 — Returning Officer Powers

The Returning Officer (an official of the Elections Department) may:
- Issue take-down notices to publishers of non-compliant AI-generated election advertising;
- Issue correction direction orders requiring publishers to add the required disclosure label;
- Refer cases to the police for investigation and prosecution;
- Apply to court for urgent injunctive relief to prevent continued publication.

### Section 7 — Platform Liability

Online platforms that fail to comply with take-down or correction direction orders within the specified timeframe may be liable for the offence of publishing non-compliant election advertising, regardless of whether the platform created the content.

---

## Exemptions

The following are not prohibited under the Act:
1. **Satire and parody** — Clearly labelled satirical or parody content, where no reasonable person would believe the content to be a real depiction of the candidate's words or actions;
2. **Historical archival use** — Use of digitally restored or enhanced historical footage for genuine documentary or educational purposes, not for election advertising;
3. **Candidate consent** — AI-generated content published by or with the written consent of the depicted candidate, subject to compliance with disclosure requirements.

---

## Relationship to POFMA and Broader AI Policy

The Elections (Integrity of Online Advertising) Amendment Act 2024 operates as a **lex specialis** (special law) for election advertising, alongside Singapore's general misinformation framework (POFMA). The two laws are complementary:

- **POFMA** addresses false statements of fact online, with government correction and take-down directions;
- **EIOA Amendment** addresses AI-generated synthetic media in election advertising specifically, with criminal penalties and Returning Officer directions.

Singapore's broader AI governance approach — which includes the Model AI Governance Framework (2020, updated 2023), AI Verify toolkit, and Project Moonshot — is non-binding and voluntary. The EIOA Amendment is notable as the **first binding, legislative AI governance obligation** in Singapore, applied narrowly to the high-risk context of election integrity.

## Parliamentary Debates — Key Points

During the second reading (October 2024), several key points were made:

1. **Targeted scope:** The government emphasized that the Act is narrowly targeted at election advertising and does not purport to regulate AI generally. Singapore's AI governance philosophy — voluntary frameworks for most sectors — was preserved.

2. **Technology neutrality:** The definitions of "digitally generated" and "digitally manipulated" are technology-neutral, applying to current AI tools (GANs, diffusion models, voice synthesis) and future technologies.

3. **Proportionate penalties:** The SGD 1,000 fine was criticized by some members as inadequate given the potential impact of AI deepfakes on election outcomes. The government responded that the criminal penalty system would be reviewed in light of international experience.

4. **Platform responsibility:** Several members called for stronger platform obligations; the government indicated that platform take-down obligations would be reviewed after implementation.

## Significance

The Singapore Elections (Integrity of Online Advertising) Amendment Act 2024 is historically significant as:

1. **First Singapore law to explicitly regulate AI** — Making Singapore a notable datapoint in comparative AI governance despite its general preference for voluntary frameworks;
2. **Targeted election integrity approach** — Illustrating how jurisdictions can address AI harms in specific high-risk contexts without adopting comprehensive AI regulation;
3. **Disclosure + prohibition model** — Combining mandatory AI content disclosure with outright prohibition on fabricated candidate depictions, creating a two-tier approach more stringent than most AI content labeling regimes;
4. **Global election AI governance trend** — Part of a wave of election-specific AI content laws enacted in 2024–2025, including comparable measures in the United States (federal and state), the European Union, and India.
