Relative to empowering the Guam Department of Education to
advance educational equity and academic success by establishing
an early childhood literacy and parenting curriculum for
pregnant and parenting students, examine the role and develop
policies and/or curriculum pertaining to artificial intelligence, and
by strengthening supports to mitigate social promotion while
ensuring all students meet essential learning standards.
1 BE IT RESOLVED BY I MINA′TRENTAI OCHO NA LIHESLATURAN
2 GUÅHAN:
3 WHEREAS, the Guam Department of Education (GDOE) is responsible for
4 ensuring that all students have access to an equitable education that promotes
5 academic achievement, social well-being, and lifelong learning, as set forth in the
1

1 Guam Education Board’s Strategic Plan and the federal Every Student Succeeds Act
2 (ESSA); and
3 WHEREAS, Guam continues to experience higher rates of teenage
4 pregnancy than the United States national average, creating barriers to high school
5 completion, long-term economic stability, and early literacy development among
6 children born to young parents; and
7 WHEREAS, the Department of Public Health and Social Services reports
8 through its Title V Maternal and Child Health Program that thousands of young
9 mothers, infants, and children are served annually in Guam, underscoring the need
10 for stronger school-based supports to help student-parents stay enrolled and
11 complete their education while promoting early childhood literacy; and
12 WHEREAS, pregnant and parenting students often face stigma, childcare
13 responsibilities, and gaps in academic and emotional support, which may lead to
14 school withdrawal or chronic absenteeism; and
15 WHEREAS, research and experience from other jurisdictions demonstrate
16 that school-based programs designed for student-parents can significantly improve
17 graduation rates, promote healthy child development, and break intergenerational
18 cycles of poverty; and
19 WHEREAS, model programs such as New York City’s LYFE (Living for the
20 Young Family through Education) and Washington State’s GRADS (Graduation,
21 Reality, and Dual-Role Skills) have shown that integrating early childhood
22 education, on-site childcare, and parenting and literacy coursework within schools
23 can help student-parents continue their education while supporting their children’s
24 cognitive development; and
25 WHEREAS, a locally adapted version of these models could be implemented
26 through a partnership among GDOE, the University of Guam, Guam Community
27 College, and the Department of Public Health and Social Services, thereby creating
2

1 pathways to postsecondary training and workforce participation for young parents;
2 and
3 WHEREAS, artificial intelligence (AI) is reshaping education systems,
4 workplaces, and civic life worldwide, and its integration into schools requires
5 deliberate, values-driven policy development; and
6 WHEREAS, Guam’s educators, students, and families must be equipped to
7 navigate both the opportunities and the risks of AI technologies, including their
8 social, economic, and environmental implications; and
9
10 WHEREAS, AI education should be culturally responsive, grounded in
11 critical thinking, and inclusive of diverse perspectives on technology, labor, power,
12 and planetary sustainability; and
13 WHEREAS, this initiative aligns with the Guam Department of Education’s
14 2025–2030 Strategic Plan, which calls for comprehensive supports addressing the
15 academic, social, and emotional needs of diverse student populations, including
16 those balancing family responsibilities with their studies; and
17 WHEREAS, at the same time, the issue of “social promotion,” or the
18 automatic advancement of students to the next grade level without meeting learning
19 benchmarks, remains a concern among educators and parents, as it can undermine
20 academic confidence and long-term achievement; and
21 WHEREAS, while grade retention has sometimes been used to address
22 learning gaps, studies show that retention alone can harm student self-esteem and
23 increase dropout risk, especially among low-income and minority students; and
24 WHEREAS, the Guam Department of Education’s Annual State of Public
25 Education Report notes that although graduation rates are improving, significant
26 numbers of students entering high school or graduating continue to need remedial
3

1 support in literacy and mathematics, demonstrating the need for early and sustained
2 interventions prior to promotion; and
3 WHEREAS, education systems across the United States are moving toward
4 evidence-based “multi-tiered systems of support” (MTSS) and standards-based
5 promotion frameworks that emphasize early intervention, individualized instruction,
6 and continuous progress monitoring rather than automatic promotion or retention;
7 and
8 WHEREAS, adopting such a framework within GDOE would allow teachers
9 to intervene early, provide targeted tutoring and summer learning opportunities, and
10 document progress through multiple measures, ensuring fairness and consistency
11 across schools; and
12 WHEREAS, strengthening educational equity for student-parents and
13 mitigating social promotion both serve Guam’s broader goal of improving literacy,
14 graduation rates, and workforce readiness while reducing intergenerational
15 disadvantage; now therefore, be it
16 RESOLVED, that I Minaʹtrentai Ocho Na Liheslaturan Guåhan does hereby,
17 on behalf of the People of Guam, empower the Guam Department of Education to
18 develop and implement an early childhood literacy and parenting curriculum
19 designed for pregnant and parenting students, in collaboration with the University
20 of Guam, Guam Community College, and the Department of Public Health and
21 Social Services; and be it further
22 RESOLVED, that I Liheslaturan Guåhan supports the establishment of on-
23 site or school-linked childcare centers at designated high schools and adult education
24 programs to enable student-parents to complete their studies while promoting early
25 literacy and developmental readiness among their children; and be it further
26 RESOLVED, that I Liheslaturan Guåhan encourages the Guam Department
27 of Education to adopt a comprehensive, evidence-based framework to reduce social
4

1 promotion by identifying struggling students early, providing timely academic and
2 counseling supports, and using multiple measures to determine readiness for
3 promotion; and be it further
4 RESOLVED, that I Liheslaturan Guåhan supports the development of
5 districtwide intervention policies that require documentation of remedial supports
6 such as tutoring, summer learning, and parental involvement before any decision on
7 promotion or retention is made; and be it further
8 RESOLVED, that I Liheslaturan Guåhan encourages the Guam Department
9 of Education to develop policies that guide the responsible, equitable, and
10 transparent use of AI technologies in Guam’s public schools; and it be further
11 RESOLVED, that I Liheslaturan Guåhan encourages the Guam Department
12 of Education make recommendations for the development of age-appropriate
13 curriculum related to artificial intelligence, which may include topics such as its
14 historical development, how it works, its applications in various fields, associated
15 career pathways, its regulation, and its social and environmental impacts; and it be
16 further
17 RESOLVED, that I Liheslaturan Guåhan ensures that any integration of AI
18 tools or content aligns with Guam’s educational goals, protects student privacy, and
19 advances equitable access to emerging technologies; and it be further
20 RESOLVED, that I Liheslaturan Guåhan further supports the creation of an
21 Educational Equity and Literacy Task Force within GDOE to coordinate these
22 initiatives, monitor outcomes, and recommend policy updates that strengthen student
23 success across all grade levels; and be it further
24 RESOLVED, that the Speaker certify, and the Legislative Secretary attest to,
25 the adoption hereof, and that copies of the same be thereafter transmitted to the
26 Honorable Lourdes A. Leon Guerrero, I Magaʹhågan Guåhan; Angel R. Sablan,
27 Chairperson of the Guam Education Board; Kenneth Erik Swanson, Superintendent
5

1 of Education; Dr. Anita Enriquez, President of the University of Guam; Dr. Mary
2 A.Y. Okada, President of Guam Community College; and Theresa C. Arriola,
3 Director of the Guam Department of Public Health and Social Services.
