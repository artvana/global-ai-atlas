Guidelines for Secure Adoption and Usage of Artificial Intelligence
National Cyber Security Agency (NCSA), State of Qatar
Version 1.0, February 2024

OVERVIEW

The National Cyber Security Agency (NCSA) of Qatar launched these guidelines to promote the secure adoption and use of artificial intelligence (AI) and to mitigate its potential negative impacts. The guidelines cover both traditional and generative AI and are available in Arabic and English.

The guidelines were designed around the established triad of people, process, and technology, following the AI lifecycle.

SCOPE

These guidelines apply to:
- Organizations in Qatar considering, implementing, or operating AI systems
- Both public and private sector entities
- AI systems of all types, including generative AI

The document provides guidance for the full AI lifecycle from procurement and implementation through ongoing operation and monitoring.

GUIDING PRINCIPLES

The NCSA AI Security Guidelines are built around eight core principles:

1. Accountability: Organizations must establish clear accountability structures for AI systems, including governance frameworks and oversight mechanisms. Responsibility for AI decisions and outcomes must be clearly assigned.

2. Safety: AI systems must be designed, implemented, and operated to minimize risks to human safety. Safety assessments should be conducted throughout the AI lifecycle.

3. Privacy: AI systems must be designed and operated in compliance with Qatar's Personal Data Privacy Protection Law (Law No. 13 of 2016) and other applicable privacy requirements. Privacy-by-design principles should be implemented.

4. Human Oversight and Monitoring: Adequate human oversight must be maintained over AI systems, particularly for high-risk applications. Mechanisms for human review, intervention, and override must be in place.

5. Robustness: AI systems must be designed to be reliable and to perform consistently across varying conditions. Systems should be resilient to adversarial attacks and data quality issues.

6. Fairness and Equity: AI systems must be designed and operated to avoid discrimination and promote equitable outcomes. Bias detection and mitigation measures must be implemented.

7. Societal and Environmental Well-being: AI systems must be evaluated for their broader societal and environmental impacts. Organizations should consider effects on employment, social cohesion, and the environment.

8. Auditability: AI systems must be designed to support audit and review. Documentation of system design, training data, decision logic, and operational history must be maintained.

PEOPLE, PROCESS, AND TECHNOLOGY FRAMEWORK

The guidelines structure requirements around the three pillars of the AI security triad:

PEOPLE
- AI governance roles and responsibilities
- Training and awareness programs
- AI ethics and responsible use policies
- Accountability frameworks for AI decisions
- Skills development for AI oversight

PROCESS
- AI risk assessment and management
- AI procurement and vendor assessment
- AI system lifecycle management
- Incident response for AI failures
- Audit and review procedures
- Change management for AI systems

TECHNOLOGY
- Secure AI system architecture
- Data governance and quality controls
- Model security and integrity
- Access controls and authentication
- Monitoring and logging systems
- Vulnerability management

THREATS AND RISKS

The guidelines address key AI-specific threats and risks including:
- Data poisoning and model manipulation
- Adversarial attacks on AI systems
- Privacy violations through AI inference
- Bias and discrimination in AI outputs
- AI system failures and unexpected behaviors
- Supply chain risks from third-party AI components
- Deepfakes and synthetic media misuse
- Over-reliance on AI without adequate human oversight

ALIGNMENT WITH OTHER QATAR FRAMEWORKS

The guidelines align with:
- Qatar National Cybersecurity Framework
- NCSA National Information Assurance Standard
- National Data Classification Policy
- Personal Data Privacy Protection Law (Law No. 13 of 2016)
- Qatar National AI Strategy 2019

The MCIT Principles and Guidelines for Ethical Use of AI (2024) reference these NCSA guidelines as a complementary framework, noting their alignment on the eight core principles.

RECOMMENDATIONS FOR ORGANIZATIONS

Key recommendations include:
- Conduct AI risk assessments before deployment
- Implement privacy-by-design in AI systems
- Establish AI governance committees or designate responsible officers
- Develop AI incident response procedures
- Maintain documentation of AI system decisions, especially high-risk ones
- Regularly audit AI systems for bias, accuracy, and security
- Ensure human oversight mechanisms are in place for consequential AI decisions
- Comply with Qatar's data protection and cybersecurity legal requirements
