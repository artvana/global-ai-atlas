# OMB Memorandum M-24-10 — Advancing Governance, Innovation, and Risk Management for Agency Use of Artificial Intelligence

**Memorandum Number:** M-24-10

**Issued:** March 28, 2024

**Issuing Authority:** Office of Management and Budget, Executive Office of the President (issued by OMB Director Shalanda Young)

**Directed to:** Heads of Executive Departments and Agencies (CFO Act agencies and all executive branch agencies)

**Status:** Rescinded and replaced by OMB M-25-21 (April 3, 2025)

---

## Overview

OMB Memorandum M-24-10 was the Biden administration's primary binding governance framework for federal agency use of artificial intelligence. It implemented key directives from **Executive Order 14110** (October 30, 2023) — the Biden AI Executive Order — by translating those directives into concrete, deadline-driven obligations for federal agencies. The memo was organized around three priorities:

1. **Strengthening AI Governance** — establishing institutional structures within agencies
2. **Advancing Responsible AI Innovation** — enabling agencies to adopt AI safely and effectively
3. **Managing Risks** — imposing minimum risk-management practices for AI that affects public rights and safety

---

## Key Requirements

### 1. Chief AI Officer (CAIO) Designation

Each agency identified in the Chief Financial Officers Act (CFO Act) was required, within **60 days** of the memorandum (by approximately May 27, 2024), to:

- Designate or appoint a **Chief AI Officer (CAIO)** at the senior official level
- The CAIO is responsible for: coordinating agency AI use; promoting responsible AI innovation; managing AI-related risks; convening the agency's AI governance board; and interfacing with OMB and the interagency CAIO Council

### 2. AI Governance Board

Each CFO Act agency was required to establish or designate an **AI governance board** (or equivalent coordination mechanism) comprising senior leaders from:
- Mission/program offices
- General Counsel / Legal
- Privacy
- Information Technology (CIO)
- Acquisition/Procurement
- Compliance and ethics functions

### 3. Annual AI Use Case Inventory

All executive agencies were required to:
- Maintain an **inventory of AI use cases** — applications employing AI to inform, influence, or execute agency decisions or actions
- Publish a **public-facing inventory** of non-sensitive AI use cases, updated at least annually
- Report inventories to OMB in a standardized format

### 4. Enterprise AI Strategy

Each CFO Act agency was required to develop a written **enterprise AI strategy** describing how the agency will advance responsible AI adoption consistent with its mission, values, and applicable law.

## Risk Classification: Rights-Impacting and Safety-Impacting AI

M-24-10 established a **two-tier risk classification framework**:

### Rights-Impacting AI
AI systems whose outputs have **legal, material, binding, or similarly significant effects** on individuals or communities, including systems used in:
- Benefits and services eligibility determinations
- Decisions affecting employment or contracting
- Civil rights and non-discrimination compliance
- Law enforcement actions and criminal justice decisions
- Consumer financial decisions
- Access to healthcare

### Safety-Impacting AI
AI applications that could **create or exacerbate physical or safety risks** to individuals or communities, including systems used in:
- Critical infrastructure operations
- Public health and safety services
- Transportation and aviation systems
- Emergency response

---

## Minimum Risk-Management Practices for Rights- and Safety-Impacting AI

Before an agency could **use** a rights-impacting or safety-impacting AI system, it was required to:

1. Complete an **AI Impact Assessment** documenting:
   - The intended purpose of the AI and its expected benefits
   - Potential risks and planned mitigation measures
   - Quality, relevance, and limitations of data used in AI design and development
   - Ongoing monitoring procedures

2. Ensure **ongoing human oversight**: post-deployment human monitoring, regular risk evaluations, and mitigation of emerging risks

3. Evaluate whether the AI system **treats individuals equitably** and produces accurate, reliable outputs consistent with legal requirements

**Comply or Cease:** By **December 1, 2024**, agencies were required to have implemented all minimum practices for rights-impacting and safety-impacting AI systems in operation. Any agency that could not comply was required to **cease using** the non-compliant AI system until compliance was achieved.

---

## Compliance Plans

Each agency was required to:
- Develop a written **AI Compliance Plan** describing how the agency will achieve consistency with M-24-10
- Submit the initial plan to OMB within **180 days** of the memorandum (by approximately September 24, 2024)
- Publish the plan (or a written determination of non-use) on the agency's public website
- Update and resubmit plans to OMB every **two years**

---

## Procurement and Vendor Requirements

M-24-10 also addressed AI **procurement**, requiring agencies to:
- Incorporate AI risk-management requirements into contracts for AI systems
- Require vendors to provide documentation enabling agencies to assess and manage risks from procured AI
- Ensure vendors cooperate with agency post-deployment monitoring obligations

---

## Current Status (as of May 2026)

OMB M-24-10 was rescinded and replaced by **OMB M-25-21** ("Accelerating Federal Use of AI through Innovation, Governance, and Public Trust"), issued on **April 3, 2025**, by the Trump administration's OMB. M-25-21 replaced the two-tier rights/safety-impacting classification with a single "high-impact AI" category, removed several prescriptive minimum-practice mandates, and reoriented federal AI governance around innovation and deregulation rather than safety-first risk management. The Chief AI Officer requirement was retained in modified form under M-25-21.

Agency compliance plans developed under M-24-10 remain publicly available on agency websites and continue to serve as documentation of agency AI use inventories.
