THE UNITED REPUBLIC OF TANZANIA
MINISTRY OF COMMUNICATION AND INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY
GUIDELINES FOR THE SECURE AND ETHICAL USE OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE
IN TANZANIA
(Draft Version One — Version 2.0, June 2024)
================================================================================

Contact: ps@mawasiliano.go.tz
Ministry of Information, Communication & Information Technology


FOREWORD BY THE PERMANENT SECRETARY
================================================================================

In recent years, the world has witnessed high growth and use of 4th industrial revolution
digital technologies in all social and economic sectors. The technologies include Artificial
Intelligence (AI), Distributed Ledger Technologies or Blockchain, cloud computing, Big
Data Analytics, Internet of Things, virtual and augmented reality, etc. Not to be left behind,
the Government of Tanzania has declared that the ICT driven fourth industrial revolution is
inevitable and has increased pace in adoption of these digital technologies. This is in line
with the development of the ten (10) years Tanzania Digital Economy Strategic Framework
(TzDESF) which acknowledges that ICTs are central to a competitive social and economic
transformation of the country. Furthermore, both the Tanzania Development Vision 2025
and 3rd National Five Year Development Plan 2021/22-2025/26 acknowledge that ICTs are
central to a competitive social and economic transformation and also they are a major
driving force for the realization of the Vision.

The GoT understands that the 4th industrial revolution technologies, especially Artificial
Intelligence (AI), brings both positive and negative impact to an economy. On the one hand,
AI offers an exciting extension of many human capabilities such as observation, processing,
and decision-making. On the other hand, AI has inherent ethical risks. Ethical problems in
AI can lead to a variety of consequences: profiling and biases of algorithms against a
particular race, gender, or specific category of people which can affect how education,
healthcare, financial, and privacy systems work. AI can also be used maliciously to fake
data, steal passwords, and interfere with the work of other software and machines.

As a starting point, the GoT through the Ministry of Communication and Information
Technology (MICIT) is developing guidelines for the development and use of ethical AI in
the country. The purpose of this AI Guideline is to provide guidance which can assist all
stakeholders, both public and private, in the development, deployment, and adoption of
AI-based solutions for various social and economic activities.

Mohammed Khamis Abdulla
Permanent Secretary, Ministry of Communication and Information Technology


INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

Artificial intelligence is one of the pillars of the Fourth Industrial Revolution. There is
sudden development and spread of the use of artificial intelligence in areas such as
healthcare, finance, education, energy, natural language processing, speech technologies,
computer vision, etc.

The Government of Tanzania (GoT) is spearheading technological innovation and digital
transformation and has recognised the significant role digital technologies play in the
economic development of the country. The use of AI in Tanzania is still in a nascent stage.
Tanzania's push towards AI technology aims to benefit the country in the areas of AI
infrastructure development, healthcare, digital economy, environmental conservation,
education, justice, and agriculture.

Current AI activity in Tanzania:
- Multidisciplinary AI4D Lab at the University of Dodoma
- Machine learning for rapid assessment of malaria mosquitoes (Ifakara Health Institute)
- Enhanced microbiological testing capacity for COVID-19 (Ifakara Health Institute)
- AI startups: Keystride, Elsa Health, Afya Intelligence, Mipango, Agripoa, AfyaTest,
  Tanzania AI Lab, Shule Direct
- Tanzania judiciary: AI-powered transcription and translation system for court efficiency

Despite Tanzania not having specific legislation for Artificial Intelligence yet, its misuse
and development can lead to penalties such as data breaches based on the Data Protection
Act of 2022. The Ministry of Communication and Information Technology (MICIT) is
developing this guideline document for AI ethical development and use to ensure AI
solutions align with all relevant ethical obligations during design, development, and use.


IMPORTANCE OF AI ETHICS
================================================================================

Ethics is the science of proper behaviour. The practice of AI ethics is the consideration of
moral problems related to the interaction of technology, humans, and society. The goal of
AI ethics is to ensure that AI is developed and used in ways that are fair, transparent,
accountable, and beneficial for society. AI ethics addresses a wide range of issues,
including data privacy, bias, discrimination, transparency, accountability and social impact.

Concerns identified at a stakeholder meeting hosted by the Ministry of Health (MoH):
- Poorly defined governance structures for AI knowledge sharing
- Lack of specifications and approach for implementing AI in the health sector
- Limited capabilities and skills to develop appropriate solutions
- Limited capacity to implement AI tools
- Lack of understanding among local technology firms on market opportunities
- Limited research and evidence generation for AI in Tanzania's health sector
- Availability and quality of relevant, reliable data
- Misconceptions and lack of awareness about AI technology generally

Additional concerns:
- Standard security, privacy, and compliance provisions are not in place when using AI
- AI tools can generate incomplete, incorrect, or biased responses — output must be
  reviewed and verified by a human
- AI-generated code should not be used for institutional ICT systems unless reviewed
  by a human


RATIONALE FOR ADOPTING THE ETHICAL AI GUIDELINES
================================================================================

Ethical AI is crucial to ensure that technology aligns with national values and ethical
standards and is used for the benefit of society.

This guideline document intends to outline key aspects to be considered, including
stakeholders, principles, and recommendations to guide the implementation and use of AI
in the country. The development of artificial intelligence systems requires that they comply
with the relevant standards throughout their lifecycle, on the basis of which it will be
possible to characterise these systems as reliable and accountable.


PURPOSE AND OBJECTIVES
================================================================================

The main purpose of adopting these guidelines is to prevent processes involving artificial
intelligence systems from endangering or marginalising humans. The strategy aims to
integrate AI across economic sectors, enhance societal welfare, and foster innovation with
the goal of building human capital, digital infrastructure, and a supportive ecosystem for AI,
ensuring ethical governance and promoting partnerships for growth.

Objectives:

i) Develop Human Capital: Elevate AI literacy and specialized skills, preparing the
workforce for the future digital economy. Focus on inclusive education and training
initiatives to ensure widespread AI competency. Establish national skills-building
programs focusing on AI and data literacy, complemented by young
professionals/apprenticeship programs to nurture local AI talent.

ii) Strengthen Digital Infrastructure: Prioritize the development of digital infrastructure
as the backbone for AI applications. Enhance data ecosystems to support AI research
and innovation. This includes the establishment of Data & AI Sandboxes for testing and
development. Position Tanzania as a regional hub for cloud infrastructure with AI-ready
storage and compute capacity.

iii) Foster Innovation and R&D: Encourage innovation in AI through supportive policies,
incentives, and investment in research and development. Collaborate with private sector
and academia to drive technological advancements. Set the foundations for world-class
AI university education and applied research through public-private funded programs.

iv) Ensure Ethical AI Use: Implement national guidelines for ethical AI use, emphasizing
transparency, accountability, and public trust. Safeguard data privacy and promote
responsible AI practices.

v) Cultivate Partnerships: Leverage national and international collaborations to advance
AI knowledge, share technology, and attract investment, enhancing Tanzania's position
in the global AI landscape.


THE AI PRINCIPLES
================================================================================

The MICIT proposes the following eight principles in the development and use of AI:

1. Valid and Reliable
   The use of AI systems should be justified, appropriate in context, and not exceed what
   is necessary and proportionate to achieve legitimate aims. AI systems should perform
   reliably within a clear set of parameters and be verifiable that they are behaving as
   intended under actual operating conditions.

2. Safe
   AI should produce results that conform to safety expectations for the environment in
   which it is used (healthcare, justice, education, transportation, etc.). AI systems should
   not be used in ways that cause or exacerbate harm, whether individual or collective,
   including harm to social, cultural, economic, natural, and political environments.

3. Managed Bias
   Standards and expectations for bias minimization should be defined prior to using AI.
   AI systems should not lead to individuals being deceived or unjustifiably impaired in
   their human rights and fundamental freedoms. Only people can see the blind spots and
   biases in AI systems, so they must be taught how to spot and correct unintended
   behaviors.

4. Resilient
   Resilience is the degree to which the AI can withstand and recover from cyber-attacks.
   AI technologies should be continuously assessed and appropriate mitigation and/or
   prevention measures taken to address adverse impacts, including on future generations.

5. Transparency
   Users should know where and when AI systems are being used and understand what
   they do and how they do it. When AI systems are used to (help) make decisions
   impacting people's careers and lives, those affected should understand how those
   decisions are made and how AI influences them.

6. Accountability
   Those who design and deploy AI systems must be accountable for how those systems
   operate. Clear owners should be identified for all AI instantiations, the processes they
   support, the results they produce, and the impact of those processes and results. It is
   the shared responsibility of developers and those who have chosen to implement AI.

7. Explainable and Interpretable
   The ability to explain how an output was generated and how to understand the meaning
   of the output. Individuals should be meaningfully informed when a decision which may
   impact their rights, fundamental freedoms, entitlements, services or benefits is informed
   by AI algorithms, and have access to the reasons for a decision and the logic involved.

8. Privacy-Enhanced
   Privacy of individuals and their rights as data subjects must be respected, protected
   and promoted throughout the lifecycle of AI systems. Adequate data protection
   frameworks and data governance mechanisms should be established or enhanced in
   line with the Data Protection Act of 2022.


AI CODE OF ETHICS
================================================================================

It is recommended that an entity/institution should have a code of ethics when developing
AI. The recommended code of ethics requires the AI system to:

a) Always be aligned with the entity/institution mission and values
b) Strive for excellence and quality
c) Adhere to ethical principles
d) Assist entity/institution in unleashing human potential easily and affordably
e) Assist entity/institution in unleashing human potential by generating value and positive
   impact
f) Be open and transparent
g) Be fair, inclusive, and diverse
h) Be reproducible
i) Have a positive impact on people
j) Not discriminate in any way against individuals and/or entity/institution
k) Be in line with the use of AI models that allow commercial use and free use for
   research purposes
l) Promote ethical conduct in data pipelines, including:
   - Data collection for model training
   - Training, evaluation, and inspection of results
   - Deployment for use in development and production environments


THE AI GUIDELINES
================================================================================

The MICIT proposes the following ten guidelines to manage risks for trustworthy ethical AI:

1. Develop a code of ethics
   Create a code of ethics in collaboration with relevant key stakeholders (employees,
   customers, industry experts) to outline values and principles the AI system should
   follow.

2. Ensure diversity and inclusion
   Ensure that the data used to train the AI system is diverse and inclusive, representative
   of different genders, races, ethnicities, and other diverse factors, to avoid
   discriminatory outcomes.

3. Monitor the AI system
   Regularly monitor the AI system through testing, auditing, and analysis to ensure it
   performs as intended and is not causing harm. Identify improvements and potential
   errors (such as biases in responses) and ensure ethical principles are up-to-date.

4. Partner with ethical providers
   Partner with ethical providers who prioritize diversity, inclusion, and transparency when
   developing and using AI systems.

5. Transparency
   AI developers must adhere to the principles of explainability and transparency. Criteria
   and models should be accessible and clear to users. Be transparent about data used to
   train the AI system, the algorithms used, and how decisions are made.

6. Bias in learning and data
   Be cautious about bias in information that could lead to discrimination. Have careful
   analysis and filters for the training of each AI system.

7. Address privacy and security concerns
   Ensure the AI system is compliant with data protection regulations, especially when
   used for customer support, sales, and marketing. Protect personal information in
   compliance with the Data Protection Act of 2022.

8. Educate entity/institution employees
   Educate employees on the ethical implications of AI and provide training on the use of
   ethical AI, helping them identify and mitigate potential ethical issues.

9. Anticipate risks
   Anticipate potential risks arising from data, algorithms, and system use, and develop
   strategies to mitigate them.

10. Conduct ethical reviews
    Conduct regular ethical reviews of the AI system to ensure alignment with expected
    standards, evaluating performance, identifying ethical issues, and taking corrective steps.


RECOMMENDATIONS ON IMPLEMENTING THE AI GUIDELINES
================================================================================

Proposal 1: Human Agency and Control
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

An artificial intelligence system should provide reliable support for decision-making while
being subject to constant human monitoring and control.

Documentation requirements for AI system development:
- Possibilities and functionalities of the AI system
- Scenarios of use
- Operational structure and configurations for robust and accountable use
- Limitations of the AI system
- Segments in which the AI system is not designed to be used
- Overview of the accuracy and regularity of the AI system's work
- Limits to expected further development without direct human intervention

If responsible persons detect anomalies that may lead to undesirable situations, they shall
have authority, at their discretion, to temporarily shut down the system, with detailed
justification, subject to review by the larger design team or relevant authority.

Recommendation 1:
i) Provide technical documentation explaining the design, subsystems, components,
   and mechanisms for monitoring and control
ii) Design a system allowing monitoring, control, and ex-post analysis of processing
   results against input data
iii) Allow option to choose between different processing results when the system
   produces more than one result with different likelihoods
iv) Include early detection of harmful impacts or side effects affecting equality and
   human rights
v) Identify and document a range of models to assess whether the system is
   functioning properly
vi) Analyse source data used to train the system algorithm to ensure it represents
   variability for all users, not only a small group
vii) Identify the persons responsible for responding to problems at all stages: from
   development and testing, through learning and training, to full exploitation
viii) Identify elements of the system, user tools and reporting tools including ability to
   understand output results (e.g. shutting down the system)
ix) Establish and document criteria for use of the system, including metrics and
   thresholds; define approach if thresholds are exceeded


Proposal 2: Technical Reliability and Security
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Key requirements for a robust AI system include technical reliability and security. As AI
technology evolves, new attack risks should be anticipated: training data poisoning,
avoidance attacks, access to sensitive training data, model theft, and adversarial attacks.

Recommendation 2:
i) Identify multiple metrics for training and monitoring assessment — use metrics such
   as user feedback surveys, system-level performance values, click-through rate,
   customer lifetime value, and rates of false-positives/false-negatives disaggregated
   by subgroups
ii) Whenever possible, verify input data — continuously analyse input data; if not
   possible (for sensitive data), analyse by calculating aggregated, anonymised group
   values and statistics
iii) Understand the limitations of datasets and models — a model trained to detect
   correlations should not be used to make decisions about causality
iv) Rigorous testing — including:
   - Rigorous modular testing of each system component individually
   - Integration tests to understand how components interact
   - Proactive detection of input data drift
   - Usage of quality datasets; updated regularly
   - Iterative user testing for different user needs
   - Build quality assurance into the system
v) Monitor the system during use — continuous monitoring with planned intervals for
   fixing problems; analyse differences before updating models
vi) Identify potential threats — develop a rigorous threat model predicting as many
   attacks as possible; assess likelihood and severity of consequences
vii) Define the procedure for removing threats — set up an internal "red team" or
   external challenge; develop procedures for removing different types of threats
viii) Ongoing education — provide education on latest threats and attacks emerging
   in the field


Proposal 3: Privacy, Personal Data Protection and Data Management
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Appropriate data management includes the quality and integrity of data, its relevance to
the area of life in which the system is developed, data access protocols, and the ability of
the system to process data in a way that protects privacy. A data holder is obliged to
protect data in an AI system. Lawful access to data must be ensured in accordance with
data protection regulations, in particular the Data Protection Act of 2022.

Recommendation 3:
i) Define data elements and data entry to create a common business vocabulary in a
   glossary
ii) Identify data attributes (metadata) and data entry
iii) Define user roles and procedures for authentication and authorisation of data access
iv) Develop policies and rules that define how specific data should be managed during
   its lifecycle
v) Manage data codebooks so that all operational and analytical systems use the same
   classifications (master data management)
vi) Develop data stores that support data encryption, anonymisation and
   pseudonymisation, and integration with the data catalogue. A data catalogue includes:
   a) A business glossary
   b) Automated data discovery, profiling, tagging, cataloguing and glossary mapping
   c) Automated detection of sensitive data and management classification
   d) Interoperability with other catalogues, tools and applications for sharing metadata


Proposal 4: Transparency, Explainability and Traceability
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Transparency
Transparency is important because:
a) AI systems can make mistakes or cause damage, and transparency is necessary to
   detect how and why
b) AI systems should be understandable to users
c) Accountability is not possible without adequate transparency

Transparency consists of three elements:
- Traceability of the AI system
- Explainability of the AI system and the system model
- Communication — dialogue with all stakeholders about the limitations of the AI system

4.2 Explainability
Decisions resulting from AI use need to be explained and understood by those directly or
indirectly affected so that the decisions can be challenged. "Black box" models require
additional attention (traceability, external evaluation, transparent communication about
scope and capacities).

4.3 Traceability
Traceability is a crucial requirement for creating robust and accountable AI systems.
In order to understand and interpret information contained in datasets, the most important
facts must be filtered out and conclusions based on knowledge.

Recommendation 4:
i) Distinguish Transparency, Explainability and Traceability at the level of data origin,
   access and extraction
ii) Distinguish Transparency, Explainability and Traceability at the level of machine
   learning algorithms and models
iii) Distinguish Transparency, Explainability and Traceability processes for automated
   data preparation and processing, and processes for generating conclusions from
   identified input factors and output recommendations


Proposal 5: Diversity, Non-Discrimination and Equality
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

For an AI system to be reliable and accountable, it must allow for inclusion and diversity in
its lifecycle. AI systems need to be user-centred and designed so that anyone can use the
AI products or services, regardless of age, gender, ability and other characteristics. It is
particularly important to make these technologies accessible to persons with disabilities.

Recommendation 5:
i) Analyse the system in real time to detect both intentional and unintentional biases
   and discriminatory patterns; analyse and understand where they come from and how
   they can be mitigated
ii) Design and develop the system without intentional bias and review regularly; also
   address unintentional bias including stereotypes
iii) Check data and data sources before starting to train the algorithm
iv) Develop and integrate mechanisms to ensure user feedback on biases and issues
v) Establish multidisciplinary teams to evaluate relevant parameters; diverse teams
   present a wider range of experiences to minimise bias and discrimination
vi) Ensure objectivity and set up a mechanism to eliminate bias
vii) If the system proves inadequate, biased, discriminatory, or unsuccessful and cannot
   be improved, withdraw it from circulation; evaluate the damage it can do to society
viii) Include members of different ages, backgrounds, genders, qualifications and cultural
   perspectives in the team
ix) Test the system starting from the early design phase, and test it often


Proposal 6: Risk Management and Accountability
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Risk management requires timely identification, assessment, documentation and
minimisation of the potentially harmful effects of the AI system. The issue of accountability
is closely linked to adequate planning for development and monitoring, as well as risk
management procedures for establishing accountability and remedying damage caused
by the use of the system.

Recommendation 6:
i) Establish clear and understandable rules and policies for designers and development
   teams to avoid disputes over tasks and responsibilities
ii) Specify where the accountability of those who developed the system ends —
   particularly important because developers have no control over how the system is
   used
iii) Keep records of the design process, functionality development and decision-making
   method of the system; regulate this procedure in a separate document
iv) When creating the system, align the use and outputs of the system with regulations
   and national standards


CONCLUSION
================================================================================

AI systems must be recognisable as such. Users must be informed that they are
interacting with a system, and they must be able to request communication with a human
being if necessary. The utility, effectiveness, efficiency and usability of an AI system are
ensured by involving end users in the design, evaluation and implementation of the
graphical user interface.

These Guidelines have been created with the aim of providing guidance to all stakeholders
in the artificial intelligence ecosystem. In the absence of a firmer legal framework, which
is being worked on, these Guidelines ensure further progress in this area, which will
continue to expand in the future.
