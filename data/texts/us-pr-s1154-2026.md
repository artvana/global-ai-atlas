                                           ACT

To add a new Article 12.27 to Act 58-2020, as amended, known as
the "Electoral Code of Puerto Rico of 2020", for the purpose of regulating the dissemination of materially
deceptive synthetic media in electoral communications; require clear
disclosure when political-electoral advertisements or communications contain content
generated or substantially altered through artificial intelligence or other analogous
technological tools; prohibit, within determined electoral periods, the malicious or
deceptive creation or dissemination of synthetic media aimed at influencing the result of
an election or harming a candidate, aspirant, party or electoral process;
establish an expedited procedure for complaints before the State Elections Commission
and judicial remedies in equity; provide administrative sanctions; and for other related
purposes.

                             STATEMENT OF MOTIVES


      The integrity of electoral processes depends, to a great extent, on the
electorate being able to receive, evaluate and contrast political information without being subject to
technological manipulations designed to falsify reality. The recent development of
generative artificial intelligence tools has facilitated the creation of images,
audios, videos and other synthetic representations capable of appearing, with a high degree of
realism, that a real person said, did or participated in events that never occurred.
When such content is used in electoral contexts to alter perception
                                           2


public, harm candidacies, suppress participation or sow distrust in
democratic processes, the institutional damage can be severe.
       Puerto Rico must not wait for that risk to materialize in an irreparable manner
to adopt a proportionate legislative response. The current electoral system
already recognizes the compelling interest of the State in protecting the purity, transparency,
security and certainty of electoral processes, but does not contain a specific
provision aimed at addressing the dissemination of materially deceptive synthetic content
in campaigns, advertisements, political messages or communications directed to the electorate.
       This Legislative Assembly recognizes that not all content generated with
artificial intelligence tools deserves prohibition or sanction. There are
legitimate uses in creative, humorous, artistic, educational, journalistic or
clearly satirical contexts. The problem this Act addresses is not the existence of synthetic
technology itself, but rather its deceptive, undisclosed and materially false use
at sensitive moments of the electoral process, with the purpose or probable effect
of confusing the electorate or unjustly affecting the reputation of persons and entities that
participate in the democratic contest.
       For this reason, this measure adopts a narrow approach compatible with constitutional
principles: it requires disclosure when an electoral communication uses
synthetic content; prohibits the dissemination of materially deceptive synthetic media,
without clear warning, within determined electoral periods and under criteria of
knowledge, intention or reckless disregard; recognizes exceptions for parody,
satire, bona fide journalism and analogous expressions that do not generate material confusion;
and creates expedited administrative and judicial remedies to address complaints
in a timely manner.
       With this Act, Puerto Rico strengthens the integrity of its democratic processes,
protects the electorate against new modalities of manipulation and affirms that
technological innovation cannot become an instrument of electoral information
fraud.

     BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY OF PUERTO RICO:

 1          Section 1.- A new Article 12.27 is added to Act 58-2020, as amended,

 2   known as the "Electoral Code of Puerto Rico of 2020", to read as follows:

 3          "Article 12.27. — Materially Deceptive Synthetic Media in Electoral

 4   Communications."

 5          (a) Definitions.

 6          For purposes of this Article, the following terms shall have the meaning hereby

 7   expressed:

 8          (1) Synthetic medium - any image, audio, video or audiovisual representation created,

 9   generated or substantially altered through artificial intelligence, machine learning or other

10   analogous technological tool.

11          (2) Materially deceptive synthetic medium - any synthetic medium that reasonably

12   appears to authentically represent a real person saying, doing or participating in

13   acts, expressions or events that did not occur in reality, in such a way that it may materially

14   mislead a reasonable elector.

15          (3) Electoral communication - any advertisement, message, publication, propaganda, printed

16   material, recording, digital broadcast or communication directed to the electorate, whether paid or

17   unpaid, that promotes, attacks, supports, rejects or refers in an identifiable manner to a candidate,

18   aspirant, party, candidacy, nomination, election, referendum, plebiscite, consultation or matter

19   submitted to a vote.

20          (4) Protected electoral period - the term comprised from ninety (90) days before

21   a primary, special or general election, or from the date of opening of the official period of advance

                                                      4


 1   or absentee voting applicable to that electoral event, whichever occurs first, until the final

 2   certification of the corresponding event.

 3          (b) Mandatory disclosure.

 4          Every electoral communication that contains a synthetic medium shall include a

 5   clear and conspicuous disclosure that informs that the image, audio or video has been generated or substantially

 6   altered through artificial intelligence or other synthetic technology. In audiovisual

 7   media, the disclosure shall be visual and, when reasonably appropriate, also

 8   audible.

 9          (c) Prohibition.

10          No person may, during the protected electoral period, create, order, publish,

11   disseminate or contract the dissemination of a materially deceptive synthetic medium, without the disclosure

12   required in subsection (b), when acting:

13          (1) with the intention of influencing the result of an election, primary, consultation,

14   referendum or plebiscite;

15          (2) with the intention of harming the reputation or electoral viability of a candidate,

16   aspirant or party; or

17          (3) with knowledge of the deceptive nature of the content, or with reckless disregard

18   as to whether the content is materially deceptive.

19          (d) Exceptions.

20          It shall constitute an affirmative defense that the challenged content:

21          (1) forms part of a parody or satire that, for a reasonable person, is clearly

22   identifiable as such;

                                                       5


 1          (2) is used in a news item, documentary, analysis, commentary or bona fide

 2   journalistic coverage that clearly and sufficiently identifies the inauthentic, recreated or disputed

 3   nature of the content;

 4          (3) is disseminated by the candidate himself or by the person represented, in a

 5   voluntary manner and with adequate disclosure; or

 6          (4) is not susceptible of materially misleading a reasonable elector as to

 7   its authenticity.

 8          (e) Complaints and administrative remedies.

 9          Any candidate, aspirant, political party, person represented in the content, or the

10   State Elections Commission on its own initiative, may file a complaint alleging

11   violation of this Article.

12          The State Elections Commission shall adopt an expedited procedure to adjudicate the

13   complaint and may order, as appropriate:

14          (1) the cease and desist of further dissemination by the respondent party;

15          (2) the issuance of corrective disclosures;

16          (3) the removal, correction or rectification of the material by the author, advertiser,

17   committee, campaign or disseminator directly responsible for the content; and

18          (4) the imposition of administrative sanctions in accordance with this Article.

19          (f) Judicial remedies.

20          Without prejudice to the administrative process, any person with active standing under

21   this Article may resort to the Court of First Instance and request provisional,

22   injunctive, declaratory or any other equitable remedy, when alleging that the

                                                       6


 1   dissemination of the content is occurring or is imminent and may cause irreparable harm to the

 2   electoral process or to its rights.

 3          (g) Administrative sanctions.

 4          Every person who violates this Article shall be subject to an administrative fine of not less than one thousand

 5   dollars ($1,000.00) nor more than five thousand dollars ($5,000.00) per violation, taking

 6   into consideration the gravity of the conduct, its scope, the proximity of the electoral event, the proven

 7   intention, recidivism and the harm caused.

 8          (h) Additional referrals.

 9          When from the facts arise possible violations of other provisions of the legal

10   system, the State Elections Commission may refer the file to the Department of Justice

11   of Puerto Rico or to any other competent entity.

12          (i) Interpretation.

13          Nothing in this Article shall be construed as authorization to impose

14   liability on an interactive service provider exclusively for third-party content,

15   beyond what is permitted by applicable federal legislation.

16          Section 2.- Regulation.

17          The State Elections Commission shall adopt, within a term of one hundred twenty

18   (120) days, the regulation necessary to implement this Act, including criteria of

19   disclosure, expedited procedures for complaints, terms of notification,

20   parameters for evaluation of the challenged content and guidelines of orientation to the public.
