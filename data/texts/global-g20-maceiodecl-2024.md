G20 Ministerial Declaration on Digital Inclusion for All
Maceió, Brazil, 13 September 2024

We, the G20 Ministers responsible for the Digital Economy, convened in Maceió, Brazil, on 13 September 2024, and conducted discussions on the following topics: digital inclusion and meaningful connectivity; digital government and digital public infrastructure; information integrity online; and artificial intelligence for sustainable development.

We reaffirm our commitment to building safety, resilience, security and trust in the digital economy while centering human rights and development needs, particularly for developing nations.

SECTION I: DIGITAL INCLUSION AND CONNECTIVITY

Paragraphs 1-7

1. We reaffirm our commitment to universal and meaningful connectivity. We note that one third of the world's population remains unconnected to the Internet and recognize the need to address the persistent digital divides, particularly those based on geography, gender, age, disability, and socioeconomic status.

2. We reaffirm our 2023 commitments to cut the gender digital divide in half by 2030 and recognize that targeted policies and programs are needed to accelerate progress.

3. We welcome the G20 Guidelines on Indicators and Metrics for Universal and Meaningful Connectivity and recognize digital literacy as essential to achieve meaningful digital inclusion.

4. We recognize that meaningful connectivity requires not just access to infrastructure but also affordable devices, relevant content, digital skills, and safety and security online.

5. We acknowledge the important role of multi-stakeholder approaches, including the private sector, civil society, and technical communities, in expanding connectivity and promoting digital inclusion.

6. We recognize the potential of emerging technologies, including AI, to accelerate connectivity and digital inclusion, while acknowledging risks of new divides.

7. We reaffirm our commitment to closing the international digital divide through international cooperation, technology transfer on mutually agreed terms, and capacity building.

SECTION II: DIGITAL GOVERNMENT AND DIGITAL PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE

Paragraphs 8-11

8. We highlight digital public infrastructure's (DPI) role in improving government responsiveness while protecting privacy and human rights. Digital public infrastructure can reduce barriers to public service access and enable inclusive economic participation when designed with appropriate safeguards.

9. We recognize that digital identity systems should enable service access without discrimination. Digital identity governance should be user-centered, rights-respecting, and interoperable across borders where appropriate.

10. We welcome guidelines on digital identity governance developed with OECD support and encourage their broad adoption.

11. Data sharing provisions emphasized compliance with applicable legal frameworks while enabling cross-border data flows and data free flow with trust. We encourage the development of interoperable frameworks that allow governments and the private sector to share data in ways that respect privacy, security, and sovereignty.

SECTION III: INFORMATION INTEGRITY

Paragraphs 12-16

12. We recognize that digital platforms play a critical role in the information ecosystem and that their design, algorithms, and business models can amplify or mitigate the spread of harmful content.

13. We note that misinformation and disinformation, hate speech and other forms of online harms are amplified by digital platforms and pose risks to trust in the digital economy, in public institutions, and to democratic processes.

14. We call for transparency from platforms regarding content moderation policies, algorithmic systems, and their impacts on information ecosystems.

15. We encourage investments in online safety education and in digital media and information literacy to build societal resilience against mis- and disinformation.

16. We recognize the role of diverse and independent media, fact-checkers, and civil society in promoting information integrity and encourage their support.

SECTION IV: ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE FOR SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT

Paragraphs 17-23

17. Safe, secure and trustworthy Artificial Intelligence may act as a catalyst for achieving economic growth and inclusive sustainable development when applied responsibly, transparently, and with appropriate safeguards.

18. We acknowledge the potential of AI to accelerate progress toward the Sustainable Development Goals and to address global challenges including climate change, health crises, and food security.

19. We acknowledge AI and other digital divides that may arise from unequal access to AI infrastructure, expertise, and data. We call for inclusive international cooperation on capacity building and technology transfer on mutually agreed terms to prevent AI from exacerbating existing inequalities.

20. We recognize the importance of developing countries' active participation in global AI governance discussions and in setting international standards and norms.

21. We reaffirm the need for AI governance frameworks that are human-rights based, trustworthy, responsible, and inclusive. We note the importance of risk-based approaches that are proportionate to the potential harms of different AI applications.

22. We encourage multistakeholder engagement in AI governance, including from civil society, the private sector, academia, and affected communities.

23. We welcome ongoing work in international forums, including OECD, UNESCO, ITU, and the Global Partnership on AI, to develop shared principles and practical tools for AI governance.

ANNEXES

Annex 1: G20 Guidelines on Indicators and Metrics for Universal and Meaningful Connectivity
Provides measurement frameworks across six dimensions:
- Connection quality
- Availability and coverage
- Affordability
- Devices and equipment
- Digital skills and literacy
- Security and safety
With specific indicators for each dimension to enable comparable international tracking.

Annex 2: G20 Principles for Digital Identity Governance
Establishes principles for digital identity governance emphasizing:
- User-centered design placing individuals in control
- Human rights protection throughout lifecycle
- Interoperability enabling cross-border use
- Accessibility ensuring inclusion
- International cooperation on standards and frameworks

Annex 3: G20 Principles for Information Integrity
Outlines five axes for information integrity:
1. Resilience strengthening — building societal capacity to identify and respond to harmful content
2. Content source sustainability — supporting trusted information producers
3. Transparency enhancement — requiring platforms to disclose policies and systems
4. Governance upgrading — improving regulatory and self-regulatory frameworks
5. Market incentivization — aligning business models with information integrity goals

Annex 4: G20 Enabling Resources for AI Development
Identifies enabling resources for AI development including:
- Infrastructure: compute, data centers, connectivity
- Skilled workforce: AI researchers, engineers, governance experts
- Data availability: open government data, data-sharing frameworks
- Governance frameworks: standards, regulations, ethics frameworks

Three strategic recommendations:
1. International collaboration on AI research and development
2. Capacity building in developing countries for AI readiness
3. Policy development for inclusive and trustworthy AI

WAY FORWARD

24. We thank partner organizations including ITU, OECD, UNESCO, and the UN Secretary-General's office for their contributions to our work.

25. We welcome South Africa's plans to further work on the topics of Artificial Intelligence, digital infrastructure deployment, and bridging digital divides as the incoming G20 presidency.

26. We call on all stakeholders to accelerate implementation of these commitments and to report on progress at the next G20 Digital Economy Ministerial meeting.

27. We express our gratitude to Brazil for hosting the 2024 G20 Presidency and for the leadership shown in advancing digital economy issues.
