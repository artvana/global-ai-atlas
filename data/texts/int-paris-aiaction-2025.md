# Statement of the AI Action Summit — Paris, February 10-11, 2025
**AI Action Summit, Paris, France**
*International — February 10-11, 2025*

---

## Overview

The Paris AI Action Summit of February 10-11, 2025, is the third in the AI safety summit series initiated at Bletchley Park in 2023. Hosted by France under President Emmanuel Macron, the summit marked a significant thematic evolution from its predecessors: while Bletchley and Seoul focused primarily on frontier AI risks and safety evaluations, the Paris summit adopted a broader agenda encompassing AI for the public good, AI governance for developing countries, AI's impact on the workplace, and open-source AI development. Approximately 60 countries and numerous international organizations adopted the Paris Statement. Notably, the United States under President Trump did not co-sign the final statement, and the UK vice president JD Vance delivered remarks warning against excessive AI regulation. The Paris Summit is notable for centering Global South perspectives on AI governance and for its commitment to ensuring developing countries benefit from AI development.

---

## Key Provisions

### AI for Public Good
- Commitment to harnessing AI to address global challenges including healthcare, climate change, and scientific research
- Establishment of working groups on AI applications in public services
- Support for open-source AI models and public access to AI infrastructure
- Commitment to creating an international AI research body under auspices of UNESCO

### AI Governance and Inclusion for Developing Countries
- Recognition that AI governance must be inclusive of perspectives from the Global South
- Commitment to technology transfer and capacity building for developing nations
- Support for multilateral AI governance through UN and international institutions
- Emphasis on ensuring AI benefits are shared equitably across income levels and regions

### AI Safety and Advanced Systems
- Reaffirmation of Bletchley and Seoul commitments on frontier AI safety
- Commitment to continued international cooperation on safety evaluation and research
- Support for the international AI Safety Institute network
- Acknowledgment of need for balanced approach to safety and innovation

### AI and the Workplace
- Recognition of AI's transformative impacts on employment and labor markets
- Commitment to developing frameworks for worker protection and transition support
- Emphasis on social dialogue between governments, employers, and workers on AI adoption

### Open Source AI
- Paris Statement notably addressed open-source AI development — an area not addressed at prior summits
- Recognition of dual benefits and risks of open-source AI (democratization vs. misuse)
- Commitment to developing governance frameworks that accommodate open-source models

### US Non-Participation
- United States under the Trump administration declined to co-sign the Paris Statement
- Vice President JD Vance addressed the summit with remarks caution against over-regulation of AI
- US non-participation marks a significant divergence from the Bletchley-Seoul consensus
- Signals potential fragmentation of the international AI safety governance architecture

### Non-Binding Voluntary Character
- Paris Statement is a political declaration, not a legally binding treaty
- Adopted by approximately 60 countries and multiple international organizations
- Intended to guide national policies and international cooperation rather than impose obligations

## Enforcement & Penalties

- No enforcement mechanism: non-binding political declaration
- Progress on commitments to be assessed through ongoing international forum processes
- Signatory governments individually responsible for domestic implementation
- UN and international institutions tasked with coordinating follow-up on specific commitments
- Successor summit expected to review implementation of Paris commitments
