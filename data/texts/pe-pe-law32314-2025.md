# Peru — Law No. 32314 (2025)

## Official Title

Ley que modifica el Código Penal y la Ley N° 30096, Ley de Delitos Informáticos, para sancionar el uso de la inteligencia artificial en la comisión de delitos.

(Law that modifies the Penal Code and Law No. 30096, the Cybercrime Law, to penalize the use of artificial intelligence in the commission of crimes.)

## Publication and Effective Date

Published in El Peruano (Peru's Official Gazette): April 28-29, 2025

## Overview

Peru's Law No. 32314, published April 28-29, 2025, modifies the Penal Code and the Cybercrime Law (Law No. 30096) to define and increase penalties for criminal conduct committed through the use of artificial intelligence (AI) or similar technologies. The legislation treats AI use as an aggravating circumstance across multiple offense categories.

This law represents a significant precedent in Latin America by formally recognizing the impact of AI on the commission of crimes and establishing specific criminal provisions for AI-facilitated offenses.

## Key Modifications to the Penal Code

### Article 46 — Aggravating Circumstances

The law adds two new aggravating factors to Article 46 of the Penal Code:
- The "improper use of artificial intelligence or similar technologies"
- Conducting crimes specifically "utilizing artificial intelligence or analogous technologies"

When AI is used to facilitate a crime, courts are required to increase the base sentence by up to one-third above the maximum penalty.

### Article 129-M — Child Sexual Abuse Material

Penalties increase for offenses involving "technologies based on artificial intelligence, including deepfakes or any multimedia content generated by artificial intelligence." Sentences range from 6 to 10 years imprisonment for violations using AI-generated content.

### Article 132 — Defamation

Using "artificial intelligence, deepfakes or other AI-generated content disseminating false or denigrating information" triggers enhanced penalties of 1-3 years imprisonment plus fines.

### Article 196-A — Aggravated Fraud

A new provision penalizes manipulating "voice, image, audio or body movement of third parties using artificial intelligence," imposing 4-8 years imprisonment.

## Intellectual Property Protections

### Articles 217-220

Law 32314 strengthens copyright enforcement:
- Criminalizes circumvention of technological protection measures
- Penalizes commercial distribution of circumvention tools or services
- Sentences of 2-4 years for violations

### Article 219 — Plagiarism

Specifically addresses works "of human authorship," establishing clearer protections against attribution violations and addressing AI-generated works that infringe moral or economic rights.

## Cybercrime Law (Law 30096) Updates

Law 30096 (Cybercrime Law) is amended to include AI use as a specific aggravating factor, allowing judges to increase sentences by up to one-third above the maximum penalty for any information-technology crime.

## Specific Criminal Applications

The new framework covers aggravating factors where:
- AI-generated videos or audios are used to defame or impersonate a person
- Images of minors are manipulated with pornographic purposes using AI
- AI is used to simulate identities or voices in economic frauds (deepfake-based scams)
- Automated bots are used for large-scale fraud

## Implementation Timeline

The Executive Branch has 60 calendar days from enactment to issue complementary regulations ensuring proper implementation.

## Context

Law 32314 is distinct from Peru's earlier Law No. 31814 (Ley que promueve el uso de la inteligencia artificial), which was enacted in 2023 to promote beneficial AI use. Law 32314 focuses specifically on criminal enforcement aspects, treating AI misuse as an aggravating factor across multiple crime categories.

The law sets a precedent in Latin America by formally recognizing the impact of AI on the commission of crimes and establishing specific criminal provisions for AI-facilitated offenses, particularly deepfakes and automated fraud.
