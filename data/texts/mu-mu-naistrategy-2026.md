Mauritius National Artificial Intelligence Strategy 2030 and FAIR Guidelines
Ministry of Information Technology, Communication and Innovation (MITCI), Republic of Mauritius
MAURITIUS | 2026

Source: https://www.undp.org/mauritius-seychelles/press-releases/mauritius-launches-national-ai-strategy-2030

Non-binding national AI strategy launched in April 2026 by Mauritius's Ministry of Information Technology, Communication and Innovation (MITCI) with support from the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP). The strategy covers the period through 2030 and is accompanied by the FAIR Guidelines — Mauritius's national ethical AI framework.

Key Elements (based on publicly reported information):

1. National AI Strategy 2030
   The strategy sets out Mauritius's vision to become an AI-powered, inclusive digital economy by 2030. It identifies priority sectors and cross-cutting themes for AI deployment.

   Priority Sectors:
   - Manufacturing: AI-assisted quality control, predictive maintenance, supply chain optimization
   - Healthcare: AI diagnostics, patient management, telemedicine
   - Agriculture: Precision agriculture, crop disease detection, irrigation management
   - Financial technology (fintech): Fraud detection, credit risk assessment, regulatory compliance
   - Transport and logistics: Smart mobility, traffic management, port operations
   - Citizen services: E-government, public administration automation, service delivery

2. FAIR Guidelines
   The FAIR Guidelines establish Mauritius's ethical framework for AI development and use, built around four principles:

   F — Fairness
   - AI systems must treat all individuals and groups equitably
   - Active monitoring for and mitigation of algorithmic bias
   - Inclusive design that accounts for diverse populations

   A — Accountability
   - Clear responsibility for AI system decisions and outcomes
   - Mechanisms for redress when AI causes harm
   - Audit trails and documentation requirements

   I — Inclusiveness / Integrity
   - AI benefits should be accessible to all segments of society
   - AI systems must maintain data integrity and system reliability
   - Particular attention to vulnerable and marginalized populations

   R — Responsibility
   - Developers and deployers bear responsibility for AI system impacts
   - Human oversight must be maintained over consequential AI decisions
   - Environmental sustainability considerations in AI deployment

3. Governance Framework
   - Inter-ministerial coordination committee for AI governance
   - National AI Advisory Board with public and private sector representation
   - Sector-specific AI working groups

4. Human Capital Development
   - AI skills integration in educational curricula
   - Professional development programs for government officials
   - Partnerships with international universities and research institutions
   - Support for Mauritian AI startups and entrepreneurs

5. Data and Infrastructure
   - National data governance framework aligned with the Data Protection Act 2017
   - Investment in digital infrastructure to support AI workloads
   - Open data initiatives for public sector datasets

6. International Cooperation
   - Partnership with UNDP for implementation support
   - Engagement with African Union AI initiatives
   - Bilateral cooperation agreements for AI capacity building
