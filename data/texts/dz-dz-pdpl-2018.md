# Loi n° 18-07 du 10 juin 2018 relative à la protection des personnes physiques dans le traitement des données à caractère personnel — Algeria
**Loi n° 18-07**
*Algeria — June 10, 2018 (effective July 15, 2018)*

---

## Overview

Algeria's Law 18-07 of June 10, 2018, is the country's comprehensive personal data protection statute, establishing a full data protection regime for the processing of personal data of Algerian residents. The law establishes the Autorité Nationale de Protection des Données à Caractère Personnel (ANPDP) as the independent supervisory authority. Drawing on the French civil law tradition and aligned with international best practices, the law applies to all processing of personal data carried out in Algeria or relating to Algerian residents, covering both public and private sectors. It represents Algeria's first comprehensive data protection legislation and was developed within the broader context of North Africa's digital governance reforms.

---

## Key Provisions

### Autorité Nationale de Protection des Données à Caractère Personnel (ANPDP)
- Independent administrative authority with powers to register, investigate, and sanction
- Issues authorizations for certain high-risk processing activities
- Receives declarations for standard processing activities
- Investigates complaints from data subjects and conducts ex officio investigations
- Issues recommendations, guidelines, and binding orders
- May impose administrative sanctions and refer criminal cases

### Lawful Processing Bases
- Unambiguous consent of the data subject
- Performance of a contract to which the data subject is party
- Compliance with a legal obligation
- Protection of vital interests of the data subject
- Legitimate interests of the controller, subject to balancing test

### Sensitive Personal Data Special Categories
- Health and medical data
- Genetic data
- Biometric data used for identification
- Political opinions
- Religious or philosophical beliefs
- Trade union membership
- Racial or ethnic origin
- Criminal record and offenses
- Processing of these categories requires explicit consent or specific legal authority

### Data Subject Rights
- Right to information about data collection and processing
- Right of access to personal data held
- Right to rectification of inaccurate or incomplete data
- Right to opposition to processing on legitimate grounds
- Right to deletion/erasure in certain circumstances

### Mandatory Registration and Authorization
- Controllers must declare standard processing activities to ANPDP
- High-risk processing (sensitive data, systematic monitoring, large-scale processing) requires prior authorization from ANPDP
- Failure to comply is an offense

### Cross-Border Data Transfers
- Transfers outside Algeria restricted unless recipient country provides adequate protection or specific safeguards
- ANPDP may approve transfers through standard contractual clauses or binding corporate rules
- Transfers to countries without adequacy require ANPDP authorization

### Security Obligations
- Data controllers must implement appropriate technical and organizational security measures
- Obligation to notify ANPDP of significant personal data breaches

## Enforcement & Penalties

- ANPDP may issue compliance orders and formal warnings
- Administrative fines for violations of the law
- Criminal penalties: fines up to DZD 5,000,000 (approximately $37,000 USD) and/or up to 5 years imprisonment for serious violations
- Aggravated penalties for violations involving sensitive personal data
- Civil liability: data subjects may seek compensation through Algerian courts
