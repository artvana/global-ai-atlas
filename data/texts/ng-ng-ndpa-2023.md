## Summary and Analysis

### Purpose and Scope

The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (Act No. 4 of 2023, "NDPA" or "the Act") was signed into law on 12 June 2023 by President Bola Ahmed Tinubu, making it Nigeria's first comprehensive, primary legislation dedicated to the protection of personal data. Prior to the NDPA, Nigeria's data protection framework rested primarily on the Nigeria Data Protection Regulation 2019 (NDPR), a subsidiary instrument issued by the National Information Technology Development Agency (NITDA) under the NITDA Act. While the NDPR was landmark in establishing data protection principles and enforcement mechanisms, its status as a subsidiary regulation raised questions about its constitutional robustness and limited the enforcement penalties available. The NDPA corrects these limitations by establishing a comprehensive statutory framework backed by primary legislation.

The NDPA applies to the processing of personal data by any person — natural or legal, public or private, domestic or foreign — where: the processing is carried out in Nigeria; the controller or processor is established in Nigeria; the processing relates to individuals who are in Nigeria even if the controller is established outside Nigeria; or the processing involves Nigerian citizens or nationals. The Act covers automated and non-automated processing of personal data held in structured systems.

Limited exemptions are provided for: processing by an individual in the course of purely personal or household activities with no connection to professional or commercial purposes; processing by competent authorities for the purposes of the prevention, investigation, detection, or prosecution of criminal offences or execution of criminal penalties; processing for national security purposes; and processing for statistical, research, or archival purposes under appropriate safeguards.

The Act establishes the **Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC)** as the primary regulatory authority, replacing the data protection functions previously exercised by NITDA. The NDPC is constituted as an independent body headed by a National Commissioner appointed by the President on the recommendation of the Minister of Communications and Digital Economy, subject to Senate confirmation. The NDPC is empowered to issue regulations and guidelines, maintain the register of data controllers, receive and investigate complaints, and impose administrative penalties.

### Key Definitions

**Personal data** means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. A natural person is identifiable if they can be identified, directly or indirectly, by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more factors specific to their physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity.

**Sensitive personal data** means personal data revealing: racial or ethnic origin; political opinions; religious or philosophical beliefs; trade union membership; biometric data for unique identification purposes; genetic data; data concerning health; data concerning sex life or sexual orientation; and such other data as the NDPC may by regulation designate.

**Data controller** means any person who, alone or jointly with others, determines the purpose and means of processing personal data.

**Data processor** means any person who processes personal data on behalf of a data controller.

**Data subject** means the natural person to whom personal data relates.

**Consent** means any freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous indication of the data subject's wishes by which they signify, by a statement or a clear affirmative action, agreement to the processing of personal data relating to them.

**Data controller of major importance** means a data controller that processes the personal data of more than a threshold number of data subjects determined by the NDPC, or that processes the personal data of data subjects in a manner likely to affect a significant portion of the Nigerian population. Data controllers of major importance are subject to enhanced obligations.

### Principles of Data Processing and Core Obligations

**Principles.** Processing of personal data must comply with the following principles under the NDPA:

- **Lawfulness, fairness, and transparency** — data must be processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject.
- **Purpose limitation** — data must be collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes and not further processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes.
- **Data minimization** — data must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary for the purposes of processing.
- **Accuracy** — data must be accurate and kept up to date.
- **Storage limitation** — data must be retained only for as long as necessary for the stated purposes.
- **Integrity and confidentiality** — data must be processed in a manner ensuring appropriate security, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing, accidental loss, destruction, or damage.
- **Accountability** — the data controller is responsible for and must demonstrate compliance with these principles.

**Lawful Bases.** Processing must be grounded in: consent of the data subject; performance of a contract; compliance with a legal obligation; protection of vital interests; performance of a public interest task; or the legitimate interests of the controller, balanced against the data subject's rights.

For sensitive personal data, the Act requires explicit consent as the primary lawful basis, with narrow exceptions for employment and social protection purposes, vital interests, public health, legal claims, and processing by non-profit bodies for their members.

**Transparency and Privacy Notices.** Controllers must provide data subjects with comprehensive information about processing at the time of data collection or as soon as practicable thereafter, covering the controller's identity, the purpose and legal basis for processing, data recipients, international transfers, retention periods, and data subject rights.

**Security Measures.** Controllers and processors must implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to protect personal data against unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, or destruction. The level of security must be proportionate to the nature of the data and the risks of the processing.

**Data Breach Notification.** Controllers are required to notify the NDPC of personal data breaches within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach, where the breach is likely to result in risk to the rights and freedoms of data subjects. Where the breach is likely to result in high risk, controllers must also notify affected data subjects without undue delay.

**Data Protection Impact Assessments.** Controllers must conduct DPIAs for processing likely to result in high risk, including large-scale processing of sensitive personal data, systematic monitoring of public areas, and processing involving new technologies.

**Registration of Data Controllers.** Data controllers of major importance must register with the NDPC and pay annual fees. The NDPC maintains a public register. Registered controllers are subject to periodic audits by NDPC-accredited data protection compliance organizations (DPCOs), which are third-party compliance entities licensed by the NDPC.

**Data Protection Compliance Organizations (DPCOs).** A distinctive feature of Nigeria's framework is the DPCO system. DPCOs are licensed private entities authorized by the NDPC to assist data controllers in achieving compliance, conducting audits, and providing training. Data controllers of major importance must engage a DPCO for annual compliance audits.

**Data Protection Officer.** Certain data controllers must appoint a DPO: data controllers of major importance; those whose core activities involve large-scale, regular, and systematic monitoring; and those whose core activities involve large-scale processing of sensitive personal data.

### Rights of Data Subjects

The NDPA provides data subjects with the following rights:

**Right to be informed.** The right to receive comprehensive information about processing before or at the time of collection.

**Right of access.** The right to obtain confirmation of whether personal data is being processed and, if so, to receive a copy of that data along with information about the processing.

**Right to rectification.** The right to have inaccurate or incomplete personal data corrected.

**Right to erasure.** The right to have personal data deleted where it is no longer necessary, consent is withdrawn, an objection is upheld, or processing was unlawful.

**Right to data portability.** The right to receive personal data in a structured, machine-readable format and to transmit it to another controller.

**Right to restriction.** The right to restrict processing while accuracy is contested or an objection is pending.

**Right to object.** The right to object to processing based on legitimate interests or for direct marketing.

**Rights in relation to automated decision-making.** The right not to be subject to solely automated decisions with significant effects and to request human review.

**Right to withdraw consent.** The right to withdraw consent at any time.

**Right to complain.** The right to file a complaint with the NDPC.

**Right to compensation.** The right to seek civil compensation for material and non-material damage resulting from violations.

Controllers must respond to data subject requests within 30 days, extendable by a further 30 days for complex cases. Refusals must be communicated with reasons.

### Automated Processing Provisions

The NDPA contains provisions on automated decision-making consistent with the international standard established by GDPR Article 22. Data subjects have the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing — including profiling — that produces legal effects or similarly significantly affects them.

The right is subject to exceptions where: the automated decision is necessary for entering into or performing a contract; the decision is authorized by law; or the data subject has given explicit consent. Where an exception applies, the controller must implement safeguards including: the ability for the data subject to obtain human intervention; the data subject's right to express their point of view; and the data subject's right to contest the decision.

Controllers using automated processing for profiling purposes must conduct DPIAs and must implement measures to prevent discriminatory or erroneous outcomes. The NDPC may issue sectoral guidance on automated processing, particularly for financial services, insurance, employment, and public administration.

### Cross-Border Transfer Rules

The NDPA establishes a framework for international data transfers requiring that transferred personal data be protected to an equivalent standard as under the Act. Transfers may occur where:

- The NDPC has issued an adequacy determination recognizing that the recipient country provides equivalent protection;
- The controller has implemented appropriate safeguards including standard contractual clauses approved by the NDPC, binding corporate rules, or adherence to an approved code of conduct; or
- A specific derogation applies, such as explicit consent, contract necessity, vital interests, legal claims, or compelling public interest.

The NDPC must maintain and publish a list of countries with adequate protection. For transfers that do not meet these requirements, controllers must apply to the NDPC for authorization.

### Enforcement and Penalties

The NDPC has broad enforcement powers, including the ability to conduct investigations, require production of information, issue compliance notices, suspend processing, and impose administrative fines.

**Administrative penalties** are tiered as follows:

For individuals: fines up to **₦10,000,000** (approximately USD 6,500) for violations of the Act's requirements.

For organizations: the higher of **₦10,000,000** or **2% of the preceding year's annual global gross revenue or turnover**, whichever is greater. This percentage-of-turnover structure mirrors the GDPR model and ensures that penalties are proportionate to the size of the violating entity.

For the most serious violations involving sensitive personal data, systematic processing without lawful basis, or obstruction of the NDPC: fines up to **₦50,000,000** (approximately USD 32,000) for individuals and the higher of **₦50,000,000** or **4% of annual global revenue** for organizations.

Violations involving children's data, health data, or biometric data are subject to the highest penalty category. The NDPC must consider the nature, gravity, and duration of the violation; the number of affected data subjects; the degree of responsibility; and any remedial actions taken when determining penalties.

Criminal liability may apply to the most egregious cases involving willful unauthorized disclosure of sensitive personal data for gain or malicious purposes, with penalties including imprisonment.

Data subjects may also pursue civil claims for compensation before the courts for material and non-material damages from violations of their rights under the Act.

### Relationship with the NDPR

The Nigeria Data Protection Regulation 2019 (NDPR) and associated frameworks issued by NITDA are progressively superseded by the NDPA and regulations issued thereunder by the NDPC. Processing activities that were compliant with the NDPR were given a transition period to achieve compliance with the NDPA's more comprehensive requirements. The NDPC has confirmed that NDPR compliance guidance remains broadly applicable during the transition period where it is consistent with the NDPA, and has issued updated guidance on areas where the NDPA introduces new or different requirements.
