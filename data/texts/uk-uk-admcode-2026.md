# The Data Protection Act 2018 (Code of Practice on AI and Automated Decision-Making) Regulations 2026 (SI 2026/425)

**Statutory Instrument 2026 No. 425**
*United Kingdom — Made 21 April 2026; in force 12 May 2026*

---

## Overview

The Data Protection Act 2018 (Code of Practice on AI and Automated Decision-Making) Regulations 2026 (SI 2026/425) is a statutory instrument made under the Data Protection Act 2018, mandating the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) to prepare, consult on, and issue a statutory code of practice governing AI systems and automated decision-making (ADM) in the United Kingdom.

The SI implements a commitment made in the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (2025 c. 18) to provide clear, binding regulatory guidance on AI and ADM practices under UK GDPR. It does not itself contain the substantive code — rather, it sets the legal basis and procedural requirements for the ICO to develop, consult on, and finalize the code. Once issued, the code will carry statutory weight under the Data Protection Act 2018: courts and the ICO must take it into account when assessing compliance with UK GDPR's automated decision-making provisions.

The ICO launched a public consultation on a draft ADM Code of Practice on 31 March 2026. The consultation closed in June 2026 (after the effective date of this SI).

## Key Provisions

### Mandate to Issue the Code

The SI creates a statutory duty on the Information Commissioner to issue a Code of Practice on AI and Automated Decision-Making within 18 months of the SI's commencement date (12 May 2026), covering:

- The application of UK GDPR Chapter III rights to automated decision-making, including profiling
- The rights created under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 in relation to automated decisions — specifically the right to human review of significant automated decisions
- Standards for meaningful explanation of automated decision logic
- Requirements for documentation and record-keeping by controllers operating ADM systems
- Sector-specific guidance for high-impact ADM contexts (employment, credit, insurance, public services)
- Guidance on the use of AI in automated processing of sensitive personal data categories

### Procedural Requirements for Code Development

The SI specifies that the Commissioner must:
- Publish a draft code for public consultation (minimum 12-week consultation period)
- Consult with specified stakeholders including: representatives of data subjects; AI developers and deployers; industry bodies; civil society organizations; and sector-specific regulators (FCA, Ofcom, CMA)
- Submit the draft code to the Secretary of State for approval before issuance
- Lay a copy before Parliament

### Legal Status of the Code

Once issued, the Code of Practice will be a statutory code under Section 121 of the Data Protection Act 2018. Its legal effects are:
- Courts and tribunals must take the Code into account when determining UK GDPR compliance in ADM-related proceedings
- The ICO must take the Code into account in regulatory decisions
- Compliance with the Code raises a presumption of compliance with relevant UK GDPR automated decision-making provisions
- The Code does not create new criminal offences or directly enforceable rights; enforcement remains via ICO regulatory action

### Relationship to Existing ICO Guidance

The statutory code, once finalized, will supersede existing non-statutory ICO guidance on automated decision-making and AI fairness, giving those obligations statutory rather than advisory status. The SI explicitly preserves existing guidance during the interim period pending code issuance.

### ICO Consultation (March 2026)

In anticipation of the SI's commencement, the ICO launched a public consultation on a draft ADM Code of Practice on 31 March 2026, covering: transparency obligations for AI-driven decisions; fairness and non-discrimination standards for automated systems; standards for "meaningful human review"; and documentation requirements. The consultation invited responses from AI developers, deployers, affected individuals, and civil society.
