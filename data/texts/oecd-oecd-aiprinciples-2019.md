# OECD Recommendation of the Council on Artificial Intelligence (OECD-LEGAL-0449)

## Overview

The OECD AI Principles were initially adopted on 22 May 2019 and updated in May 2024. They constitute the first intergovernmental standard on AI, with 47 adherents as of 2024. The Principles promote the use of AI that is innovative and trustworthy and that respects human rights and democratic values. The European Union, the Council of Europe, the United States, the United Nations, and other jurisdictions use the OECD's definition of an AI system and lifecycle in their legislative and regulatory frameworks and guidance.

## Definition of an AI System

An AI system is a machine-based system that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infers, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments. Different AI systems vary in their levels of autonomy and adaptiveness after deployment.

## AI System Lifecycle

The AI system lifecycle includes all phases: planning and design, data collection and processing, model building and training, verification and validation, deployment and operation, and monitoring and evaluation—ending when the system is decommissioned.

## AI Actors

AI actors are those who play an active role in the AI system lifecycle, including organisations and individuals that deploy or operate AI systems. Governments and organisations that deploy or operate AI systems are also considered AI actors.

## SECTION 1: PRINCIPLES FOR RESPONSIBLE STEWARDSHIP OF TRUSTWORTHY AI

### Principle 1.1 – Inclusive Growth, Sustainable Development and Well-Being

Stakeholders should proactively engage in responsible stewardship of trustworthy AI in pursuit of beneficial outcomes for people and the planet, such as augmenting human capabilities and enhancing creativity, advancing inclusion of underrepresented populations, reducing economic, social, gender and other inequalities, and protecting natural environments, thus invigorating inclusive growth, well-being, sustainable development and environmental sustainability.

**Rationale:** This principle acknowledges that AI development and deployment should advance inclusive growth, sustainable development, and human well-being while advancing global development objectives. The framework recognizes AI's potential to contribute to the Sustainable Development Goals across education, health, transport, agriculture, and environmental sectors.

Key concerns addressed include:

- **Inequality risks:** Technology access disparities may widen divides between developed and developing countries.
- **Bias and disparate impact:** AI systems risk perpetuating existing biases affecting vulnerable populations including ethnic minorities, women, children, the elderly, and less-educated individuals.
- **Lifecycle accountability:** Throughout the AI system lifecycle, AI actors and stakeholders can, and should, encourage the development and deployment of AI for beneficial outcomes with appropriate safeguards.

The principle emphasizes that multidisciplinary collaboration, inclusive public dialogue, and multi-stakeholder engagement strengthen both trustworthy AI development and public confidence in these systems.

---

### Principle 1.2 – Human-Centred Values and Fairness

AI systems should be designed in a way that respects the rule of law, human rights, democratic values and diversity, and should include appropriate safeguards to ensure a fair and just society. To this end, AI actors should respect and uphold the following throughout the AI system lifecycle:

- Non-discrimination and equality
- Freedom and dignity of persons
- Individual autonomy and privacy
- Privacy and data protection
- Diversity and fairness
- Social justice
- Internationally recognized labor rights

**Extended Guidance:** AI actors must honor the rule of law, human rights, and human-centered values throughout an AI system's lifecycle. The principle addresses misinformation and disinformation amplified by AI, while respecting freedom of expression and other rights and freedoms protected by applicable international law.

**Implementation Mechanisms:** Organisations should establish mechanisms and safeguards, such as capacity for human agency and oversight, including to address risks arising from uses outside of intended purpose, intentional misuse, or unintentional misuse in a manner appropriate to the context and consistent with the state of the art.

**Supporting Measures:** The principle acknowledges tools for promoting alignment with human-centered values, including human rights impact assessments, human rights due diligence, human determination ("human in the loop"), ethical conduct codes, and quality labels or certifications.

---

### Principle 1.3 – Transparency and Explainability

AI actors should commit to transparency and responsible disclosure regarding AI systems. To this end, they should provide meaningful information, appropriate to the context, and consistent with the state of the art:

1. **Foster Understanding:** To foster a general understanding of AI systems, including their capabilities and limitations.
2. **Disclose Interactions:** To make stakeholders aware of their interactions with AI systems, including in the workplace.
3. **Explain Decisions:** Where feasible and useful, to provide plain and easy-to-understand information on the sources of data/input, factors, processes and/or logic that led to the prediction, content, recommendation or decision.
4. **Enable Challenge:** To provide information that enable those adversely affected by an AI system to challenge its output.

**Rationale:** The principle distinguishes between transparency (disclosing when AI is used and how systems operate) and explainability (enabling people to understand outcomes). The OECD acknowledges trade-offs: requiring detailed explanations may reduce system accuracy, increase costs, or conflict with intellectual property protections. Organisations should provide context-appropriate explanations of "main factors in a decision" while respecting data protection obligations.

---

### Principle 1.4 – Robustness, Security and Safety

AI systems must function in a robust, secure and safe way throughout their lifetimes, and potential risks should be continually assessed and managed.

**Key Requirements:**

- **Functional Standards:** AI systems should be robust, secure and safe throughout their entire lifecycle so that, in conditions of normal use, foreseeable use or misuse, or other adverse conditions, they function appropriately and do not pose unreasonable safety and/or security risks.
- **Safety Mechanisms:** Mechanisms should be in place, as appropriate, to ensure that if AI systems risk causing undue harm or exhibit undesired behaviour, they can be overridden, repaired, and/or decommissioned safely as needed.
- **Information Integrity:** Mechanisms should also, where technically feasible, be in place to bolster information integrity while ensuring respect for freedom of expression.

**Implementation Approaches:** The principle emphasizes two primary strategies: traceability with subsequent analysis for accountability, and systematic risk management throughout the AI lifecycle. Risk management involves identifying, assessing, prioritizing, and mitigating potential risks affecting system behavior and outcomes. Documentation of these decisions supports transparency and accountability principles.

---

### Principle 1.5 – Accountability

Organisations and individuals developing, deploying or operating AI systems should be held accountable for their proper functioning in line with the above principles.

**Key Requirements:**

AI actors must ensure accountability based on their roles and context by:

1. **Traceability:** Maintaining documentation of datasets, processes and decisions made during the AI system lifecycle to enable analysis and responses to inquiries, appropriate to circumstances and current technical standards.
2. **Risk Management:** Applying a systematic risk management approach to each phase of the AI system lifecycle on an ongoing basis and adopting responsible business practices to address AI-related risks including harmful bias, human rights, safety, security, privacy, labor rights, and intellectual property concerns.
3. **Cooperation:** Working with other actors—including suppliers, users, and stakeholders—to manage risks effectively.

**Definition Context:** The principle distinguishes accountability (ethical/moral expectations guiding conduct) from liability (legal consequences) and responsibility (causal links to outcomes). Accountability here means organisations and individuals will ensure the proper functioning, throughout their lifecycle, of the AI systems they develop or deploy, demonstrating this through documentation and auditing where justified.

## SECTION 2: NATIONAL POLICIES AND INTERNATIONAL CO-OPERATION FOR TRUSTWORTHY AI

### Recommendation 2.1 – Investing in AI Research and Development

Governments should facilitate public and private investment in research and development to spur innovation in trustworthy AI.

**Detailed Recommendation:**

- **R&D Investment:** Governments should evaluate sustained public funding and stimulate private sector investment in research, development, and open science—particularly interdisciplinary initiatives. This focus should address both technical challenges and AI-related social, legal and ethical implications and policy issues.
- **Open Resources:** Governments should contemplate public funding (and encourage private funding) for open-source tools and datasets that are representative, privacy-respecting, and data-protection-compliant. These resources aim to eliminate harmful algorithmic bias and enhance interoperability through standardization.

**Rationale:** The principle recognizes that AI breakthroughs could address major societal challenges and generate new industries. While commercial entities lead applied research spending, governments must sustain long-horizon basic research—especially in under-resourced areas. Public research tackles technical problems benefiting multiple stakeholders and addresses societal concerns like transparency and explainability. The recommendation emphasizes representative datasets as critical infrastructure, noting that bias results when datasets that are not sufficiently representative are deployed inappropriately, regardless of discriminatory intent.

---

### Recommendation 2.2 – Fostering an Inclusive AI-Enabling Ecosystem

Governments should foster the development of, and access to, an inclusive, dynamic, sustainable, and interoperable digital ecosystem for trustworthy AI. This ecosystem should encompass data, AI technologies, computational and connectivity infrastructure, and mechanisms for sharing AI knowledge. Governments should consider promoting data trusts and similar mechanisms to support safe, fair, legal, and ethical data sharing.

**Key Components:**

The enabling ecosystem requires:

- **Infrastructure:** Affordable high-speed broadband networks, computing power, data storage, and supporting technologies like the Internet-of-Things.
- **Knowledge Sharing:** Open source tools, quality training datasets, code, algorithms, models, and research that respect privacy and intellectual property.
- **Data Mechanisms:** Structured approaches like data trusts that manage risks including confidentiality breaches, IP protection, and security concerns.
- **Inclusive Datasets:** Diverse and representative data sources that promote equitable AI development.

**SME Considerations:** Policymakers should prioritize small and medium-sized enterprise access to data, AI technologies, connectivity, computing capacity, and cloud platforms to foster digital entrepreneurship and innovation.

---

### Recommendation 2.3 – Enabling Policy Environment for AI

Governments should ensure that policy and regulatory frameworks are appropriate to foster innovation and the deployment of trustworthy AI, while ensuring effective protection of the public interest.

**Key Guidance Points:**

- **Policy Agility and Experimentation:** Governments should promote an agile policy environment that supports transitioning from the research and development stage to the deployment and operation stage. This includes using controlled testing environments where AI systems can be evaluated and scaled appropriately.
- **Flexibility and Interoperability:** Authorities should adopt outcome-based approaches that provide flexibility in achieving governance objectives and cooperate across jurisdictions to establish compatible governance frameworks.
- **Regulatory Adaptation:** Governments must review and adapt, as appropriate, their policy and regulatory frameworks and assessment mechanisms as they apply to AI systems to encourage innovation and competition for trustworthy AI.

**Supporting Context:** The recommendation emphasizes balancing adaptability with safety and legal certainty. It recognizes experimentation's value as a mechanism for transparent, controlled testing of AI systems in "start-up mode," where initiatives can be deployed, evaluated, modified, and scaled based on outcomes. The framework also encourages self-regulatory mechanisms, including industry codes of conduct and voluntary standards, complementing formal governance structures.

---

### Recommendation 2.4 – Building Human Capacity and Preparing for Labour Market Transformation

Governments should equip people with the skills for AI and support workers to ensure a fair transition.

**Three Key Commitments:**

1. **Skills Development and AI Capability:** Governments must work closely with stakeholders to prepare for the transformation of the world of work and empower people to effectively use and interact with AI systems across the breadth of applications, including by equipping them with the necessary skills.

2. **Fair Worker Transition:** Nations should take steps, including through social dialogue, to ensure a fair transition for workers as AI is deployed, such as through training programmes along the working life, support for those affected by displacement, including through social protection, and access to new opportunities in the labour market.

3. **Responsible Workplace AI Integration:** Governments must work closely with stakeholders to promote the responsible use of AI at work, to enhance the safety of workers, the quality of jobs and of public services, to foster entrepreneurship and productivity, and aim to ensure that the benefits from AI are broadly and fairly shared.

**Rationale:** The principle addresses AI's transformative impact on employment, requiring coordinated policy responses spanning education, social protection, labour standards, and collective bargaining to manage transitions equitably while leveraging AI's potential to augment human capabilities.

---

### Recommendation 2.5 – International Co-operation for Trustworthy AI

Governments should co-operate across borders and sectors to share information, develop standards and work towards responsible stewardship of trustworthy AI.

**Key Sub-Points:**

- **Cooperative Implementation:** Governments, including developing nations and stakeholders, should actively advance these principles and progress responsible stewardship of trustworthy AI.
- **Knowledge Sharing:** Governments should collaborate through the OECD and other global forums to foster AI knowledge sharing. They should encourage international, cross-sectoral, and open multi-stakeholder initiatives to build long-term AI expertise.
- **Technical Standards:** Governments should promote development of multi-stakeholder, consensus-driven global technical standards for interoperable and trustworthy AI.
- **Measurement and Assessment:** Governments should encourage development and use of internationally comparable indicators measuring AI research, development, and deployment, establishing evidence to evaluate principle implementation progress.

**Rationale:** This principle addresses global AI opportunities and challenges through international cooperation, including advancing implementation across OECD and partner countries. Cooperation leverages forums to share knowledge, develop trustworthy standards, and create metrics assessing system performance regarding accuracy, efficiency, fairness, and robustness while enabling secure transborder data flows.

## Adherents

As of 2024, there are 47 adherents to the OECD AI Principles. OECD Member countries adhering include: Australia, Austria, Belgium, Canada, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Ireland, Israel, Italy, Japan, Korea, Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Mexico, Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Slovak Republic, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Türkiye, United Kingdom, and United States. The European Union adheres to the Principles. Non-Member adherents include: Argentina, Brazil, Egypt, Malta, Peru, Romania, Singapore, and Ukraine. G20 members committed to the G20 AI Principles drawn from the OECD AI Principles include China, India, Indonesia, Russia, Saudi Arabia, and South Africa.
