# Law No. 132 of 23 September 2025

## Provisions and Delegations to the Government Regarding Artificial Intelligence

**Italian Republic · Effective: 1 November 2025 · Reference: 25G00143**

---

## Chapter I — Principles and Objectives

### Article 1 — Purpose and Scope of Application

This law sets out principles regarding research, experimentation, development, adoption, and application of artificial intelligence systems and models. It promotes correct, transparent, and responsible use, in an anthropocentric dimension, of artificial intelligence. It guarantees supervision of economic and social risks and of the impact on fundamental rights of artificial intelligence. The provisions of this law are interpreted and applied in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the EU AI Act).

### Article 2 — Definitions

a) **artificial intelligence system**: the system defined in Article 3(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689;

b) **data**: any digital representation of acts, facts, or information, also in the form of sound, visual, or audiovisual recording;

c) **artificial intelligence models**: the models defined in Article 3(63) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

### Article 3 — General Principles

1. Research, experimentation, development, adoption, application, and use of artificial intelligence systems and models take place in compliance with fundamental rights and freedoms provided for by the Constitution, European Union law, and the principles of transparency, proportionality, security, protection of personal data, confidentiality, accuracy, non-discrimination, gender equality, and sustainability.

2. The development of artificial intelligence systems and models takes place on data and through processes whose correctness, reliability, security, quality, appropriateness, and transparency must be guaranteed and supervised, according to the principle of proportionality in relation to the sectors in which they are used.

3. Artificial intelligence systems and models must be developed and applied in compliance with human autonomy and decision-making power, prevention of harm, knowability, transparency, explainability, ensuring human oversight and intervention.

4. The use of artificial intelligence systems must not prejudice the conduct of institutional and political life by democratic method, nor the freedom of democratic debate from unlawful interference.

5. This law does not create new obligations compared to those provided for by Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 for artificial intelligence systems and artificial intelligence models for general purposes.

6. Cybersecurity must be ensured as an essential precondition throughout the entire life cycle of artificial intelligence systems, according to a risk-based and proportionate approach.

7. This law guarantees persons with disabilities full access to artificial intelligence systems on an equal basis and without any form of discrimination.

### Article 4 — Principles Regarding Information and Confidentiality of Personal Data

1. The use of artificial intelligence systems in information takes place without prejudice to the freedom and pluralism of the media, freedom of expression, and the objectivity, completeness, impartiality, and fairness of information.

2. The use of artificial intelligence systems guarantees lawful, correct, and transparent processing of personal data, in accordance with European Union law.

3. Information and communications relating to data processing connected to the use of artificial intelligence systems are provided in clear and simple language, in order to guarantee the user knowledge of the related risks and the right to object to authorized processing of their personal data.

4. Access to artificial intelligence technologies by minors under fourteen years of age requires the consent of those exercising parental responsibility. A minor between fourteen and eighteen years of age may express their own consent for the processing of personal data connected to the use of artificial intelligence systems, provided that the relevant information is easily accessible and understandable.

### Article 5 — Principles Regarding Economic Development

The State and other public authorities:

a) promote the development and use of artificial intelligence as a tool to improve productivity across all value chains and organizational functions, as well as a useful tool for starting new economic activities;

b) favor the creation of an innovative, fair, open, and competitive artificial intelligence market;

c) facilitate the availability and access to high-quality data for companies that develop or use artificial intelligence systems;

d) direct public e-procurement platforms to favor AI solutions that guarantee localization and processing of strategic data in data centers located in national territory, with disaster recovery and business continuity also in national territory;

e) favor collaborative research between companies, research bodies, and technology transfer centers in the field of artificial intelligence.

## Chapter II — Sectoral Provisions

### Article 7 — Use of Artificial Intelligence in the Health and Disability Sector

1. The use of artificial intelligence systems contributes to the improvement of the health system, to the prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of diseases, in compliance with the rights, freedoms, and interests of the person.

2. The use of artificial intelligence systems in healthcare cannot select and condition access to healthcare services according to discriminatory criteria.

3. The data subject has the right to be informed about the use of artificial intelligence technologies.

4. This law promotes the development, study, and dissemination of artificial intelligence systems that improve the living conditions of persons with disabilities.

5. Artificial intelligence systems in the health sector constitute a support in the processes of prevention, diagnosis, treatment, and therapeutic choice, leaving the decision unaffected, which is always left to those practicing the medical profession.

6. Artificial intelligence systems used in the health sector and the related data used must be reliable, periodically verified, and updated in order to minimize the risk of errors and improve patient safety.

### Article 8 — Scientific Research and Experimentation in the Creation of Artificial Intelligence Systems in the Health Sector

1. The processing of personal data, carried out by public and private non-profit subjects and Scientific Hospitalization and Care Institutes (IRCCS), for scientific research and experimentation in the creation of artificial intelligence systems for purposes of prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of diseases, development of drugs, therapies, and rehabilitation technologies, creation of medical devices, and study of physiology and human biology, are declared to be of significant public interest.

2. For the same purposes, the secondary use of personal data lacking direct identifying elements, also belonging to special categories, is always authorized without further consent of the data subject, except in cases where knowledge of the identity of the data subjects is unavoidable or necessary for the purpose of protecting their health.

3. Processing for the purposes of anonymization, pseudonymization, or synthesis of personal data, also belonging to special categories, is always permitted, subject to information to the data subject.

### Article 11 — Provisions on the Use of Artificial Intelligence in Matters of Work

1. Artificial intelligence is used to improve working conditions, protect the psychophysical integrity of workers, and increase the quality of work performance and productivity.

2. The use of artificial intelligence in the workplace must be safe, reliable, transparent, and cannot take place in contrast with human dignity or violate the confidentiality of personal data. The employer or client is required to inform the worker of the use of artificial intelligence.

3. Artificial intelligence in the organization and management of the employment relationship guarantees respect for the inviolable rights of the worker without discrimination based on sex, age, ethnic origin, religious belief, sexual orientation, political opinions, and personal, social, and economic conditions.

### Article 12 — Observatory on the Adoption of Artificial Intelligence Systems in the World of Work

An Observatory on the adoption of artificial intelligence systems in the world of work is established at the Ministry of Labor and Social Policies, with the task of defining a strategy on the use of artificial intelligence in the workplace, monitoring the impact on the labor market, and identifying the work sectors most affected by the advent of artificial intelligence.

### Article 13 — Provisions Regarding Intellectual Professions

1. The use of artificial intelligence systems in intellectual professions is aimed solely at the exercise of instrumental activities and support for the professional activity, with prevalence of the intellectual work.

2. Information regarding the artificial intelligence systems used by the professional is communicated to the recipient of the intellectual service with clear, simple, and exhaustive language.

### Article 14 — Use of Artificial Intelligence in Public Administration

1. Public administrations use artificial intelligence in order to increase the efficiency of their activity, reduce the time for defining procedures, and increase the quality and quantity of services provided to citizens and businesses, ensuring interested parties knowledge of its functioning and traceability of its use.

2. The use of artificial intelligence takes place as an instrumental function and support for decision-making activity, in compliance with the autonomy and decision-making power of the person, who remains the sole responsible for the measures and procedures in which artificial intelligence has been used.

3. Public administrations adopt technical, organizational, and training measures aimed at guaranteeing responsible use of artificial intelligence.

### Article 15 — Use of Artificial Intelligence Systems in Judicial Activity

1. In cases of use of artificial intelligence systems in judicial activity, every decision on the interpretation and application of the law, on the evaluation of facts and evidence, and on the adoption of measures is reserved for the judge.

2. The Ministry of Justice regulates the uses of artificial intelligence systems for the organization of services relating to justice, for the simplification of judicial work, and for accessory administrative activities.

3. Until the complete implementation of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the experimentation and use of artificial intelligence systems in ordinary judicial offices are authorized by the Ministry of Justice.

### Article 16 — Delegation to the Government Regarding Data, Algorithms, and Mathematical Methods for Training AI Systems

The Government is delegated to adopt, within twelve months, one or more legislative decrees to define an organic discipline regarding the use of data, algorithms, and mathematical methods for training artificial intelligence systems, including: identification of legal regimes for use of data for AI training; protection measures of a compensatory or injunctive nature; and a sanctioning apparatus for violations.

## Chapter III — National Strategy, National Authorities, and Promotion Actions

### Article 19 — National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence

1. The national strategy for artificial intelligence is prepared and updated by the structure of the Presidency of the Council of Ministers competent for technological innovation and digital transition, in agreement with the National Authorities for artificial intelligence, and is approved at least biennially by the Interministerial Committee for Digital Transition (CITD).

2. The strategy favors collaboration between public administrations and private subjects regarding the development and adoption of artificial intelligence systems, coordinates public administration activity, promotes research and dissemination of knowledge in the field of artificial intelligence, and directs measures and incentives aimed at entrepreneurial and industrial development.

3. The strategy takes into account the principles of international humanitarian law, for the purpose of developing and promoting artificial intelligence systems that protect human rights.

### Article 20 — National Authorities for Artificial Intelligence

1. In order to guarantee the application and implementation of national and EU legislation on artificial intelligence:

a) **AgID** (Agency for Digital Italy) is responsible for promoting innovation and the development of artificial intelligence, for defining procedures and exercising functions regarding notification, evaluation, accreditation, and monitoring of subjects responsible for verifying conformity of AI systems, in accordance with national and EU legislation;

b) **ACN** (National Cybersecurity Agency), also for the purpose of ensuring cybersecurity, is responsible for the supervision, including inspection and sanctioning activities, of artificial intelligence systems, in accordance with national and EU legislation;

c) AgID and ACN, each within their respective competence, ensure the establishment and joint management of experimentation spaces for creating AI systems in compliance with national and EU legislation.

2. AgID is designated as the notifying authority pursuant to Article 70 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and ACN is designated as the market supervisory authority and single point of contact with EU institutions.

## Chapter IV — Provisions for the Protection of Users and Regarding Copyright

### Article 25 — Protection of the Copyright of Works Generated with the Aid of Artificial Intelligence

The following amendments are made to Law 22 April 1941, No. 633 (Italian Copyright Law):

a) In Article 1, the term "works of the mind" is amended to "human works of the mind" and reference is added to works "created with the aid of artificial intelligence tools, provided they constitute the result of the intellectual work of the author";

b) A new Article 70-septies is inserted: Reproductions and extractions from works or other materials contained online or in databases to which there is legitimate access, for the purpose of extracting text and data through AI systems including generative ones, are permitted in compliance with existing text and data mining provisions.

## Chapter V — Criminal Provisions

### Article 26 — Amendments to the Criminal Code and Further Criminal Provisions

1. A new aggravating circumstance is added to Article 61 of the criminal code: having committed the offense through the use of AI systems, when the latter constituted an insidious means, or when their use hindered public or private defense, or aggravated the consequences of the crime.

2. The penalty for electoral fraud (Article 294) is increased to imprisonment from two to six years if the deception is carried out through the use of artificial intelligence systems.

3. A new offense is created — Article 612-quater: Unlawful dissemination of content generated or altered with artificial intelligence systems. Whoever causes unjust damage to a person, by transferring, publishing, or otherwise disseminating, without their consent, images, videos, or voices falsified or altered through AI systems and capable of inducing deception as to their genuineness, is punished with imprisonment from one to five years.

4. The penalty for market manipulation (Article 2637 of the civil code) is increased to imprisonment from two to seven years if committed through the use of AI systems.

5. A new copyright offense is added: reproducing or extracting text or data from works available online or in databases in violation of text and data mining provisions, including through AI systems, constitutes a copyright infringement.
