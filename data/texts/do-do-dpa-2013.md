# Ley No. 172-13 sobre Protección Integral de los Datos Personales Contenidos en los Bancos de Datos Públicos y Privados — Dominican Republic
**Ley No. 172-13**
*Dominican Republic — December 15, 2013 (effective January 15, 2014)*

---

## Overview

The Dominican Republic's Law No. 172-13 on Comprehensive Protection of Personal Data Contained in Public and Private Databases is the country's primary personal data protection law, enacted on December 15, 2013, and effective from January 15, 2014. The law designates the Instituto Dominicano de las Telecomunicaciones (INDOTEL) — the national telecommunications regulator — as the supervisory authority for personal data protection in addition to its existing telecom mandate. The law covers all personal data held in public and private databases, with initial emphasis on credit bureau and financial data. It provides both administrative remedies through INDOTEL and a judicial habeas data remedy for individuals. The law applies to both public and private sector data processing in the Dominican Republic.

---

## Key Provisions

### INDOTEL as Supervisory Authority
- Instituto Dominicano de las Telecomunicaciones designated as data protection supervisory authority
- Maintains oversight of personal data databases in both public and private sectors
- Receives complaints from data subjects and investigates violations
- Issues compliance orders and enforcement notices
- May impose administrative sanctions and refer criminal violations to prosecutors
- Issues regulatory guidance on data protection compliance

### Data Subject Rights
- Right of Access: individuals may request access to their personal data held in any database
- Right of Rectification: correction of inaccurate, incomplete, or outdated data
- Right of Cancellation: deletion of data processed unlawfully or no longer necessary
- Right of Opposition: to processing for specific purposes including direct marketing
- Habeas Data judicial action: constitutional remedy allowing individuals to enforce data rights through courts

### Lawful Processing Requirements
- Processing of personal data requires legal authorization or consent of the data subject
- Data controllers must specify and disclose purposes for data collection
- Data limited to what is necessary for the declared purposes
- Data must be accurate and kept current

### Sensitive Personal Data
- Special categories requiring heightened protection including explicit consent or specific statutory authority
- Categories include: health data, political opinions, religious beliefs, criminal record, and other intimate personal data

### Mandatory Database Registration
- Data controllers operating personal databases must register with INDOTEL
- Registration discloses the nature of data held, purposes, data subjects, and security measures
- Failure to register is an offense

### Credit and Financial Data Focus
- The law has particular provisions for credit bureau data and financial databases
- Individuals have specific rights to access, correct, and challenge credit information held about them
- Temporal limits on retention of negative credit information

### Habeas Data Judicial Remedy
- Constitutional habeas data action available to individuals before Dominican courts
- Courts may order access, correction, suppression, or confidentiality of personal data
- Judicial remedy operates independently of INDOTEL administrative enforcement
- Courts may grant urgent interim measures

### Cross-Border Data Transfers
- Transfers outside the Dominican Republic subject to restrictions ensuring equivalent protection

### Security Obligations
- Controllers must implement technical and organizational measures to protect personal data
- Obligations of confidentiality for staff and processors

## Enforcement & Penalties

- INDOTEL may issue compliance orders, formal warnings, and corrective measures
- Administrative fines for violations of the law
- Criminal penalties: up to 2 years imprisonment for the most serious offenses including unlawful processing and falsification of data
- Civil liability: data subjects may seek compensation through Dominican courts
- Habeas data judicial remedy provides private enforcement pathway
