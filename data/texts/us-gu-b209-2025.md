I MINAʹTRENTAI OCHO NA LIHESLATURAN GUÅHAN
2025 (FIRST) Regular Session
Bill No. 209-38 (COR)
Introduced by: Tina Rose Muña Barnes
AN ACT TO ADD A NEW ARTICLE 6 TO CHAPTER 8 OF
TITLE 3, RELATIVE TO PROHIBITING THE CREATION
AND DISTRIBUTION OF MATERIALLY DECEPTIVE
MEDIA PRODUCED BY GENERATIVE ARTIFICIAL
INTELLIGENCE IN CONNECTION WITH ELECTIONS.
1 BE IT ENACTED BY THE PEOPLE OF GUAM:
2 Section 1. Legislative Findings and Intent. I Liheslaturan Guahan finds that
3 advancements in artificial intelligence (AI) have enabled the creation of highly
4 realistic yet fabricated media. This technology can synthesize or alter voices and
5 videos of real people to depict them saying or doing things that never occurred.
6 While such technology may have legitimate creative and educational applications, it
7 also poses serious risks when used to deceive the public, particularly through the
8 production and dissemination of so-called “deepfakes.”
9 Deepfakes have become increasingly difficult for the public to distinguish
10 from authentic recordings, eroding trust in media and posing a direct threat to
11 democratic elections. In South Korea, ahead of the April 2025 legislative elections,
12 the National Election Commission detected hundreds of deepfake videos and social
13 media posts targeting leading candidates, spreading falsified images and statements.
14 Similarly, in the United States, intelligence agencies have issued warnings of foreign
1

1 and domestic actors deploying AI-generated disinformation campaigns to influence
2 public opinion and suppress voter confidence before elections. This phenomenon
3 has occurred in many democracies around the world, that countries and jurisdiction
4 in the United States have pushed forward legislation to prohibit and criminalize the
5 use of “deepfakes” targeted during elections.
6 It is the intent of I Liheslaturan Guåhan to safeguard the integrity of elections
7 in Guam by prohibiting the knowing creation or distribution of materially deceptive
8 media produced through generative artificial intelligence during election periods,
9 except when clearly and prominently labeled as altered or generated content. This
10 Act is not intended to restrict constitutionally protected speech, but rather to prevent
11 deceptive uses of technology that undermine public confidence in the electoral
12 process.
13 Section 2. A new Article 6 is added to Chapter 8, Title 3, Guam Code
14 Annotated, to read:

16 PROHIBITION OF DECEPTIVE MEDIA PRODUCED BY ARTIFICIAL

19 § 8601. Definitions.
20 For the purposes of this Act, the following terms have the following
21 meanings:
22 (a) “Artificial Intelligence” means any artificial system or generative
23 artificial intelligence system that performs tasks under varying and
24 unpredictable circumstances without significant human oversight, or that can
25 learn from experience and improve performance when exposed to data sets.
26 (b) “Creator” means any candidate, candidate campaign committee,
27 political party, political action committee, other political committee or entity,
2

1 any employee, representative, or agent of the foregoing, or any other person
2 who prepares, creates, or causes the preparation or creation and the
3 dissemination of any political advertising, material, or media produced by
4 generative artificial intelligence.
5 The term does not include a broadcaster, cable provider, digital
6 newspaper, online service, Internet service provider, streaming platform,
7 provider, or developer of any technology in the generation of media by
8 artificial intelligence, or any employee, representative, or agent thereof, solely
9 for the distribution of a creator, sponsor, or purchaser’s political advertising,
10 material, or media.
11 (c) “Depicted Individual” means an individual who is falsely
12 represented in materially deceptive media.
13 (d) “Election” means a federal, state, legislative, judicial, local, general,
14 primary, runoff, or special election.
15 (e) “Materially Deceptive Media” means any image, audio, or video
16 that meets all of the following requirements:
17 (1) The media falsely depicts an individual engaging in speech
18 or conduct in which the depicted individual did not in fact engage;
19 (2) A reasonable viewer or listener would incorrectly believe that
20 the depicted individual engaged in the speech or conduct depicted; and
21 (3) The media was produced by artificial intelligence.
22 (f) “Sponsor” means a person at whose request or on whose behalf any
23 political advertisement, material, or media is created, prepared, placed,
24 published, or disseminated.
25 § 8602. Prohibition on Distribution of Materially Deceptive Media.
26 (a) Except as provided in subsection (b), a person shall not distribute,
27 or enter into an agreement with another person to distribute, materially
3

1 deceptive media if all of the following apply:
2 (1) The person knows the media falsely represents a depicted
3 individual.
4 (2) The person intends the distribution to harm the reputation or
5 electoral prospects of a candidate in the coming election, and the
6 distribution is reasonably likely to cause that result.
7 (3) The person intends the distribution to change the voting
8 patterns of voters in the coming election by deceiving voters into
9 incorrectly believing that the depicted individual in fact engaged in the
10 speech or conduct depicted, and the distribution is reasonably likely to
11 cause that result.
12 (b) The prohibition in subsection (a) does not apply if all of the
13 following conditions are met:
14 (1) The creator, sponsor, or purchaser includes a disclaimer in
15 any presentation of the media informing the viewer both that the media
16 has been manipulated by technical means and depicts speech or conduct
17 that did not occur. The following disclaimer is sufficient, but not
18 necessary, to satisfy the requirements of this subdivision:
19 "This media has been manipulated by technical means and
20 depicts speech or conduct that did not occur."
21 (2) If the media is a video, the disclaimer meets all of the
22 following requirements:
23 (A) Appears throughout the entirety of the video.
24 (B) Is clearly visible to and readable by the average
25 viewer.
26 (C) Is in letters in a size that is easily readable by the
27 average viewer.
4

1 (D) Is in the same language as the language used in the
2 video media.
3 (3) If the media consists only of audio and contains no image or
4 video, the disclaimer is read at the beginning and end of the media in a
5 clearly spoken manner, in a pitch that can be easily heard by the average
6 listener, and in the same language as the audio media.
7 (4) If the media is an image, the disclaimer meets is clearly
8 visible to and readable by the average viewer and in the same language
9 as the language used in the image media.
10 (5) If the media was generated by editing an existing image,
11 audio, or video, the media includes a citation directing the viewer or
12 listener to the original source from which the unedited version of the
13 existing image, audio, or video was obtained.
14 (c) A violation of this section shall constitute a misdemeanor, except
15 that a second or subsequent conviction within five (5) years shall constitute a
16 felony.
17 § 8603. Exemptions.
18 (a) Distribution of material that is prohibited by this section shall not
19 be a violation of this section if the distributor does not have actual knowledge
20 that the material is prohibited, the distributor does not intend to injure or harm
21 the reputation or prospects of the depicted individual, influence an election,
22 the results of an election, or the voting patterns in an election, or deter any
23 individual from voting in an election, and the distributor is:
24 (1) an Internet website, interactive computer service, or radio or
25 television broadcasting station, including, but not limited to, a cable or
26 satellite television operator;
27 (2) a regularly published newspaper, magazine, or other
5

1 periodical, including, but not limited to, an Internet or electronic
2 publication, programmer, or producer; or
3 (3) a website or streaming service, including, but not limited to,
4 an information service as defined in 47 U.S.C. § 153.
5 (b) This act shall not be construed to alter any rights, obligations, or
6 immunities created by 47 U.S.C. Chapter 5 or under the regulations of the
7 Federal Communications Commission pertaining to the broadcast or
8 distribution of political programming or campaign advertising, or under any
9 other federal law.
10 (c) This act shall not be construed to alter any rights, obligations, or
11 immunities created by 47 U.S.C. § 230.
12 (d) This act shall not apply to content that constitutes satire or parody
13 which is substantially dependent on the ability of an individual to impersonate
14 a candidate physically or verbally and not upon technology or artificial
15 intelligence.
16 (e) This act shall not apply to a radio or television broadcasting station,
17 including a cable or satellite television operator, programmer, or producer,
18 streaming provider, Internet website, or a regularly published newspaper,
19 magazine, or other periodical of general circulation, including an Internet or
20 electronic publication, that routinely carries news and commentary of general
21 interest that distributes any materially deceptive media prohibited by this act
22 as part of a bona fide newscast, news interview, news documentary, or on-the-
23 spot coverage of a bona fide news event if the broadcast or publication clearly
24 acknowledges, through content or disclosure, in a manner that can easily be
25 heard and understood by the average listener or viewer, that there are
26 questions about the authenticity of the media.
27 (f) A distributor shall not intentionally remove a disclaimer included

2 § 8604. Enforcement and Remedies.
3 (a) All of the following may seek permanent injunctive relief against a
4 person that violates this section:
5 (1) The Attorney General.
6 (2) A depicted individual.
7 (3) A candidate for office who has been injured or is likely to be
8 injured by the distribution of materially deceptive media.
9 (4) Any entity that represents the interests of voters likely to be
10 deceived by the distribution of materially deceptive media.
11 (b) If a court determines that a complaint filed under this Section is
12 frivolous, the court shall suspend the defendant’s obligation to respond and
13 order the plaintiff to show cause why the complaint should not be dismissed.
14 If the plaintiff fails to respond, or if the plaintiff’s response confirms that the
15 complaint is frivolous, the court shall dismiss the complaint, may award costs
16 and attorney’s fees to the defendant, and may issue appropriate sanctions
17 against the plaintiff or the plaintiff’s attorney. If the plaintiff demonstrates that
18 the complaint is not frivolous, the court shall direct the defendant to answer
19 the complaint.
20 (c) A plaintiff seeking permanent injunctive relief under subsection (a)
21 must prove by clear and convincing evidence that the defendant against whom
22 the injunction is sought knew the media at issue falsely represented the
23 depicted individual.
24 (d) If a plaintiff, other than the Attorney General, is awarded permanent
25 injunctive relief under this section, the court may award costs and attorney
26 fees to the plaintiff.
27 (e) In addition to permanent injunctive relief authorized under this
7

1 Section, a plaintiff may seek a temporary restraining order or preliminary
2 injunction upon a showing of a likelihood of success on the merits and
3 potential electoral harm. The court shall give such actions expedited
4 consideration due to the time-sensitive nature of election-related harm.
5 § 8605. Section 4. Disclaimer Removal.
6 It shall be unlawful for any person to knowingly and intentionally
7 remove, obscure, or alter a disclaimer included with any image, audio, or
8 video media by the creator, sponsor, or purchaser of such media, when the
9 disclaimer was required pursuant to this Act.”
