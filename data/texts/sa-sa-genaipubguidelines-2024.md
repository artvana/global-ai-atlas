# Generative AI Guidelines for the Public

**Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority (SDAIA)**

*January 2024*

## Introduction

The Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority (SDAIA) published the Generative AI Guidelines for the Public to guide individuals, developers, deployers, and organisations across Saudi Arabia on the responsible use of generative AI systems. The Guidelines provide non-binding, risk-based guidance on mitigating key harms while aligning with existing Saudi laws including the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL).

**Scope:** All entities in Saudi Arabia involved in designing, developing, deploying, or using generative AI systems, including:
- Developers (natural or legal entities creating GenAI products and services, including AI developers, data scientists, and researchers);
- Deployers (organisations integrating generative AI into products and services); and
- Users (any individual or organisation, including companies, NGOs, and individuals, utilising generative AI systems).

---

## Key Definitions

**Generative Artificial Intelligence:** A machine learning model capable of producing new examples from its training dataset, including text, images, sound, video, code, and other media formats that automate cognitive tasks.

**Developers:** Natural or legal entities involved in creating generative AI products and services.

**Users:** Any individual or organisation utilising generative AI systems and services.

## Seven Core Ethical Principles

### Principle 1: Fairness

Organisations must mitigate bias, discrimination, and stigmatisation in generative AI design and deployment.

**Requirements include:**
- Testing models against embedded bias using clean, representative training datasets;
- Understanding training data origins, content, and selection criteria;
- Building organisational knowledge around bias recognition, diversity, and anti-discrimination practices; and
- Monitoring deployed systems for the emergence of biased outputs.

### Principle 2: Reliability and Safety

Generative AI systems must operate according to predetermined specifications without posing societal harm.

**Key obligations:**
- Design systems to be resilient to uncertainty and volatility;
- Develop assessment standards and reliability protocols;
- Implement human oversight triggers for emergency or high-stakes situations;
- Ensure AI-generated content is identifiable to facilitate fact-checking;
- Maintain continuous dataset quality; and
- Review AI-generated outputs for factual accuracy before publication or use in consequential decisions.

### Principle 3: Transparency and Explainability

Generative AI systems should clearly communicate their automated nature and decision-making processes.

**Implementation:**
- Publicly disclose when generative AI capabilities are deployed in products or services;
- Label all AI-generated content explicitly;
- Provide alternative (non-AI) communication channels for users who prefer them; and
- Deploy watermarking tools for AI-generated content identification.

### Principle 4: Accountability and Responsibility

All stakeholders bear ethical and legal responsibility for generative AI system outcomes.

**Required actions:**
- Ensure proper data acquisition, classification, and processing;
- Conduct consistent data quality validation;
- Comply with the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) and intellectual property regulations; and
- Regularly consult legal professionals on deployment risks and regulatory compliance.

### Principle 5: Privacy and Security

Organisations must protect data privacy through adequate security measures.

**Standards:**
- Deploy appropriate privacy and security measures for sensitive data used in AI training;
- Implement required data protection mechanisms;
- Assess generative AI-specific risks regularly;
- Conduct privacy impact and risk assessments; and
- Apply privacy-by-design frameworks to AI system architecture.

### Principle 6: Humanity

Generative AI development must prioritise positive human impact and be designed with human dignity, user choice, and individual agency at its centre.

**Requirements:**
- Apply ethical methodology when developing generative AI models;
- Design systems that support user autonomy rather than manipulate or coerce; and
- Ensure generative AI systems do not undermine fundamental human rights.

### Principle 7: Social and Environmental Benefits

Generative AI systems should benefit communities and avoid accelerating harm.

**Requirements:**
- Design and deploy AI systems that contribute to social and environmental progress;
- Assess the aggregate societal impact of AI deployments; and
- Avoid applications whose harms outweigh their benefits to individuals and communities.

## Seven Critical Risk Areas and Mitigation Strategies

### Risk 1: Deepfakes and Misrepresentation

**Threats:** Scams, financial fraud, blackmail, identity theft, political and social manipulation through AI-generated synthetic media.

**Mitigation measures:**
- **Watermarking:** All AI-generated content must carry identification markers flagging it as AI-generated;
- **Know-Your-Customer (KYC) protocols:** Organisations providing computing resources for AI implement verification procedures to restrict access by malicious actors;
- **Output verification:** Analyse generated audio-visual content for inappropriate elements and verify against public figures' data; and
- **Digital literacy:** Public and private organisations educate citizens on deepfake risks and data protection measures.

### Risk 2: Safety Threats

**Threats:** Generative AI producing harmful content that compromises public safety or security.

**Mitigation measures:**
- **Content moderation:** Implement filtration mechanisms detecting and preventing malicious outputs, updated continuously based on feedback;
- **Dataset filtration:** Filter training data to exclude harmful information; and
- **Closed access models:** Restrict high-risk AI capabilities to verified developers who confirm model safety and state legitimate use objectives.

### Risk 3: Misinformation and Hallucination

**Threats:** AI-generated false information appearing factual ("hallucination"), contributing to public misinformation.

**Mitigation measures:**
- **Citation and verification:** Build mechanisms estimating information accuracy and require source citation for user cross-referencing;
- **Content labelling:** Implement watermarks indicating AI generation based on content type;
- **User vigilance:** Users must proactively verify accuracy through fact-checking and reliable source comparison; and
- **Awareness building:** Educate users on output accuracy variance and standard verification processes.

### Risk 4: Data Breaches

**Threats:** Employees inadvertently exposing sensitive organisational information through generative AI tools.

**Mitigation measures:**
- **Usage protocols:** Establish strict policies prohibiting entry of classified or sensitive information into AI systems; provide employee guidelines on ethical use;
- **Employee training:** Conduct workshops covering data governance, cybersecurity, intellectual property rights, and data protection compliance; and
- **Access controls:** Limit user access to critical information based on job necessity.

### Risk 5: Certification Fraud

**Threats:** Misusing generative AI to complete assessments or qualifications without genuine learning, undermining credential reliability.

**Mitigation measures:**
- **Assessment review:** Regularly adjust assessments to account for evolving AI capabilities;
- **Education and training:** Conduct internal training on responsible AI use and suspicious behaviour identification;
- **Prompt proficiency:** Train staff on effective prompt drafting to minimise problematic AI outputs; and
- **Clear guidelines:** Establish organisational AI policies aligned with ethical principles.

### Risk 6: Intellectual Property Infringement

**Threats:** Unauthorised use of copyrighted or otherwise protected material in AI training datasets.

**Mitigation measures:**
- **IP licensing:** Developers must obtain legal permission for all copyrighted content used in training; provide users with confirmation that training data is appropriately licensed;
- **Creator permissions:** Secure original IP holder approval before including content in training datasets; and
- **Compensation mechanisms:** Establish fair compensation arrangements for content used in AI training.

### Risk 7: Output Variability

**Threats:** Generative AI systems produce non-deterministic outputs that change based on model updates, making reliability validation more complex than traditional software.

**Mitigation measures:**
- **Code annotation:** Include detailed annotations indicating where AI tools were used (e.g., ChatGPT, Copilot);
- **Verification and validation:** Regularly verify AI-generated code and content, especially where accuracy is critical;
- **Continuous learning:** Developers stay updated on generative AI model developments; and
- **Build expertise:** Develop deep understanding of generative AI capabilities for assessing output validity.

## Implementation Framework

Organisations should:
1. Integrate the seven principles across the full generative AI lifecycle (design, development, deployment, and monitoring);
2. Establish risk assessment and mitigation protocols aligned with the seven risk areas;
3. Implement both technical and procedural controls;
4. Conduct regular compliance reviews; and
5. Train personnel on ethical deployment.

## Relationship to Other Saudi AI Instruments

These Guidelines complement the broader Saudi AI governance framework:
- **SDAIA AI Ethics Principles** (September 2023) — applicable to all AI systems;
- **Generative AI Guidelines for Government Entities** (SDAIA, January 2024) — sector-specific guidance for government;
- **Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL)** — binding obligations on personal data processing; and
- **AI Adoption Framework** (SDAIA) — guidance on responsible AI adoption pathways.
