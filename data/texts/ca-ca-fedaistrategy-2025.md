# AI Strategy for the Federal Public Service 2025-2027

Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat
Published: March 3, 2025 (launched March 4, 2025)
Catalogue Number: BT48-55/2025E-PDF

## Vision

To serve Canadians better through responsible AI adoption in the federal public service.

## Overview

Canada launched its first AI Strategy for the Federal Public Service on March 4, 2025, led by the Treasury Board Secretariat (TBS). The strategy establishes a whole-of-government approach to artificial intelligence adoption that is responsible, ethical, and aligned with Canadian values.

The Government of Canada will use AI to enhance its scientific and research capabilities, improve the productivity of its workforce, and deliver enhanced digital services to Canadians. The safe, ethical, and responsible use of AI is a priority for the federal government.

## Strategic Priority Areas

The Strategy advances four key priority areas:

### Priority 1: Establish an AI Centre of Expertise

The Government of Canada will establish an AI Centre of Expertise to support and help coordinate government-wide AI efforts. This centre will:
- Provide technical guidance and support to federal departments and agencies
- Facilitate knowledge sharing and best practices across government
- Support capacity building and training for public servants
- Help coordinate AI governance and oversight activities

### Priority 2: Ensure AI Systems Are Secure and Used Responsibly

The strategy commits to ensuring that AI systems deployed in the federal public service are:
- Secure and protected from adversarial threats
- Used in accordance with existing laws and policies, including the Directive on Automated Decision-Making
- Transparent and explainable where appropriate
- Subject to appropriate human oversight
- Respectful of privacy and individual rights

Key measures include:
- Adherence to the Directive on Automated Decision-Making
- Application of the Algorithmic Impact Assessment tool
- Compliance with Treasury Board policies on privacy and data management
- Integration with the Government of Canada's Digital Ambition

### Priority 3: Provide Training and Talent Development Pathways

The government will invest in the AI skills and capabilities of the federal public service through:
- AI literacy training for all public servants
- Specialized technical training for AI practitioners
- Leadership development programs on AI governance and ethics
- Partnerships with academic institutions and the private sector
- Pathways for attracting and retaining AI talent in government

### Priority 4: Build Trust Through Openness and Transparency

The government commits to being open about how and where AI is being used in the federal public service:
- Publication of an AI Register documenting federal AI use cases (launched November 2025)
- Proactive disclosure of automated decision-making systems
- Public reporting on AI adoption and outcomes
- Engagement with Canadians on AI use in government
- International cooperation on AI governance frameworks

## Development Process

From May to October 2024, the Government of Canada sought input from various groups through engagement and consultation. In January 2025, the government published a "What We Heard" report summarizing the views shared by Canadians, public servants, academia, civil society, and industry.

## Implementation

In 2025-26, the Treasury Board Secretariat will:
- Engage departments on the actions laid out in the AI strategy
- Confirm action leads across government
- Develop a detailed implementation plan including resources, responsibilities, timelines, and milestones
- Monitor progress and report publicly on implementation

### AI Register

In November 2025, the Government of Canada published its first public AI Register that provides Canadians with information about where and how AI is being used within the federal government. This marked an important milestone in the implementation of the federal public service's AI Strategy.

## Relationship to Existing Policy Framework

The AI Strategy builds on and complements:
- The Directive on Automated Decision-Making (2019, updated 2023)
- The Government of Canada Digital Ambition 2023-2026
- The Policy on Service and Digital
- Canada's National AI Strategy (Pan-Canadian AI Strategy, led by ISED)
- International commitments including the Hiroshima AI Process and the Global Partnership on AI (GPAI)

## Sector Coverage

The strategy applies to all federal departments and agencies subject to the Financial Administration Act. It covers AI use cases across:
- Administrative and internal operations
- Service delivery to Canadians
- Scientific research and analysis
- Law enforcement and national security (with additional safeguards)
- Economic development and policy analysis
