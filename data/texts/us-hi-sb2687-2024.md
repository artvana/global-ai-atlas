# Hawaii Senate Bill 2687 / Act 191 — Prohibition on Materially Deceptive AI-Generated Deepfakes in Elections

**SB 2687 / Act 191 | Hawaii Legislature, 2024 Regular Session**
*Hawaii — Enacted July 3, 2024*

---

## Overview

Hawaii Senate Bill 2687, enacted as Act 191 of 2024, prohibited the creation and distribution of materially deceptive AI-generated media (deepfakes) in connection with political campaigns without a prominent disclaimer identifying the content as AI-generated. The law applied within 90 days of a covered election and provided for civil and criminal enforcement.

However, the law's practical effect has been significantly curtailed: a federal district court issued a preliminary injunction against enforcement in 2024, finding that the law likely violated the First Amendment, and subsequently issued a permanent injunction against enforcement in early 2026. The law remains on the books but is unenforceable. Hawaii's experience has become a reference point in national debates about the constitutionality of state AI deepfake election laws.

## Key Provisions

### Original Prohibition (As Enacted)

- Prohibited any person from distributing materially deceptive AI-generated media depicting a candidate for public office, a political party, or an elected official within 90 days before a primary, special, or general election.
- "Materially deceptive" media meant AI-generated or AI-manipulated audio, video, or images that a reasonable person would believe to be authentic depictions of the candidate's actual conduct or statements when they were not.
- Prohibition applied to content published, shared, or broadcast within Hawaii or targeting Hawaiian voters.

### Disclaimer Requirement (As Enacted)

- Any AI-generated media used in political advertising or communications (including non-deceptive use) was required to include a prominent disclaimer stating the content was AI-generated.
- Disclaimer had to be displayed prominently enough for a reasonable viewer to notice it before engaging with the content.

### Enforcement and Penalties (As Enacted)

- Civil penalties enforceable by the Hawaii Attorney General.
- Criminal misdemeanor penalties available for knowing violations.
- AG enforcement could include injunctive relief to remove violating content.

### First Amendment Challenge and Injunction

- Opponents of the law challenged it as a violation of the First Amendment's protections for political speech.
- The U.S. District Court for the District of Hawaii issued a preliminary injunction in 2024, finding plaintiffs were likely to succeed on the merits because the law imposed content-based restrictions on protected political speech without surviving strict scrutiny.
- Key findings: the state's interest in election integrity was significant but the law was not narrowly tailored; false political speech may retain First Amendment protection (per United States v. Alvarez and related precedent); the 90-day window was overbroad.
- A permanent injunction against enforcement was issued in early 2026.

### Current Status

- Act 191 remains codified in Hawaii law but is permanently enjoined from enforcement.
- The law cannot be enforced unless the permanent injunction is reversed on appeal or the law is amended to satisfy constitutional requirements.
- Hawaii's case is cited as a cautionary example in other states' legislative debates about AI deepfake election laws.
