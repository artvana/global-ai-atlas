 SECOND REGULAR SESSION 
 
 SENATE BILL NO. 1502 
 
 103RD GENERAL ASSEMBLY 
 INTRODUCED BY SENATOR SCHROER. 
 
 
 
 AN ACT 
 
 To amend chapter 434, RSMo, by adding thereto one new section relating to contracts for the use of an individual&#39;s identifiable attributes, with penalty provisions. 
 
 
 
 
 Be it enacted by the General Assembly of the State of Missouri, as follows: 
 
 
 Section A. Chapter 434, RSMo, is amended by adding thereto one new section, to be known as section 434.120, to read as follows: 
 
 434.120. 1. This section shall be known and may be cited as the &quot;Missouri Consent and Likeness Protection Act&quot;. 
 
 
 2. As used in this section, the following terms mean: 
 
 
 (1) &quot;Adhesion contract&quot;, any contract, waiver, release, disclaimer, or terms of entry that is: 
 
 
 (a) Presented on a take-it-or-leave-it basis; 
 
 
 (b) Not subject to meaningful negotiation; or 
 
 
 (c) Required as a condition of entry, participation, service, or access; 
 
 
 (2) &quot;Disclaimer or waiver&quot;, any written, posted, digital, or verbal statement purporting to limit liability, obtain consent, or waive legal rights, including, but not limited to, terms or language provided by any posted sign, ticket, website, or mobile application, linked by a quick response code, or by statements asserting consent by entering, by participating, by remaining on the premises, or similar conditions; 
 
 
 (3) &quot;Identity attributes&quot;, an individual&#39;s name, image, photograph, voice, likeness, signature, biometric identifiers, digital replica, persona, or any substantially similar identifying characteristics. 
 
 
 3. No adhesion contract, disclaimer or waiver, or condition of entry shall be valid or enforceable if it: 
 
 
 (1) Waives or limits an individual&#39;s right to bring a civil action for the unauthorized use of the individual&#39;s identity attributes; or 
 
 
 (2) Grants a company or third party the right to use an individual&#39;s identity attributes without allowing the individual to seek judicial relief. 
 
 
 4. Any provision that purports to release liability, require arbitration, waive jury trial, limit damages, or provide consent without reasonable and just compensation shall be void as against public policy when applied to the use of identity attributes. 
 
 
 5. For purposes of this section, consent shall not be implied by: 
 
 
 (1) Entry onto premises; 
 
 
 (2) Presence in a public or quasi-public space; 
 
 
 (3) Participation in an activity; 
 
 
 (4) Purchase of a ticket; or 
 
 
 (5) Failure to read or object to posted terms. 
 
 
 6. Consent for the commercial or promotional use of identity attributes shall be valid only if the consent is: 
 
 
 (1) Affirmative, explicit, and written; 
 
 
 (2) Negotiated by all parties in good faith; 
 
 
 (3) Separate and distinct from any general terms or conditions; 
 
 
 (4) Clearly describing: 
 
 
 (a) The specific use of the identity attributes; 
 
 
 (b) That the use of the identity attributes is limited in duration, which shall not to exceed a period of twenty years; 
 
 
 (c) The parties authorized to use the identity attributes; and 
 
 
 (d) Whether reasonable and just compensation for the identity attributes is provided relevant to the negotiated duration; and 
 
 
 (5) Freely revocable at any time. 
 
 
 7. Consent for the commercial or promotional use of identity attributes obtained through adhesion contracts or disclaimers or waivers shall be conclusively presumed invalid. 
 
 
 8. Any individual whose identity attributes are used in violation of this section may bring a civil action for damages in a court of competent jurisdiction. The court may award a prevailing plaintiff: 
 
 
 (1) Actual damages; 
 
 
 (2) Statutory damages of not less than ten thousand dollars per violation, with each unauthorized use of an individual&#39;s identity attributes constituting a separate violation; 
 
 
 (3) Disgorgement of profits; 
 
 
 (4) Injunctive relief; 
 
 
 (5) Declaratory relief; and 
 
 
 (6) Reasonable attorney&#39;s fees and costs. 
 
 
 9. Whenever any individual or entity is engaged or appears to be engaged in any act or practice which is a violation of this section, the attorney general may: 
 
 
 (1) Investigate possible violations of this section; 
 
 
 (2) Bring an enforcement action to enjoin such act or practice; and 
 
 
 (3) Upon a showing of a violation, seek a civil penalty of up to twenty-five thousand dollars per violation, with each unauthorized use of an individual&#39;s identity attributes constituting a separate violation. 
 
 
 10. The rights and requirements established by this section shall not be waived by contract. 
 
 
 11. This section shall apply to any entity doing business in this state and to any individual physically present in this state at the time of the alleged violation.
