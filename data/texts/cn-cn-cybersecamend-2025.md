# Decision of the NPC Standing Committee on Amending the Cybersecurity Law of the PRC (2025)

**Adopted at the 13th Session of the Standing Committee of the 14th National People's Congress | October 28, 2025**

---

## Overview

The 2025 amendments to the Cybersecurity Law of the People's Republic of China (first enacted 2017) significantly update China's foundational cybersecurity framework to address AI systems, critical information infrastructure, data security, and new categories of network products and services. The amendments incorporate and systematize provisions from previously scattered administrative regulations (notably the Data Security Law 2021, the PIPL 2021, and the AI Governance Regulations 2023–2025) into the unified cybersecurity framework.

---

## Key Amendments to the Cybersecurity Law (as amended 2025)

### Chapter II — Network Security Support and Promotion (amended Articles 15–20)

**Article 15 (amended):** The State shall develop and implement national cybersecurity strategies that specifically address AI-enabled cybersecurity risks, including AI-generated cyberattacks, automated vulnerability exploitation, and adversarial uses of large language models. The competent cybersecurity authority (CAC) shall coordinate with MIIT and the Ministry of Public Security to update these strategies no less than every three years.

**Article 16 (amended):** Critical information infrastructure operators and network platform operators with significant social influence must establish AI security governance mechanisms, including:
(1) Designating an AI cybersecurity responsible officer;
(2) Conducting annual AI security risk assessments for AI systems used in network operations;
(3) Establishing emergency response procedures for AI system failures, adversarial attacks, and model poisoning incidents.

**Article 18 (amended):** Network technology research and development activities involving AI, including training of large-scale AI models and autonomous network security systems, must comply with applicable technology export control regulations and obtain necessary approvals before transferring core AI security technologies to overseas entities.

---

### Chapter III — Network Operation Security (amended Articles 21–38)

**Article 21 (amended):** Network operators are required to implement technical measures to:
(1) Detect and prevent the use of their network infrastructure for training or deploying AI systems that conduct prohibited activities under the Cybersecurity Law;
(2) Identify AI-generated content transmitted through their networks that constitutes cybersecurity threats or illegal information as defined in Article 12;
(3) Maintain logs of significant AI model deployments within their network infrastructure for a minimum of six months.

**Article 22 (amended) — AI System Security Requirements:** Network products and services that incorporate AI models or automated decision-making systems are subject to the following mandatory security requirements:
(1) AI systems used in critical network operations must pass a **security assessment** conducted by a CAC-certified testing institution before deployment;
(2) The assessment must evaluate: model robustness against adversarial inputs, data poisoning resistance, output predictability, and alignment with cybersecurity policies;
(3) AI systems classified as "network security AI products" must be certified under the national cybersecurity product certification scheme (GB/T standards applicable to AI security products).

**Article 23 (amended) — Prohibited AI Cybersecurity Activities:** It is prohibited to:
(1) Develop, deploy, or sell AI tools designed or primarily used to conduct cyberattacks, including automated vulnerability scanners used offensively, AI-assisted intrusion tools, and AI-generated phishing or social engineering systems;
(2) Use generative AI to impersonate network administrators, security personnel, or government cybersecurity officials to obtain unauthorized network access;
(3) Train AI models on data obtained through illegal network intrusion, unauthorized access to computer systems, or interception of network communications;
(4) Use AI systems to circumvent mandatory real-name registration requirements for network users.

**Article 27 (amended):** Critical information infrastructure operators must implement the following additional AI-specific security measures:
(1) Segregated deployment of AI systems from core operational technology networks, unless the AI system has passed enhanced security assessment;
(2) Human oversight mechanisms for AI-assisted decisions affecting critical infrastructure operations (power grids, water systems, financial clearing, telecommunications backbone);
(3) Mandatory incident reporting to CAC within 24 hours when an AI system deployed in critical infrastructure experiences a security incident, anomalous output, or suspected adversarial manipulation.

**Article 34 (amended) — AI in Network Emergency Response:** Network operators that use AI systems in cybersecurity incident detection, monitoring, or response are required to:
(1) Maintain human-in-the-loop oversight for AI-recommended defensive actions that would substantially affect network availability;
(2) Document all AI-recommended actions taken during cybersecurity incidents in the mandatory incident response records;
(3) Conduct post-incident reviews specifically evaluating the accuracy and reliability of AI systems used during the incident.

---

### Chapter IV — Network Information Security (amended Articles 40–50)

**Article 40 (amended):** Network operators that process personal information using AI systems, including AI-based profiling, recommendation, and automated decision-making systems, must comply with both the PIPL and the following cybersecurity-specific requirements:
(1) Personal information processed by AI systems must be protected against model inversion attacks, membership inference attacks, and other AI-specific privacy threats through appropriate technical safeguards;
(2) Network operators must disclose to the CAC, upon request, the categories of personal information used to train AI models deployed in their networks.

**Article 44 (amended) — AI-Generated Disinformation:** Network operators must take technical measures to:
(1) Detect and label AI-generated content that constitutes illegal information under Article 12 of this Law;
(2) Implement content moderation systems that specifically address AI-generated disinformation, deepfakes, and synthetic media distributed through their platforms;
(3) Cooperate with public security authorities in investigating AI-generated content used for fraud, social manipulation, or threatening national security.

**Article 47 (amended):** Network operators must not use AI systems to conduct discriminatory content moderation that systematically disadvantages users based on political views expressed through lawful means, subject to the overriding obligation to comply with information content management regulations.

---

### Chapter V — Monitoring, Early Warning, and Emergency Response (amended Articles 51–58)

**Article 53 (new) — AI Security Threat Intelligence:** CAC shall establish a national AI cybersecurity threat intelligence platform to:
(1) Collect and analyze reports of AI-enabled cyberattacks, adversarial AI incidents, and AI model security vulnerabilities;
(2) Issue early warnings to critical information infrastructure operators regarding newly identified AI attack vectors;
(3) Share threat intelligence with international partners through bilateral and multilateral cybersecurity cooperation frameworks.

**Article 56 (amended):** In the event of a major cybersecurity incident, the competent authorities may order the temporary suspension of AI systems operating in affected networks if those systems are assessed to be exacerbating the incident or operating anomalously. Network operators must comply with such orders within two hours of receipt.

---

### Chapter VI — Legal Liability (amended Articles 59–75)

**Article 59 (amended):** Violations of Article 22 (AI system security requirements for network products) are subject to:
(1) A warning and deadline for rectification for first violations;
(2) A fine of RMB 100,000 to 1,000,000 for failure to rectify;
(3) Suspension or revocation of the network product's license for repeated or serious violations.

**Article 63 (amended):** Violations of the prohibitions in Article 23 (prohibited AI cybersecurity activities) by individuals are subject to criminal liability under the Criminal Law; by organizations, fines of RMB 500,000 to 10,000,000 and revocation of business licenses.

**Article 74 (new) — AI Developer Liability:** AI model developers and providers whose systems are used to conduct cyberattacks may bear joint liability if:
(1) The developer knew or should have known that the AI system would be used for prohibited cybersecurity activities;
(2) The developer failed to implement adequate technical safeguards against foreseeable misuse for cyberattacks;
(3) The developer failed to cooperate with law enforcement investigations into cyberattacks conducted using their AI systems.

## Relationship to Other AI Regulations

The 2025 Cybersecurity Law amendments complement China's existing AI governance framework:
- **PIPL (2021):** Personal information protection for AI processing
- **Data Security Law (2021):** Data classification and cross-border transfer for AI training data
- **Generative AI Interim Measures (2023):** Content governance for generative AI services
- **AI Labelling Standard GB 45438-2025:** Mandatory labelling of AI-generated content

The amendments give CAC explicit jurisdiction over AI cybersecurity, resolving previous ambiguity between CAC, MIIT, and the Ministry of Public Security.
