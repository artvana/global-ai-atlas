# Law No. 31814 — Law Promoting the Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence in Peru

**Adopted by:** Congress of the Republic of Peru  
**Promulgation Date:** July 20, 2023  
**Published in:** Official Gazette El Peruano, July 22, 2023  
**Regulatory Decree:** Supreme Decree No. 115-2025-PCM (implementing regulations, 2025)  

---

## Title I: General Provisions

### Article 1: Purpose

This Law aims to promote the responsible, ethical, and human-centered development and use of artificial intelligence (AI) in Peru, establishing a framework of principles and obligations to ensure that AI systems are designed, developed, deployed, and used in a manner that is safe, transparent, fair, and respectful of the fundamental rights of persons.

The Law also establishes the basis for the State's use of AI in public administration and for the promotion of AI as a strategic technology for Peru's economic and social development.

### Article 2: Scope of Application

This Law applies to:

(a) Natural persons and legal entities, whether public or private, domiciled in Peru that develop, market, distribute, deploy, or use AI systems;

(b) Natural persons and legal entities domiciled outside Peru that develop or deploy AI systems intended for use by persons in Peru or that affect the rights or interests of persons in Peru.

This Law does not apply to AI systems used exclusively for military or national defense purposes, or to activities already comprehensively regulated by special legislation to the extent provided by such legislation.

### Article 3: Definitions

For the purposes of this Law, the following definitions apply:

(a) **Artificial intelligence (AI)**: A set of computational technologies that enables machines to perform tasks that, when performed by humans, would require intelligence, including the ability to perceive, learn, reason, solve problems, and produce natural language, images, or other types of output;

(b) **AI system**: A machine-based system that, given a set of objectives defined by humans, generates predictions, recommendations, decisions, or content that influences real or virtual environments. AI systems vary in their level of autonomy;

(c) **AI provider**: A natural or legal person that develops, designs, manufactures, or markets an AI system;

(d) **AI deployer**: A natural or legal person that uses an AI system under its authority and responsibility in a professional context, including the State;

(e) **High-risk AI system**: An AI system that presents a significant risk to the fundamental rights, safety, or well-being of persons, as determined by the implementing regulations;

(f) **Automated decision-making**: Any process by which a decision that significantly affects a natural person is made solely or predominantly on the basis of automated processing of data, without meaningful human intervention;

(g) **Algorithmic bias**: The systematic and unjustified production of results that are unfair or discriminatory due to the structure, training data, or deployment context of an AI system.

### Article 4: Principles

The development, deployment, and use of AI in Peru shall be governed by the following principles:

(a) **Human-centered AI**: AI systems shall be designed and deployed to benefit individuals and society, placing human well-being, dignity, and fundamental rights at the center of AI development;

(b) **Non-maleficence and safety**: AI systems shall be designed to avoid causing harm, and shall incorporate safety measures to prevent foreseeable harmful consequences for individuals, society, and the environment;

(c) **Fairness and non-discrimination**: AI systems shall not generate, perpetuate, or exacerbate discrimination based on race, ethnic origin, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, age, disability, religion, political opinion, socioeconomic status, or any other protected characteristic;

(d) **Transparency and explainability**: AI systems shall operate in a manner that allows users and affected persons to understand how the system works, what data is used, and how decisions are produced, to the extent technically feasible;

(e) **Accountability**: Those who develop and deploy AI systems shall be responsible for ensuring compliance with this Law and shall be accountable for the impacts of their systems;

(f) **Privacy and data protection**: AI systems shall be designed and deployed in accordance with applicable personal data protection laws and shall incorporate privacy-by-design principles;

(g) **Robustness and reliability**: AI systems shall function reliably and accurately and shall be resilient to foreseeable errors, manipulation, and unexpected inputs;

(h) **Social and environmental sustainability**: AI development shall take into account impacts on social welfare, employment, and the environment, and shall contribute to Peru's sustainable development objectives;

(i) **Inclusion and access**: AI development shall promote the participation of marginalized populations and shall seek to reduce rather than deepen socioeconomic inequalities.

## Title II: Obligations of AI Providers and Deployers

### Article 5: General Obligations of AI Providers

AI providers operating in Peru shall:

(a) Ensure that AI systems they develop and market comply with the technical requirements and quality standards established in this Law and its implementing regulations;

(b) Conduct risk assessments of AI systems before they are placed on the market or put into service, proportionate to the level of risk;

(c) Implement technical and organizational measures to ensure the accuracy, reliability, and safety of AI systems;

(d) Maintain technical documentation of AI systems, including their purpose, capabilities, training data, validation and testing results, performance metrics, and known limitations;

(e) Provide clear and accessible information to users and deployers about the purpose, capabilities, and limitations of their AI systems;

(f) Establish mechanisms for reporting and addressing defects, incidents, and unintended consequences of AI systems;

(g) Cooperate with supervisory authorities in the exercise of their oversight functions.

### Article 6: General Obligations of AI Deployers

AI deployers operating in Peru shall:

(a) Use AI systems only for the purposes for which they were designed and in the conditions and contexts specified by providers;

(b) Ensure that persons affected by AI-assisted decisions are informed of the use of AI and have access to meaningful explanations of such decisions;

(c) Implement appropriate human oversight of AI-assisted decision-making processes, particularly in high-stakes contexts;

(d) Conduct ongoing monitoring of AI system performance and promptly address risks or harmful outcomes identified in deployment;

(e) Maintain records of the AI systems they deploy, including their purpose, scope of use, and human oversight procedures;

(f) Ensure that AI systems are used in compliance with applicable data protection and personal information protection laws.

### Article 7: Obligations Specific to High-Risk AI Systems

Providers and deployers of high-risk AI systems, as identified in the implementing regulations, shall comply with the following additional requirements:

(a) **Pre-deployment conformity assessment**: Providers of high-risk AI systems shall conduct a conformity assessment to verify compliance with applicable requirements before placing the system on the market or into service;

(b) **Technical documentation**: Providers shall maintain comprehensive technical documentation throughout the lifecycle of the AI system and shall make it available to supervisory authorities upon request;

(c) **Logging and auditability**: High-risk AI systems shall incorporate automatic logging capabilities to enable the reconstruction and auditing of operations;

(d) **Human oversight**: Deployers of high-risk AI systems shall implement measures to ensure effective human oversight, including the ability for human operators to monitor, understand, and intervene in the AI system's operations;

(e) **Accuracy and robustness**: High-risk AI systems shall meet specified standards for accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity appropriate to their risk level and intended purpose;

(f) **Transparency to affected persons**: Deployers shall inform individuals subject to decisions of high-risk AI systems of the fact that AI is being used and shall provide meaningful explanations of decisions upon request.

### Article 8: Prohibition on Certain AI Practices

The following uses of AI are prohibited in Peru:

(a) AI systems that deploy subliminal or deceptive techniques to manipulate persons' behavior in ways that are contrary to their interests and that they would not have consented to if aware;

(b) AI systems that exploit the vulnerabilities of specific groups — including children, the elderly, or persons with cognitive or psychological vulnerabilities — to manipulate their behavior;

(c) AI systems used for social scoring of the general population by public authorities on the basis of social behavior or personal characteristics in a manner that results in unjustified adverse treatment of persons;

(d) AI systems that conduct real-time remote biometric identification of persons in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement purposes, except in the limited circumstances specified in Article 9;

(e) AI systems used to infer sensitive personal characteristics such as political opinions, sexual orientation, or religious beliefs from biometric data without the explicit consent of the individual.

### Article 9: Exceptions for State Security

Notwithstanding Article 8(d), the use of real-time biometric identification AI systems in public spaces may be authorized by prior judicial authorization, in the following specific cases:

(a) The targeted search for specific victims of crimes, including missing persons;

(b) The prevention of specific and imminent threats to the life or physical security of persons;

(c) The prosecution of serious crimes as defined in the Penal Code.

Such authorizations shall be strictly limited in scope and duration and shall be subject to judicial oversight.

## Title III: AI in Public Administration

### Article 10: Use of AI by the State

State entities at the national, regional, and local levels may use AI systems in the exercise of their functions and in the provision of public services, subject to the following requirements:

(a) AI use in public administration shall comply with the principles established in Article 4, with particular emphasis on non-discrimination, transparency, and human oversight;

(b) State entities shall make information publicly available about the AI systems they use, including their purpose, the decisions they inform, and the scope of their application;

(c) Decisions that significantly affect the rights or interests of individuals shall not be made solely by automated AI processes; adequate human review mechanisms shall be implemented;

(d) State entities shall ensure that individuals subject to AI-assisted administrative decisions have the right to request human review of such decisions;

(e) State entities shall conduct data protection impact assessments before deploying AI systems that process personal data.

### Article 11: Transparency in Government AI

State entities that use AI systems shall publish, in accessible language on their official websites and through other accessible channels:

(a) A catalogue of the AI systems used, including their purpose and the decisions they inform;

(b) An explanation of how the AI systems work, in general terms accessible to the public;

(c) Information on the data used to train or operate the AI systems;

(d) Information on the measures implemented to ensure human oversight and to address errors or discriminatory outputs;

(e) Contact information for individuals who wish to request review of AI-assisted decisions or to obtain explanations.

### Article 12: AI in Judicial and Administrative Proceedings

AI systems may be used in judicial and administrative proceedings to support analysis, case management, and documentation purposes. However:

(a) Judicial decisions and administrative sanctions shall not be based solely on AI outputs;

(b) Parties to proceedings shall be informed of any AI system used in the preparation of decisions affecting them;

(c) AI-generated analysis used in proceedings shall be clearly identified as such.

## Title IV: Rights of Persons

### Article 13: Right to Information

Persons interacting with AI systems or subject to AI-assisted decisions shall have the right to be informed:

(a) That they are interacting with or subject to decisions informed by an AI system;

(b) Of the purpose of the AI system;

(c) Of the principal logic of automated processing, if a decision is based on such processing.

This right applies when persons interact with AI systems designed to interact with humans in a manner that could be mistaken for interaction with a human being.

### Article 14: Right to Explanation and Human Review

Persons subject to decisions made solely or predominantly by automated AI processing that significantly affects their rights or interests shall have the right to:

(a) Receive a clear and accessible explanation of the principal factors and logic underlying the automated decision;

(b) Request that the decision be reviewed by a qualified human who has the authority to modify or reverse it;

(c) Express their views and contest the decision through an appropriate complaints mechanism.

### Article 15: Right to Non-Discrimination

Persons shall have the right not to be subject to discrimination resulting from the design, training, or deployment of AI systems. Where an AI system produces discriminatory outcomes, affected persons shall have the right to seek redress through the mechanisms established under this Law and applicable anti-discrimination legislation.

### Article 16: Right to Data Protection

Persons retain all rights under Peru's personal data protection legislation — Law No. 29733 and its implementing regulations — with respect to the processing of their personal data by AI systems.

## Title V: Governance and Institutional Framework

### Article 17: Competent Authority

The Presidency of the Council of Ministers (PCM), through the National Secretariat of Digital Government or the body designated for this purpose, shall be the competent authority responsible for:

(a) Coordinating the national policy on responsible and safe AI;

(b) Developing regulations, guidelines, and standards for AI governance in coordination with relevant sectoral regulators;

(c) Maintaining a registry of high-risk AI systems;

(d) Supervising and enforcing compliance with this Law in areas not covered by sectoral regulation;

(e) Conducting public outreach and promoting AI literacy;

(f) Representing Peru in international forums on AI governance.

### Article 18: Sectoral Regulators

Sectoral regulators — including the Superintendency of Banking and Insurance (SBS), the National Telecommunications Regulator (OSIPTEL), the health authorities, and others — shall be responsible for supervising compliance with this Law within their respective sectors, in coordination with the competent authority designated under Article 17.

### Article 19: Multi-Stakeholder Advisory Council

An advisory council on AI governance is established, comprising representatives of:

(a) Relevant government ministries and agencies;

(b) Private sector AI developers and deployers;

(c) Civil society organizations, including consumer protection and human rights groups;

(d) Academia and research institutions;

(e) Technical experts.

The advisory council shall provide recommendations on AI governance policy, standards, and regulatory implementation.

### Article 20: National AI Strategy

The Executive Branch, through the competent authority, shall formulate and maintain a National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence, to be updated at least every four years, which shall:

(a) Identify priority sectors and use cases for AI development and adoption;

(b) Establish goals for AI talent development and research capacity;

(c) Define a roadmap for the development of AI infrastructure;

(d) Set out Peru's approach to international AI cooperation;

(e) Identify mechanisms to ensure that AI benefits reach underserved populations.

## Title VI: Sanctions and Liability

### Article 21: Administrative Sanctions

Violations of this Law by AI providers and deployers shall be subject to administrative sanctions proportionate to the severity of the violation, including:

(a) Written warnings;

(b) Fines proportionate to the economic capacity of the infringer and the gravity of the violation, as determined by the implementing regulations;

(c) Temporary suspension of AI systems pending compliance;

(d) Permanent prohibition on the use or marketing of AI systems found to be in violation of this Law.

The implementing regulations shall establish the criteria and procedures for determining and imposing sanctions.

### Article 22: Liability for Harm

AI providers and deployers shall bear civil liability for material and non-material harm caused to natural or legal persons as a result of defects or violations in AI systems for which they are responsible, in accordance with the applicable civil liability rules. The implementing regulations may establish specific liability regimes for high-risk AI systems.

### Article 23: Criminal Liability

Where acts in connection with AI systems constitute offenses under the Penal Code or other criminal legislation, criminal liability shall be pursued in accordance with law.

---

## Title VII: Promotion of AI Development

### Article 24: AI as a National Priority

The State recognizes the strategic importance of AI for Peru's economic and social development, and shall implement measures to:

(a) Promote investment in AI research and development;

(b) Support the development of a competitive AI industry in Peru;

(c) Foster AI talent development at all educational levels;

(d) Reduce the digital divide to ensure broad access to the benefits of AI;

(e) Promote AI applications that advance Peru's sustainable development goals.

### Article 25: Regulatory Sandboxes

The competent authority and sectoral regulators may establish regulatory sandbox programs to allow providers to test innovative AI applications under a controlled regulatory environment, subject to appropriate safeguards and oversight.

---

## Title VIII: Final Provisions

### Article 26: Implementing Regulations

The Executive Branch shall, within one hundred and eighty (180) calendar days from the promulgation of this Law, issue the implementing regulations by Supreme Decree endorsed by the Presidency of the Council of Ministers. The implementing regulations shall specify, among other matters:

(a) The criteria and procedures for classifying AI systems as high-risk;

(b) The technical requirements for high-risk AI systems;

(c) The conformity assessment procedures;

(d) The administrative sanctions and procedures;

(e) The registry of high-risk AI systems.

### Article 27: Entry into Force

This Law enters into force the day following its publication in the Official Gazette, El Peruano. The specific obligations applicable to high-risk AI systems shall enter into force upon publication of the implementing regulations.
