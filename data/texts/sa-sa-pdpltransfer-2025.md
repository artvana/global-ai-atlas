Regulation on Transfer of Personal Data Outside the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia (SDAIA, 2025)
SAUDI ARABIA | 2025

Binding regulation under the Saudi Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) framework governing cross-border transfers of personal data outside the Kingdom. Requires adequacy assessments of recipient jurisdictions, standard contractual clauses, or binding corporate rules as lawful transfer mechanisms. Directly relevant to AI systems processing Saudi personal data internationally.

Source: https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/RegulationsandRules/Pages/default.aspx

The Saudi Data and AI Authority (SDAIA) issued the Personal Data Transfer Regulations in 2025 as implementing regulations under the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL, Royal Decree No. M/19 of 1443H). These regulations establish the legal framework for the transfer of personal data from Saudi Arabia to foreign countries and international organizations.

Key provisions (based on publicly reported information):

1. Transfer Conditions: Personal data may only be transferred outside Saudi Arabia where:
   - The recipient country or organization ensures an adequate level of personal data protection
   - Appropriate contractual safeguards are in place between the controller and the recipient
   - The data subject has provided explicit consent after being informed of the risks
   - The transfer is necessary to perform a contract with the data subject or to implement pre-contractual measures
   - The transfer is necessary for public interest reasons recognized by Saudi law

2. Adequacy Determinations: SDAIA has authority to publish a list of countries and international organizations deemed to provide adequate levels of personal data protection. Factors considered include the recipient's legal framework, enforcement track record, and data protection standards.

3. Appropriate Safeguards: Where adequacy is not established, controllers may rely on:
   - Standard contractual clauses approved by SDAIA
   - Binding corporate rules for intra-group transfers
   - Approved codes of conduct
   - Certification mechanisms

4. Documentation and Record-Keeping: Controllers must maintain detailed records of all cross-border transfers including the legal basis, categories of data transferred, and recipient details.

5. Sensitive Personal Data: Heightened requirements apply to the cross-border transfer of sensitive personal data categories, including health data, biometric data, genetic data, and data relating to criminal records.

6. Enforcement: Violations of the transfer restrictions are subject to administrative fines and sanctions under the PDPL enforcement framework administered by SDAIA and the National Data Management Office (NDMO).
