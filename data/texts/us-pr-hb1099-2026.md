GOVERNMENT OF PUERTO RICO
    20th Legislative Assembly								           3rd Regular Session
HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES
House Bill No. 1099
FEBRUARY 2, 2026
                                         Presented by Representative Pérez Cordero
Referred to the Committee on the Judiciary
ACT
To amend subsection (b) of rule 404 of the Puerto Rico Rules of Evidence, for the purpose of clarifying when the Public Prosecutor's Office shall justify the character evidence it intends to present; to add a new rule 516 and renumber rules 516, 517, and 518, for the purpose of establishing the journalist's privilege not to disclose his confidential sources or information; to amend the new and renumbered rule 519 of the Puerto Rico Rules of Evidence, for the purpose of clarifying that the new rule 516 shall not be interpreted restrictively; to amend subsections (e), (f), and (r) of rule 805 of the Puerto Rico Rules of Evidence, for the purpose of clarifying that the jury may only hear hearsay evidence, and to require an affidavit instead of a certification by the custodian of the evidence or qualified witness; to create a new subsection (c) of rule 901 of the Puerto Rico Rules of Evidence, for the purpose of addressing challenges to authenticated evidence that turned out to be created through generative artificial intelligence; to amend subsection (l) of rule 902 of the Puerto Rico Rules of Evidence, for the purpose of creating new subsections (l) and (m) on how to authenticate certified records generated by an electronic process or system, and certified data copied from an electronic device, storage medium, or file; and for other related purposes.
STATEMENT OF MOTIVES
	Rule 404(b) of evidence of Puerto Rico, in its second paragraph, adopted the previous federal rule of the same number and letter:
"If the accused person so requests, the Public Prosecutor's Office shall notify them of the general nature of all evidence that the Public Prosecutor's Office proposes to present under this subsection (B)."
The federal rule was subsequently amended to add notification of the purpose intended by the Public Prosecutor's Office when invoking admissibility:
"(B) articulate in the notice the permitted purpose for which the prosecutor intends to offer the evidence and the reasoning that supports the purpose;"
With this measure, rule 404 of evidence of Puerto Rico is brought into harmony with its federal counterpart.
In Izquierdo v. Cruz, the Supreme Court of Puerto Rico established the qualified constitutional privilege of the journalist. In this measure, a new rule of evidence is included to codify the journalist's privilege not to disclose his confidential sources or information. The new and renumbered rule 519 — formerly 518 — is also amended to clarify that this new privilege shall not be interpreted restrictively because it is one of constitutional rank.
Rule 805(e) and (r) of evidence of Puerto Rico contains provisions on hearsay documentary evidence that is admitted. Like their federal counterparts, these subsections seek to prevent such evidence from going to the deliberation room. That is, that the document be admitted and read in the proceeding, but that it not form part of the materials that the jury takes to the deliberation room. However, the text of rule 805(e) and (r) is not drafted in those terms.
In this bill, the aforementioned subsections are amended to align them with the corresponding federal rule of evidence. Thus, the intention of such subsections is clarified, that the jury not give excessive weight to the written word over the oral testimony heard.
 Rule 805(f) of evidence of Puerto Rico configures the so-called business records exception. Although it largely follows its federal counterpart, it includes governmental activity in the definition of the term business. Subsection (h) of the referenced rule covers records of governmental activities. In light of the principle of specialty, rule 805(f) is amended to remove the term governmental activity from the definition of business record.
The advance of generative artificial intelligence has caused questioning about the viability of the rules of evidence — both federal and state and territorial — to address the authentication and admissibility of evidence that could have been created or manipulated through generative artificial intelligence. Academics on the topic have proposed adapting rule 901 to address these matters. In this bill, one such proposal is partially adopted with the creation of a new subsection (c) for rule 901.
 Rule 902(k) of evidence of Puerto Rico adopted the federal regulation regarding the authentication of business records through a rebuttable presumption. To activate it, the proponent must accompany an affidavit signed by the records custodian or analogous figure. This is different from the federal rule used as a reference.
In the aforementioned rule 805(f), the certification mechanism was left as prima facie evidence — pursuant to the federal rule — instead of the affidavit. In this measure, the language of rule 805(f) is amended so that it also includes the affidavit as it appears in rule 902(k).
Rule 902(l) of evidence of Puerto Rico combined in one subsection — electronic record — what in the federal rule appears in two subsections: how to authenticate certified records generated by an electronic process or system, and certified data copied from an electronic device, storage medium, or file. Consistent with the federal rule, in this bill, rule 902(l) is eliminated and new subsections (l) and (m) are created. In both subsections, the reference to subsection (k) — already outlined — on how to authenticate the aforementioned electronic evidence is included.
	This Legislative Assembly has the authority to amend the Puerto Rico Rules of Evidence. Thus, the drafting of the rules is improved to help the parties and the courts clarify the truth of judicial controversies. The rules are also adapted to the inevitable changes brought by the jurisprudence of the Supreme Court and technological advances.

Section 1. Subsection (b) of rule 404 of the Puerto Rico Rules of Evidence, as amended, is amended to read as follows:
"Rule 404. Character evidence is not admissible to prove conduct; exceptions; evidence of the commission of other offenses
(a) ...
(b) Evidence of specific conduct, including the commission of other offenses, civil wrong, or other acts, is not admissible to prove a propensity to engage in such conduct and for the purpose of inferring that one acted in conformity with such propensity. However, evidence of such conduct is admissible if it is relevant for other purposes, such as proof of motive, opportunity, intent, preparation, plan, knowledge, identity, absence of mistake or accident, or to establish or refute a defense.
If the accused person so requests, the Public Prosecutor's Office shall notify them of the general nature of all evidence that the Public Prosecutor's Office proposes to present under this subsection. It shall also justify the purpose for which it proposes to present such evidence. The notification shall be provided sufficiently in advance of trial[, but the]. (c) ..."
Section 2. Rules 516, 517, and 518 are renumbered as the new rules 517, 518, and 519 of the Puerto Rico Rules of Evidence, as amended.
Section 3. A new rule 516 is added to the Puerto Rico Rules of Evidence, as amended, to read as follows:
"Rule 516. Journalist's privilege not to disclose his sources or
confidential information
The journalist has a privilege not to disclose his confidential sources or information. Whoever requests that information shall demonstrate the relevance of the identity of the journalistic source or of the confidential information for the adjudication of the action. Once such relevance is established, the court shall evaluate whether the party interested in discovering the requested information presented evidence to establish: that what was published is false and defamatory; that he employed reasonable efforts to discover the source or confidential information by other means; and that it is necessary to know the identity of the source or confidential information to establish his cause of action."
Section 4. The new rule 519 of the Puerto Rico Rules of Evidence, as amended, is amended to read as follows:
"Rule 519. Restrictive interpretation
The rules of privileges shall be interpreted restrictively in relation to any determination on the existence of a privilege, except for Rules 501, 502 [and], 512, and 516 of this appendix relating to privileges of constitutional rank."

Section 6. A new subsection (c) is added to rule 901 of the Puerto Rico Rules of Evidence, as amended, to read as follows:
"Rule 901. Authentication or identification requirement.
(a) ...
(b) ...
(c) A party may challenge the authenticity of computer-generated evidence or other electronic evidence with evidence demonstrating that the challenged evidence was manipulated or fabricated, in whole or in part, with generative artificial intelligence. The court shall decide on the admissibility of the challenged evidence pursuant to Rule 109."
Section 7. Rule 902 of the Puerto Rico Rules of Evidence, as amended, is amended to read as follows:
"Rule 902. Prima Facie Authentication.
Extrinsic evidence of authentication shall not be required as a precondition to admissibility for:
(a) ...

[(l) Electronic record.— The integrity of the record shall be presumed if:
(1) It is established by affidavit that it was recorded or stored by a party adverse to the one proposing it, or

(l) Certified records generated by an electronic process or system.— A record generated by an electronic process or system that produces an accurate result, as demonstrated by the certification of a qualified person that meets the certification requirements of subsection (k) of this rule. (m) Certified data copied from an electronic device, storage medium, or file.— Data copied from an electronic device, storage medium, or file, if authenticated through a digital identification process, as demonstrated by a certification of a qualified person that meets the certification requirements of subsection (k) of this rule. The proponent shall also comply with the notice requirements of subsection (k) of this rule.
