# Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019 (Uganda)
**Act of 2019**
*Uganda — February 25, 2019*

---

## Overview

Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 is the country's primary data protection law, implementing the constitutional right to privacy under Article 27 of the 1995 Constitution. The Act establishes the Personal Data Protection Office (PDPO) under the National Information Technology Authority — Uganda (NITA-U) as the supervisory authority. Implementing regulations — the Data Protection and Privacy Regulations 2021 — provide operational detail on compliance requirements. The Act applies to all organizations processing personal data of Uganda residents, whether the processing occurs in Uganda or elsewhere. The Act covers both automated and manual processing of personal data.

---

## Key Provisions

### Personal Data Protection Office (PDPO)
- Established under NITA-U as the supervisory and enforcement authority
- Registers data collectors, data processors, and data controllers
- Investigates complaints from data subjects
- Issues compliance orders and enforcement notices
- May impose administrative sanctions and refer criminal cases for prosecution
- Issues guidelines and codes of practice for sectors

### Six Data Protection Principles
- Lawfulness, fairness, and transparency in processing
- Purpose limitation: data collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes
- Data minimization: limited to what is necessary for the purpose
- Accuracy: data must be accurate and kept up to date
- Storage limitation: not retained longer than necessary
- Integrity and confidentiality: appropriate security measures

### Data Subject Rights
- Right to access personal data held about them
- Right to correction of inaccurate or incomplete data
- Right to deletion/erasure of data no longer necessary
- Right to object to processing for direct marketing or other specified purposes
- Right to lodge a complaint with the PDPO

### Sensitive Personal Data
- Special categories requiring explicit consent or specific justification: health and medical data, genetic data, biometric data, racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious beliefs, trade union membership, criminal record
- Heightened security and confidentiality obligations for sensitive categories

### Mandatory Registration
- Data collectors, processors, and controllers must register with PDPO
- Registration disclosures include types of data, purposes, and data subjects
- Failure to register is an offense

### Cross-Border Data Transfers
- Transfers outside Uganda restricted unless recipient country provides adequate protection or specific safeguards are in place (contract, binding corporate rules, consent)

### Breach Notification
- Data controllers must notify the PDPO of significant personal data breaches
- Notification to affected data subjects required where breach poses significant risk

### Security Obligations
- Implement technical and organizational measures appropriate to the risk
- Conduct risk assessments before implementing high-risk processing

## Enforcement & Penalties

- PDPO may issue compliance notices and enforcement orders
- Administrative fines may be imposed for violations
- Criminal penalties: fines up to UGX 250 million (approximately $67,000 USD) and/or up to 10 years imprisonment for the most serious (aggravated) offenses
- Standard offenses carry lesser fines and terms of imprisonment
- Civil liability: data subjects may sue for damages caused by unlawful processing
