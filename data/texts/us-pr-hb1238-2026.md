GOVERNMENT OF PUERTO RICO
20th Legislative Assembly                          3rd Regular Session
HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES
H. B. 1238
APRIL 24, 2026
Introduced by Representative Burgos Muñiz
Referred to the Committee on the Judiciary
ACT
To add a new Article 268A to Act No. 146 of July 30, 2012, as amended, and known as the "Penal Code of Puerto Rico," for the purpose of classifying as criminal conduct that any person who, through the use of artificial intelligence, produces images, audio, videos, and/or documents with the intention of deceiving persons or officials with authority by law to conduct investigations of a criminal nature, as well as deceiving with information produced through the use of artificial intelligence any official in the discharge of duties of the Government of Puerto Rico, shall incur a misdemeanor; as well as if the act produced with artificial intelligence is one that constitutes a felony, the person shall incur a felony and shall be sanctioned with a fixed term of three (3) years of imprisonment; and for other related purposes.
STATEMENT OF MOTIVES
The use of artificial intelligence (AI) is growing exponentially in the postmodern society we live in. The development of artificial intelligence (AI) represents one of the most advanced technological breakthroughs. Its use has transformed sectors such as education, health, governmental administration, and to some extent, public safety. However, this accelerated and progressive use brings with it a series of pressing concerns and risks when used improperly, particularly with the purpose of deceiving or manipulating legal and administrative processes of the State.
In Puerto Rico, like in other state jurisdictions and nations of the world, the use of artificial intelligence (AI) with the intention of deceiving or manipulating the authorities has begun to be glimpsed. The use of artificial intelligence (AI) with the intention of deceiving or manipulating contains entirely false or partially false content when it simulates a truth that misleads law enforcement officials and the State, through the creation of images, audio, videos, and documents. These actions are commonly known as "deepfakes," which have the capacity to simulate events, statements, or evidence that never occurred, which can provoke the improper initiation of criminal investigations, the diversion of public resources, and affect the investigative processes of the State.
Recently, a twenty-two-year-old young man was arrested after pulling a prank on agents of the Municipal Police of San Juan by using a video created with artificial intelligence (AI). The arrestee, identified as Alexis Jesús Martínez, approached the police officers at a fast-food establishment at Parada 18 in Santurce and showed them a video in which a man was seen getting into the patrol car, which is the official vehicle of the Police. Upon seeing this video, the agents immediately mobilized to the outside of the establishment, but found the patrol car parked, thus confirming that the scene in the video was false. Subsequently, the investigation revealed that the video was the product of the use of artificial intelligence with false content.
The foregoing makes us recognize that our Legal System does not expressly contemplate this type of conduct when it is produced by artificial intelligence (AI). Although there are general provisions on fraud, falsification, and obstruction of justice, the ease of access to artificial intelligence (AI) requires a specific response to specifically prosecute such conduct. In order to protect the order and the work of our officials, it is imperative to establish reasonable controls regarding the use of artificial intelligence (AI), particularly when it is employed with the intention of deceiving law enforcement officials charged with enforcing the law or any other official in the exercise of their duties. This conduct not only undermines confidence in the institutions, but puts at risk the fundamental rights of third parties, by promoting unfounded investigations or official actions based on false information.
For all the foregoing, it is meritorious that this Legislative Assembly amend the Penal Code to add a new article and criminalize as a misdemeanor the intentional use of artificial intelligence with the purpose of deceiving public order officials or any governmental official, inducing them to initiate criminal investigations or to make official decisions based on fabricated or manipulated information. This amendment responds to establishing reasonable controls and in no way prevents the legitimate and beneficial development of artificial intelligence for human beings.
BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY OF PUERTO RICO:
Section 1.- Article 268A is hereby added to Act No. 146 of July 30, 2012, as amended, to read as follows:
Article 268A. – Use of Artificial Intelligence with the intention of deceiving the authorities.
Any person who, through the use of artificial intelligence, produces images, audio, videos, and/or documents with the intention of deceiving persons or officials with authority by law to conduct investigations of a criminal nature, as well as deceiving with manipulated and/or false information in videos, photos, documents produced by the use of artificial intelligence any official of the Government of Puerto Rico, shall incur a misdemeanor.
If the act produced with artificial intelligence is one that constitutes a felony, the person shall incur a felony and shall be sanctioned with a fixed term of three (3) years of imprisonment.
Section 2.- Severability.
If any clause, paragraph, article, provision, section, subsection, or part of this Act is annulled or declared unconstitutional by a court with jurisdiction and competence, the judgment rendered to such effect shall not affect, prejudice, or invalidate the other provisions of this Act. The effect of said judgment shall be limited to the clause, paragraph, article, provision, section, subsection, or part of this Act that has been annulled or declared unconstitutional.
Section 3.- Effectiveness.
This Act shall take effect immediately upon its approval.
