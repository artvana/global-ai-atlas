# Personal Data Protection Law No. 24 of 2023
**Law No. 24 of 2023**
*Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan — Enacted and in force September 24, 2023*

---

## Overview / Background

Law No. 24 of 2023 on the Protection of Personal Data (the Jordan PDPL) is Jordan's first comprehensive personal data protection statute. Enacted on September 24, 2023, and entering into force on the same date, the PDPL establishes the legal framework for the protection of personal data in the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan and creates an independent supervisory authority — the Personal Data Protection Council (PDPC) — to oversee and enforce compliance.

The Jordan PDPL was developed in the context of Jordan's e-government modernization agenda and reflects the Kingdom's engagement with international data protection norms including the OECD Privacy Guidelines, the Arab League's data protection model law, and elements of the EU GDPR. Jordan's law shares structural similarities with Bahrain's PDPL 2018 and Oman's PDPL 2022, reflecting a broader convergence in GCC and Levant data protection frameworks.

The PDPL applies to any natural or legal person (public or private sector) that collects, processes, or uses personal data in Jordan, or that processes personal data of Jordanian citizens or residents from outside Jordan. The Personal Data Protection Council serves as the primary enforcement authority with powers to investigate complaints, issue guidance and orders, and impose penalties.

As of 2026, implementing regulations required to operationalize several provisions of the PDPL are still in development, which has led to uncertainty about certain procedural requirements. However, the core principles, rights, and obligations under the law are in force.

## Key Provisions / Chapters

### Establishment of the Personal Data Protection Council (PDPC)
- The PDPL establishes the Personal Data Protection Council as an independent governmental body responsible for:
  - Issuing implementing regulations and guidelines
  - Receiving and investigating complaints from data subjects
  - Conducting audits and inspections of data controllers and processors
  - Imposing administrative penalties
  - Coordinating with other regulatory authorities
  - Representing Jordan in international data protection forums
- The PDPC is headed by a President appointed by Royal Decree

### Core Data Protection Principles
- **Lawfulness, fairness, and transparency:** Personal data must be processed on a valid legal basis, in a fair manner, and with transparency to data subjects.
- **Purpose limitation:** Data may only be collected for specific, explicit, and legitimate purposes.
- **Data minimization:** Processing must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary.
- **Accuracy:** Data must be kept accurate and up to date.
- **Retention limitation:** Data must not be kept in identifiable form longer than necessary.
- **Security and confidentiality:** Appropriate security measures must be implemented.

### Lawful Bases for Processing
- Controllers must rely on one of the following lawful bases:
  - Consent of the data subject (freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous)
  - Performance of a contract with the data subject
  - Compliance with a legal obligation
  - Protection of vital interests
  - Performance of a task in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority
  - Legitimate interests of the controller or third party (with balancing test)

### Special Categories of Sensitive Personal Data
- The PDPL designates the following categories as sensitive, requiring explicit consent or specific statutory authorization:
  - Health and medical data
  - Genetic data
  - Biometric data used for unique identification of natural persons
  - Racial or ethnic origin
  - Political opinions
  - Religious or philosophical beliefs
  - Criminal convictions, offenses, and security measures
  - Financial data including bank account information and credit records
- Processing of sensitive data without explicit consent or a specific legal basis is prohibited and attracts enhanced penalties

### Data Subject Rights
- **Right of access:** Data subjects may request and receive a copy of their personal data held by a controller, along with information about processing purposes, recipients, and retention periods.
- **Right to correction:** Data subjects may request correction of inaccurate or incomplete data; controllers must respond within the timeframes set by implementing regulations.
- **Right to deletion:** Data subjects may request erasure of their data where it is no longer necessary, consent is withdrawn, or processing is unlawful.
- **Right to object:** Data subjects may object to processing of their data for direct marketing or on grounds relating to their specific situation.
- **Right to data portability:** Where processing is based on consent or contract and conducted by automated means, data subjects may request their data in a structured, machine-readable format.
- **Right to withdraw consent:** Consent may be withdrawn at any time; withdrawal does not affect prior processing.

### Data Protection Officer (DPO)
- Controllers and processors that:
  - Process personal data on a large scale
  - Process sensitive personal data as a core activity
  - Carry out systematic monitoring of data subjects
  must appoint a Data Protection Officer registered with the PDPC.
- The DPO must have knowledge of data protection law and practice.

### Mandatory Data Breach Notification
- Controllers must notify the PDPC of personal data breaches without undue delay upon discovery.
- Where the breach poses a high risk of harm to data subjects, affected individuals must also be notified.
- Implementing regulations will specify the timelines and content of breach notifications.

### Cross-Border Transfer Restrictions
- Personal data may not be transferred outside Jordan unless:
  - The destination country provides equivalent data protection (PDPC adequacy determination)
  - Appropriate safeguards are implemented (contractual clauses, binding corporate rules)
  - A specific derogation applies (consent, contract, legal claims, vital interests)
- Transfers to countries without equivalent protection require PDPC approval or the use of approved safeguards

### Data Processor Agreements
- Controllers must enter into written agreements with processors governing the nature, purpose, and duration of processing.
- Processors may only process data on documented instructions from the controller.
- Sub-processors may only be engaged with the controller's prior authorization.

## Enforcement & Penalties

- **Administrative fines:** The PDPC may impose fines of up to JOD 1,000,000 (approximately $1.4 million USD) for serious violations of the PDPL.
- **Criminal penalties:** Intentional violations of the PDPL — particularly involving unauthorized processing of sensitive data or obstruction of PDPC investigations — may result in criminal prosecution.
- **PDPC enforcement powers:** The PDPC may issue binding corrective orders, suspend processing activities, and publish enforcement decisions.
- **Civil liability:** Data subjects whose rights are violated may seek compensation for material and non-material damages through Jordanian courts.
- **Aggravated penalties:** Violations involving sensitive data (health, biometric, financial) attract enhanced penalties.
- **Implementing regulations pending:** As of 2026, full enforcement capacity is contingent on the issuance of implementing regulations specifying procedures, timelines, and enforcement guidelines.
