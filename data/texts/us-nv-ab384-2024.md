# Nevada AB 384 — Deceptive Artificial Intelligence in Elections Act

**Enacted June 3, 2024 | Nevada Revised Statutes Chapter 294A**

---

## Purpose

Nevada AB 384 prohibits the creation and distribution of materially deceptive AI-generated media depicting candidates for public office within a specified period before an election, and requires disclosure of AI-generated content in political advertising.

---

## Definitions

**"Materially deceptive AI-generated media"** means any image, audio recording, or video recording of a candidate for public office:
(a) that was created using artificial intelligence, machine learning, or other digital technology that was not created or enacted by the depicted candidate; and
(b) that a reasonable person would believe depicts the actual conduct or speech of the candidate.

**"Candidate for public office"** means a person who files for or seeks nomination for any elective office in Nevada, including federal, state, and local offices.

**"Satire or parody"** means an obviously exaggerated work that a reasonable person would not mistake for a factual portrayal.

---

## Section 1 — Prohibition on Materially Deceptive AI Political Media (NRS 294A.355)

**(a) Prohibition.** It is unlawful for any person to, with knowledge that the media is materially deceptive AI-generated media, create, sponsor, pay for, or distribute any materially deceptive AI-generated media concerning a candidate for public office within **60 days** before a primary or general election in which that candidate appears on the ballot, **unless** the media includes the disclosure required by this section.

**(b) Required disclosure.** AI-generated media subject to this section must include a clear and conspicuous disclaimer that reads: **"This [image/audio/video] has been manipulated or generated by artificial intelligence."**

For video content: the disclaimer must appear in a font size no smaller than 4% of the picture height and remain on screen for the duration of the content.

For audio content: the disclaimer must be spoken clearly at the beginning and end of the audio at the same pace as the other spoken content.

For still images: the disclaimer must appear in a font size that clearly stands out and be placed either at the top or bottom of the image.

**(c) Exemptions.** This section does not apply to:
(1) A news organization or journalist reporting on news of public concern, provided the materially deceptive nature is clearly identified
(2) Obvious satire or parody that a reasonable person would identify as such
(3) Archival or documentary material

---

## Section 2 — Civil Cause of Action

**(a) Standing.** A candidate for public office who is depicted in materially deceptive AI-generated media in violation of this section has a cause of action against the person who created or distributed the media.

**(b) Relief available.** A successful plaintiff may recover:
- Injunctive relief to prevent further distribution
- Actual damages caused by the media, including lost campaign contributions and reputational harm
- Statutory damages between $500 and $10,000 per violation
- Attorney's fees and costs

**(c) Expedited proceedings.** Courts shall expedite proceedings brought under this section when the violation occurs within 30 days before an election.

---

## Section 3 — Criminal Penalties

A person who knowingly violates the prohibition in this section is guilty of a misdemeanor. Each separate distribution of materially deceptive AI-generated media constitutes a separate violation.

---

## Effective Date

This Act became effective June 3, 2024, and applies to media created or distributed on or after that date.
