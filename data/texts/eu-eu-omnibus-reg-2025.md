# European Commission Proposal — Digital Omnibus Regulation Amending GDPR, NIS2, Data Act, DORA and Related Instruments

**Commission Legislative Proposal (COM/2025)**
*European Union — Proposed 19 November 2025; pending as of May 2026*

---

## Overview

The Digital Omnibus digital law amendment proposal is the companion instrument to the AI Act amendment proposal, forming part of the European Commission's November 2025 Digital Omnibus package. This proposal amends six major EU digital regulations simultaneously in a single omnibus legislative act, a legislative technique designed to reduce the complexity and cost of sequential reforms.

The proposal does not establish a new regulatory framework; it is a horizontal amendment instrument modifying: the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, Regulation (EU) 2016/679); the NIS2 Directive (Directive (EU) 2022/2555); the Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854); the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA, Regulation (EU) 2022/2554); the Critical Entities Resilience (CER) Directive; and the European Digital Identity (eIDAS 2.0) Regulation.

While the instrument is not AI-specific, its amendments to GDPR and NIS2 have significant implications for AI development, deployment, and data governance practices across the EU.

## Key Provisions

### GDPR Amendments

**Narrowed Definition of Personal Data.** The proposal introduces a clarification to Article 4(1) GDPR's definition of personal data, codifying guidance from the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) and Court of Justice (CJEU) case law. Under the proposed amendment, identifiability is assessed from the perspective of the controller and processors acting on their behalf, not hypothetical third parties. This change is intended to reduce over-compliance by organizations treating publicly accessible, non-targeted data as personal data.

**Cookie Consent Streamlining.** Cookie consent obligations, currently derived from the ePrivacy Directive (2002/58/EC), would be incorporated into the GDPR framework directly. The proposal introduces a harmonized browser-level or device-level consent mechanism, reducing the proliferation of per-site cookie banners. This change affects AI systems that rely on cookie-based tracking for personalization or behavioral advertising.

**Legal Basis for AI Training.** The proposal introduces guidance-level clarification (through a new Recital) supporting the use of GDPR's legitimate interests basis (Article 6(1)(f)) for AI training on publicly available personal data, subject to a balancing test and opt-out mechanisms. This complements the AI Act amendment's legitimate interest provisions.

**Extended Breach Notification Window.** The mandatory personal data breach notification period for controllers to notify supervisory authorities is extended from **72 hours** to **96 hours** under proposed amendments to Article 33 GDPR. The 72-hour window was widely criticized as unworkable for complex multi-system breaches.

### NIS2 Directive Amendments

The proposal amends NIS2 (Directive (EU) 2022/2555) to:
- Streamline incident reporting timelines, extending the initial notification period from 24 to 36 hours for certain categories of cyber incidents
- Harmonize the definition of "significant incident" across member state transpositions to reduce fragmentation
- Clarify the scope of NIS2 obligations for AI infrastructure providers (cloud, data centers, managed service providers) that support AI systems

### Data Act Amendments

Amendments to the Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854) clarify the interaction between Data Act data-sharing obligations and AI training activities, particularly regarding: (i) the scope of "product data" accessible by users; and (ii) the conditions under which AI-generated data from IoT devices constitutes product data subject to sharing rights.

### DORA Amendments

Amendments to DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) address the treatment of AI-based financial services tools, clarifying that AI systems used for algorithmic trading, credit scoring, and risk assessment fall within DORA's ICT risk management framework. The amendments add AI-specific ICT incident classification guidance.

### eIDAS 2.0 / Digital Identity Amendments

Technical amendments to the European Digital Identity Regulation streamline the interoperability requirements for AI-driven identity verification systems and clarify the use of AI in electronic attestation of attributes (EAAs).

## Legislative Status

The proposal was published 19 November 2025. Given the complexity of amending six instruments simultaneously, and the concurrent trilogue negotiations on the AI Act amendment companion proposal, the legislative timeline is uncertain. Parliamentary and Council committee work was ongoing as of May 2026. Adoption is not expected before late 2026 at the earliest.
