                     GOVERNMENT OF PUERTO RICO

 20th Legislative Assembly                                                             3rd Regular Session


                     SENATE OF PUERTO RICO

                              Senate Bill No. 1153
                                  March 23, 2026


                          Presented by Ms. Soto Aguilú
                                        Referred to

                                           ACT
To create the "Act of the Labor Reconversion and Training Program in Artificial
Intelligence and Technological Skills for Displaced Workers or Workers at Risk of
Displacement in Puerto Rico"; to establish as the public policy of the Government of Puerto
Rico the training, retraining, and labor reconversion of workers
affected, or at risk of being affected, by processes of automation, digitalization,
artificial intelligence, and technological transformation; to create a program attached to the
Department of Labor and Human Resources, in coordination with
governmental, educational, and productive sector entities; to provide eligibility criteria and
service priorities; to authorize the offering of courses, competency credentials, and
support services for labor insertion; to establish mechanisms for interagency and
institutional collaboration; to provide for its financing, regulation, and
reports; and for other related purposes.

                             STATEMENT OF MOTIVES


      Puerto Rico is undergoing a profound transformation in the nature of work.
The automation of processes, the digitalization of services, the incorporation of
artificial intelligence tools, and the functional reorganization of multiple economic
sectors are rapidly altering the skills required to remain
and progress in the labor market. That change no longer constitutes a remote possibility
nor a purely academic discussion; it constitutes a concrete reality that impacts the
                                            2


stability of employment, occupational mobility, and the capacity of thousands of workers
to sustain themselves and adapt within an increasingly technified economy.
      In that context, the principal challenge for the State is not only to address the
consequences of unemployment once they materialize, but to anticipate the structural
changes that are redefining contemporary employability. The partial
or total loss of functions due to automation, digitalization, or technological substitution,
as well as the progressive obsolescence of certain labor skills, demands a public response
that is articulated, modern, and oriented toward occupational reconversion.
      Public policy cannot be limited to reacting belatedly to
displacement; it must create concrete mechanisms so that the workforce can
update its competencies, reposition itself in the market, and access new
opportunities for economic development. This reality particularly intensely affects
workers in sectors where routine tasks, repetitive administrative functions,
standardized service operations, or productive activities susceptible to technological reorganization predominate. It also impacts adult persons who,
even while retaining valuable work experience, face barriers to integrating into new
modalities of digital or technologically assisted work.
      Likewise, it impacts underemployed workers, persons with
interrupted work trajectories, and citizens who require retraining to
avoid being permanently left behind in the face of the transformation of the productive
environment. The State has the duty to respond to this conjuncture with effective
training and labor transition instruments. At the federal level there exist programmatic
structures for employment services, retraining, and support for displaced workers,
and in Puerto Rico the Department of Labor and Human Resources
administers resources and programs aimed at fostering employability and occupational
training.
      However, the speed at which artificial intelligence and other
emerging technologies advance makes it indispensable to have a more specific, focused, and
deliberately oriented response toward technological labor reconversion. Puerto Rico needs to
                                            3


establish a public policy that expressly recognizes that the technological
transformation of the labor market may generate exclusion if it is not accompanied by
real opportunities for retraining. It also needs to recognize that artificial
intelligence and digital tools should not be conceived solely as factors of
substitution, but also as instruments that, if properly understood
and used, can expand productivity, improve occupational insertion, and open
new routes of employment, self-employment, and entrepreneurship.
       The legislative challenge consists, precisely, of avoiding that technological change
deepen existing inequalities and, instead, transforming it into an opportunity for
mobility and economic strengthening for the working population. This Legislative Assembly deems it indispensable to establish an affirmative and
structured public response that facilitates the acquisition of new competencies in digital literacy,
information management, technological productivity, responsible use of
artificial intelligence tools, basic automation, and other skills relevant to the contemporary
labor market.
       That response must be articulated in a flexible, coordinated, and fiscally
responsible manner, taking advantage of the existing institutional infrastructure, promoting
alliances with educational entities and productive sectors, and allowing for the development of routes
of training adjusted to the real needs of the workforce. To these ends, this
Act creates the Labor Reconversion and Training Program in Artificial Intelligence and
Technological Skills, attached to the Department of Labor and Human Resources, with
the purpose of providing concrete opportunities for retraining and occupational
strengthening to workers displaced or at risk of displacement due to
automation, digitalization, artificial intelligence, and analogous technological
transformations.
       The measure also seeks to foster collaboration between the State, institutions of higher education, local workforce development boards, employers,
and other collaborating entities, in order to expand public response capacity and
maximize the reach of the training offered. Through this Act, the
                                                  4


     Legislative Assembly of Puerto Rico reaffirms that technological progress should not produce
     labor exclusion or irreversible social displacement. On the contrary, it must be
     accompanied by a public policy that places the worker at the center of the
     economic transformation and provides him or her with real tools to adapt, compete,
     and prosper.
            With this measure, Puerto Rico takes a concrete step to strengthen the resilience of
     its workforce, expand employment opportunities, and ensure that technological
     innovation contributes to collective well-being and the economic development of the Island.

     BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY OF PUERTO RICO:

 1          Article 1.- Title.

 2          This Act shall be known and may be cited as the "Act of the Labor Reconversion

 3   and Training Program in Artificial Intelligence and Technological Skills for

 4   Displaced Workers or Workers at Risk of Displacement in Puerto Rico."

 5          Article 2.- Declaration of Public Policy.

 6          It is declared the public policy of the Government of Puerto Rico to promote the

 7   labor reconversion, occupational retraining, and technological training of

 8   workers who are unemployed, underemployed, displaced, or at risk of displacement

 9   as a result of processes of automation, digitalization, functional reorganization,

10   technological innovation, artificial intelligence, or other transformations of the

11   productive environment, with the following purposes:

12          (a) to strengthen the employability and adaptability of the workforce

13   of Puerto Rico;
                                                   5


 1           (b) to facilitate the labor reinsertion of persons displaced or affected by technological

 2   changes;

 3           (c) to expand access to digital skills, technological competencies, and

 4   contemporary productivity instruments;

 5           (d) to foster occupational mobility toward emerging sectors or those of greater

 6   demand;

 7           (e) to reduce barriers to access to relevant training for adult workers;

 8           (f) to promote collaboration between the Government, educational institutions, the

 9   productive sector, and other collaborating entities;

10           (g) to foster the ethical, responsible, and productive use of artificial intelligence

11   tools and other emerging technologies; and

12           (h) to strengthen the institutional capacity of the State to respond in an

13   anticipatory, orderly, and effective manner to changes in the labor market.

14           The implementation of this public policy shall be carried out in accordance with the availability

15   of funds, the administrative capacity of the entities concerned, and the programmatic priorities

16   established by regulation.

17           Article 3.- Creation of the Program.

18           There is hereby created, attached to the Department of Labor and Human Resources, the Program

19   of Labor Reconversion and Training in Artificial Intelligence and Technological Skills

20   for Displaced Workers or Workers at Risk of Displacement in Puerto

21   Rico.

 1          The Program shall have as its purpose to design, coordinate, offer, promote, and

 2   facilitate opportunities for training, retraining, labor reconversion, and

 3   strengthening of technological competencies for unemployed,

 4   underemployed, laid-off persons, persons with substantial reduction of functions, or with reasonable

 5   probability of occupational impact, as a result of processes of automation,

 6   digitalization, technological reorganization, adoption of artificial intelligence, substitution

 7   of tasks, or analogous structural changes in the place of employment or in the corresponding economic

 8   sector.

 9          The Department of Labor and Human Resources shall administer the Program in

10   coordination with local workforce development boards, institutions of higher education,

11   vocational or technical schools, economic development entities, employers,

12   and other authorized collaborating entities, in order to take advantage of the existing

13   infrastructure, available human resources, educational platforms, and funds

14   intended for employment and training services.

15          Article 4.- Objectives and Functions of the Program.

16          The Program shall have, among others, the following functions:

17          (a) to identify and recruit eligible participants;

18          (b) to develop training and retraining routes oriented toward

19   employability and labor reconversion;

20          (c) to offer or coordinate courses, workshops, modules, or certifications in technological

21   skills relevant to the contemporary labor market, including digital literacy,

22   handling of computing tools, productivity platforms, analysis and
                                                   7


 1   handling of information, functional use of artificial intelligence tools,

 2   basic automation, digital collaboration, basic information security, and other

 3   analogous competencies;

 4          (d) to facilitate occupational orientation, referral to employment opportunities,

 5   labor counseling, and support services for occupational transition;

 6          (e) to promote alliances with educational institutions, employers, and third

 7   sector entities to expand the program offering;

 8          (f) to design competency assessment mechanisms and outcome measurement;

 9          (g) to coordinate dissemination activities about retraining opportunities

10   for unemployed, underemployed persons, or those at risk of displacement;

11          (h) to identify priority economic sectors, occupations, and labor groups; and

12          (i) to render the reports required by this Act.

13          Article 5.- Programmatic Components.

14          The Program may include, without being limited to, the following components:

15          (a) basic digital literacy and functional use of technological platforms;

16          (b) training in tools of digital productivity and remote collaboration;

17          (c) orientation and training in the responsible, ethical, and productive use of

18   artificial intelligence tools;

19          (d) training in analysis and handling of information, basic automation, and

20   digital organization of work;

21          (e) sectoral modules adapted to industries, occupations, or specific occupational

22   profiles;
                                                8


 1          (f) workshops on resume writing, interviews, job searches, occupational

 2   transition, and self-employment;

 3          (g) complementary training in entrepreneurship, independent work, or

 4   hybrid modalities of service provision;

 5          (h) diagnostic evaluation of competencies and retraining needs; and

 6          (i) any other component that the Department of Labor and Human

 7   Resources determines to be necessary by regulation.

 8          The specific content of the courses, modules, or training routes shall be

 9   determined administratively by the Department of Labor and Human Resources

10   or by the authorized collaborating entities, in accordance with the needs of the labor

11   market and technological evolution.

12          Article 6.- Eligibility.

13          The following persons may be eligible to participate in the Program, subject to regulation:

14

15          (a) unemployed workers who have lost their employment in whole or in part

16   due to technological changes, automation, digital reorganization, or transformation of

17   functions;

18          (b) workers at reasonable risk of occupational displacement due to technological

19   changes in their industry, occupation, or employer;

20          (c) underemployed persons requiring technological retraining to

21   improve their employability;
                                                  9


 1          (d) adults seeking to reinsert themselves in the labor market through pertinent

 2   technological training;

 3          (e) workers derived or referred by employment programs, local

 4   workforce development boards, employers, or collaborating entities; and

 5          (f) any other group that the Department of Labor and Human Resources

 6   identifies by regulation as a priority for purposes of technological labor

 7   reconversion.

 8          Article 7.- Service Priorities.

 9          In administering the Program, the Department of Labor and Human Resources

10   may establish criteria of priority to attend, among others, to:

11          (a) long-term unemployed persons;

12          (b) workers with notice of layoff, reduction of work hours, or elimination of

13   functions;

14          (c) persons coming from economic sectors vulnerable to intensive automation

15   processes;

16          (d) women heads of household, young adults, elderly persons with

17   the need for retraining, and persons with significant barriers to access to employment;

18          (e) participants of labor assistance or occupational development programs; and

19          (f) residents of municipalities or regions with limited employment or

20   technological training opportunities.

 1          Article 8.- Credentials and Certifications.

 2          The Department of Labor and Human Resources, by itself or through authorized

 3   collaborating entities, may issue certificates, attestations of participation,

 4   documentary evidence of completion, or analogous certifications that accredit the

 5   satisfactory completion of courses, modules, or training routes, or the demonstrated

 6   acquisition of specific competencies relevant for purposes of employability,

 7   occupational mobility, or insertion in employment opportunities.

 8          Such credentials or certifications shall not, by themselves, imply a guarantee

 9   of employment or automatic equivalence with professional licenses, professional collegiations, or

10   occupational certifications regulated by special legislation.

11          Article 9.- Interagency and Institutional Collaboration.

12          The Department of Labor and Human Resources may enter into collaborative

13   agreements with agencies, instrumentalities, public corporations, municipalities,

14   local workforce development boards, universities, educational institutions, employers,

15   nonprofit organizations, and other pertinent entities for the implementation of

16   this Act.

17          Such agreements may comprise, among other matters, curricular design, the

18   offering of courses, the use of facilities or digital platforms, the referral of

19   participants, the co-financing of services, the identification of priority sectors, the

20   validation or certification of competencies, and the rendering of employment services or occupational

21   support.

 1          Article 10.- Collaboration with Employers.

 2          The Department of Labor and Human Resources shall promote mechanisms of

 3   collaboration with employers to identify needs for labor reconversion and to facilitate

 4   the training of workers displaced or at risk of displacement due to

 5   technological transformation.

 6          To these ends, it may:

 7          (a) establish protocols for the voluntary referral of workers to the Program;

 8          (b) offer guidance to employers on retraining and

 9   labor transition opportunities;

10          (c) develop collaborative agreements for sectoral or company training;

11          (d) incorporate coordination or dissemination provisions in subsidized

12   or collaborative programs, in accordance with regulations and law; and

13          (e) carry out any other action compatible with the legal system to

14   facilitate the early identification of eligible persons.

15          Nothing in this Article shall be interpreted as an automatic

16   imposition of civil, criminal, or administrative employer liability, beyond what is

17   expressly provided by applicable law or regulation.

18          Article 11.- Financing.

19          The implementation of this Act shall be subject to the availability of state, federal,

20   competitive, special, donations, collaborative agreements, and any other

21   legally available financing mechanism funds.

18          (e) collaboration mechanisms with public or private entities;

19          (f) criteria for the use of available funds;

20          (g) performance and program evaluation indicators; and

21          (h) any other provision necessary for the effective administration of the

22   Program.

 1          Article 13.- Reports.

 2          The Department of Labor and Human Resources shall render to the Legislative

 3   Assembly of Puerto Rico an annual report on the implementation of this Act.

 4          Said report shall include, at a minimum:

 5          (a) the number of participants served;

 6          (b) the general profile of the populations impacted;

 7          (c) the courses, modules, or training routes offered;

 8          (d) the credentials or certifications issued;

 9          (e) the funds used or identified;

10          (f) the collaborative agreements entered into;

11          (g) the general results in terms of completion, referral, or labor

12   reinsertion, when such information is available; and

13          (h) the pertinent administrative or legislative recommendations to strengthen

14   the Program.

15          Article 14.- Severability Clause.

18   jurisdiction, the judgment rendered to such effect shall not affect, prejudice, or invalidate the

19   remaining provisions of this Act, which shall continue in full force and effect.

20          Article 15.- Effectiveness.

21          This Act shall take effect immediately upon its approval.
