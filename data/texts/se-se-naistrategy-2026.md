## Overview

Sweden published its first comprehensive national artificial intelligence strategy in February 2026. The strategy is Sweden's most ambitious AI policy to date, aiming to position Sweden among the world's top ten AI nations and to make Sweden a global leader in the use of AI within public administration.

The strategy takes a holistic approach, aligning three main areas: societal development, sustainable development, and competitiveness and innovation. It covers everything from the labour market and education to rules simplification, energy and defence, and extends to the business sector, entrepreneurship, and research. The document establishes both a vision and a concrete action plan spanning regulatory reform, data access, infrastructure, research, and security.

---

## Strategic Vision and Goals

Sweden's two primary ambitions under the strategy are:

1. **Top-10 global AI ranking**: Sweden aims to be among the world's top ten nations in AI adoption and development. At time of publication, Sweden ranked 17th in the Global AI Index.
2. **World-leading public administration**: Sweden aims to be the global leader in the use of AI within government and public services.

The strategy emphasises that AI should be used and developed in pursuit of:
- Societal benefit and welfare
- Sustainable development
- National competitiveness and innovation
- Technological independence

---

## Three Strategic Focus Areas

### 1. Societal Development
The strategy addresses how AI will reshape the Swedish labour market, education system, and public services. Key elements include:
- Expanding AI use across government agencies and public institutions
- Addressing workforce transitions and skills requirements
- Applying AI to the healthcare, tax administration, pension systems, and forestry management
- Making Sweden a model for democratic, rights-respecting deployment of AI in government

### 2. Sustainable Development
The strategy connects AI deployment with Sweden's broader sustainability goals:
- Applying AI to climate-related challenges and Sweden's 2045 net-zero goals
- Supporting energy planning alongside AI infrastructure expansion
- Expanding subsea and land-based data cable development to enhance global connectivity and computing capacity
- Aligning computing infrastructure investment with Sweden's access to fossil-free electricity and favourable climate conditions

### 3. Competitiveness and Innovation
The strategy targets Sweden's position as an innovation hub:
- Strengthening the business sector, entrepreneurship, and research ecosystem
- Allocating at least $11 million (approximately SEK 100+ million) annually for new AI research institutes and specialised excellence clusters
- Supporting domestically built Swedish language models and digitising national archives to support model training
- Expanding international positioning through a Nordic-Baltic AI centre

---

## Key Concrete Measures

The strategy includes a detailed action plan. Specific measures include:

- **National AI Coordinator**: Appointing a national AI coordinator for Swedish language models
- **AI Workshop for Public Administration**: Establishing a national AI workshop led by the Swedish Social Insurance Agency (Försäkringskassan) and the Swedish Tax Agency (Skatteverket), targeted to be fully operational by 2030
- **Team Sweden AI**: Launching a collaborative platform called "Team Sweden AI" to strengthen Sweden's global positioning in AI
- **Agency Assignments**: Directing more than 100 government agencies and universities to strengthen AI and data capabilities
- **Language Model Funding**: Increased funding for domestically built language models
- **Data Sharing Legislation**: Presenting a legislative proposal to facilitate data sharing between government agencies
- **Research Investment**: Funding for new AI research institutes and specialised excellence clusters
- **Nordic-Baltic AI Centre**: Creating a regional AI cooperation hub
- **Annual Oversight**: Directing the Agency for Digital Government (DIGG) and the Swedish Post and Telecom Authority (PTS) to provide annual oversight, tracking key metrics

---

## Regulatory Simplification and Framework

The strategy includes a commitment to simplify regulations that currently create barriers to AI implementation:
- Reviewing and updating frameworks including GDPR implementation, public procurement rules
- Facilitating secure data sharing across government agencies
- Expanding digital infrastructure aligned with energy planning, cybersecurity, and defence priorities

---

## Responsible and Secure AI

The strategy emphasises responsible AI development and deployment:
- Adherence to laws, human rights, and Swedish democratic values
- Actively mitigating bias, discrimination, security, and safety risks
- Alignment with international AI governance frameworks including the EU AI Act
- Protection of privacy and individual rights

---

## Current Strengths Acknowledged

The strategy recognises Sweden's existing advantages:
- World-leading research in machine-learning, language models, computer vision, and AI security (via WASP — Wallenberg AI, Autonomous Systems and Software Program)
- Good access to fossil-free electricity and a favourable climate for computing power infrastructure
- Stable democracy and innovation-friendly business environment
- Well-organised public sector holding valuable data assets
- Diverse exporting enterprises and active startup community

---

## Key Quote

Minister for Public Administration Erik Slottner stated: "We need to increase the use of new technology within broad segments of society, not least..." highlighting how this advancement can strengthen welfare systems and build Swedish technological independence.

---

## AI Sweden's Response

AI Sweden (the national centre for applied AI) welcomed the strategy but noted the ambitions require more substantial backing. Mikael Ljungblom, Director of Public Policy and International Relations at AI Sweden, stated: "To realize these goals, we need significant investment, new ways of organizing, and courageous decisions." AI Sweden called for a dedicated central governmental function within Sweden's Government Offices — modelled after Estonia's approach — to monitor AI developments, formulate policy, and coordinate implementation continuously.
