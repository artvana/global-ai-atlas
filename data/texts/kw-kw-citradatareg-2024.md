Data Privacy Protection Regulation 2.0
Kuwait Communication and Information Technology Regulatory Authority (CITRA)
Regulation No. 26 of 2024
Effective: February 19, 2024

OVERVIEW

The Communication and Information Technology Regulatory Authority (CITRA) of Kuwait issued Regulation No. 26 of 2024 on February 19, 2024, establishing comprehensive data privacy protections. This regulation supersedes the previous Regulation No. 42 of 2021 (Data Privacy Protection Regulation 1.0) and continues to guide lawful, fair, and transparent processing of personal data by telecommunications and information technology service providers.

SCOPE AND APPLICABILITY

The regulation governs all entities processing personal data in Kuwait, including:
- Data controllers (entities determining purposes and means of processing)
- Data processors (entities processing on behalf of controllers)
- Telecommunications service providers
- Information technology service providers
- Both public and private sector organizations

Any transfer of personal data outside Kuwait must ensure the receiving country provides an adequate level of data protection.

KEY PROVISIONS

CONSENT REQUIREMENTS

Service providers must obtain explicit consent from individuals before collecting or processing their personal data. Key consent requirements include:
- Consent must be freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous
- For individuals under 18 years of age, consent must be secured from a legal guardian
- Individuals retain the right to withdraw consent at any time
- Service providers are obligated to facilitate consent withdrawals without detriment to the individual

DATA SUBJECT RIGHTS

Individuals are granted seven fundamental rights:
1. Right to Access — Individuals may request confirmation of whether their data is processed and obtain a copy
2. Right to Rectification — Individuals may request correction of inaccurate or incomplete personal data
3. Right to Erasure ('Right to be Forgotten') — Individuals may request deletion of personal data in specified circumstances
4. Right to Restriction of Processing — Individuals may request limitation of processing in specified circumstances
5. Right to Data Portability — Individuals may receive their data in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format
6. Right to Object to Data Processing — Individuals may object to processing based on legitimate interests or for direct marketing
7. Right to Not be Subject to Automated Individual Decision-Making, Including Profiling — Individuals have the right not to be subject to decisions based solely on automated processing that significantly affects them

DATA BREACH NOTIFICATION

Service providers must notify CITRA within 72 hours of becoming aware of a personal data breach. Additional requirements:
- Notification must include the nature of the breach, categories and numbers of affected individuals, likely consequences, and measures taken or proposed
- If the breach poses high risk to individuals, affected individuals must also be informed without undue delay
- Documentation of all breaches must be maintained regardless of notification threshold

DATA SECURITY MEASURES

Service providers must implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to safeguard personal data against unauthorized access, loss, or destruction. Required measures include:
- Encryption and pseudonymization of personal data where appropriate
- Ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems
- Regular assessments of data processing activities
- Adherence to CITRA's data classification policies
- Access controls and authentication mechanisms
- Incident detection and response capabilities

LAWFUL BASIS FOR PROCESSING

Personal data may only be processed where:
- The data subject has given explicit consent
- Processing is necessary for the performance of a contract
- Processing is required for compliance with a legal obligation
- Processing is necessary to protect vital interests of the data subject
- Processing is in the public interest
- Processing is necessary for legitimate interests pursued by the controller, where not overridden by individual rights

SPECIAL CATEGORIES OF DATA

The regulation provides enhanced protection for sensitive categories of data including:
- Health and medical data
- Biometric data
- Genetic data
- Religious or political beliefs
- Racial or ethnic origin
- Criminal records

Processing of special category data requires explicit consent or specific legal authorization.

DATA RETENTION

Personal data must not be kept for longer than necessary for the purposes for which it was collected. Retention periods should be:
- Determined before data collection
- Documented in data processing records
- Enforced through data lifecycle management procedures

RECORD-KEEPING AND ACCOUNTABILITY

Entities must maintain comprehensive records of all personal data processing activities, making them available to CITRA upon request. Records must include:
- Name and contact details of the controller and, where applicable, processor
- Purposes of processing
- Categories of data subjects and personal data
- Recipients of personal data
- International data transfers
- Retention schedules
- Security measures

ENFORCEMENT

Non-compliance results in administrative fines based on violation severity and duration. The regulation allows fines as a percentage of annual turnover or a specific amount in local currency. CITRA has authority to:
- Investigate complaints from data subjects
- Conduct audits of data processing activities
- Issue compliance orders
- Impose administrative sanctions
- Suspend or prohibit data processing activities
