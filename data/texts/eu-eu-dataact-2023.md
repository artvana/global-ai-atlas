# Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 — EU Data Act

**Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 December 2023 on harmonised rules on fair access to and use of data**

*Applies from 12 September 2025*

---

## Chapter I — General Provisions

### Article 1 — Subject matter and scope

Establishes harmonised rules on:
(a) making data from connected products and related services accessible to users;
(b) data sharing between data holders and data recipients under Union law;
(c) unfair contractual terms in B2B data sharing;
(d) public sector access to private-sector data in exceptional need;
(e) switching between data processing services;
(f) interoperability standards for data spaces.

Applies to both personal and non-personal data.

### Article 2 — Definitions (selected)

- **"data"**: any digital representation of acts, facts or information
- **"connected product"**: an item that obtains, generates or collects data concerning its use or environment and that is able to communicate product data
- **"related service"**: a digital service connected with the product (excluding electronic communications services)
- **"data processing service"**: a digital service that enables ubiquitous and on-demand network access to shared pools of configurable computing resources
- **"data holder"**: a legal or natural person who has the right or obligation to use and make available data
- **"data recipient"**: a legal or natural person to whom data is made available
- **"user"**: a natural or legal person who owns or has a right to use a connected product or receives a related service

## Chapter II — Business-to-Consumer and Business-to-Business Data Sharing (Articles 3–7)

### Article 3 — Obligation to make product data accessible to users

Connected products shall be designed and manufactured — and related services designed and provided — such that product data is, **by default**:
- easily, securely, **free of charge** accessible to the user
- in a **comprehensive, structured, commonly used and machine-readable format**
- directly accessible where technically feasible

Before concluding a purchase contract, sellers must disclose: type and format of data the product can generate, whether data is stored on-device or remotely, retention periods, and methods of access and erasure.

### Article 4 — Rights and obligations regarding access and use

Data holders must make data **readily available without undue delay**, in machine-readable format, and **free of charge**. Data holders cannot:
- make access "unduly difficult" through non-neutral means
- coerce, deceive, or manipulate users
- retain unnecessary access logs

Trade secret disclosure requires proportionate technical and organisational measures and confidentiality agreements. Data holders may suspend sharing if confidentiality measures are not implemented. In exceptional circumstances, data holders may refuse access if they can demonstrate likely serious economic damage.

### Article 5 — Right of users to share data with third parties

Upon user request, data holders must provide data to **third parties** "without undue delay" in structured, machine-readable format, free of charge to the user. **Gatekeepers** designated under the Digital Markets Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/1925) cannot be eligible third parties. Third parties cannot use coercive means to access data or use data to derive competitive insights about the data holder.

### Article 6 — Obligations of third parties receiving data

Third parties receiving data must:
- use data only for purposes and conditions agreed with the user
- comply with EU data protection law
- erase data when no longer necessary for the agreed purpose
- not profile users except where necessary for the agreed service
- not share data with other third parties unless contractually authorised
- not make data available to gatekeepers
- not use data to develop competing products
- not ignore agreed confidentiality measures for trade secrets

### Article 7 — SME exemptions

Chapter II obligations do not apply to data generated by microenterprises or small enterprises (as defined in Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC), unless they are subcontracted. Medium-sized enterprises that recently qualified as such are exempt for one year.

## Chapter III — Obligations for Data Holders Under Union Law (Articles 8–12)

### Article 8 — Conditions for making data available in B2B relations

Where a data holder is obliged under Union law to make data available, it must do so under **fair, reasonable, and non-discriminatory (FRAND)** terms and conditions in a transparent manner. Data holders must treat comparable categories of recipients uniformly. Exclusive data access arrangements require user request under Chapter II.

### Article 9 — Compensation for making data available

Compensation agreed between data holders and data recipients must be **non-discriminatory and reasonable**. Compensation calculation must consider: costs of formatting and disseminating data, and investments in data collection and production. Where the recipient is an SME or not-for-profit research organisation, compensation is capped at direct costs (excluding investment return). The Commission must develop compensation guidelines.

### Article 10 — Dispute settlement

Users, data holders, and data recipients have access to certified dispute settlement bodies. Bodies must be impartial and independent with necessary expertise. Decisions must be adopted within **90 days** of receipt of request. Binding decisions require explicit consent of the parties. Rights to pursue judicial remedies are preserved.

### Article 11 — Technical protection measures

Data holders may apply technical protection measures (including smart contracts and encryption) to prevent unauthorised access, provided such measures do not discriminate between recipients or hinder user rights. Third parties and recipients must comply with data holder requests to erase data, cease production of goods derived from unlawfully used data, and provide compensation for misuse.

## Chapter IV — Unfair Contractual Terms (Article 13)

### Article 13 — Unfair contractual terms between enterprises

A contractual term concerning data access, use, liability, or remedies, **unilaterally imposed** by one enterprise on another, is not binding on the latter if it is **unfair**. A term is unfair if it "grossly deviates from good commercial practice in data access and use, contrary to good faith and fair dealing."

**Conclusively unfair terms** include those that:
- exclude liability for intentional acts or gross negligence
- eliminate remedies for non-performance
- grant one party exclusive rights to interpret the contract

**Presumptively unfair terms** (rebuttable) include those that:
- inappropriately limit remedies or extend liability beyond what is commercially reasonable
- allow detrimental access to commercially sensitive information
- prevent use of provided data
- restrict termination rights or prevent data copying
- enable unreasonably short termination notice
- permit unilateral price changes without adequate justification

"Unilaterally imposed" means supplied without meaningful negotiation opportunity; the supplying party bears the burden of proof.

## Chapter V — Public Sector Access on Exceptional Need (Articles 14–22)

### Article 15 — Circumstances of exceptional need

Public sector bodies may request data from private data holders where all of the following apply:
(a) the data is necessary to respond to a public emergency
(b) the data holder does not make the data available on request
(c) the data cannot be obtained by other means on equivalent timelines and cost
(d) the data is genuinely necessary to fulfil a statutory task in the public interest

### Article 16 — Obligations of data holders upon public sector requests

Data holders must respond promptly and make data available through appropriate technical means. They may be compensated at cost (not exceeding direct costs of making data available). Data provided under this Chapter must not be used for commercial purposes or shared with third parties beyond the stated purpose.

## Chapter VI — Switching Between Data Processing Services (Articles 23–31)

### Article 23 — Removing obstacles to effective switching

Providers must not impose and must remove commercial, technical, contractual, and organisational obstacles that prevent customers from:
(a) terminating service contracts after the notice period
(b) signing agreements with alternative providers
(c) transferring exportable data and digital assets
(d) achieving functional equivalence after migration
(e) separating bundled data processing services

### Article 25 — Contractual terms concerning switching

Switching-related terms must be clearly set out in a written contract, including:
- Switching clauses permitting transitions **without undue delay and within a maximum transitional period of 30 calendar days**
- Maximum notice period not exceeding **two months**
- Full specification of portable data categories
- Minimum **30-calendar-day** data retrieval period following the transitional period
- Full erasure of all exportable data after the retrieval period
- Switching charges specifications per Article 29

### Article 29 — Gradual elimination of switching charges

- From **12 January 2027**: providers **may not impose any switching charges**
- From **11 January 2024 to 12 January 2027**: providers may impose reduced switching charges not exceeding direct costs linked to the switching process

### Article 30 — Technical aspects of switching

Providers must take all reasonable measures to enable functional equivalence after switching. Non-infrastructure providers must make open interfaces available at no cost and ensure compatibility with common interoperability specifications. When common specifications have not been published, providers must export all exportable data "in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format" upon customer request.

---

## Chapter VIII — Interoperability (Articles 33–36)

### Article 33 — Essential requirements for interoperability of data spaces

Operators of data spaces must provide a description of their data space through a single point of contact and implement technical specifications for data quality, portability, security, and governance.

### Article 34 — Essential requirements for smart contracts for data sharing

Smart contracts used for data sharing obligations must:
- be robust and safe from unintended termination or interruption
- include access controls limiting operations to contractually specified parties
- ensure data is stored, transmitted, and processed in compliance with applicable law
- include mechanisms to terminate data processing and delete data
- include logging to ensure traceability

---

## Penalties

Member States must establish penalties for infringement that are **effective, proportionate, and dissuasive**. For violations by legal persons, maximum fines must be set under national law. The Commission may establish guidelines and model contractual terms to assist parties in complying with their obligations.
