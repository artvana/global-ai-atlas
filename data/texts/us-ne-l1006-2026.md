 LEGISLATURE OF NEBRASKA 
 ONE HUNDRED NINTH LEGISLATURE 
 SECOND SESSION 
 
 LEGISLATIVE BILL 1006 
 
 Introduced by Guereca, 7. 
 Read first time January 13, 2026 
 Committee: 
 
 
 A BILL FOR AN ACT relating to consumer protection; to adopt the Protecting Consumers and Jobs from Predatory Pricing Act; and to provide severability. 
 
 
 
 Be it enacted by the people of the State of Nebraska, 
 
 
 Section 1. 
 Sections 1 to 6 of this act shall be known and may be cited as the Protecting Consumers and Jobs from Predatory Pricing Act. 
 
 
 Sec. 2. 
 The Legislature finds and declares that: 
 
 
 (1) It is the policy of Nebraska to protect consumers from predatory pricing schemes in whatever iteration they appear or evolve; 
 
 
 (2) Advancements in algorithms, in artificial intelligence, and in electronic shelving technology have created commercial and retail environments where it is possible to set pricing for a product or service based on personal or protected data; 
 
 
 (3) Current law does not sufficiently prohibit companies from using personal or protected data to set prices; 
 
 
 (4) The use of personal or protected data to set prices presents a distinct potential for companies to violate consumer privacy rights and may result in discrimination; 
 
 
 (5) While offering goods at different prices due to customer demand is not new, there are subtle and important differences between price optimization and dynamic and surveillance pricing; 
 
 
 (6) It is in the interest of the state and its consumers to regulate technologies or business models which can impact consumers or competition by monetizing consumers&#39; personal information; 
 
 
 (7) Business models in dynamic and surveillance pricing have results that will lead to the elimination of numerous jobs, the existence of which are relied upon by the communities of the state for the livelihood of their residents and by the state directly for economic growth; 
 
 
 (8) Protection of these jobs will further protect consumers who are part of this economic ecosystem and will add to the growth of the economy and job market of Nebraska; and 
 
 
 (9) By enacting laws regulating dynamic, surveillance, and personalized algorithmic pricing, Nebraska can protect and promote the growth of jobs in the future. 
 
 
 Sec. 3. 
 For purposes of the Protecting Consumers and Jobs from Predatory Pricing Act: 
 
 
 (1) Algorithm means a computational process that uses a set of rules to define a sequence of operations, including, but not limited to, artificial intelligence systems and facial-recognition software; 
 
 
 (2) Business means any person, firm, organization, partnership, association, corporation, or any other entity doing business in Nebraska; 
 
 
 (3) Clear and conspicuous disclosure means disclosure in the same medium as and provided on, at, or near and contemporaneous with every advertisement, display, image, offer, or announcement of a price for which notice is required using lettering and wording that is easily visible and understandable to the average consumer; 
 
 
 (4) Consumer means an individual who is seeking or solicited to purchase, lease, or receive a good or service not for resale in the ordinary course of the individual&#39;s trade or business but for personal, family, or household use; 
 
 
 (5) Consumer data means any data that identifies or could reasonably be linked, directly or indirectly, with a specific individual or device, excluding location data; 
 
 
 (6) Dynamic pricing means pricing that fluctuates dependent on conditions that algorithmic or artificial intelligence models retrain or recalibrate on information in near real-time, excluding promotional pricing offers, loyalty program benefits, or other temporary discounts or changes to pricing related to retention of existing customers; 
 
 
 (7) Electronic shelving label means electronic and wireless paper displays that present product and pricing information; 
 
 
 (8) Grocery store means an establishment that is primarily engaged in retailing a general line of food, such as canned or frozen foods, fresh fruits or vegetables, or fresh or prepared meats, fish, or poultry; 
 
 
 (9)(a) Nondigital presentation of price means: 
 
 
 (i) A sign which offers the unit price for one or more brands or sizes of a given commodity; 
 
 
 (ii) A sticker, stamp, sign, label, or tag, affixed to the shelf upon which the commodity is displayed; and 
 
 
 (iii) A sticker, stamp, sign, label, or tag, affixed to the commodity itself. 
 
 
 (b) If a single sticker, stamp, sign, label, or tag does not provide the unit price information for more than one brand or size of a given commodity, then the following information shall be provided: 
 
 
 (i) The identity of the commodity; 
 
 
 (ii) The brand name of the commodity; 
 
 
 (iii) The quantity of the packaged commodity if more than one package size per brand is displayed; 
 
 
 (iv) The total sale price of the commodity; and 
 
 
 (v) The price per appropriate unit. 
 
 
 (c) Where a sign providing unit price information for one or more sizes or brands of a given commodity is used, the sign shall be centrally located as close as practical to all items to which the sign refers, and the unit price information displayed thereon shall be presented in a clear, distinct, and nondeceptive manner; 
 
 
 (10) Personalized algorithmic pricing means dynamic pricing or surveillance pricing derived from or set by an algorithm that uses consumer data which may vary among consumers and groups of consumers; 
 
 
 (11) Protected class data means information about an individual or group of individuals that directly, in combination, or by implication identifies a characteristic that is legally protected from discrimination under state or federal law, including, but not limited to, ethnicity, national origin, age, disability, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity and expression, pregnancy outcomes, and reproductive health care; and 
 
 
 (12) Surveillance pricing means offering or setting a customized price for a good or service for a specific consumer or group of consumers, based, in whole or in part, on information collected through electronic surveillance technology. Surveillance pricing includes the use of technological methods, systems, or tools, including, but not limited to, sensors, cameras, device tracking, biometric monitoring, or other forms of observation or data collection that are capable of gathering information about the behavior, characteristics, location, or other personal attributes of a consumer, whether in physical or digital environments. 
 
 
 Sec. 4. 
 (1) Any business that knowingly advertises, promotes, labels, or publishes a statement, display, image, offer, or announcement that uses personalized algorithmic pricing using consumer data specific to a particular consumer is required to provide a clear and conspicuous disclosure to consumers that the price was set by an algorithm using the personal data of the consumer. 
 
 
 (2) A grocery store larger than ten thousand square feet shall not use electronic shelving labels or any digital shelf display technology, and shall use a nondigital presentation of price. This prohibition does not limit the ability of any grocery store or business to provide a consumer a discount, promotional price, or loyalty program benefit based on the consumer&#39;s previous purchase history. 
 
 
 (3) No business shall use electronic shelving labels or any digital shelf display technology to display personalized algorithmic pricing or surveillance pricing. 
 
 
 (4) No business shall use personalized algorithmic pricing or surveillance pricing for any reason. 
 
 
 (5) No business shall collect or use the data of an individual younger than seventeen years of age for targeted advertising or personalized algorithmic pricing under any circumstances. 
 
 
 (6) No business shall use protected class data in setting a price for, offering, marketing, or selling any good or service if any of the following are true: 
 
 
 (a) The use of such data has the effect of withholding or denying any of the accommodations, advantages, or privileges accorded to other individuals or groups; or 
 
 
 (b) The price for such good or service is different from the price offered to other individuals or groups based, in whole or in part, on the use of protected class data. 
 
 
 Sec. 5. 
 The Protecting Consumers and Jobs from Predatory Pricing Act shall not apply to any: 
 
 
 (1) Person providing financial services, including, but not limited to, financial institutions, financial institution affiliates, broker- dealers, registered investment advisors, and persons that provide consumer credit products such as credit cards, personal loans, and mortgages; or 
 
 
 (2) Insurer licensed, regulated, or otherwise authorized to do business in Nebraska, including any person, agent, or affiliate acting on behalf of such insurer. 
 
 
 Sec. 6. 
 (1) Whenever a potential violation of the Protecting Consumers and Jobs from Predatory Pricing Act occurs, the Attorney General may bring an action in the name of the State of Nebraska in a court of competent jurisdiction to enjoin and restrain such violation and its continuance. 
 
 
 (2) In connection with any such action, the Attorney General is authorized to take evidence, make a determination of the relevant facts, and to issue subpoenas. 
 
 
 (3) If it appears to the satisfaction of the court that the defendant has, in fact, violated the Protecting Consumers and Jobs from Predatory Pricing Act, the court may issue an injunction, enjoining and restraining such violation and its continuance without requiring proof that any person has, in fact, been injured or damaged by the violation. 
 
 
 (4) Whenever a court determines that a violation of the Protecting Consumers and Jobs from Predatory Pricing Act has occurred, the court may impose a civil penalty of not more than seven thousand five hundred dollars for each violation. 
 
 
 (5) In addition to any other remedies provided in the Protecting Consumers and Jobs from Predatory Pricing Act, any person aggrieved by a violation of the act may bring a civil action against the violator. 
 
 
 (6) Nothing in this section shall in any way limit rights or remedies which are otherwise available under law to the Attorney General or any other person authorized to bring an action under this section. 
 
 
 Sec. 7. If any section in this act or any part of any section is declared invalid or unconstitutional, the declaration shall not affect the validity or constitutionality of the remaining portions.
