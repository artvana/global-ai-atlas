# Generative AI Guidelines for Government Entities

**Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority (SDAIA)**

*January 2024*

## Executive Summary

These Guidelines provide non-binding but authoritative guidance for Saudi government entities on the responsible adoption, use, and oversight of generative AI systems. They establish principles, risk frameworks, data governance protocols, institutional roles, and implementation checklists aligned with existing Saudi regulatory instruments including the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) and national cybersecurity standards.

---

## 1. Key Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Generative AI** | Models and systems that produce novel content including text, images, audio, video, and code |
| **Data Classification** | Labelling regime categorising information as: public, internal, sensitive, or classified |
| **Human-in-the-Loop** | Oversight mechanisms requiring human review of high-risk system outputs |
| **Model Provider** | Third-party organisations offering generative AI services to government entities |
| **Prompt** | User input submitted to a generative AI system |
| **Output Verification** | Validation and fact-checking processes for AI-generated content |

## 2. Governance and Institutional Framework

### 2.1 National Oversight

SDAIA serves as the national oversight body for generative AI in government, while compliance responsibility is delegated to individual government entities.

### 2.2 Required Institutional Roles

Each government entity using generative AI must establish or designate:

1. **Data Management Office (DMO)** or equivalent AI governance unit;
2. **Data Protection Officer (DPO)** — mandated under the Personal Data Protection Law for entities processing personal data;
3. **GenAI Champion** — internal advocate and coordinator for responsible AI adoption;
4. **Risk Assessors** — responsible for evaluating the safety and compliance profile of each AI use case; and
5. **Procurement Review Board** — vetting vendor selections for AI systems.

### 2.3 Coordination Mechanisms

- Inter-agency coordination pathways for cross-government AI use cases;
- Escalation procedures to SDAIA for high-impact deployments;
- Vendor risk assessment protocols; and
- Contractual accountability structures binding external providers.

## 3. Priority Risk Areas

The Guidelines identify six priority risk areas requiring specific controls:

### 3.1 Data Protection and Privacy

- Rigorous handling of personal and sensitive government information;
- Conformance with the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL);
- Privacy-by-design principles in AI system procurement and deployment; and
- Prohibition on submitting classified or sensitive personal data to external generative AI models without appropriate controls.

### 3.2 Information Integrity

- Safeguards against AI-generated misinformation and fabricated content (hallucinations);
- Deepfake detection and prevention measures for government communications;
- Watermarking and provenance metadata standards for AI-generated government content where feasible; and
- Mandatory human review before publishing AI-generated content in official communications.

### 3.3 National Security

- Strict prohibitions or controls on using generative AI systems with classified datasets;
- Secure handling of sensitive national security information;
- Requirement that foreign-hosted AI models not be used for classified government functions; and
- Defence-related information protection aligned with national security requirements.

### 3.4 Critical Public Services

- Enhanced human oversight requirements for AI use in healthcare, justice, emergency response, and other critical services;
- Higher assurance levels for citizen-facing applications;
- Mandatory audit trails for decisions made using AI in public services; and
- Regular independent assessment of AI systems used in critical functions.

### 3.5 Vendor Governance

- Security due diligence protocols for AI system vendors;
- Formal testing requirements before vendor systems are deployed;
- Contractual obligations on data handling, security, and model provenance; and
- Disclosure requirements for training data provenance (where feasible).

### 3.6 Transparency and Accountability

- Comprehensive logging of AI system inputs and outputs;
- Impact assessments for citizen-affecting AI services;
- Documentation of AI involvement in official government decisions; and
- Public disclosure of significant AI deployments.

## 4. Risk Classification Framework

The Guidelines classify generative AI use cases by impact level:

| Classification | Characteristics | Required Mitigations |
|---|---|---|
| **Low Risk** | Limited scope, non-sensitive data, routine internal functions | Basic logging, standard training |
| **Medium Risk** | Broader impact, sensitive data involvement, discretionary decisions | Enhanced controls, monitoring, documented assessments |
| **High Risk** | Critical services, personal data processing, significant public impact | Human-in-the-loop, advanced logging, third-party audits, SDAIA reporting |

## 5. Implementation Framework

### 5.1 Phased Adoption

**Phase 1 (0–3 months):** Initial risk screening; adopt compliance checklist for pilot uses; governance roles established.

**Phase 2 (3–6 months):** Controlled pilot deployments; staff training programmes launched.

**Phase 3 (6–12 months):** Approval and rollout of low- and medium-risk use cases; feedback loops established.

**Phase 4 (12+ months):** Full integration; periodic audits; continuous monitoring and improvement.

### 5.2 Technical Requirements

- Access control mechanisms restricting AI system use to authorised personnel;
- Encryption protocols for data transmission and storage;
- Secure prompt handling procedures preventing data leakage;
- Prompt engineering standards documentation;
- Output watermarking or labelling systems where technically feasible;
- Model provenance logging; and
- Regular vulnerability testing and security assessments.

### 5.3 Procurement Standards

When procuring generative AI systems, government entities must require providers to:
- Disclose model training data provenance (where feasible);
- Hold relevant security certifications;
- Commit to incident response timelines; and
- Provide binding data handling guarantees.

## 6. Monitoring and Evaluation

### 6.1 Monitoring Requirements

Entities must implement:

**Technical Monitoring:**
- Model behaviour and output drift detection;
- Bias metrics and safety incident tracking;
- Hallucination rate tracking; and
- False positive/negative analysis for critical tasks.

**Operational Monitoring:**
- User training completion records;
- Incident response performance; and
- System uptime and availability.

**Periodic Policy Reviews:**
- Alignment with evolving threats and regulatory updates; and
- Best practice incorporation from international developments.

### 6.2 Logging and Audit Requirements

- Record prompts, inputs, outputs, and AI-assisted decisions;
- Apply data minimisation and privacy constraints to logs;
- Retain logs according to data classification rules;
- Enable third-party audits for high-risk systems; and
- Report significant incidents to SDAIA within defined timelines.

### 6.3 Key Evaluation Metrics

- Accuracy measurements for task-critical outputs;
- Hallucination frequency rates;
- Bias indicators;
- Critical task error rates;
- User satisfaction; and
- Audit findings and remediation completion.

---

## 7. Compliance Checklist

Government entities must document completion of:

- [ ] Data classification completed for each generative AI use case;
- [ ] Risk assessment (impact level and mitigations) documented;
- [ ] Human-in-the-loop controls implemented for medium- and high-risk outputs;
- [ ] Vendor due diligence completed and contractual clauses in place;
- [ ] Logging and provenance mechanisms implemented; and
- [ ] User and staff training completed and recorded.

---

## 8. Enforcement and Accountability

### 8.1 Legal Consequences

Although these Guidelines are non-binding, violations of underlying legal obligations may trigger consequences under:
- The Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL);
- National cybersecurity regulations; and
- Sectoral compliance requirements.

### 8.2 Escalation

Repeated or serious non-compliance may result in:
- Enhanced SDAIA oversight;
- Formal audits; and
- Referrals to the competent authority.
