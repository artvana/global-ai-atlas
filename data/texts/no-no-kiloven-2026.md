# Norway — KI-loven (Artificial Intelligence Act — EEA Implementation)

**KI-loven (Lov om kunstig intelligens)**
*Norway — Consultation closed September 2025; introduction to Storting expected 2026*

---

## Overview

Norway is in the process of enacting KI-loven (the AI Act), implementing the EU's Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) into Norwegian law through the European Economic Area (EEA) Agreement. As an EEA EFTA state, Norway is not an EU member but is obligated to incorporate EU Single Market legislation — including the EU AI Act — into its national legal order following a formal EEA Agreement amendment process.

The Norwegian Ministry of Digitalisation and Public Governance published a consultation package on the implementing legislation on 30 June 2025. The consultation closed on 30 September 2025. Following review of consultation responses, the government planned to introduce a formal bill (Odelstingsproposisjon) to the Storting (Norwegian parliament) during the 2026 legislative session. Formal adoption and EEA entry into force are expected in 2026–2027, subject to the EEA Joint Committee decision incorporating the EU AI Act into Annex XX of the EEA Agreement.

## Key Provisions

### EEA Incorporation Mechanism

The EU AI Act cannot apply directly in Norway without EEA Agreement amendments. The EEA incorporation process involves:
1. The EEA Joint Committee (comprising EU and EEA EFTA representatives) deciding to include the EU AI Act in Annex XX of the EEA Agreement
2. Norway enacting domestic implementing legislation
3. Formal exchange of notes confirming the EEA Agreement amendment

The consultation package proposed that KI-loven would serve as the domestic implementing legislation, directly referencing the EU AI Act and making it part of Norwegian law with national supplements.

### Designation of National AI Supervisory Authority

The Norwegian Communications Authority (Nasjonal kommunikasjonsmyndighet — Nkom) is proposed as Norway's primary coordinating national AI supervisory authority. Nkom already has cross-sectoral regulatory competence in electronic communications and has been involved in digital regulation enforcement.

Sector-specific authorities would retain jurisdiction over AI in their domains:
- Financial Supervisory Authority of Norway (Finanstilsynet) for financial sector AI
- Norwegian Medicines Agency for healthcare AI
- Norwegian Data Protection Authority (Datatilsynet) for data protection aspects of AI Act compliance

A coordination mechanism is proposed to manage multi-authority cases.

### Scope and Adaptation

KI-loven would incorporate the EU AI Act's substantive obligations for AI system providers, deployers, importers, and distributors operating in Norway. Key adaptation points addressed in the consultation:
- **Language.** Technical documentation and user instructions must be available in Norwegian (Bokmål and Nynorsk)
- **Currency.** Penalty thresholds denominated in euros would be converted to Norwegian kroner (NOK) at a specified conversion date
- **Public sector.** Norwegian public bodies using high-risk AI systems in public services would be subject to EU AI Act obligations as deployers, with no blanket public authority exemption
- **EEA-specific provisions.** References to EU institutions in the AI Act would be adapted to address EEA Joint Committee and EFTA Surveillance Authority roles

### AI Regulatory Sandbox

The consultation package included provisions for a national AI regulatory sandbox aligned with Article 57 of the EU AI Act, to be operated by Nkom in cooperation with sector authorities. The sandbox is intended to support Norwegian AI innovators, particularly SMEs, in testing systems against regulatory requirements.

### Relationship to EU AI Act

Norway's implementation will mirror the EU AI Act's substantive provisions closely, with adaptations only for EEA-specific institutional arrangements and national procedural rules. Norwegian entities operating in the EU remain subject to direct EU AI Act enforcement by EU member state market surveillance authorities in those jurisdictions.

### EFTA Surveillance Authority Role

The EFTA Surveillance Authority (ESA) would take on an analogous monitoring and enforcement role to the European Commission for Norway-specific AI Act compliance issues, subject to EEA Agreement enforcement procedures.

## Legislative Timeline

- June 2025: Consultation package published
- September 2025: Consultation closed
- 2026: Government bill introduction to Storting anticipated
- 2026–2027: Expected adoption and EEA entry into force (pending EEA Joint Committee decision)
