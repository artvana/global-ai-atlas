REPUBLIC OF GHANA
NATIONAL ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE STRATEGY
2025-2035
Ministry of Communication, Digital Technology and Innovations
December 2025
================================================================================


FOREWORD
================================================================================

The world stands on the precipice of a profound technological revolution. Artificial
intelligence (AI) has rapidly emerged as a general-purpose technology with the
unprecedented potential to reshape economies, redefine public service delivery, and
transform human well-being. For the Republic of Ghana, the advent of AI represents
more than a mere technological shift; it is a critical launchpad to accelerate our national,
economic, and sustainable development objectives.

The Republic of Ghana National Artificial Intelligence Strategy: 2025-2035 is our
comprehensive roadmap to harnessing this transformative power. Building upon the
strong foundations of the Ghana Integrated Digital Transformation Blueprint, the Ghana
Digital Economy Policy and Strategy, and the ICT for Accelerated Development (ICT4AD)
policy, this strategy sets forth a bold ambition. Our vision is clear: Ghana 2035: The AI-
Powered Society. By 2035, we envision a transformed society where AI advances the
potential of our people, government, businesses, and systems to achieve inclusive
social and economic transformation. We are committed to equipping Ghanaians with
the capabilities and enabling environment necessary to compete in the global digital
economy, firmly positioning Ghana as a trailblazer for AI leadership in Africa and beyond.

We recognise that while the majority of AI development is currently concentrated in
advanced economies, its potential to address our unique local challenges is vast.
Strategic adoption of advanced digital technologies can drive productivity in key sectors of
our economy. From deploying predictive models to increase crop yields in agriculture, to
utilising natural language processing for cultural preservation, and leveraging automated
diagnostics to bridge structural inequalities in healthcare, AI can catalyse our progress
towards the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). By establishing
a National AI Fund and cultivating a vibrant local ecosystem, we project that AI will
contribute 500 billion Ghanaian cedis to our GDP by 2035.

To realise this immense potential, this strategy is built upon eight essential pillars. We
will decisively expand AI education and empower our youth for the jobs of the future,
ensuring that our growing, dynamic population becomes the driving force of our digital
economy rather than victims of automation. We will deepen our digital infrastructure and
computing capacity while facilitating robust, secure data access and governance. We
will coordinate a resilient AI community ecosystem, invest heavily in applied, producer-
led research—including the establishment of a National Deep Science Institute—and
accelerate AI adoption across both the private and public sectors.

However, we are acutely aware that the opportunities of AI are inextricably linked with
substantial risks. Algorithmic bias, the widening of digital divides, cybersecurity threats,
and the misuse of personal data pose genuine ethical and societal challenges. It is
imperative that our AI ecosystem remains responsible, inclusive, and sustainable. To
this end, our strategy is fully aligned with the UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics
of Artificial Intelligence. To champion this ethical deployment, drive implementation, and
monitor our progress, the Government will establish an independent, well-resourced
Responsible AI Authority (RAI Authority).

The success of this strategy does not rest with the Government alone. It requires a whole-
of-society approach. I issue a strong call to action to our vibrant start-up ecosystem, our
esteemed academic institutions, our traditional leaders, civil society, and our international
development partners: let us collaborate to build an internationally connected, yet proudly
Ghanaian, AI ecosystem. Together, we can harness the power of artificial intelligence to
improve the lives of all people in Ghana and set a resounding example of digital leadership
for the African continent.

HON. SAMUEL NARTEY GEORGE (MP)
MINISTER, MINISTRY OF COMMUNICATION, DIGITAL TECHNOLOGY AND INNOVATIONS


EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

Ghana's national, economic, and sustainable development objectives could be
accelerated through the application of artificial intelligence (AI). A national AI strategy
that builds upon existing digital policies and aligns key stakeholders will serve as a
transformative catalyst for Ghana's AI ecosystem and leadership position in Africa.

The Government of Ghana, through the Ministry of Communication Digital Technology and
Innovations, led the development of Ghana's National AI Strategy, with support from GIZ
FAIR Forward, the British High Commission's Foreign, Commonwealth and Development
Office (FCDO), the Data Protection Commission, the Responsible Artificial Intelligence
Lab, KNUST and MinoHealth AI Labs/GUDRA AI.

Mission: "To harness AI and AGI for inclusive growth across all sectors and to improve the
lives of people in Ghana, becoming a trailblazer for AI leadership in Africa and beyond."

Vision: "Ghana 2035: The Artificial Intelligence-Powered Society: By 2035, people
living in Ghana will experience a transformed society where AI advances the potential of
people, government, businesses and systems to achieve inclusive social and economic
transformation and quality of life. Ghanaians would have the capabilities and enabling
environment to be competitive in the global digital economy, positioning Ghana as the
leading African AI hub."

National AI Fund:
- 5 billion Ghanaian cedis 5-year fund (2025-2030)
- Scaling to 15 billion 5-year fund (2030-2035)
- Target: 200 billion Ghanaian cedis in FDI and local private sector investment by 2035
- GDP contribution target: 200 billion cedis by 2030; 500 billion cedis by 2035

The strategy is built upon eight essential pillars for an ethical, responsible and inclusive
AI ecosystem:

1. Expand AI Education & Training (AI Ecosystem Enabler)
2. Empower Youth for AI Jobs of the Future (AI Ecosystem Enabler)
3. Deepen Digital Infrastructure & Inclusion (AI Ecosystem Enabler)
4. Facilitate Data Access & Governance (AI Ecosystem Enabler)
5. Coordinate a Robust AI Community Ecosystem (AI Ecosystem Accelerator)
6. Accelerate AI Adoption across Sectors (AI Ecosystem Accelerator)
7. Invest in Applied AI Research (AI Ecosystem Accelerator)
8. Promote AI Adoption in the Public Sector (AI Ecosystem Accelerator)

Target sectors: Healthcare, Agriculture, Financial Services, Transportation, Public
Administration, Lands & Natural Resources, Environment & Circular Economy, Culture

Implementation will be led by the Responsible AI Authority (RAI Authority), an
independent, well-resourced institution to be established in the first year, modelled on
the Singapore National AI Office (NAIO), Egypt National AI Council, and the United
Kingdom's Office for AI.


CONTEXT
================================================================================

The application of artificial intelligence (AI) has the potential to accelerate Ghana's
movement toward achieving its economic and sustainable development goals (SDGs).
The National AI Strategy of Ghana functions as a comprehensive roadmap that enables
the country to capitalise on AI's socio-economic benefits while simultaneously mitigating
its hazards. Artificial intelligence can boost human and economic development across
Ghana in line with its national objectives, as well as progress towards the achievement
of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

The Ghanaian government, acting through the Ministry of Communication, Digital
Technology and Innovations, has been the driving force behind the creation of the country's
National Artificial Intelligence Strategy. This work has been made possible with assistance
from GIZ FAIR Forward, Smart Africa, the FCDO through the British High Commission and
it has been facilitated by The Future Society (TFS), RAIL and MinoHealth Labs.

What is AI?
AI broadly refers to "an array of technologies relying on algorithms at their core to 'think' or
'act' towards solving a problem. AI includes tasks such as learning, reasoning, planning,
perception, language understanding, and robotics." Machine learning (ML) is the sub-
set of AI which has garnered the most traction and enthusiasm in recent years, creating
opportunities for rapid AI developments and applications.

METHODOLOGY
================================================================================

Ghana's National AI Strategy is grounded in stakeholder consultations and analysis of the
country's AI and digital ecosystem and policy landscape. Around 40 expert interviews were
conducted with key actors and institutions in Ghana's AI ecosystem from the public sector,
academia, start-ups and private sector and civil society, complemented by four high-level
public sector consultation workshops.

Research methodology:
- Policy analysis & stakeholder mapping
- 4 Consultation workshops with key public sector actors
- ~40 Expert interviews (private sector, startups, academia, public sector, civil society)
- SWOT analysis of Ghana's AI Ecosystem
- AI ethical guidelines aligned with UNESCO's AI Ethical Guidelines
- Peer Learning on AI Policy & database of national AI policies
- Implementation plan for practical recommendations & roadmap
- Iteration & validation with key stakeholders

Initial workshop held in March 2022; subsequent workshops from May to October 2022.
The Strategy is guided by the UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial
Intelligence, adopted by Ghana in 2021.


AI FOR SUSTAINABLE AND INCLUSIVE DEVELOPMENT
================================================================================

AI can be a launch pad for Africa to make a paradigm shift to more efficient, optimal and
transparent delivery of public services and infrastructure development. Ghana has numerous
advantages and strengths that can be harnessed to enable a local Ghanaian AI ecosystem,
including Google's first AI centre in Africa in Accra.

Key opportunities across sectors:
- Healthcare: addressing structural inequalities, shortages of qualified healthcare
  professionals, barriers to accessibility and affordability
- Agriculture: predictive models for crop yields, soil management, pest detection
- Financial services: algorithmic predictions for credit scoring, fraud detection
- Transportation: urban traffic management, autonomous vehicles
- Education: personalized tutoring, curriculum content generation
- Environment: air quality monitoring, smart water management, recycling optimization
- Culture: digitization of oral histories, cultural preservation


RISKS OF AI
================================================================================

Risks requiring governance:
- Algorithmic bias and discrimination
- Widening inequality gaps and digital divides
- Cybersecurity, privacy and safety concerns
- Lack of transparency and explainability in AI systems
- AI-generated deep fakes and disinformation campaigns
- Social harm, citizen surveillance, privacy infringement
- Job losses from automation
- Data violations during collection, sharing, processing and storage

Ghana is working with UNESCO to undertake the AI Readiness Assessment Methodology
(RAM) to ensure that AI is developed and deployed ethically and responsibly.


DIAGNOSTIC ASSESSMENT OF GHANA'S AI ECOSYSTEM - SWOT ANALYSIS
================================================================================

STRENGTHS:
- Vibrant and growing digital, e-commerce and innovation start-up ecosystem (Ghana NLP,
  Mazzuma, Artificial Intelligence Association of Ghana)
- Digital connectivity expansion, including 800km fibre optic cables, submarine gateways
- Local digital hubs (Ghana Digital Centres Limited AI Lab) and business-friendly
  environment for international investment
- Growing international investment (Google, Twitter, Glovo)
- Presence of supportive global actors (Tony Blair Institute, Ghana-India Kofi Annan
  Centre of Excellence - GI-KACE)
- Hub in the West African sub-region
- Relatively strong availability of university graduates in computer science, data analysis,
  mathematics
- Young and fast-growing population
- Robust national vision, policies and objectives towards digital, ICT and innovation
- Championing data governance and e-payments in Africa
- AfCFTA headquarters in Ghana

WEAKNESSES:
- Low awareness about AI opportunities; low demand for local AI projects
- Skills gap and lack of political will among government actors
- Lack of coordination among AI ecosystem actors
- Limited access and low affordability of reliable electricity, high-speed internet, cloud
  computing and data centres
- Low digital skills & literacy in rural areas
- Insufficient local digitised datasets (accurate, updated, representative)
- Data inaccessible across sectors; siloed
- Low awareness and compliance with data privacy and cybersecurity policies
- Limited availability of university professors and AI courses
- Gaps in internet coverage; 4G penetration at 41% rural vs 88% urban
- Lack of access to venture capital and traditional bank loans for start-ups
- Lack of large-scale, local AI projects

OPPORTUNITIES:
- Leverage growing digital ecosystem and international investment for regional hub
- AI tools to drive inclusion and efficiency in healthcare, transportation, energy, agriculture,
  education, culture, public services
- Professionals accessing remote international AI jobs
- Growing niche in NLP for local languages (translation, chatbots)
- Skills and employment across AI value chain
- Potential to become AI secretariat of Africa, similar to AfCFTA

THREATS:
- Lack of coordination and investment in implementing the National AI Strategy
- Low accessibility and inclusion in AI adoption
- Risk of failures and discrimination from AI models trained on foreign data
- Loss of data privacy, security and public trust
- Lack of coordination in existing policies
- Potential for job loss due to automation
- Brain drain of AI talent
- Limited long-term sustainability of donor-financed initiatives

DRIVING IMPLEMENTATION: GHANA'S RESPONSIBLE AI AUTHORITY
================================================================================

The Responsible AI Authority (RAI Authority) will be established in the first year to
spearhead implementation of the Ghana National AI Strategy. Its mandate includes:

1. Develop an agenda for legislative enablement, cross-sectoral regulatory alignment,
   and cross-agency operational standardisation
2. Coordinate across institutions to implement the National AI Strategy
3. Lead quarterly monitoring, measurement, and iteration of the strategy
4. Conduct deeper analyses for market sizing opportunities for AI across sectors
5. Act on context-relevant studies (UNESCO RAM, Ghana AI Readiness Index)
6. Actively participate in global AI governance platforms (UNESCO, GPAI, OECD AIGO)

The RAI Authority will be evaluated over three years with the goal of transforming it into
a full commission. It is modelled on the Singapore National AI Office (NAIO), Egypt
National AI Council, and the United Kingdom's Office for AI.

To support its launch, the Data Protection Commission may act as a nurturing enclave
and incubator to form the institution.


ACTION PLAN
================================================================================

PILLAR 1: EXPAND AI EDUCATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommendations:
1. Conduct an annual skills gap assessment
   - Baseline study for current AI talent needs and availability
   - Framework for 5-yearly baseline assessment and adjustment
   - Consultations with industry associations to build database of skill demands

2. Launch the "AI Ready Ghana" program
   - Train over 1,000,000 AI-ready youth by 2033
   - Ensure at least 10,000 mid-level to senior AI researchers by 2033
   - Update STEM and IT curricula in secondary education
   - Incorporate basics of data science and coding in primary education
   - Include training on AI ethics as core part of the programme
   - Expand beyond data protection to cover UNESCO Recommendation dimensions

3. Expand education courses in AI
   - Coordinate among universities to create a database of existing courses
   - Plan rollout of additional modules, courses or degree programs in AI
   - Develop plan to attract lecturers and research programs in AI

4. Promote training courses for teachers

5. Include AI for the disabled, public, and informal sector

6. Strengthen practical and inclusive AI training
   - Mandatory internships, fellowships, and capstone projects
   - Annual "AI for Ghana Challenge"
   - Industry-academia advisory boards
   - "Women in AI" initiative with scholarships, mentorship, and venture support
   - 40% gender participation target for AI fellowships and technical training
   - Training for media personnel on responsible AI communication
   - Training for the Judiciary on AI (UNESCO Global Toolkit on AI and Rule of Law)

Lead actors: MoCDTI, Ministry of Education, Ghana Tertiary Education Council, RAI
Authority, AIMS, KNUST, Universities
Timeline: 2025


PILLAR 2: EMPOWER YOUTH FOR JOBS OF THE FUTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommendations:
1. Upskill youth, especially in marginalized areas
   - AI and digital skills training through rural tech hubs, mobile labs, TVET centres
   - Scholarships, stipends, and free access to AI learning materials
   - Set up regional AI learning centres

2. Facilitate remote AI jobs or internships

3. Tax incentives and support for youth AI start-ups
   - Import duty exemptions for hardware (compute clusters)
   - Dedicated AI innovation funds for youth-led start-ups
   - Seed funding, grants, and low-interest loans

4. Develop AI Fellowship programs

5. Support continuous training and short courses

6. Repatriate skilled African AI professionals
   - Incentives for diaspora professionals to return or contribute remotely
   - Visiting researchers and teaching fellowships
   - Relocation packages and research grants

7. Create new civil service professional grades for Data Protection and Cybersecurity
   Officers

8. Future-proof Ghana's workforce through reskilling
   - National AI Reskilling Fund for workers impacted by automation
   - Embed AI and digital literacy in TVET programs

Lead actors: Responsible AI Authority, universities, MoCDTI, Ministry of Employment
Timeline: 2025


PILLAR 3: DEEPEN DIGITAL INFRASTRUCTURE & INCLUSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommendations:
1. Review and identify gaps in implementation of existing digital policies
   - Harmonise existing policies (ICT4AD, e-Transform, Ghana Digital Economy Policy)
   - Increase Ghana's compute cluster capacity to 10^21 FLOPS by 2027
   - Increased by 0.5 OOM every year to 10^25 FLOPS total capacity by 2035
   - Drastically increase Ghana's energy capacity 0.5 to 1 OOM per year

2. Initiate partnerships with world-class cloud computing providers
   - Affordable access for AI start-ups (NVIDIA, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, AWS)

3. Orchestrate market dynamics that incentivise AI entrepreneurs

4. Build innovation hubs outside Accra

5. Invest in internet, data accessibility, and talent
   - Expand broadband to underserved and rural areas

6. Use local languages to structure datasets
   - Fund initiatives to collect, transcribe, and label datasets in local languages

7. Strengthen digital infrastructure for AI and advanced technologies

8. Expand digital infrastructure and energy capacity
   - Accelerate 5G licensing and rural connectivity
   - Solar-powered edge data centres
   - Compute-as-a-Utility model for affordable GPU/TPU access

Lead actors: MoCDTI, Ghana Chamber of Telecommunications, GI-KACE, Data Protection
Commission, Ministry of Energy
Timeline: 2025-2035


PILLAR 4: FACILITATE DATA ACCESS & GOVERNANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommendations:
1. Disseminate and drive enforcement of existing data sharing & governance policies
   - Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843); Right to Information Act 2019; Cybersecurity
     Act 2020; National Data Sharing Policy; Electronic Transaction Act

2. Clarify data privacy and data sharing agreements and regulations for partnerships

3. Disseminate guidance on trustworthy, safe, secure and ethical AI practices
   - Leverage OECD AI Principles, UNESCO Recommendation on Ethics of AI
   - Share free self-assessment tools (EU AI Trust Label, Singapore ISAGO)

4. Apply to international and regional AI governance platforms
   - OECD AIGO, GPAI, UNESCO, ITU, Smart Africa, African Union

5. Rollout of the Ghana Open Data Initiative (GODI), Ghana Data Exchange Hub
   - By 2030: 1 trillion tokens worth of Ghanaian datasets
   - 20% publicly available; 80% accessible to Ghanaians with Ghana card verification

6. Develop national data classification and sovereignty framework
   - Open data: agricultural and meteorological data
   - Restricted: health, financial and national security data

7. Create frameworks for responsible data monetization

8. Ensure well-regulated data processing in government bids

9. Transform the Data Protection Commission into a Data Protection Authority with
   regional offices in all 16 regions

10. Improve data access, sovereignty, and transparency
    - Mandatory transparency reports for all public-sector AI systems
    - Bias Mitigation Fund for testing models with diverse Ghana-relevant datasets

Lead actors: MoCDTI, Data Protection Commission (DPC), Cyber Security Authority, NITA
Timeline: 2025


PILLAR 5: COORDINATE A ROBUST AI COMMUNITY ECOSYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommendations:
1. Build & convene an online and in-person inclusive AI community
   - Live online repository of AI communities (Artificial Intelligence Association of Ghana,
     Ghana NLP, Google Developers, Python Ladies in Accra, Deep Learning Indaba,
     Ghana Tech Lab, Hacklab Foundation)

2. Expand and establish AI innovation hubs across the country
   - Especially in marginalised communities

3. Collaborate with traditional institutions
   - Engage chieftaincies, religious bodies, community leadership
   - Integrate traditional knowledge systems and indigenous languages

4. Define multi-stakeholder collaboration frameworks
   - National AI Multi-Stakeholder Forum
   - Create the Ghanaian Global AI Summit
     - Target: 80 billion Ghanaian cedis in investment in Ghanaian AI companies by 2030
     - Target: 1,000 strategic partnerships by 2030

5. Implement targeted strategies to reduce Urban-Rural Digital divide

6. Reverse brain-drain and leverage global expertise

7. Institutionalize multi-stakeholder collaboration

Lead actors: Responsible AI Authority, MEST, National House of Chiefs
Timeline: 2025


PILLAR 6: ACCELERATE AI ADOPTION ACROSS SECTORS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommendations:
1. Launch 5 AI pilot projects from across key sectors
   - Healthcare, transportation, agriculture, financial services, public administration,
     culture and environment

2. Review and clarify laws for copyright, patents, and intellectual property

3. Implement incentives for AI start-ups (tax breaks for R&D or employment)

4. Support the creation of a Ghanaian private AGI lab
   - Competing globally (comparable to DeepSeek/OpenAI-level)
   - Access to more than 10^10 compute; trains frontier foundation models
   - More than 10 billion Ghanaian cedis in mixed funding
   - Ensures Ghana's leadership as the AI field evolves into the AGI field

5. Support the creation of 10 Ghanaian AI unicorns by 2035
   - Companies valued over a billion dollars

6. Promote context-aware AI adoption through targeted incubation programs

Lead actors: Responsible AI Authority, MoCDTI, MEST, sectoral ministries
Timeline: 2025


PILLAR 7: INVEST IN APPLIED AI RESEARCH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommendations:
1. Establish a Special Research Initiative for climate-smart agriculture and forestry

2. Establish a Natural Language Processing (NLP) Centre of Excellence (CoE)
   - Expand Ghanaian language dictionary
   - Develop translating chips using AI for local language translation
   - Build legal tech exploring NLP for legal advice in Ghanaian languages
   - Coordinate with Ghana NLP, KNUST, Google

3. Develop a framework for a National Deep Science Institute
   - Research in deep tech, cognitive tech, ML & AI
   - Develop capabilities in general purpose AI
   - Develop context-relevant human-AI collaboration

4. Support the digitisation of oral histories

5. Call for AI sandboxing environments

6. A more producer-led approach to AI development (vs consumer-led)

7-8. Set up Ghana's National AI Fund
   - 5 billion Ghanaian cedis 5-year fund (2025-2030)
   - Scaling to 15 billion 5-year fund (2030-2035)
   - 200 billion cedis in FDI and local private sector investment by 2035
   - Use royalties from extractive industries (mining, oil) via National Research Fund

9. Position GI-KACE as Ghana's central hub for AI and digital innovation research

10. Support applied AI research through financial incentives and IP protection

Lead actors: MEST, CSIR-INSTI, GI-KACE, Universities (KNUST RAIL, AIMS), MoCDTI
Timeline: 2025


PILLAR 8: PROMOTE AI ADOPTION IN THE PUBLIC SECTOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommendations:
1. Develop a program to increase access for digital & AI-focused start-ups to public
   procurement processes

2. Evolve public sector mindset to view algorithms as IP; co-create IP with vendors

3. Establish a program to train civil & public servants on AI projects in public services

4. Incentivise an AI culture with a reward system for service delivery excellence using AI

5. Develop a comprehensive public administration data dashboard/interface

6. Initiate a programme to identify critical public service AI use-cases
   - Virtual agents for citizen services (taxes, licences, identification)
   - AI for public infrastructure maintenance (pothole detection from bus video feeds)

7. Develop a workflow and operational framework for public sector agility & data-driven
   decision making

8. Create GhanaChat
   - Large language model trained with data from various government agencies
   - Used privately by government to improve productivity
   - Prevents transmission of confidential information to foreign AI systems (security risk)

9. Mandate adoption of GhanaChat and other locally created AI solutions across all
   government agencies
   - Use cases: tax filing, complaint management, policy education, regulatory compliance,
     permit/license/benefit applications

10. Establish a National AI Office under MoCDTI for AI policy and inter-ministerial
    coordination

11. Create dedicated Data Protection and Cybersecurity Officer grades in civil service

12. Integrate AI use case implementation into performance assessments for ministries

13. Strengthen public sector AI adoption through ethical guidelines and startup-friendly
    policies
    - Require all government agencies using AI to publish algorithmic accountability
      guidelines

Lead actors: MoCDTI, NITA, GI-KACE, Ghana Digital Centres Limited, Public Procurement
Authority, Office of the President
Timeline: 2025


CONCLUSION
================================================================================

The Republic of Ghana stands at the face of a profound technological revolution. The
National Artificial Intelligence Strategy 2025-2035 sets forth a bold roadmap to realize
our vision of "Ghana 2035: The AI-Powered Society". By harnessing artificial intelligence as
a general-purpose technology, we are committed to driving inclusive economic growth,
redefining the delivery of public services, and improving the quality of life for all people in
Ghana.

The transformation of Ghana into an AI-powered society requires a whole-of-society
approach uniting start-ups, academic institutions, private sector industries, civil society,
traditional leaders, and international development partners.

Key financial projections:
- National AI Fund: 5 billion cedis (2025-2030), scaling to 15 billion cedis (2030-2035)
- FDI and private investment target: 200 billion cedis by 2035
- GDP contribution: 200 billion cedis by 2030; 500 billion cedis by 2035
- Ghanaian Global AI Summit investment target: 80 billion cedis by 2030

Key institutional targets:
- Responsible AI Authority established in Year 1
- 1,000,000 AI-ready youth trained by 2033
- 10,000 mid-level to senior AI researchers by 2033
- 10 Ghanaian AI unicorns by 2035
- 1 private Ghanaian AGI lab competing globally
- 1 trillion tokens of Ghanaian datasets by 2030
- NLP Centre of Excellence established
- National Deep Science Institute established
- Data Protection Commission upgraded to Authority with regional offices
