# Australia's Artificial Intelligence Action Plan 2021 — National AI Strategy

**Department of Industry, Science, Energy and Resources (DISER)**
*Published June 2021 by the Australian Government.*

---

## Overview

Australia's Artificial Intelligence Action Plan 2021 is the Australian Government's national strategy for maximising the economic and social benefits of AI while ensuring its responsible and ethical use. Published by the Department of Industry, Science, Energy and Resources (DISER, now DISR — Department of Industry, Science and Resources) in June 2021, it replaced the earlier "AI Technology Roadmap" consultations and established a government-wide framework for AI investment, adoption, and governance.

The Action Plan is a policy document, not legislation. It sets out priorities and government commitments rather than legally binding obligations on the private sector. Its primary audiences are government agencies (which are expected to lead by example in responsible AI adoption), Australian businesses (particularly SMEs), and the research community.

The Action Plan was developed as part of a broader package including the Australian Government's responses to public consultation on AI ethics (leading to Australia's AI Ethics Principles, 2019) and international commitments including the OECD AI Principles and the Global Partnership on AI (GPAI). It is superseded in practical terms by subsequent Australian AI policy documents including the Safe and Responsible AI in Australia consultation (2023) and the Voluntary AI Safety Standard (September 2024), though the 2021 strategy's three-pillar structure continues to shape Australian AI policy.

## Three Strategic Pillars

### Pillar 1 — Getting AI Foundations Right

This pillar addresses the preconditions for AI adoption: data, infrastructure, and trust.

**Data availability and quality**:
- Government commitment to release high-value public datasets for AI research and development, particularly through data.gov.au
- Support for development of industry data standards enabling interoperability and data sharing
- Investment in data infrastructure including the Australian Research Data Commons (ARDC)

**Digital infrastructure**:
- Investment in compute infrastructure for AI research (National Computational Infrastructure — NCI)
- High-speed internet and connectivity infrastructure as a prerequisite for AI deployment
- Government cloud strategy enabling secure AI deployment in public sector contexts

**Trust and governance**:
- Government adoption of the AI Ethics Principles (2019) in all government AI development and procurement — 8 voluntary principles including human-centred values, fairness, privacy protection, reliability and safety, transparency and explainability, contestability, and accountability
- Encouragement of private sector adoption of the AI Ethics Principles
- Development of technical standards for trustworthy AI in collaboration with Standards Australia and ISO/IEC

**Skills and talent**:
- Investment in AI literacy programs for workers
- Support for university AI research and education
- Skilled migration pathways for AI talent (Global Talent Program)

---

### Pillar 2 — Growing Australia's AI Capability

This pillar addresses Australia's competitive position in AI development and deployment.

**Research investment**:
- Ongoing support for AI research through the Australian Research Council (ARC) and CSIRO's Data61 (the primary national AI research body)
- Establishment of the National AI Centre (NAIC) at CSIRO Data61 to coordinate national AI capability development and provide business advisory services
- Investment in applied AI research cooperating with industry through the Cooperative Research Centres (CRC) Program

**Business adoption**:
- The AI Technology Roadmap and associated grants programs targeting SME adoption of AI
- The Digital Solutions program providing advisory services to small businesses on digital and AI adoption
- Export Finance Australia support for AI-capable businesses seeking international markets

**Government as a lead adopter**:
- Federal government agencies are expected to adopt AI solutions in service delivery and operations, demonstrating responsible AI in practice
- Whole-of-government AI adoption strategy coordinated by the Digital Transformation Agency (DTA)
- Government AI procurement requirements referencing AI Ethics Principles

**International engagement**:
- Active participation in OECD AI Policy Observatory
- Founding member of the Global Partnership on AI (GPAI)
- Bilateral AI cooperation agreements (with the US, UK, Singapore, and Japan)
- Standards development: Australia contributes to ISO/IEC JTC1/SC42 (AI standardisation)

---

### Pillar 3 — AI for the Greater Good

This pillar focuses on applying AI to national challenges and ensuring broad societal benefit.

**Government service delivery**:
- AI applications in health (medical imaging diagnostics, patient flow optimisation)
- AI in environmental monitoring (bushfire detection, agricultural yield prediction, Great Barrier Reef monitoring)
- AI in social services (automating administrative processes to free caseworker time)

**Health and wellbeing**:
- Investment in the Australian Centre for Artificial Intelligence in Medical Imaging (ACAIMI) — deploying AI diagnostics at scale across radiology
- COVID-19 response applications of AI for contact tracing, resource allocation, and vaccine rollout logistics

**Environment and agriculture**:
- AgTech AI applications including precision agriculture, soil health monitoring, and supply chain optimisation
- Environmental AI: satellite imagery analysis, biosecurity surveillance, climate modelling

**Regional and rural Australia**:
- Specific focus on ensuring AI benefits reach regional, rural, and remote communities
- AI-enabled healthcare and education delivery in areas with limited physical access

**Inclusion and equity**:
- Government commitment to ensuring AI does not entrench existing inequalities
- Specific programs targeting First Nations communities and ensuring AI development is conducted with, not for, First Nations peoples
- Alignment with the Closing the Gap strategy for AI-relevant programs

## AI Ethics Principles (Cross-Cutting Framework)

The Action Plan reinforces Australia's voluntary AI Ethics Principles, originally released in 2019. These are:

1. **Human, societal, and environmental wellbeing** — AI systems should benefit individuals, society, and the environment
2. **Human-centred values** — AI systems should respect human rights, diversity, and autonomy
3. **Fairness** — AI systems should be inclusive and accessible, and should not involve or result in unfair discrimination
4. **Privacy protection and security** — AI systems should respect and uphold privacy rights and data protection, and ensure data security
5. **Reliability and safety** — AI systems should reliably operate in accordance with their intended purpose throughout their lifecycle
6. **Transparency and explainability** — There should be transparency about AI systems and the ability to explain AI systems and their outcomes
7. **Contestability** — Where an AI system significantly impacts a person, there should be a mechanism to contest the AI system and its impacts
8. **Accountability** — Those responsible for AI systems should be identifiable and accountable for the outcomes of those systems

The Action Plan commits the Australian Government to applying these principles in all government AI procurement and development, and encourages (but does not require) private sector adoption.

## Governance and Accountability Mechanisms

**Inter-agency coordination**:
- The National AI Centre (NAIC) at CSIRO Data61 serves as the operational hub
- The Digital Transformation Agency (DTA) coordinates government AI adoption
- DISER (now DISR) retains overall policy coordination responsibility

**International alignment**:
- Australia's AI governance approach is explicitly designed to be consistent with OECD AI Principles and compatible with international trading partners' frameworks
- The Action Plan references the G20 AI Principles (2019) as a baseline

**Review and monitoring**:
- Government committed to monitoring AI-related developments and updating policy as the technology evolves
- No specific review timeline or accountability mechanism is specified in the Action Plan itself

## Subsequent Developments and Supersession

The 2021 Action Plan has been substantially developed and partially superseded by subsequent Australian AI policy:

| Date | Development |
|------|------------|
| June 2021 | Action Plan published |
| 2022 | ARC Centre of Excellence for Automated Decision-Making & Society (ADM+S) established |
| June 2023 | Safe and Responsible AI in Australia consultation paper published |
| November 2023 | Government response to AI consultation: commitment to voluntary guardrails |
| September 2024 | Voluntary AI Safety Standard — 10 Mandatory Guardrails published (the primary operational AI governance document as of 2026) |
| 2024–2026 | High-risk AI mandatory regime under development (legislative drafting ongoing as of May 2026) |

## Limitations and Criticisms

- **High-level strategy**: The Action Plan sets aspirational goals rather than specific, measurable commitments
- **Non-binding**: No obligations on the private sector; AI Ethics Principles are voluntary
- **No enforcement mechanism**: No regulator is given specific AI governance powers by the Action Plan
- **Pre-generative AI**: Published before ChatGPT and the generative AI wave; does not address large language models, foundation models, or generative AI risks
- **Gaps on high-risk AI**: No mandatory requirements for high-risk AI use cases (e.g., facial recognition, automated welfare decisions)
- **Superseded**: The Voluntary AI Safety Standard (2024) and the forthcoming mandatory AI regime are the operational policy instruments; the 2021 Action Plan is primarily of historical/contextual significance
