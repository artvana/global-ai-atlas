 AN ACT to amend the general business law, in relation to improving safety during third-party food deliveries by bicycles with electric assist or electric scooter 
 
 
 
 
 The People of the State of New York, represented in Senate and Assembly, do enact as follows: 
 
 
 Section 1. This act shall be known and may be cited as the &quot;safe delivery act&quot;. 
 Section 2. The general business law is amended by adding a new section 391-x to read as follows: 
 
 Section 391-x. Third-party food delivery applications. 1. For the purposes of this section, the following terms shall have the following meanings: 
 
 
 (a) &quot;Food service establishment&quot; means a place where food is provided for individual portion service directly to the consumer whether such food is provided free of charge or sold, and whether consumption occurs on or off the premises or is provided from a pushcart, stand or vehicle. 
 
 
 (b) &quot;Third-party food delivery service&quot; means any website, mobile application or other internet service that offers or arranges for the sale or delivery of food and beverages prepared by, and the same-day delivery or same-day pickup of food and beverage from, a food service establishment located in the state. 
 
 
 (c) &quot;Third-party food delivery platform&quot; means the online or mobile platform of the third-party food delivery service on which a consumer can view products available for sale and place an order for a food service establishment&#39;s products. 
 
 
 (d) &quot;Third-party delivery worker&quot; means a person who directly delivers products from a food service establishment through a third-party food delivery platform. 
 
 
 2. No third-party food delivery platform shall use or employ algorithms that have the effect of promoting delivery of products through such platform by a third-party delivery worker in a timeframe that cannot be achieved using a bicycle with electric assist or electric scooter, as defined in the vehicle and traffic law, traveling at a speed greater than fifteen miles per hour and/or operating while observing state and local traffic laws. This prohibition shall include, but is not limited to, any monetary or non-monetary incentive for shorter delivery or pickup times, or any monetary or non-monetary penalty for not completing a delivery or pickup in a time less than one hour more than can be achieved using a bicycle with electric assist or electric scooter traveling at a speed greater than fifteen miles per hour and operating while observing state and local traffic laws. 
 
 
 3. No third-party food delivery platform shall penalize a third-party delivery worker for violating applicable traffic laws during a delivery. No platform shall penalize a third-party delivery worker more than five dollars in any one day for a traffic violation, and such violation must be documented by a traffic enforcement camera. 
 
 
 4. The chief information officer, the attorney general, and a representative of the mayor of a city with a population of one million or more shall have the authority to audit algorithms of third-party delivery platforms which provide services in the state for violations of this section. 
 
 
 5. A violation of this section shall be subject to a fine of no more than two hundred fifty thousand dollars for a first violation and no more than five hundred thousand dollars for each subsequent violation. 
 
 Section 3. This act shall take effect on the sixtieth day after it shall have become a law.
