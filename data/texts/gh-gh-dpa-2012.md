# Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843) — Ghana
**Act 843 of 2012**
*Ghana — July 16, 2012*

---

## Overview

Ghana's Data Protection Act 2012 (Act 843) is one of sub-Saharan Africa's earliest comprehensive personal data protection frameworks. It implements the constitutional right to privacy under Article 18(2) of the 1992 Constitution and establishes the Data Protection Commission (DPC) as the independent supervisory and enforcement authority. The Act applies to all data controllers who process personal data in or from Ghana, making Ghana an early regional leader in data protection regulation. A distinctive feature is the mandatory registration requirement for data controllers and data processors — an approach that differs from more modern frameworks that have moved away from registration.

---

## Key Provisions

### Data Protection Commission
- Establishes an independent Data Protection Commission with a board appointed by the President
- The DPC registers data controllers and processors, investigates complaints, issues compliance notices, and may bring criminal prosecutions
- DPC maintains a public register of data controllers and processors
- DPC may conduct audits and inspections of organizations processing personal data

### Eight Data Protection Principles
- Accountability: data controller is responsible for compliance
- Lawfulness: processing must have a lawful basis
- Specification: purposes must be specified and legitimate
- Compatibility: processing limited to specified purposes
- Quality: data must be accurate and kept up to date
- Openness: data subjects must be informed of processing
- Data security: appropriate technical and organizational measures required
- Data subject participation: data subjects have rights to access and correct their data

### Mandatory Registration
- All data controllers must register with the DPC before processing personal data
- Registration requires disclosure of purposes, data categories, data subjects, and recipients
- Failure to register is a criminal offense

### Data Subject Rights
- Right of access to personal data held by a data controller
- Right to correction of inaccurate data
- Right to object to processing that causes substantial damage or distress
- Data subjects may lodge complaints with the DPC

### Sensitive Personal Data
- Special categories requiring heightened protection: health, financial, biometric, genetic, criminal record, and other sensitive categories
- Processing of sensitive data requires explicit consent or specific legal justification

### Cross-Border Transfers
- Transfers of personal data outside Ghana are restricted unless the destination country provides adequate protection
- DPC may approve transfers on a case-by-case basis or through approved mechanisms

### Data Controller Obligations
- Implement appropriate security measures
- Appoint a responsible person for data protection compliance
- Notify the DPC of significant data breaches
- Comply with DPC compliance notices and enforcement notices

## Enforcement & Penalties

- The DPC may issue compliance notices requiring remediation within a specified period
- The DPC may issue enforcement notices requiring specific steps to comply with the Act
- Criminal penalties: fines up to GHS 60,000 (approximately $4,000 USD) and/or up to 2 years imprisonment for serious violations including unlawful processing and failure to register
- The DPC may refer cases to the Attorney General for prosecution
- Civil liability: individuals harmed by breaches may seek compensation through the courts
