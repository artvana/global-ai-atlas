# Connecticut Public Act 25-113 — An Act Concerning Artificial Intelligence

**Public Act No. 25-113 (SB 2)**
*Connecticut General Assembly — 2025 Session*
*Effective: October 1, 2025*

---

## Overview

Connecticut Public Act 25-113 amends existing Connecticut law to require certain disclosures regarding artificial intelligence systems, with a particular focus on large language model (LLM) training data transparency. The Act establishes non-binding disclosure frameworks applicable to developers and deployers of AI systems sold or offered in Connecticut.

---

## Key Provisions

### Training Data Disclosure (Section 1)

- Developers of large language models and generative AI systems that are sold or offered for use in Connecticut must make available, upon request, a general description of the categories of data used to train the model
- Disclosure must include whether the training data included copyrighted works, personal data, or data from Connecticut residents
- Disclosures are required to be published on the developer's website or provided in product documentation

### Consumer-Facing Disclosures (Section 2)

- Deployers of AI-generated content tools must disclose to end users when content has been substantially generated by AI
- Disclosure requirement applies to text, images, audio, and video generated primarily by AI systems
- Exempts news articles and factual reporting that have been independently verified

### Exemptions

- Research and development use that does not involve public-facing deployment
- Internal business use not involving consumers
- Open-source models with publicly available model cards meeting equivalent disclosure requirements

---

## Enforcement

- Connecticut Attorney General has enforcement authority
- No private right of action created
- Civil penalties not to exceed $5,000 per violation
- Non-binding provisions: guidance and voluntary compliance framework

---

## Notes

This Act is characterized as establishing a voluntary/non-binding framework for AI disclosures in Connecticut, pending development of more comprehensive AI regulation at the state or federal level.

*Effective: October 1, 2025*
