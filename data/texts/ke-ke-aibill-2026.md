# The Artificial Intelligence Bill, 2026

**Senate Bills No. 4 of 2026 — Parliament of Kenya, The Senate**
*Sponsor: Sen. Karen Nyamu. Published 19 February 2026. First Reading 2 April 2026. Ordinary Bill.*

---

## Overview

The principal object of the Bill is to regulate and govern artificial intelligence in Kenya, ensuring ethical, transparent and accountable use while fostering innovation and safeguarding human rights, data protection and public welfare. It establishes the Office of the Artificial Intelligence Commissioner.

The stated purposes are to provide a regulatory framework for AI in Kenya; ensure ethical and transparent AI use; support and foster innovation; safeguard human rights and data protection of the public; establish the Office of the Artificial Intelligence Commissioner; promote AI literacy and advise county governments on integrating AI in devolved sectors; and align Kenya with international AI standards.

## Definition of artificial intelligence

AI is defined as a machine-based system or technology using technologies that leverage machine learning, data processing, algorithmic systems, or other methods to operate with varying levels of autonomy and adaptiveness, inferring outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations or decisions from inputs, and includes systems or technologies that perform tasks typically requiring human intelligence, such as automated decision-making, language processing and computer vision.

## The Office of the Artificial Intelligence Commissioner

The Bill establishes the Office of the Artificial Intelligence Commissioner as a state office under Article 260(q) of the Constitution, independent in its functions, with perpetual succession and the capacity to sue and be sued.

**Appointment.** The Public Service Commission initiates recruitment, inviting applications within seven days of being notified of a vacancy; within twenty-one days of receiving applications it shortlists, publicises names, interviews transparently, and nominates three qualified applicants in order of merit to the President. The President nominates and, with the approval of Parliament, appoints the Commissioner.

**Qualifications.** A master's degree in artificial intelligence, computer science, ICT, engineering, data science, law, ethics or a related field; experience in governance, data protection, technology policy or regulatory oversight, ethics, human rights and risk management; and compliance with Chapter Six of the Constitution.

**Term.** Five years, renewable once for a further five years.

**Removal.** The office falls vacant on death, written resignation, or conviction and sentence to imprisonment exceeding six months without the option of a fine. The President may remove the Commissioner on the Cabinet Secretary's recommendation for serious violation of the Constitution or any other law, gross misconduct, physical or mental incapacity, incompetence, or bankruptcy. Members of the public may petition the Public Service Commission for removal; the Commissioner must be told the reasons and given an opportunity to respond before any action.

**Powers.** Inspect premises, systems and records; require information from AI providers; issue enforcement notices or impose fines; summon persons to give evidence; collaborate with international bodies; establish an appeals mechanism for its own decisions; and delegate powers to authorised officers or to a regulator established by an Act of Parliament.

**Functions** include registering and overseeing the development, deployment and use of AI systems in Kenya; establishing and managing regulatory sandboxes; receiving and investigating AI-related complaints; maintaining a public register of high-risk AI systems, including those used by county governments; advising national and county governments on policy integration of AI in devolved functions; promoting AI literacy and capacity building; conducting research and monitoring trends; and promoting equitable access to AI infrastructure and benefits.

## Advisory Committee on Artificial Intelligence

A multi-stakeholder committee chaired by the Commissioner, comprising representatives of the Ministry of ICT, the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner, NACOSTI, two persons with expertise in AI ethics and human rights appointed by the Cabinet Secretary (three-year terms, renewable once), two persons of each gender nominated by the Council of Governors, and one civil society representative with technology or human rights expertise. It advises on emerging trends, risks and opportunities; reviews proposed guidelines, standards and regulations; facilitates stakeholder engagement; promotes multidisciplinary research on AI governance; and advises on workforce re-skilling and transition strategies. It meets at least four times per financial year.

## Risk classification

The Commissioner classifies AI systems according to the level of risk they pose to health, safety, fundamental rights, the environment or societal welfare:

- **Unacceptable risk** — systems posing severe threats
- **High risk** — systems used in critical sectors including healthcare, education, agriculture, finance, security, employment or public administration
- **Limited risk** — systems with moderate risks
- **Minimal risk** — systems with negligible risks

Classifications are updated periodically through recommendations to the Cabinet Secretary, having regard to international standards.

## Obligations for high-risk AI systems

Providers and deployers of high-risk AI must:

- conduct risk and human rights impact assessments before deployment and implement mitigation measures;
- ensure transparency, traceability and explainability of the system's decision-making processes;
- keep dataset and system documentation;
- comply with the Data Protection Act;
- ensure robustness and cybersecurity; and
- obtain consent when using a person's likeness or voice.

The Commissioner reviews assessments for public-sector systems, prescribes assessment guidelines, and prescribes guidelines on risk management, security protocols, bias detection and ethical standards for high-risk AI. The Commissioner maintains a public register of high-risk systems, including those used by county governments.

## Transparency

AI providers and deployers must disclose the nature and purpose of the system, how much automation is involved, and measures taken to mitigate bias. Users must ensure human intervention rights where automated decisions affect individuals.

## Regulatory sandboxes

Controlled environments created by the Commissioner for testing AI systems under regulatory supervision to promote safe innovation, with priority given to AI solutions addressing national needs.

## Ethical use

The Commissioner must establish ethical guidelines addressing bias, discrimination and inclusion; privacy and human dignity; human oversight and accountability; environmental sustainability; equitable access to benefits; and preventing harmful synthetic media and misuse of personal likeness to misinform or harm reputation.

## AI literacy

The Commissioner must roll out nationwide AI literacy programmes at national and county level, including through schools, ICT hubs and county partnerships.

## Human-centric AI

Designers and deployers must ensure AI systems enhance rather than replace human capabilities, incorporate features supporting human involvement, and provide human oversight in critical decisions — including outputs where decisions may affect human rights, safety or societal well-being. The Cabinet Secretary is to prescribe by regulation how systems enhance human capabilities, the features supporting human involvement, which critical decisions require human oversight, and review mechanisms for intervention or override.

## Workforce impact obligations

Where AI impacts employment, providers must conduct workforce impact assessments including an assessment of potential job displacement, and implement mitigation measures including re-skilling programmes in collaboration with national and county government agencies. The Commissioner is to develop workforce transition guidelines, including vocational training partnerships and incentives for job-creating AI adoption.

## Public sector

All public entities, including counties, must comply with the Act when using AI systems.

## Offences and penalties

A person commits an offence who:

(a) deploys or operates an AI system classified as unacceptable risk under section 25, except as prescribed by regulations;
(b) deploys a high-risk AI system without conducting the required risk assessment or implementing prescribed mitigation measures (section 26);
(c) fails to comply with disclosure or transparency obligations under section 28;
(d) participates in a regulatory sandbox under section 29 without adhering to prescribed conditions;
(e) fails to conduct a workforce impact assessment under section 32;
(f) contravenes ethical guidelines published under the Act, resulting in bias, discrimination or harm to individuals;
(g) uses an AI system in the public sector contrary to the Act, causing prejudice to public benefit or rights;
(h) obstructs the Office in the performance of its functions, including by providing false information or failing to submit required reports; or
(i) generates, deploys or distributes AI-generated content, including synthetic media, using a person's image, voice or likeness without their explicit consent, where such content causes or is likely to cause harm, misinformation, defamation or infringement of privacy.

**Penalties.** For offences (a), (b), (d), (e), (g) or (i): a fine not exceeding KES 5,000,000 or imprisonment not exceeding two years, or both. For offences (c), (f) or (h): a fine not exceeding KES 1,000,000 or imprisonment not exceeding six months, or both. Where the offence is committed by a body corporate, every director or officer who knew of the commission of the offence and did not exercise due diligence to ensure compliance is guilty of the offence.

## Subsidiary legislation

The Cabinet Secretary, in consultation with the Commissioner, is to make regulations on risk classification criteria and procedures; forms, processes and timelines for risk assessments, human rights impact assessments and conformity audits for high-risk AI; sandbox conditions, eligibility, monitoring and exit strategies; the content and review of ethical guidelines, transparency disclosures and compliance reports; data governance frameworks including secure data sharing, anonymisation, dataset diversity and localisation; enforcement, investigation and appeals procedures; public participation and AI literacy programmes and collaboration with county governments; fees, forms and administrative procedures; procedures for obtaining consent, labelling AI-generated content and reporting non-consensual synthetic media; and exceptions for legitimate uses of image manipulation with safeguards against abuse.

## Review

Every three years the Cabinet Secretary must review the Act — consulting the Commissioner, the Advisory Committee, stakeholders and the public, assessing emerging risks, opportunities and international best practices — and within six months of completing the review submit a report with recommendations to Parliament.

## Status

Read a First Time in the Senate on 2 April 2026 and committed to the Senate Standing Committee on Information, Communication and Technology. Under Senate Standing Order 148 the Committee was required to report within thirty calendar days of committal, that is by 2 May 2026; public memoranda were invited to the Office of the Clerk of the Senate and closed 5 May 2026. As of 27 July 2026 no committee report has been tabled and the Bill has not reached Second Reading.

Because the Bill concerns county governments, it must also be considered by the National Assembly before it can be presented for presidential assent.
