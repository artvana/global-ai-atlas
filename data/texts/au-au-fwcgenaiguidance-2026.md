Guidance note
Use of generative artificial intelligence in
Commission cases
Introduction
1. 	This Guidance Note explains what you must do if you use generative artificial
intelligence (GenAI) to help you prepare an application to the Fair Work
Commission or any other document to be lodged in a Commission case.
2. 	The Fair Work Commission’s Artificial intelligence transparency statement
outlines how the Commission itself may use artificial intelligence (AI).
3. 	Because GenAI is developing at a rapid pace, this Guidance Note will be amended
from time to time. You are responsible for keeping up to date on its requirements.
4. 	In this Guidance Note the Commission, we and us means the Fair Work
Commission. Other definitions are at the end of this Guidance Note.
Commencement date
5. 	This Guidance Note commences on 20 October 2026.
Application
6. 	This Guidance Note applies when you use GenAI to help you create, write, edit,
modify or otherwise prepare an application to the Commission or any other
document to be lodged in a Commission case.
7. 	In this Guidance Note ‘use GenAI’ does not include using GenAI only to check
spelling or grammar in a document or to format a document.
Requirements when you use GenAI to help you prepare a document
Requirement 1
8. 	You must state in the document that GenAI was used and how you used it.
Requirement 2
9. 	You must check the document and make any necessary changes so that all
details in the document are correct and relevant to the case. You must also
state in the document that you have done this.

10. In checking the document you must make sure that:
• 	all references to facts and evidence in the document are correct and the facts
and evidence exist
• 	all cases, legislation, textbooks and articles referred to in the document exist
and support the legal positions you say they support, and
• 	all extracts or quotes in the document are correct and come from the sources
you say they come from.
11. We recommend that you also include hyperlinks to all cases you refer to in the
document.
Requirement 3
12. If the document is your witness statement or declaration then you must check
the document and make any necessary changes so that the statement or
declaration is based on your own knowledge, reflects your own words and is
true to the best of your knowledge.
13. You must also state in the document that the statement or declaration is based
on your own knowledge and reflects your own words, and declare it is true to the
best of your knowledge.
Personal and confidential information
14. As well as the above requirements, you should not give personal information
about another person involved in the case or confidential information about the
case, to public GenAI or to any GenAI that may not keep the information secure
from disclosure.
What may happen if you do not comply with the requirements
15. If you do not comply with the above requirements this may affect your case. This
might include:
• 	your documents being given less weight by the Commission or being
disregarded by the Commission
• 	the Commission ordering you to pay costs incurred by another party, or
• 	your case being dismissed.
16. The following sections of this Guidance Note explain the reasons for the above
requirements, and tell you how you can comply with them.

Reasons for the requirements – benefits, limitations and risks of
GenAI
17. Requirements 2 and 3 set basic standards for checking documents that anyone
should comply with whether or not they use GenAI. Because of the limitations and
risks of GenAI, this document checking is particularly important if you use GenAI
and so you are also required to tell us you used GenAI and how you used it, and to
confirm you did the checking.
18. GenAI can assist parties to Commission cases by improving efficiency in
preparing submissions and other documents. GenAI can also support access to
justice for self-represented litigants by helping them to express themselves more
clearly.
19. However, GenAI has limitations and there are risks in using it. GenAI generates an
output by analysing large databases of training information and identifying and
mimicking patterns in the data. GenAI is not capable of reasoning or
understanding information as a person would.
20. GenAI is not a substitute for legal advice or legal research. You should not rely on
GenAI to tell you whether you have a case you can win or what outcome you may
get if you start a case. GenAI may give you unrealistic and over-optimistic
predictions about whether you will win and what you will get.
21. GenAI may give you wrong or misleading information about the law or how it
applies in your case:
• 	GenAI may misstate the law, the outcome of cases or the legal positions that
cases stand for, or may apply laws that have since changed or cases that have
since been overturned
• 	GenAI may apply laws or cases from other Australian jurisdictions or from
overseas that do not apply in the Commission
• 	GenAI may make up fake cases, citations or quotes, or refer to laws, articles
or legal texts that do not exist – and fake material may look as though it comes
from a real source
• 	the training information that GenAI uses may not be reliable and GenAI output
may be biased, and
• 	GenAI may get facts wrong and confirm that information or evidence is true if
asked, even when it is not.

22. GenAI collects and retains user data, including your conversation history and
prompts. Information, documents or photos you give to GenAI may be stored in
the training database. As a result, personal, confidential or legally privileged
information you give to GenAI could become publicly known.
23. If you give GenAI a document or information that is subject to a Commission order
prohibiting or restricting publication, this might breach the order and be an
offence under the Fair Work Act.
24. There is also a risk that GenAI outputs will contain plagiarised material or other
persons’ intellectual property.
How to comply with the requirements
How to comply with requirement 1 – you must state in the document that
GenAI was used and how you used it
25. If you use GenAI to help you create, write, edit, modify or otherwise prepare a
document that is to be lodged in a Commission case, you must state in the
document that GenAI was used and how you used it.
26. Telling us you have used GenAI will not change how we treat the merits of your
document, provided you comply with the requirements in this Guidance Note.
Not telling us you have used GenAI or how you used it might result in your
document being given less weight or being disregarded, you being ordered to pay
costs, or your case being dismissed.
27. If the document is a Commission form or template that includes a section on use
of GenAI, you can comply with requirement 1 by completing that section of the
form or template. Otherwise, you can comply by stating in the document ‘GenAI
was used in preparing this document’ and how you used GenAI – for example, you
might state that you used GenAI to:
• 	research or get advice about the law, eligibility to make my application,
possible outcomes of my case or Commission processes
• 	organise facts, evidence, the timeline or documents relating to my case
• 	transcribe a person’s voice into written words or translate another language
into English
• 	write or create content I have used in the document, or
• 	modify or edit content of the document that I created.

How to comply with requirement 2 – you must make sure that all details in
the document are correct and relevant to the case, and state in the
document that you have done this
28. Requirement 2 applies in addition to requirement 1.
29. If you use GenAI to help you create, write, edit, modify or otherwise prepare a
document that is to be lodged in a Commission case, before the document is
lodged with the Commission you must check all details in the document and
make any necessary changes so that all of the details are correct and relevant to
the case.
30. In checking the details in the document you must make sure that:
• 	all references to facts and evidence in the document are correct and the facts
and evidence exist
• 	all cases, legislation, textbooks and articles referred to in the document exist
and support the legal positions you say they support, and
• 	all extracts or quotes in the document are correct and come from the sources
you say they come from.
31. You cannot check whether the details of a document are correct and relevant by
asking the same GenAI tool or a different GenAI tool to check the document – the
checking must be done by a person. You can check the details by looking at
sources of information known to be correct. Sources of information known to be
correct include:
• 	for legal principles and discussion of Commission decisions – Commission
case law Benchbooks and legal textbooks
• 	for Commission decisions – Find decisions and orders
• 	for Court decisions – Austlii and Jade, and
• 	for Commonwealth legislation – the Fair Work Act 2009 and other legislation
published on the Federal Register of Legislation.
32. We recommend that you also include hyperlinks to all cases you refer to in the
document. This will help you to make sure the cases exist and help the
Commission and all of the parties to find the cases.
33. After checking the document and making any necessary changes, you must state
in the document that you have done this. If the document is a Commission form
or template that includes a section on use of GenAI, you can do so by completing

Giving false or misleading information is a serious offence
A person who knowingly gives false or misleading information to
the Commission is guilty of an offence. The punishment is
imprisonment for up to 12 months. See section 137.1 of the
Criminal Code.
How to comply with requirement 3 – the witness/declarant must make
sure that their statement or declaration is based on their own knowledge,
reflects their own words and is true. The witness/declarant must also
state in the document that it is based on their own knowledge and reflects
their own words, and must declare that it is true to the best of their
knowledge
34. Requirement 3 applies in addition to requirements 1 and 2.
35. You should not use GenAI to create the factual content of a witness statement or
declaration. A witness statement or declaration must reflect the witness’s or
declarant’s own knowledge, not AI-generated content.
36. You can use GenAI to transcribe a person’s voice into written words or translate
another language into English, but witness statements and declarations must
reflect the witness’s or declarant’s own words.
37. If GenAI is used to help create, write, edit, modify or otherwise prepare a witness
statement or declaration then the witness/declarant must check the document
and make any necessary changes so that the statement or declaration is based
on their own knowledge, reflects their own words and is true to the best of their
knowledge.
38. The witness/declarant must then state in the document that they have done this.
If the document is a Commission form or template that includes a section on use
of GenAI, the witness/declarant can do so by completing that section of the
document and signing the declaration at the end of the document. Otherwise, the
witness/declarant can do so by stating in the document ‘This
statement/declaration is based on my own knowledge and reflects my own
words’ and signing a declaration at the end of the document that ‘This
statement/declaration is true to the best of my knowledge’.

False or misleading evidence
A witness commits an offence if they give sworn or affirmed
evidence in a case before the Commission and the evidence is
false or misleading. The punishment is imprisonment for up to
12 months. See section 678 of the Fair Work Act.
You should not give personal information about another person involved in the
case or confidential information about the case to public GenAI or to any GenAI
that may not keep the information secure from disclosure.
39. Before using GenAI to help you prepare a document in a Commission case, you
should understand the GenAI’s privacy policies and your own privacy obligations.
Everyone involved in a Commission case must maintain the confidentiality of
confidential information they obtain in the course of the case.
40. Because information provided to some GenAI may become public, you should not
give personal information about another person involved in a Commission case,
or confidential information, to public GenAI (such as ChatGPT, Claude, CoPilot or
Gemini) or to any GenAI that may not keep the information secure from
disclosure. We also recommend that you do not give your own personal
information to public GenAI.
41. Examples of personal information are:
• 	name, private contact details, home address and signature
• 	race, colour, age
• 	health information
• 	employment history
• 	financial information
• 	membership of a professional association or trade union, and
• 	opinions or claims about an individual – such as a claim they have engaged in
some kind of misbehaviour or misconduct.
42. Examples of confidential information are:
• 	information from settlement discussions in a Commission conference

• 	a document or information that is subject to a Commission order prohibiting

Additional obligations of legal practitioners and professional
representatives
44. The standard for responsible use of GenAI in Commission cases is more onerous
for a legal practitioner. In addition to complying with the requirements in this
Guidance Note, a legal practitioner must maintain high ethical standards and
comply with their professional obligations in any use of GenAI in a Commission
case. If a legal practitioner fails to do so, a complaint may be made to their legal
services regulator.
45. In addition to complying with the other requirements in this Guidance Note, a
professional representative who uses GenAI in preparing any document that is to
be lodged in a Commission case, must include in the document hyperlinks to all
case law referred to in the document (provided hyperlinks are available).
Definitions
46. In this Guidance Note:
document means any record of information, including a written document, a
photograph and anything from which sounds, images or writing can be
reproduced. It includes Commission forms, Commission template documents,
submissions, chronologies, declarations, witness statements, statements of
agreed facts and other evidentiary materials.
Fair Work Act means the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth).
generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) is an AI tool that uses models to learn
patterns from existing data and to generate new text, images, audio and other
material in response to a user’s prompts.
ChatGPT, Claude, CoPilot and Gemini are examples of GenAI.

‘GenAI’ does not include:
• 	e-discovery software, or
• 	a search engine that identifies existing content in response to a user’s search
query (but an AI tool that generates a summary of the output of such a search
engine is GenAI).
lawyer and paid agent have the same meaning as in the Fair Work Act.
personal information means information or an opinion about an identified
individual, or an individual who is reasonably identifiable:
• 	whether the information or opinion is true or not, and
• 	whether the information or opinion is recorded in a material form or not.
professional representative means a person who is representing another person
in a Commission case and is:
• 	a lawyer or paid agent (including a lawyer or paid agent who is an employee or
officer of the other person)
• 	a human resources adviser employed by the other person, or
• 	an employee or officer of a union, employer organisation, peak council or a
not-for-profit association or body, that is representing the other person.
public GenAI means GenAI available to the general public and includes both free
and paid or subscription versions of ChatGPT, Claude, CoPilot and Gemini.
