# Seoul Ministerial Statement and Frontier AI Safety Commitments — AI Seoul Summit
**AI Seoul Summit, Republic of Korea**
*International — May 21-22, 2024*

---

## Overview

The AI Seoul Summit of May 21-22, 2024, produced two significant outputs that together form the Seoul AI Safety package: (1) the Seoul Ministerial Statement on AI Safety — signed by governments; and (2) the Frontier AI Safety Commitments — voluntary commitments signed by major AI companies. Building directly on the Bletchley Declaration, the Seoul Summit deepened the international AI safety agenda by establishing concrete company-level commitments on safety evaluations, dangerous capability thresholds, and information sharing. Participating AI companies included Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, xAI, Amazon, Samsung, and others. The summit also formally established the international AI Safety Institute network, connecting national institutes from the UK, United States, Japan, Canada, Australia, South Korea, France, and others. The Seoul outputs represent the most operationally specific international AI safety agreements concluded to that point.

---

## Key Provisions

### Seoul Ministerial Statement (Government Commitments)
- Governments commit to developing national AI safety frameworks and policies
- Commitment to establish or strengthen national AI Safety Institutes
- Agreement to coordinate on safety evaluation methodologies and share findings
- Commitment to international information sharing on AI risks and incidents
- Support for inclusive AI governance that addresses needs of developing countries
- Agreement to continue the AI Safety Summit process

### Frontier AI Safety Commitments (Company Commitments)
**Safety Evaluations**
- Signatory companies commit to conducting pre-deployment safety evaluations for frontier AI models
- Evaluations must cover dangerous capabilities including weapons of mass destruction assistance, cyberoffense capabilities, and loss of control risks
- Companies commit to publishing safety evaluation frameworks and methodologies

**Dangerous Capability Thresholds**
- Companies commit to defining "red lines" — capability thresholds at which they would not deploy a model without additional safety measures
- Commitment to cease or significantly limit deployment if a model crosses defined safety thresholds

**Information Sharing**
- Companies commit to sharing safety-relevant information with governments and peer organizations
- Commitment to participate in the international AI Safety Institute network
- Information sharing on incidents and near-misses that reveal safety-relevant information

**Incident Reporting**
- Commitment to report significant safety incidents to relevant national authorities
- Companies agree to cooperate with government safety evaluations and audits

### International AI Safety Institute Network
- Formally established at Seoul to coordinate national AI Safety Institutes
- Members include UK AISI, US AISI, Japanese AI Safety Institute, and others
- Tasked with joint safety evaluations, shared methodologies, and coordinated response to AI risks

### Inclusion of AI Companies as Signatories
- Unprecedented direct engagement of major AI labs as signatories to safety commitments
- Represented a shift from government-only declarations to multi-stakeholder governance
- Companies signed voluntarily; commitments are non-binding under international law

### Non-Binding Political and Voluntary Character
- Government statements are political commitments, not binding treaties
- Company commitments are voluntary; no enforcement mechanism beyond reputational accountability
- Follow-up compliance review to be conducted at Paris 2025 Summit

## Enforcement & Penalties

- No formal enforcement mechanism for either government or company commitments
- Compliance and progress assessed at subsequent summits (Paris 2025)
- Signatory AI companies subject to reputational accountability through public reporting
- National AI Safety Institutes may conduct independent evaluations of company commitments
- Individual signatory governments may implement domestic regulations giving effect to commitments
