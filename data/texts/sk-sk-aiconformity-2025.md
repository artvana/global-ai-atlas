# Slovakia Act No. 318/2025 Z.z. Amending the Act on Conformity Assessment of Products (AI Act Adaptation)

**Act No. 318/2025 Coll. (zákon č. 318/2025 Z.z.)**
*Slovakia — Enacted 1 November 2025; in force 1 January 2026*

---

## Overview

Slovakia enacted Act No. 318/2025 Z.z. on 1 November 2025, amending the Act on Conformity Assessment of Products (zákon o posudzovaní zhody výrobkov) to align the national conformity assessment framework with the requirements of the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) for notified bodies. The Act entered into force on 1 January 2026.

This statute is the first component of Slovakia's two-part EU AI Act implementation legislative package. It addresses the notified body (conformity assessment body) side of AI Act implementation — specifically the organizations authorized to conduct third-party conformity assessments of high-risk AI systems. A companion governance bill (LP/2025/401), designating market surveillance authorities and establishing enforcement procedures, remained pending in the Slovak National Council as of May 2026.

## Key Provisions

### Designation of UNMS as Notifying Authority

The Slovak Office for Standards, Metrology and Testing (Úrad pre normalizáciu, metrológiu a skúšobníctvo Slovenskej republiky — UNMS SR) is formally designated as the notifying authority for AI conformity assessment bodies under the EU AI Act. The UNMS has existing competence as the notifying authority for notified bodies across a range of EU product safety directives and regulations, and this Act extends that role to AI.

As notifying authority, UNMS SR is responsible for:
- Assessing applications from organizations seeking designation as notified bodies for AI Act conformity assessments
- Formally notifying the European Commission of designated bodies via NANDO (New Approach Notified and Designated Organisations) database
- Ongoing monitoring and periodic review of notified body performance
- Suspension or withdrawal of notified body designations where required
- Coordinating with ENISA and the EU AI Office on technical standards for AI conformity assessment

### Requirements for Notified Body Designation

The Act specifies the criteria that conformity assessment bodies must satisfy to obtain designation as notified bodies under the EU AI Act. Requirements include:

**Legal and Organizational Requirements:**
- Legal establishment in Slovakia or an EU/EEA state with reciprocal recognition
- Financial stability and liability insurance sufficient for conformity assessment activities
- Independence from AI system providers, importers, and distributors (avoiding conflicts of interest)
- Impartiality — no commercial relationships that could compromise objectivity
- Organizational structure with clear accountability for conformity assessment decisions

**Technical Competence Requirements:**
- Staff qualifications and expertise covering AI system architectures, relevant technical standards, and sector-specific requirements (where high-risk AI is sector-specific)
- Documented quality management system
- Processes for handling appeals and complaints from AI system providers

**Procedural Requirements:**
- Defined methodology for conducting conformity assessments under Annex VI (internal control) and Annex VII (full quality management system) of the EU AI Act
- Processes for issuing, suspending, and withdrawing EU technical documentation and conformity assessment certificates
- Participation in AI Act coordination group activities

### Transitional Provisions

The Act includes transitional provisions allowing existing notified bodies under product safety and medical device regulations to apply for expanded designation covering AI systems without full re-application procedures, where their existing competence areas substantially overlap with the high-risk AI system categories in Annex III of the EU AI Act.

### Pending Companion Legislation

The Act explicitly acknowledges that it covers only the notified body dimension of EU AI Act implementation. Market surveillance authority designation, national enforcement procedures, national penalty structures, and the AI regulatory sandbox framework are deferred to the companion bill LP/2025/401, which was subject to parliamentary committee review as of May 2026.
