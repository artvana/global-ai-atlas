# The Bletchley Declaration by Countries Attending the AI Safety Summit, 1-2 November 2023
**Bletchley Park AI Safety Summit Declaration**
*International — November 1, 2023*

---

## Overview

The Bletchley Declaration is the first international political declaration specifically addressing the risks of frontier AI systems. Signed on November 1-2, 2023, at Bletchley Park, United Kingdom, by representatives of 28 countries and the European Union, the declaration marks the beginning of a structured international dialogue on AI safety. Signatories include the United States, United Kingdom, European Union, China, France, Germany, Japan, South Korea, India, Australia, Brazil, Canada, and other major economies. The declaration acknowledges that frontier AI models — those at the cutting edge of capability — could pose serious, potentially catastrophic risks, and commits signatories to cooperative action on safety evaluation, research, and governance. It is a non-binding political declaration but launched the ongoing AI Safety Summit series (Seoul 2024, Paris 2025) and catalyzed the creation of national AI Safety Institutes in multiple signatory countries.

---

## Key Provisions

### Recognition of Frontier AI Risks
- Acknowledges that frontier AI, particularly through powerful large-scale models, presents potentially transformative benefits but also potentially catastrophic risks
- Identifies risks including those relating to deliberate misuse (e.g., biological, chemical, nuclear, radiological weapons development), accidents from loss of control, and disruption of critical infrastructure
- Recognizes that most risks may manifest globally, not just in states where AI is developed

### Commitments to International Cooperation
- Commits signatories to sustain an inclusive international dialogue on AI safety
- Agreement to share information about safety testing and evaluation methods
- Commitment to collaborate on identifying and evaluating frontier AI risks
- Support for development of internationally recognized safety guidelines and codes of conduct

### Safety Research and Evaluation
- Signatories acknowledge the importance of safety-focused research on frontier AI
- Commitment to support development of technical tools and evaluation methods for assessing dangerous capabilities
- Recognition that international collaboration on safety standards and benchmarks is essential

### Inclusion of China
- Notable for including China as a signatory alongside Western democracies
- Represented a rare moment of US-China alignment on technology governance
- China's participation acknowledged as important for any meaningful global AI safety framework

### Foundation for AI Safety Summit Process
- Established the AI Safety Summit series as an ongoing international forum
- Provided the mandate for subsequent summits in Seoul (May 2024) and Paris (February 2025)
- Catalyzed creation of national AI Safety Institutes (UK AISI, US AISI, others)
- Established international AI Safety Institute network for technical cooperation

### Non-Binding Political Character
- Declaration is a political commitment, not a legally binding treaty
- Does not create enforceable obligations on signatory governments
- Does not regulate private sector AI development directly
- Intended as a framework for further cooperation rather than an operative governance instrument

## Enforcement & Penalties

- No enforcement mechanism: the declaration is a non-binding political agreement
- Compliance monitored through reputational and diplomatic channels
- Follow-up summits (Seoul, Paris) serve as accountability fora for assessing progress on commitments
- Signatory governments individually responsible for implementing domestic measures consistent with the declaration
