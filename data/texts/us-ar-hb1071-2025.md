## Arkansas House Bill 1071 (2025)
### Publicity Rights Protection Act — Extension to Artificial Intelligence

**95th General Assembly, 2025 Regular Session**
**Enacted:** April 10, 2025 | **Effective:** July 1, 2025

---

## Overview

Arkansas HB 1071 (2025) amends Arkansas's existing Publicity Rights Protection Act to explicitly extend its protections to AI-generated and AI-manipulated representations of individuals' names, voices, images, photographs, and likenesses. The Act prohibits the unauthorized commercial use of a person's identity — including digital replicas created by generative AI — without prior written consent.

HB 1071 is part of a wave of five AI-related identity-protection measures enacted by Arkansas in its 2025 regular session, positioning the state among the most active in the country on digital likeness rights legislation. The law closely parallels Tennessee's ELVIS Act (2024) and California's AB 1836 (2024), adapted for Arkansas's existing publicity rights framework.

---

## Legislative Background

Arkansas law has long protected individuals' rights of publicity under the Arkansas Code. Prior to HB 1071, the Publicity Rights Protection Act protected against unauthorized commercial use of a person's name, photograph, voice, or likeness in advertising, fundraising, or solicitation. However, the rise of generative AI technology — enabling realistic AI-generated voice clones, photorealistic digital likenesses, and video deepfakes — created a gap in protection, as the existing statute did not explicitly address AI-generated replicas.

HB 1071 closes this gap by:
1. Defining "digital replica" and "AI-generated likeness" in the statute.
2. Explicitly including AI-generated content within the scope of prohibited unauthorized commercial uses.
3. Preserving and extending existing civil remedies — actual damages, disgorgement of profits, statutory damages, and injunctive relief — to unauthorized AI uses.

---

## Key Definitions

**Digital Replica** — A computer-generated, highly realistic electronic representation of a person's voice, image, likeness, or photograph, whether or not the individual participated in the creation of the content, produced using artificial intelligence, machine learning, deepfake technology, or similar computational methods.

**AI-Generated Likeness** — An electronic reproduction, simulation, or imitation of an identifiable individual's appearance, voice, facial features, or persona created in whole or in substantial part through the use of artificial intelligence or automated computational processes.

**Commercial Purpose** — Use of an individual's name, photograph, voice, likeness, or digital replica in advertising, solicitation, fundraising, merchandise, or endorsement contexts for commercial gain.

---

## Core Prohibitions

### Section 1 — Unauthorized Use of AI-Generated Likeness

No person may, for commercial purposes:
- Produce, distribute, display, or transmit an AI-generated likeness or digital replica of a living or deceased individual without prior written consent of the individual (or, in the case of a deceased individual, their estate or authorized representative);
- Use generative AI tools to create synthetic voice recordings, video deepfakes, or digital images that falsely represent a person's endorsement, association, or participation in commercial activity;
- License, sell, or transfer AI-generated likenesses of individuals for commercial use without the consent of the person whose likeness is represented.

### Section 2 — Posthumous Rights

The rights established under this Act extend to deceased individuals. The estate, heir, or legally authorized representative of a deceased person retains the right to consent to or prohibit commercial use of AI-generated likenesses for a period of **10 years** following the individual's death, consistent with Arkansas's existing posthumous publicity rights framework.

### Section 3 — Consent Requirements

Consent for AI use of an individual's likeness must:
- Be in writing and signed by the individual or their authorized representative;
- Specify the scope of permitted uses, the duration of consent, and whether the consent covers commercial contexts;
- Be freely revocable except where a contract for valuable consideration limits revocability.

---

## Exemptions and Safe Harbors

The following uses are not prohibited under HB 1071:

1. **News and Commentary** — Factual reporting, news coverage, documentary content, commentary, criticism, or satire on matters of public concern involving a public figure, where AI is used editorially and not for commercial exploitation.

2. **Educational and Research Use** — Use in educational instruction, academic research, or scholarly publication where the AI-generated content is clearly identified as synthetic.

3. **Transformative Creative Works** — Use in artistic, literary, or dramatic works where the AI-generated content is clearly transformative and not primarily commercial in purpose.

4. **Consent-Based Professional Use** — Use pursuant to a valid written consent agreement, including in entertainment, advertising, or commercial contexts.

5. **Political Speech** — Use in bona fide political advertising or commentary, provided the content includes a disclosure that it contains AI-generated material.

---

## Civil Remedies

### Section 4 — Private Right of Action

Any person whose rights under this Act are violated may bring a civil action in an Arkansas court of competent jurisdiction against the violator for:

1. **Actual damages** — Compensation for economic harm caused by the unauthorized use, including lost licensing fees, reputational harm, and emotional distress where proven;

2. **Disgorgement of profits** — Recovery of any profits the violator derived from the unauthorized use of the individual's AI-generated likeness;

3. **Statutory damages** — In lieu of actual damages, the plaintiff may elect statutory damages of not less than **$10,000** per violation;

4. **Punitive damages** — Where the violation is found to be willful or intentional, the court may award punitive damages not to exceed three times the actual or statutory damages award;

5. **Injunctive relief** — The court may issue preliminary and permanent injunctions prohibiting continued unauthorized use;

6. **Attorney fees and costs** — A prevailing plaintiff is entitled to reasonable attorney fees and court costs.

### Section 5 — Attorney General Enforcement

The Arkansas Attorney General may bring an action in the name of the State of Arkansas to enforce this Act, seek civil penalties of up to **$5,000 per violation**, and obtain injunctive relief.

---

## Relationship to Existing Law

HB 1071 amends and supplements Arkansas Code Annotated § 4-75-1101 et seq. (Publicity Rights Protection Act). It does not repeal or limit existing protections under the Act; rather, it adds AI-generated content as an explicit category of prohibited unauthorized use.

The Act also does not limit or supersede any federal intellectual property rights, including copyright protections for AI-generated works, or any applicable federal law governing AI-generated content.

## Significance

Arkansas HB 1071 represents the state's entry into the rapidly growing body of AI personality and likeness rights legislation. By amending an established publicity rights framework rather than creating a standalone AI law, Arkansas takes an incremental but legally robust approach — embedding AI protections within a mature civil liability structure with clear remedies and enforcement mechanisms. The $10,000 statutory damages floor and private right of action make this one of the more plaintiff-friendly state AI likeness laws in effect as of 2025.
