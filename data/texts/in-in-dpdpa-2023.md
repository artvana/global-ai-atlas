# Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023
**Act No. 22 of 2023**
*Received Presidential assent: August 11, 2023*
*Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology*

---

## Chapter I — Preliminary

### Section 1 — Short Title, Extent and Commencement
(1) This Act may be called the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.
(2) It extends to the whole of India and also applies to the processing of digital personal data outside the territory of India if such processing is in connection with any activity related to offering of goods or services to Data Principals within India.
(3) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification, appoint.

### Section 2 — Definitions
In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires:

**"Consent Manager"** means a person registered with the Board who acts as a single point of contact to enable a Data Principal to give, manage, review, and withdraw consent through an accessible, transparent, and interoperable platform.

**"Data Fiduciary"** means any person who alone or in conjunction with other persons determines the purpose and means of processing of personal data.

**"Data Principal"** means the individual to whom the personal data relates, and where such individual is a child, includes the parent or lawful guardian of such child.

**"Data Processor"** means any person who processes personal data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary.

**"personal data"** means any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to such data.

**"processing"** means wholly or partly automated operation or set of operations performed on digital personal data, including collection, recording, organisation, structuring, storage, adaptation, retrieval, use, alignment, combination, indexing, sharing, disclosure by transmission, dissemination, restriction, erasure, or destruction.

**"Significant Data Fiduciary"** means any Data Fiduciary or class of Data Fiduciaries as may be notified by the Central Government on the basis of an assessment of relevant factors, including volume and sensitivity of personal data processed, risk to rights of Data Principals, potential impact on sovereignty and integrity of India, risk to electoral democracy, security of the State, and public order.

## Chapter II — Grounds for Processing Personal Data

### Section 4 — Processing of Personal Data Only for Lawful Purpose
A person may process personal data of a Data Principal only in accordance with the provisions of this Act and for a lawful purpose:

(a) for which the Data Principal has given consent; or
(b) for certain legitimate uses specified in this Act (Section 7).

### Section 6 — Notice
Before seeking consent, the Data Fiduciary must give the Data Principal a notice in clear and plain language containing:

(a) the personal data sought to be collected and the purpose of processing such personal data;
(b) the manner in which the Data Principal may exercise rights under Section 12 of this Act; and
(c) the manner in which the Data Principal may make a complaint to the Board.

The Data Principal's consent, once given, shall be free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous, given through a clear affirmative action.

A Data Principal has the right to withdraw consent at any time, and the ease of withdrawal shall be comparable to the ease with which consent was given. Upon withdrawal, the Data Fiduciary shall cease processing within a reasonable time, provided that lawful processing prior to withdrawal is not affected.

### Section 7 — Certain Legitimate Uses
A Data Fiduciary may process personal data of a Data Principal without consent for:

(a) a specified purpose to which the Data Principal has voluntarily provided personal data;
(b) the State or instrumentalities of the State in providing subsidies, benefits, services, certificates, licenses, or permits;
(c) medical emergency, threat to life, epidemic, or disaster;
(d) employment purposes to the extent that the Data Principal has provided personal data in the context of employment;
(e) performance of functions by the State or its instrumentalities under law.

## Chapter III — Obligations of Data Fiduciary

### Section 8 — General Obligations of Data Fiduciary
(1) A Data Fiduciary shall:

(a) be responsible for compliance with the provisions of this Act in respect of any processing undertaken by it or on its behalf by a Data Processor;
(b) process personal data of a Data Principal only for the purpose specified in the notice given under Section 6 and not further process such data in a manner incompatible with that purpose;
(c) ensure completeness, accuracy, and consistency of personal data that is likely to be used to make a decision about the Data Principal or to be shared with another person;
(d) implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure effective observance of this Act;
(e) implement appropriate safeguards to prevent personal data breach;
(f) erase personal data upon withdrawal of consent by Data Principal or when the purpose of processing is no longer served;
(g) publish the contact details of a Data Protection Officer or other person able to respond to questions about processing of personal data.

### Section 9 — Processing of Personal Data of Children
(1) A Data Fiduciary shall not process personal data of a child without verifiable parental consent.

(2) A Data Fiduciary shall not undertake tracking or behavioural monitoring of children or targeted advertising directed at children.

(3) Appropriate mechanisms for age verification and parental consent must be implemented by Data Fiduciaries.

(4) The Central Government may notify categories of Data Fiduciaries exempt from specific provisions of this Section based on the nature of processing and demonstrated safeguards.

### Section 10 — Additional Obligations for Significant Data Fiduciaries
A Significant Data Fiduciary shall:

(a) appoint a Data Protection Officer who shall be based in India and shall be the representative of the Significant Data Fiduciary and shall be responsible to the Board of Directors or similar governing body;
(b) appoint an independent data auditor to evaluate compliance with this Act and applicable rules;
(c) undertake periodic Data Protection Impact Assessments and audits;
(d) carry out such other measures as may be prescribed.

## Chapter IV — Rights of Data Principal

### Section 11 — Right of Access to Information About Personal Data
A Data Principal shall have the right to obtain, from the Data Fiduciary:

(a) confirmation as to whether personal data relating to the Data Principal is being processed by the Data Fiduciary;
(b) a summary of personal data relating to the Data Principal that is being processed by the Data Fiduciary and processing activities undertaken by the Data Fiduciary in respect of such personal data.

### Section 12 — Right of Correction and Erasure
(1) A Data Principal shall have the right to correct and update personal data held by a Data Fiduciary upon making a request to the Data Fiduciary.

(2) A Data Principal shall have the right to erasure of personal data held by a Data Fiduciary, upon making a request to the Data Fiduciary, subject to the Data Fiduciary being required to retain such data for compliance with any law.

### Section 13 — Right of Grievance Redressal
A Data Principal shall have the right to:

(a) have readily available means of grievance redressal provided by a Data Fiduciary or Consent Manager in respect of any act or omission of such Data Fiduciary or Consent Manager in relation to the data or rights of the Data Principal;
(b) have the grievance redressed within such period as may be prescribed;
(c) complain to the Data Protection Board of India if the Data Principal is not satisfied with the response from the Data Fiduciary.

### Section 14 — Right to Nominate
A Data Principal shall have the right to nominate any other individual who shall, in the event of death or incapacity of the Data Principal, exercise rights under this Act.

## Chapter V — Duties of Data Principals

### Section 15 — Duties of Data Principal
A Data Principal shall:

(a) not impersonate another person while providing personal data for any specified purpose;
(b) not suppress any material information while providing personal data for any document, unique identifier, proof of identity, or proof of address;
(c) not register a false or frivolous grievance or complaint with a Data Fiduciary or the Board.

---

## Chapter VI — Exemptions

### Section 17 — Exemptions
(1) Nothing in the provisions of Chapter II (obligations on Data Fiduciaries), Chapter III (rights and duties of Data Principals), and Chapter IV (rights of Data Principal) shall apply to processing of personal data:

(a) by the State or any instrumentality of the State in the interests of sovereignty and integrity of India, security of the State, friendly relations with foreign states, maintenance of public order, or prevention of incitement to any cognisable offence;

(b) for the purposes of legal proceedings or compliance with judicial orders;

(c) for purposes of prevention, detection, investigation, or prosecution of any offence or contravention of any law;

(d) for research, archiving, or statistical purposes with appropriate safeguards.

---

## Chapter VII — Data Protection Board of India

### Section 18 — Establishment of Data Protection Board of India
The Central Government shall establish the Data Protection Board of India with such members as may be prescribed to:

(a) adjudicate upon complaints in respect of personal data;
(b) direct Data Fiduciaries and Data Processors to perform their obligations;
(c) impose financial penalties;
(d) direct provision of relief to Data Principals;
(e) refer matters to appropriate authority.

## Chapter VIII — Financial Penalties

### Section 33 — Penalties
Financial penalties for non-compliance:

(a) Failure of Significant Data Fiduciary to conduct Data Protection Impact Assessment or audit: up to Rs 150 crore (approximately USD 18 million).

(b) Failure to implement reasonable security safeguards resulting in personal data breach: up to Rs 250 crore (approximately USD 30 million).

(c) Failure to notify the Board and affected Data Principals of personal data breach: up to Rs 200 crore (approximately USD 24 million).

(d) Non-fulfilment of obligations in relation to children's data: up to Rs 200 crore (approximately USD 24 million).

(e) General non-compliance with provisions of this Act: up to Rs 50 crore (approximately USD 6 million).

Penalties shall be assessed based on the nature, gravity, and duration of non-compliance, and paid into the Consolidated Fund of India.

### Section 40 — Cross-Border Data Transfers
The Central Government may, after an assessment of relevant factors (including adequacy of data protection standards and enforcement in the receiving country), restrict transfer of personal data to any foreign country or territory by notification. Transfer shall be permitted to notified countries with adequate protections.
