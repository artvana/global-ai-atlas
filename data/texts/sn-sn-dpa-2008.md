# Loi n° 2008-12 du 25 janvier 2008 portant sur la protection des données à caractère personnel — Senegal
**Loi n° 2008-12**
*Senegal — January 25, 2008*

---

## Overview

Senegal's Law 2008-12 of January 25, 2008, on the protection of personal data is one of West Africa's earliest and most comprehensive personal data protection statutes. Enacted in 2008, the law establishes the Commission de Protection des Données Personnelles (CDP) as an independent administrative authority. Modeled partly on the EU Directive 95/46/EC and the French data protection tradition, the law has served as a reference point for ECOWAS (Economic Community of West African States) member states developing their own frameworks. It applies to all processing of personal data on Senegalese territory, covering both automated and partly automated processing as well as certain manual filing systems.

---

## Key Provisions

### Commission de Protection des Données Personnelles (CDP)
- Independent administrative authority established under the law
- Receives declarations and authorizations for processing activities
- Investigates complaints from data subjects
- Issues opinions, recommendations, and formal warnings
- May impose sanctions and refer criminal matters to prosecutors
- Serves as a reference point for ECOWAS data protection cooperation
- CDP members appointed to ensure independence from government and industry

### Five Core Data Protection Principles
- Lawfulness: processing must have a lawful basis
- Purpose specification: purposes must be specific, explicit, and legitimate
- Proportionality: data must be adequate, relevant, and not excessive
- Accuracy: data must be accurate and kept up to date
- Storage limitation: not kept longer than necessary for the specified purpose

### Mandatory Declaration and Authorization
- Standard processing activities must be declared to the CDP
- Sensitive data processing and certain high-risk processing require prior CDP authorization
- Public sector processing by government authorities follows a specific authorization regime
- Failure to declare or obtain authorization is a criminal offense

### Sensitive Personal Data
- Special categories requiring explicit consent or specific legal authority: health and medical data, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, biometric data, genetic data, racial or ethnic origin, criminal record and judicial measures

### Data Subject Rights
- Right to information at the time of data collection
- Right of access to personal data held by any controller
- Right to rectification of inaccurate, incomplete, or outdated data
- Right to opposition to processing for legitimate and compelling reasons, including direct marketing

### Cross-Border Data Transfers
- Transfers outside Senegal restricted unless adequate protection in the destination country or specific safeguards approved by the CDP
- CDP may authorize transfers on a case-by-case basis

### Security Obligations
- Controllers must implement technical and organizational measures to prevent unauthorized access, loss, or alteration of data
- Obligation of confidentiality for staff handling personal data

## Enforcement & Penalties

- CDP may issue formal warnings, compliance orders, and referrals to prosecutors
- Criminal penalties: fines up to XOF 10,000,000 (approximately $17,000 USD) and/or up to 5 years imprisonment for the most serious violations
- Penalties apply to unlawful processing, failure to declare or authorize, and violations of data subject rights
- Civil liability: individuals may seek compensation through Senegalese courts for harm caused by unlawful processing
