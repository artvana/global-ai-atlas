# Law on the Latvian Artificial Intelligence Centre (Mākslīgā intelekta centra likums)

**Latvian Statute (Mākslīgā intelekta centra likums)**
*Latvia — Adopted 6 March 2025; in force 20 March 2025*

---

## Overview

The Law on the Latvian Artificial Intelligence Centre (Mākslīgā intelekta centra likums) was adopted by the Saeima (Latvian parliament) on 6 March 2025 and entered into force on 20 March 2025. It establishes the Latvian Artificial Intelligence Centre (AI Centre) as a public-private-academic foundation under Latvian law, making Latvia the first European Union member state to create a dedicated national AI institution by statute.

The Law is primarily an institutional and governance instrument — it creates and defines the mandate, structure, and financing of the AI Centre — rather than a substantive regulation of AI systems or providers. Its significance lies in establishing Latvia's national infrastructure for AI policy, research coordination, regulatory sandbox operation, and international engagement under the EU AI Act framework.

## Key Provisions

### Establishment and Legal Form

The AI Centre is established as a foundation under Latvian law, combining public (state) and private sector participation. This public-private-academic hybrid structure is designed to bring together government ministries, research institutions, and technology companies in a single coordinating body. The Centre has legal personality and operates independently from line ministries, reporting to a supervisory board representing all founding constituencies.

### Mandate and Functions

The AI Centre's statutory mandate encompasses:

**AI Policy Coordination.** The Centre coordinates Latvia's national AI strategy implementation, liaises with EU institutions (including the EU AI Office) on behalf of Latvia, and provides expert input to government on AI regulation and policy.

**Research and Innovation Support.** The Centre supports AI research and development by academic institutions and SMEs, administers grant programs, and operates shared computing infrastructure for AI development.

**Regulatory Sandbox Operation.** The Law creates a statutory legal basis for a special regulatory testing environment — an AI regulatory sandbox — in which AI developers and deployers may test AI systems in a controlled environment with limited regulatory liability. Within the sandbox:
- Participants benefit from limited waivers of certain administrative compliance requirements during the testing period
- The Centre provides regulatory guidance and monitoring
- Sandbox participation does not exempt participants from EU AI Act obligations, but enables supervised pre-market testing
- Sandbox agreements define scope, duration, and any applicable data handling conditions

**International Cooperation.** The Centre represents Latvia in international AI governance forums, coordinates with EEA counterparts, and manages bilateral cooperation agreements with foreign AI research institutions.

**AI Competency and Literacy.** The Centre develops and delivers AI literacy programs for the public sector and supports curriculum development in Latvian educational institutions.

### Governance Structure

The AI Centre is governed by:
- A **Supervisory Board** comprising representatives of contributing public bodies, research institutions, and private sector members
- A **Management Board** responsible for operational management
- An **Advisory Council** of international AI experts providing strategic guidance

### Financing

The AI Centre is financed through a combination of state budget allocations, contributions from private sector founding members, EU structural funds, and income from services provided to participants (including regulatory sandbox participation fees). The initial state budget commitment for 2025–2027 is specified in the Law.

### Relationship to EU AI Act

The Law explicitly references the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) and positions the AI Centre as supporting Latvia's implementation of EU AI Act obligations, including: coordinating market surveillance with the designated national competent authority; supporting SMEs seeking to use the AI regulatory sandbox established under Article 57 of the EU AI Act; and contributing to the GPAI Code of Practice process.

The Law does not itself designate a market surveillance authority for the EU AI Act — that designation is handled through separate administrative instruments.
