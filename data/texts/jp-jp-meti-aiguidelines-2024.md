# AI Business Guidelines (AI事業者ガイドライン)
**Joint Publication — Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) and Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC)**
*Japan — Published April 19, 2024*

---

## Overview / Background

The AI Business Guidelines (AI事業者ガイドライン) were jointly published by Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) and Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC) on April 19, 2024. They represent Japan's primary operational governance document for AI systems and serve as the government's principal guidance instrument for AI developers, operators, and users prior to the enactment of any dedicated AI statute.

The guidelines were developed as part of Japan's broader AI governance strategy, building on the Hiroshima AI Process — the G7-led international framework Japan led during its 2023 G7 Presidency — as well as Japan's long-standing AI principles. The document consolidates earlier guidelines from METI and MIC, aligning them with evolving international standards including the OECD AI Principles and the Hiroshima Process International Guiding Principles for Advanced AI.

Compliance with these guidelines is entirely voluntary. They do not create binding legal obligations. However, they are intended to inform regulatory expectations and may inform future enforcement under existing laws (e.g., the APPI, consumer protection, and unfair competition laws). The guidelines are structured around three tiers of AI supply chain actors:
1. **AI Developers** — entities that develop foundation models or AI technologies
2. **AI Providers (Operators)** — entities that deploy AI systems as services or products
3. **AI Users** — businesses and individuals using AI tools in their operations

## Key Provisions / Chapters

### Ten Guiding Principles
The guidelines establish ten cross-cutting principles applicable to all actors in the AI ecosystem:

1. **Human-Centric AI** — AI must respect human dignity, autonomy, and fundamental rights
2. **Education and Literacy** — AI actors should promote AI literacy and understanding among users and the public
3. **Privacy Protection** — AI systems must respect applicable privacy laws and principles (including APPI); personal data should not be used beyond original collection purposes
4. **Safety** — AI systems must be designed and operated to prevent harms to life, health, property, and social infrastructure
5. **Security** — AI systems must implement appropriate cybersecurity measures to prevent unauthorized access, tampering, and adversarial attacks
6. **Fairness** — AI must not generate inappropriate discrimination or bias on grounds of race, gender, disability, nationality, or other protected characteristics; appropriate testing for bias is expected
7. **Accountability** — AI actors should be accountable for the impacts of their AI systems and maintain appropriate governance structures
8. **Transparency** — AI actors should disclose relevant information about AI systems to enable users to make informed decisions; disclosure of AI-generated content is encouraged
9. **Innovation** — AI development should support Japan's economic competitiveness and societal benefit; excessive regulatory burden should be avoided
10. **Social Contribution** — AI should be developed and used to contribute positively to society and address global challenges

### Tier-Specific Guidance: AI Developers
- Conduct safety testing and red-teaming prior to release of foundation models
- Implement safety filters and content moderation mechanisms
- Provide documentation (model cards, system cards) enabling downstream operators to understand capabilities and limitations
- Disclose information about training data scope and processes to the extent possible without compromising proprietary interests
- Implement safeguards against misuse for generating illegal content, cyberattacks, or weapons-related applications
- Cooperate with government incident reporting and information-sharing frameworks

### Tier-Specific Guidance: AI Providers (Operators)
- Conduct appropriate due diligence on AI models and components before deployment
- Disclose to users when they are interacting with an AI system (AI interaction disclosure)
- Implement access controls and monitoring appropriate to the risk level of the AI application
- Maintain human oversight mechanisms for high-risk decisions (medical, legal, financial, employment)
- Establish complaint and redress mechanisms for users adversely affected by AI outputs
- Comply with applicable sector-specific regulations (financial services, medical devices, etc.)

### Tier-Specific Guidance: AI Users (Businesses)
- Conduct appropriate risk assessments before deploying AI in business operations
- Train employees on appropriate and responsible AI use
- Establish internal governance policies for AI use
- Not use AI to generate illegal content, spread disinformation, or circumvent legal obligations

### Sector-Specific Appendices
The guidelines include sector-specific appendices addressing AI governance in:
- Healthcare and medical devices
- Financial services
- Legal services
- Education
- Employment and human resources
- Critical infrastructure

### Alignment with International Frameworks
- Explicitly references and aligns with the OECD AI Principles (2019, revised 2024)
- Implements the Hiroshima Process International Guiding Principles for Advanced AI Systems (2023)
- Notes alignment with EU AI Act principles while maintaining Japan's less prescriptive approach
- Encourages Japanese companies to participate in international standards development (ISO/IEC SC42)

## Enforcement & Penalties

These guidelines are voluntary and carry no direct enforcement mechanism or penalty regime. Non-compliance does not constitute a violation of law. However:

- The guidelines are intended to inform the PPC's enforcement of the APPI with respect to AI systems processing personal data
- METI and MIC may use adherence to guidelines as a factor in procurement decisions and public recognition programs
- Future legislation may incorporate elements of these guidelines as binding requirements
- Industry associations and sector regulators may reference the guidelines in their own compliance frameworks
- Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency may consider guideline violations as evidence of unfair business practices under consumer protection law
