================================================================================
CALIFORNIA — AB 1946 (2026) — Reporting Mechanism: Child Sexual Abuse Material
2025–2026 Regular Session
================================================================================

CALIFORNIA ASSEMBLY BILL 1946
Introduced by Assembly Members Wicks and Krell
(with coauthors)
February 13, 2026
Last amended: April 27, 2026

An act to amend Sections 3273.65, 3273.66, 3273.67, and 3345.1 of the Civil Code,
relating to social media platforms.

LEGISLATIVE COUNSEL'S DIGEST

AB 1946, as amended, Wicks. Reporting mechanism: child sexual abuse material.

This bill amends provisions requiring social media platforms to establish clear reporting
mechanisms for child sexual abuse material (CSAM) and expands the definition of CSAM
to include "intimate visual depictions" involving identifiable minors, regardless of obscenity
status, including AI-generated or digitally altered content.

THE PEOPLE OF THE STATE OF CALIFORNIA DO ENACT AS FOLLOWS:

SECTION 1. Section 3273.65 of the Civil Code is amended to read:

3273.65. For purposes of this article, the following definitions apply:

(a) "Child sexual abuse material" means any of the following:
(1) Child pornography, as defined in Section 311.4 of the Penal Code.
(2) An intimate visual depiction involving an identifiable individual who is, or reasonably
appears to be, a minor.

(b) "Clear and conspicuous" means that the mechanism is easily accessible and visible to
users and is not obscured by dark patterns or design elements that discourage its use.

(c) "Depicted individual" means an individual shown as a minor in child sexual abuse
material.

(d) "Digital forgery" means content created or altered using artificial intelligence,
machine learning, software, or similar technological means to depict a real individual
engaging in conduct the individual did not actually engage in.

(e) "Hash-matching process" means an automated process of comparing digital files to
known databases of hashes, including PhotoDNA or similar technology, to identify
known child sexual abuse material.

(f) "Intimate visual depiction" means a visual depiction of any of the following:
(1) An uncovered human genitals, pubic area, anus, or post-pubescent female nipple.
(2) The transfer of bodily sexual fluids onto another individual or an individual's body.
(3) An identifiable individual engaging in sexually explicit conduct, as defined in
18 U.S.C. Section 2256.

(g) "Reporting user" means a natural person who makes a report through the reporting
mechanism established pursuant to this article.

(h) "Social media company" has the same meaning as set forth in Section 22675 of the
Business and Professions Code, but does not include a nonprofit organization.

(i) "Social media platform" has the same meaning as set forth in Section 22675 of the
Business and Professions Code, but does not include a platform that provides end-to-end
encrypted communications or a nonprofit platform.

SECTION 2. Section 3273.66 of the Civil Code is amended to read:

3273.66. A social media platform shall do all of the following:

(a) Provide a clear and conspicuous mechanism accessible to California residents for
reporting material the reporting user reasonably believes is child sexual abuse material
displayed or accessible on the platform. The mechanism shall not incorporate dark patterns
or other designs that discourage use.

(b) Collect contact information from the reporting user, review reports through a hash-
matching process, and, if no established hash match exists for the material and the material
is not otherwise blocked, review the report by a natural person.

(c) Block access to instances of reported material if there is a reasonable basis to believe
the material constitutes child sexual abuse material and basic identifying information permits
location of the material.

(d) Provide written confirmation of receipt to the reporting user within 36 hours.

(e) Provide status updates every 7 days from the date of initial confirmation until a final
determination is made.

(f) Issue a final determination within 48 hours of completing review, stating whether the
reported material was determined to be child sexual abuse material and blocked, was
determined not to be child sexual abuse material, or could not be found on the platform.
If circumstances beyond the platform's reasonable control prevent a final determination
within 48 hours, the platform shall notify the reporting user and shall issue a final
determination within 5 days.

SECTION 3. Section 3273.67 of the Civil Code is amended to read:

3273.67. (a) A social media platform that fails to maintain a functional reporting mechanism
shall be liable for a civil penalty of up to two hundred fifty thousand dollars ($250,000) for
each day the mechanism is unavailable or nonfunctional. Civil penalties shall be recoverable
by the Attorney General, a district attorney, a city attorney, or a county counsel. Penalties
collected by the Attorney General shall be deposited in the Survivor Support Fund.

A reporting mechanism is deemed unavailable or nonfunctional if any of the following
applies:
(1) Inaccessible due to technical failures or removal from the platform.
(2) Does not permit reporting as required.
(3) Fails to provide confirmation of receipt within specified timeframes.

(b) A depicted individual or a reporting user who is the depicted individual may bring a
civil action against a social media platform that violates subdivisions (b), (c), (d), or (f)
of Section 3273.66. A prevailing plaintiff is entitled to:
(1) Actual damages.
(2) Statutory damages of up to two hundred fifty thousand dollars ($250,000) per violation,
reduced to one hundred twenty-five thousand dollars ($125,000) if the material was blocked
before the filing of the complaint, or reduced to seventy-five thousand dollars ($75,000)
if the platform participates in and the material was reported through the NCMEC Take It
Down service before the filing of the complaint.
(3) Reasonable attorney's fees and costs.

(c) A depicted individual who is not a reporting user may bring a civil action against a
social media platform that violates subdivision (c) of Section 3273.66.

(d) Failure to comply with subdivisions (b), (c), or (d) of Section 3273.66 within 60 days
creates a rebuttable presumption of statutory damages liability.

(e) Civil penalties recovered by the Attorney General shall be deposited in the Survivor
Support Fund established under this section, after deducting reasonable attorney's fees.

SECTION 4. Section 3345.1 of the Civil Code is amended to read:

3345.1. (a) A social media platform shall not knowingly facilitate, aid, or abet the
commercial sexual exploitation of a minor or a nonminor dependent.

(b) A social media platform that violates this section shall be liable for statutory damages
between one million dollars ($1,000,000) and four million dollars ($4,000,000) per act.

(c) Social media platforms must submit biannual audit programs to the Attorney General
and, upon request, to other designated public officials, demonstrating that they have
implemented and maintained policies and procedures sufficient to prevent the knowing
facilitation of commercial sexual exploitation of minors.

(d) It is an affirmative defense to liability under this section if the platform submits to
twice-yearly third-party audits by qualified independent auditors and takes action within
30 days to mitigate detected risks.

SECTION 5. Severability.

If any provision of this act or its application to any person or circumstance is held invalid,
that invalidity shall not affect other provisions or applications that can be given effect
without the invalid provision or application.
