# Denmark Act on Supplementary Provisions to the AI Regulation

## Lov om supplerende bestemmelser til forordningen om kunstig intelligens

**Law No.:** 467 of 14 May 2025
**Adopted by:** Folketing (Danish Parliament)
**Parliamentary Adoption Date:** 8 May 2025
**Royal Assent / Signature:** 14 May 2025
**Entry into Force:** 2 August 2025 (mainland Denmark)
**Geographic Scope:** Applies to Denmark proper; does not apply to the Faroe Islands or Greenland
**Enabling instrument for:** EU Artificial Intelligence Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the "EU AI Act")

---

## Overview and Significance

Law No. 467 of 14 May 2025 (the "Danish AI Supplementary Act" or "Supplerende AI-lov") is Denmark's national implementing legislation for the EU Artificial Intelligence Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), which entered into force on 1 August 2024 and is directly applicable across all EU Member States. Denmark was one of the first EU Member States to enact national supplementary legislation, completing the designation of national competent authorities required by Article 70 of the EU AI Act before the August 2, 2025 deadline.

The Act does not re-implement the substantive AI rules contained in the EU AI Act — those rules apply directly as EU law. Rather, the Act:

1. **Designates** the national competent authorities responsible for supervising and enforcing the EU AI Act in Denmark;
2. **Allocates** market surveillance responsibilities among multiple Danish authorities according to their existing domain expertise;
3. **Establishes** the procedural powers of those authorities (inspection rights, information demands, remedial orders, temporary prohibitions);
4. **Creates** a domestic legal basis for administrative enforcement measures including administrative fines;
5. **Sets** procedural rules for coordination between authorities and with the European AI Office;
6. **Implements** provisions on complaints, appeals, and legal protection for individuals affected by AI systems.

---

## Background: The EU AI Act Framework

To understand the Danish Act, it is necessary to briefly summarize the EU AI Act framework it implements.

The EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) establishes a risk-tiered regulatory framework for AI systems:

- **Unacceptable Risk (Prohibited) AI:** A small category of AI applications prohibited outright, including social scoring by governments, real-time biometric surveillance in public spaces (with narrow exceptions), AI that exploits subconscious vulnerabilities, and AI that manipulates individuals against their own interests.

- **High-Risk AI:** A significant category of AI systems subject to pre-market conformity assessment, technical documentation, quality management, transparency obligations, human oversight requirements, and accuracy and robustness standards. High-risk applications include AI in critical infrastructure, educational and vocational training, employment, essential private and public services, law enforcement, migration and border control, and administration of justice.

- **Limited-Risk AI (Transparency Obligations):** AI systems that interact with humans (chatbots, emotion recognition, deepfake generators) must meet specific transparency requirements, including disclosure that the user is interacting with an AI system.

- **Minimal Risk:** Most AI systems fall into this category and are subject to voluntary codes of conduct rather than mandatory requirements.

- **General Purpose AI (GPAI) Models:** Foundation models and other large AI models that can be adapted for a wide range of uses are subject to specific obligations regarding transparency, documentation, and (for those posing systemic risk) safety evaluation and incident reporting.

The EU AI Act requires each Member State to designate one or more "national competent authorities" to act as market surveillance authorities, and to designate a single national supervisory authority to serve as the notifying authority (responsible for notifying the European Commission about conformity assessment bodies) and as Denmark's principal contact point with the European AI Office.

## Designated National Competent Authorities (Section 1–3)

The core function of the Danish Act is to designate the authorities responsible for supervising and enforcing the EU AI Act in Denmark. The Act makes the following designations:

### Digitaliseringsstyrelsen (Agency for Digital Government)

**Role:** Authorizing authority and central supervisory contact for Denmark under the EU AI Act.

**Designation:** Digitaliseringsstyrelsen is designated as Denmark's primary national competent authority and single point of contact with the European AI Office. It serves as the "notifying authority" responsible for assessing, designating, notifying, and monitoring conformity assessment bodies (notified bodies) that can certify high-risk AI systems in Denmark.

**Market Surveillance:** Digitaliseringsstyrelsen has primary market surveillance responsibility for AI systems deployed in sectors not otherwise allocated to specialized authorities.

**Coordination:** Digitaliseringsstyrelsen coordinates the activities of Denmark's market surveillance authorities, ensures coherent national implementation, and represents Denmark in the European AI Board.

**Information and Complaints:** Digitaliseringsstyrelsen operates the national information and complaints portal through which individuals and businesses can seek information about the EU AI Act and report potential violations.

### Datatilsynet (Danish Data Protection Authority)

**Role:** Market surveillance authority for AI systems that involve processing of personal data.

**Designation:** Datatilsynet is designated as a market surveillance authority with jurisdiction over AI systems insofar as their operation involves the processing of personal data and raises issues under the EU AI Act. This includes, in particular:

- AI systems in high-risk categories that process personal data of EU residents;
- Prohibited AI practices involving biometric identification, biometric categorization, or emotion recognition;
- AI systems used in law enforcement, border control, or justice administration that process personal data;
- AI systems that present specific risks to data subjects' rights.

**Rationale:** Datatilsynet already has extensive expertise in data processing regulation and enforcement under the GDPR and Danish data protection law. Its designation leverages this existing expertise and avoids jurisdictional fragmentation between the AI Act and GDPR for data-intensive AI use cases.

**Coordination with GDPR Enforcement:** The Act includes provisions to coordinate Datatilsynet's AI Act enforcement with its GDPR supervisory activities, including rules on information sharing between its AI Act and GDPR enforcement functions.

### Domstolsstyrelsen (Danish Court Administration)

**Role:** Market surveillance authority for AI systems deployed in the administration of justice and court systems.

**Designation:** Domstolsstyrelsen is designated as the market surveillance authority for AI systems used in or in connection with the administration of justice, including AI systems used by courts, prosecution services, and legal aid institutions in Denmark.

**Scope:** This includes high-risk AI systems under Annex III, point 8 of the EU AI Act (AI used to assist a judicial authority in researching and interpreting facts and the law and in applying the law to a concrete set of facts).

**Rationale:** Given the sensitivity of AI use in judicial decision-making and the importance of judicial independence, designating the Court Administration — which serves the courts rather than the executive branch — provides an appropriate institutional framework for supervising AI in justice contexts.

## Inspection and Information Powers (Section 4–8)

The Act grants Denmark's market surveillance authorities extensive powers to supervise AI providers, deployers, importers, and distributors operating in Denmark. Key powers include:

### Right of Inspection

Market surveillance authorities may conduct on-site inspections of any business premises, facilities, or IT systems relevant to the development, deployment, or use of AI systems within their supervisory remit. Inspections may be conducted:

- On an announced basis, with prior notice to the entity;
- On an unannounced basis, where the authority has reasonable grounds to believe that prior notice would defeat the purpose of the inspection.

Inspectors must carry appropriate identification and must provide the inspected entity with a summary of the inspection findings.

### Right to Demand Information

Market surveillance authorities may demand, in writing and within a specified time period, any information necessary for their supervisory functions, including:

- Technical documentation required under the EU AI Act;
- Records of testing, validation, and conformity assessment;
- Contracts with operators and users;
- Records of incidents and near-misses;
- Training data documentation;
- Information about the persons responsible for the AI system.

Failure to provide demanded information within the specified time period without reasonable excuse constitutes a violation of the Act and may give rise to an administrative fine.

### Right to Conduct Test Purchases and Technical Testing

Market surveillance authorities may acquire AI systems or services — including through undercover test purchases — to evaluate their compliance with the EU AI Act. Authorities may conduct technical testing of acquired AI systems.

### Cooperation Obligations

Persons and entities subject to supervision must cooperate with market surveillance authorities, provide access to premises, IT systems, and records, and designate contact persons for supervision purposes.

## Remedial Measures and Temporary Prohibitions (Section 9–13)

### Remedial Orders

Where a market surveillance authority establishes or reasonably suspects a violation of the EU AI Act, it may issue a remedial order requiring the provider, deployer, or other responsible party to take specified corrective actions within a specified time period. Remedial orders may require:

- Correction of non-compliant AI system characteristics;
- Implementation of required risk management measures;
- Provision of outstanding technical documentation;
- Implementation of transparency disclosures required under the Act;
- Restriction of the AI system's scope of use or target user base.

### Temporary Prohibitions

Where a market surveillance authority determines that an AI system poses a serious risk to health, safety, or fundamental rights — or where a provider or deployer is not cooperating with supervision — the authority may impose a temporary prohibition on:

- The placing on the market of the AI system;
- The putting into service of the AI system;
- The continued use of the AI system by identified deployers;
- The making available of the AI system to users.

Temporary prohibitions take immediate effect upon notification and remain in force until the authority lifts them or they are overturned on appeal.

### Recall and Withdrawal

In cases of serious risk, market surveillance authorities may order the recall of AI systems already deployed or their withdrawal from the Danish market.

### Publication of Decisions

The Act requires that final enforcement decisions (remedial orders, temporary prohibitions, fines) be published on the authority's website, subject to redaction of commercially confidential information. Publication serves both a deterrence function and a transparency function, enabling users and affected individuals to learn about compliance issues.

## Administrative Fines and Enforcement (Section 14–17)

### Relationship to EU AI Act Penalties

The EU AI Act (Article 99) establishes a penalty framework under which Member States must "lay down rules" on penalties applicable to violations. The Act provides Denmark's national legal basis for imposing these penalties.

**EU AI Act Penalty Ceiling:**
- Up to EUR 35 million or 7% of total worldwide annual turnover (whichever is higher) for violations related to prohibited AI practices;
- Up to EUR 15 million or 3% of total worldwide annual turnover for violations of other obligations;
- Up to EUR 7.5 million or 1.5% of total worldwide annual turnover for providing incorrect, incomplete, or misleading information to authorities.

### Bødeforelæg (Administrative Fine Settlements)

The Act introduces the Danish legal mechanism of *bødeforelæg* — on-the-spot administrative fine settlements — for less complex violations of the EU AI Act. This mechanism allows market surveillance authorities to offer a settlement to a violating entity that, if accepted, resolves the violation without formal administrative proceedings. Key features:

- Available for clear, uncontested violations;
- The fine offered in settlement must not exceed a statutory cap (set by ministerial order);
- Acceptance of the settlement is voluntary; if rejected, the case proceeds to formal enforcement proceedings;
- Settlement fines are generally lower than the maximum penalties available through formal proceedings, providing an incentive for early resolution.

### Criminal Penalties

Deliberate or grossly negligent violations of the EU AI Act's most serious obligations — particularly those involving prohibited AI practices — may also attract criminal liability under Danish criminal law, as supplemented by the Act.

## Appeals and Legal Remedies (Section 18–20)

### Administrative Appeals

Decisions by market surveillance authorities under the Act may be appealed to the relevant administrative appeals body or, where no specific appeals body is designated, directly to the courts. Appeal deadlines and procedures vary by authority:

- Decisions by Digitaliseringsstyrelsen may be appealed to the Ministry of Digitalisation within 4 weeks;
- Decisions by Datatilsynet may be appealed to the courts in accordance with standard GDPR judicial review procedures.

### Judicial Review

Final administrative decisions — including remedial orders, temporary prohibitions, and fines — may be challenged by judicial review in the ordinary Danish courts (Byretten/Landsretten/Højesteret depending on amount and significance).

### Rights of Affected Individuals

The Act includes provisions enabling individuals who believe their rights have been affected by a non-compliant AI system to file complaints with the relevant market surveillance authority. Authorities must acknowledge receipt, investigate complaints that raise substantive concerns, and provide the complainant with a summary of actions taken.

---

## Coordination with the European AI Office (Section 21–23)

The Act includes provisions implementing Denmark's obligations to cooperate with the European AI Office, established within the European Commission to oversee GPAI models and coordinate enforcement across Member States. These include:

- Obligations on Danish authorities to share information about enforcement actions with the European AI Office and other national competent authorities through the EU AI Act's cross-border notification system;
- Procedures for joint investigations with other national authorities in cross-border cases;
- Procedures for Denmark to request AI Office assistance or guidance on complex cases.

---

## Provisions on General Purpose AI Models (Section 24–26)

For GPAI (General Purpose AI) models, the EU AI Act assigns primary supervisory responsibility to the European AI Office rather than national authorities. The Danish Act implements the national dimension of GPAI oversight by:

- Designating Digitaliseringsstyrelsen as the Danish authority for receiving GPAI-related complaints;
- Establishing procedures for Digitaliseringsstyrelsen to forward complaints involving GPAI models to the European AI Office;
- Specifying the cooperation obligations of Danish-based GPAI providers with Danish authorities and the European AI Office.

---

## Provisions Addressing Specific High-Risk Sectors

### Employment and HR AI

Given the widespread use of AI in recruitment, performance monitoring, and HR management in Denmark, the Act includes provisions specifying how Digitaliseringsstyrelsen will prioritize supervision of high-risk AI systems in employment contexts. Particular attention is given to AI systems used in:

- Automated screening of job applications;
- Performance monitoring and assessment;
- Decisions about promotions, terminations, and disciplinary actions.

### AI in Public Services

The Act addresses the use of AI systems by public authorities in Denmark, noting that many Danish government services are already highly digitalized and that AI is increasingly integrated into public administration. Key provisions include:

- Obligations on public sector deployers to conduct fundamental rights impact assessments before deploying high-risk AI systems;
- Requirements for transparency in public sector AI deployment, including publication of registers of high-risk AI systems in use;
- Special obligations on authorities that use AI in decisions affecting Danish residents' access to public benefits, services, or rights.

---

## Entry into Force and Transitional Provisions

The Act entered into force on **2 August 2025**, the date on which the EU AI Act's provisions on prohibited AI practices and GPAI models became applicable across the EU. This alignment was deliberate, ensuring that Danish enforcement machinery was in place from the first day that the most critical EU AI Act obligations applied.

Transitional provisions address cases where AI systems were already in use in Denmark before the Act's entry into force, generally providing an opportunity for providers and deployers to bring existing systems into compliance before enforcement commences.

The Act does not apply to the Faroe Islands or Greenland, which are autonomous territories of the Danish Realm that have a distinct relationship with the EU. Separate consideration of AI regulation in those territories is expected to follow in due course.

---

## Significance for EU AI Act Implementation

Denmark's early adoption of national AI Act supplementary legislation made it a notable example of proactive implementation among EU Member States. The Act's division of supervisory responsibilities across three specialized authorities — reflecting the view that AI regulation is most effectively integrated into existing domain expertise rather than consolidated in a new AI-specific agency — has been noted as one possible model for other Member States.

The Act's inclusion of *bødeforelæg* (administrative fine settlements) as an enforcement tool reflects a broader Danish administrative law tradition of swift, efficient resolution of clear violations, and is expected to be particularly useful for addressing less complex transparency and documentation violations by smaller AI providers.
