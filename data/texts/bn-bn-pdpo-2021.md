# Personal Data Protection Order 2021
**Personal Data Protection Order 2021 (S 96/2021)**
*Brunei Darussalam — Enacted May 2021; Effective January 1, 2022*

---

## Overview / Background

The Personal Data Protection Order 2021 (PDPO) is Brunei Darussalam's first comprehensive personal data protection statute, enacted by His Majesty the Sultan and Yang Di-Pertuan of Brunei Darussalam under the Emergency (Essential Powers) Act. The Order was gazetted in May 2021 and entered into force on January 1, 2022, with full implementation phased through 2024.

The PDPO was developed by the Authority for Info-communications Technology Industry of Brunei Darussalam (AITI), which serves as the primary enforcement authority. AITI has statutory powers to investigate complaints, issue enforcement notices, and impose financial penalties. The PDPO brings Brunei into alignment with ASEAN's Model AI Governance Framework and broader regional data protection norms, positioning Brunei as a credible digital economy participant in the context of ASEAN frameworks.

The PDPO applies to any organization (private sector entity) that controls the collection, holding, processing, or use of personal data in Brunei, or that processes personal data in Brunei on behalf of another organization. Certain exemptions apply to: personal or domestic data processing; journalistic, literary, or artistic purposes; data already publicly available; and national security or defense-related processing. Public sector entities are governed by separate administrative arrangements rather than the PDPO.

## Key Provisions / Chapters

### Nine Data Protection Principles
The PDPO is structured around nine data protection principles, modeled on frameworks in Singapore and other Commonwealth jurisdictions:

1. **Purpose Limitation Principle** — Personal data may only be collected for purposes that a reasonable person would consider appropriate in the circumstances, and must only be used for those purposes.
2. **Consent Principle** — Organizations must obtain the consent of individuals before or at the time of collecting their personal data, subject to specific exceptions (e.g., where necessary for contract performance, legal obligation, or national interest).
3. **Accuracy Principle** — Organizations must make reasonable efforts to ensure personal data is accurate and complete where it may be used to make decisions affecting the individual.
4. **Retention Limitation Principle** — Personal data must not be retained for longer than necessary for the purpose it was collected; appropriate retention schedules and disposal procedures must be implemented.
5. **Transfer Limitation Principle** — Personal data may not be transferred to a country or territory outside Brunei unless the receiving jurisdiction provides a standard of protection at least comparable to that under the PDPO, or the transfer is otherwise authorized.
6. **Data Access Principle** — Individuals have the right to request access to their personal data held by an organization and information about the use or disclosure of that data within one year prior to the request.
7. **Correction Principle** — Individuals have the right to request correction of errors or omissions in their personal data held by an organization.
8. **Openness Principle** — Organizations must make information about their data protection policies and practices publicly available.
9. **Protection / Security Principle** — Organizations must implement reasonable security arrangements to protect personal data against unauthorized access, collection, use, disclosure, copying, modification, disposal, or similar risks.

### Sensitive Personal Data
- The PDPO identifies categories of sensitive personal data requiring heightened protection: health and medical data; genetic and biometric data; racial or ethnic origin; religious or philosophical beliefs; criminal offenses and proceedings; and financial information.
- Processing of sensitive personal data requires explicit consent or falls within narrowly defined exceptions.
- Biometric data used for identification purposes is classified as sensitive and requires explicit consent or a specific legal basis.

### Data Subject Rights
- **Right of access:** Individuals may request confirmation of whether their personal data is held, and receive a copy within 30 days (extendable).
- **Right to correction:** Individuals may request correction of inaccurate or incomplete data; organizations must respond within 30 days.
- **Right to withdraw consent:** Individuals may withdraw consent at any time; organizations must cease processing within a reasonable time of withdrawal.
- **Right to know about disclosures:** Individuals may request information about third parties to whom their data has been disclosed in the preceding 12 months.

### Cross-Border Transfer Restrictions
- Transfers of personal data outside Brunei are prohibited unless: (1) the destination country provides an equivalent standard of protection; (2) the transfer is covered by contractual arrangements providing equivalent protection; or (3) specific exceptions apply (e.g., the individual has consented with knowledge of the reduced protections).
- AITI may maintain a list of approved jurisdictions and approve specific transfer mechanisms.

### Mandatory Data Breach Response
- While the PDPO does not mandate notification to data subjects in all breach scenarios (distinguishing it from GDPR and some other regimes), organizations are expected to have incident response procedures and to notify AITI in cases of significant breaches.
- Organizations must implement reasonable measures to prevent breaches and must be able to demonstrate their security arrangements upon AITI request.

### Phased Implementation
- Phase 1 (January 1, 2022): Core data protection principles, consent requirements, and individual rights provisions came into force.
- Phase 2 (2022-2024): Implementing regulations on specific sectors, cross-border transfer mechanisms, and enforcement procedures issued progressively.
- Full implementation with all enforcement mechanisms activated by 2024.

## Enforcement & Penalties

- **Enforcement notices:** AITI may issue enforcement notices requiring organizations to take remedial steps within a specified period.
- **Financial penalties:** Organizations may be fined up to BND 100,000 (~$75,000 USD) for breaches of the data protection principles.
- **Criminal penalties:** Serious violations involving intentional or reckless breaches (e.g., unauthorized selling of personal data) may attract criminal penalties including imprisonment of up to 3 years.
- **AITI investigations:** AITI has power to investigate complaints from data subjects, compel organizations to produce documents and records, and conduct on-site inspections.
- **Appeals:** Organizations or individuals aggrieved by AITI enforcement decisions may appeal to the Minister.
