# Ley No. 18.331 — Protección de Datos Personales y Acción de Habeas Data — Uruguay
**Ley No. 18.331**
*Uruguay — August 11, 2008*

---

## Overview

Uruguay's Law 18.331 of August 11, 2008, on Personal Data Protection and the Habeas Data Action is the country's primary data protection law and one of the most internationally recognized data protection frameworks in Latin America. Uruguay received adequacy recognition from the European Commission in 2012 under EU Directive 95/46/EC — the first and only Latin American country to achieve this status as of 2026. This recognition significantly facilitates data flows between Uruguay and EU member states. The law establishes the Unidad Reguladora y de Control de Datos Personales (URCDP) as the supervisory authority and provides both administrative remedies through URCDP and judicial remedies through the habeas data action.

---

## Key Provisions

### Unidad Reguladora y de Control de Datos Personales (URCDP)
- Independent supervisory authority with powers of registration, investigation, and enforcement
- Maintains a registry of personal data databases
- Issues binding opinions and guidelines on data protection
- Investigates complaints from data subjects and conducts ex officio investigations
- May order suspension of processing and impose sanctions
- International coordination: URCDP recognized as adequate supervisory authority by EU

### Seven Data Protection Principles
- Lawfulness: processing must have a legal basis
- Purpose: data collected for specific, explicit, and legitimate purposes
- Consent: data subjects must give informed and unambiguous consent unless another lawful basis applies
- Quality: data must be accurate, adequate, and relevant
- Security: appropriate technical and organizational measures
- Reserved or confidentiality: data handlers bound by professional secrecy
- Accountability: controllers responsible for demonstrating compliance

### Lawful Processing Bases
- Unambiguous consent of the data subject
- Performance of a contract to which the data subject is party
- Compliance with a legal obligation
- Protection of vital interests
- Legitimate interests of the controller (subject to balancing)

### Sensitive Personal Data
- Special categories requiring explicit consent or specific justification: health and medical data, racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, criminal record, biometric data
- Sensitive data databases subject to mandatory URCDP registration and heightened security requirements

### Data Subject Rights (ARCO)
- Right of Access: to personal data held by any controller
- Right of Rectification: of inaccurate or incomplete data
- Right of Cancellation: deletion of data no longer necessary or processed unlawfully
- Right of Opposition: to processing for direct marketing or other legitimate grounds
- Rights exercisable free of charge within specified response timeframes

### Habeas Data Judicial Action
- Individuals may bring a habeas data action before Uruguayan courts to enforce their data rights
- Courts may order access, correction, deletion, or suppression of personal data
- Provides a private right of action independent of URCDP administrative process

### Mandatory Database Registration
- Data controllers must register all personal data databases with URCDP
- Registration discloses type of data, purposes, data subjects, and recipients
- Sensitive data databases subject to heightened registration requirements

### Cross-Border Data Transfers
- Transfers outside Uruguay permitted to countries with adequate protection (including EU member states following adequacy decision)
- Transfers to non-adequate countries require URCDP authorization or specific safeguards
- Uruguay's EU adequacy status means EU → Uruguay transfers also facilitated

## Enforcement & Penalties

- URCDP may issue compliance orders, warnings, and suspension of processing
- Administrative fines for violations
- Criminal penalties for serious offenses including unlawful processing and violation of data subject rights
- Habeas data judicial remedy provides private enforcement mechanism
- Civil liability: individuals may seek compensation for harm caused by unlawful processing
