# Basic Act on the Development of Artificial Intelligence and the Establishment of a Foundation for Trustworthiness

**Republic of Korea — Law No. 20676**
**Enacted: 21 January 2025 · Effective: 22 January 2026**

*Source: Korean Law Information Center, Ministry of Government Legislation. Translation: CSET / Etcetera Language Group, Inc., 9 July 2025.*

---

## Chapter 1 — General Provisions

### Article 1 (Purpose)

The purpose of this Act is to safeguard human rights and dignity, raise quality of life, and strengthen national competitiveness, by laying down the essential rules required for the sound development of artificial intelligence (AI) and for building a foundation of trust in it.

### Article 2 (Definitions)

1. **"Artificial Intelligence"** means the electronic implementation of human intellectual capabilities such as learning, reasoning, perception, judgement, and language understanding.

2. **"AI System"** means an AI-based system that, with varying degrees of autonomy and adaptability, infers outputs — such as predictions, recommendations, or decisions — that affect physical or virtual environments in pursuit of a given objective.

3. **"AI Technology"** means the hardware, software, or applied technologies needed to implement AI.

4. **"High-Impact AI"** means an AI system whose use could materially affect human life, safety, or fundamental rights, deployed in any of the following domains: energy supply; drinking-water production; healthcare services; medical devices and digital medical devices; nuclear facility safety; biometric information analysis for criminal investigation or arrest; decisions or evaluations that meaningfully affect an individual's rights or obligations (e.g. employment screening or credit assessment); operation or management of transport vehicles, facilities, or systems; decisions by National Institutions affecting members of the public (e.g. eligibility determination or fee assessment for public services); student assessment in early-childhood, primary, or secondary education; or any other field designated by Presidential Decree.

5. **"Generative AI"** means an AI system that produces outputs — text, sound, images, video, or other forms — by mimicking the structure and characteristics of input data.

6. **"AI Industry"** means industries that develop, manufacture, produce, or distribute AI products, or that supply related AI services.

7. **"AI Business Operator"** means a corporation, organisation, individual, or government agency engaged in AI-industry activities, either as an AI Developer (develops and supplies AI) or as an AI-using Business Operator (supplies AI products or services using AI obtained from a developer).

8. **"User"** means anyone who receives AI products or services.

9. **"Affected Person"** means anyone whose life, physical safety, or fundamental rights are materially affected by an AI product or service.

### Article 3 (Basic Principles and Duties of the State)

1. AI technology and the AI industry should develop in a direction that promotes safety and reliability and improves quality of life.

2. Affected persons should receive a clear and meaningful explanation of the principal criteria and reasoning used to produce AI outcomes, to the extent technically and reasonably feasible.

3. National and local government bodies should respect the creative spirit of AI business operators and work to create a safe environment for AI use.

4. National and local government bodies should design policies that allow everyone to adapt smoothly to the social, economic, cultural, and everyday-life changes brought about by AI.

### Article 4 (Scope of Application)

1. This Act applies to conduct occurring outside the Republic of Korea where that conduct affects the domestic market or users in the Republic of Korea.

2. This Act does not apply to AI developed and used solely for national defence or national security, as specified by Presidential Decree.

### Article 5 (Relationship to Other Laws)

Unless other statutes provide special rules concerning AI, this Act shall apply. When other laws on AI are enacted or amended, they should be aligned with the purposes of this Act.

## Chapter 2 — Promotion System

### Article 6 (Establishment of the AI Basic Plan)

Every three years, the Minister of Science and ICT shall establish, amend, and implement an AI basic plan to promote AI technology and the AI industry and strengthen national competitiveness, subject to deliberation and resolution by the National AI Committee. The Basic Plan shall include: the basic direction and strategy for AI policy; talent training; legal, institutional, and cultural measures for a sound AI society including diffusion of AI ethics; financial resources and investment direction; measures for ensuring fairness, transparency, accountability, and safety of AI; and the direction of AI-technology development and societal responses.

### Article 7 (National AI Committee)

1. A National AI Committee ("the Committee") shall be established under the President to deliberate and resolve major policy matters for AI development and the establishment of a trust foundation.

2. The Committee shall consist of no more than 45 members including a chairperson and vice-chairperson. The President serves as chairperson.

3. Committee members shall not disclose confidential information learned in the course of their duties, nor use such information for purposes other than their duties.

4. The Committee shall remain in existence for five years from the date this Act takes effect.

### Article 8 (Functions of the Committee)

The Committee shall deliberate and resolve on: the Basic Plan; AI-related policy matters; AI R&D and investment strategy; regulations obstructing AI-industry development; AI data centre infrastructure; AI use across industrial sectors; international cooperation on AI norms; the regulation of high-impact AI; and social changes and policy responses concerning high-impact AI. The Committee may issue recommendations or opinions to National Institutions, AI business operators, and others on proper AI use, AI ethics, and safety and reliability.

### Article 11 (AI Policy Center)

The Minister of Science and ICT may designate an AI Policy Center to handle AI policy development and international norms formulation.

### Article 12 (AI Safety Research Institute)

The Minister of Science and ICT may operate an AI Safety Research Institute to define and analyse AI-safety risks, conduct AI-safety policy research, research evaluation criteria and methods, research safety technology and standardisation, and engage in international exchange on AI safety.

## Chapter 3 — Development of AI Technology and Industry

### Article 13 (Support for AI-Technology Development and Safe Use)

The government may support AI-technology R&D, testing, evaluation, deployment, transfer, and commercialisation. The government may also support R&D for: safety-by-design technologies; emergency-stop functions; privacy-protective design; social impact assessments; technologies ensuring AI respects human dignity and fundamental rights; and education and outreach on safe AI use.

### Article 14 (Standardisation of AI Technology)

The government may pursue standardisation of AI technology, learning data, and AI safety and reliability; publish standards and recommend compliance; support private-sector standardisation; and cooperate with international standards bodies.

### Article 15 (Policies for AI Learning Data)

The Minister of Science and ICT shall pursue measures to facilitate the production, collection, management, distribution, and use of data used for AI development ("learning data"), and shall build and operate an integrated provision system for free private-sector access.

### Article 16 (Support for AI Technology Adoption)

National and local governments may support enterprises and public bodies in adopting and using AI technology, including consulting, training, and financial support for SMEs.

### Article 17 (Special Support for SMEs)

SMEs shall be given priority consideration in AI support policies. The Minister may support SMEs in implementing high-impact AI safety measures and impact assessments.

### Article 19 (Promotion of AI Convergence)

The government shall design measures for converging the AI industry with other industries and expanding AI use across all sectors.

### Article 20 (Institutional Improvement)

The government shall work to improve relevant institutions — including the development of laws and regulations — in order to develop the AI industry and build a foundation of trust.

### Article 21 (Securing Expert Talent)

The Minister of Science and ICT shall train and support AI experts, and pursue measures to attract overseas experts in AI and AI technology.

### Article 22 (Support for International Cooperation and Market Expansion)

The government shall track international AI trends, promote international cooperation, and support AI-industry overseas market entry.

## Chapter 4 — Ensuring AI Ethics and Reliability

### Article 27 (AI Ethics Principles)

The government may establish and announce AI ethics principles including: safety and reliability to prevent harm in AI development and use; ensuring AI products and services are freely and conveniently accessible to everyone; and AI development and use that contributes to human wellbeing and prosperity. The Minister shall gather opinions from society to develop practical measures for putting the ethics principles into practice.

### Article 28 (Establishment of Private Autonomous AI Ethics Committees)

Educational institutions, research centres, and AI business operators may establish a private autonomous AI ethics committee to comply with the ethics principles. Such committees shall: ensure ethics compliance in AI R&D and use; investigate AI safety and human-rights protection; investigate and supervise AI R&D procedures and outcomes; educate researchers and employees on ethics principles; and prepare field-specific AI ethical guidelines. Committees shall include persons not affiliated with the institution and shall not be composed entirely of one gender.

### Article 29 (Policies for Establishing a Foundation of Trust for AI)

The government shall establish policies to minimise risks to citizens' lives from AI and build a foundation of trust for safe AI use, including: creation of a safe and reliable AI-use environment; forecasting AI's effects on citizens' daily lives; support for safety and certification technologies; education and outreach on trustworthy AI and AI ethics; support for AI business operators in implementing autonomous safety regulations; and support for private-sector ethical guideline development.

### Article 30 (Support for AI Verification and Certification)

The Minister of Science and ICT may support verification and certification activities to ensure AI safety and reliability, including dissemination of development guidelines, research support, equipment and systems support, and talent training. AI business operators shall endeavour to obtain verification and certification before supplying high-impact AI. Where National Institutions intend to use high-impact AI, they shall give priority to AI-based products and services that have received verification and certification.

### Article 31 (Duty to Ensure AI Transparency)

1. AI business operators that supply products or services using high-impact AI or generative AI shall notify users in advance that the product or service is AI-based.

2. When supplying generative AI products or services, AI business operators shall notify users in advance that the products or services are GenAI-generated.

3. AI business operators shall clearly notify or label users where virtual sounds, images, or videos are AI-generated and may be hard to distinguish from authentic ones. Where the output forms part of an artistic or creative work, notification or labelling may be done in a manner that does not interfere with its display or enjoyment.

### Article 32 (Duty to Ensure AI Safety)

1. AI business operators shall implement the following measures for AI systems whose cumulative compute used for training meets or exceeds the threshold set by Presidential Decree:

   a) Identification, assessment, and mitigation of risks across the AI lifecycle;

   b) Building a risk-management system to monitor and respond to AI-related safety incidents.

2. AI business operators shall submit the results of these measures to the Minister of Science and ICT.

### Article 33 (Confirmation of High-Impact AI)

AI business operators that supply AI-based products or services shall review in advance whether the AI qualifies as high-impact AI and may request the Minister of Science and ICT to confirm. The Minister shall provide guidelines on criteria for and examples of high-impact AI.

### Article 34 (Duties of High-Impact AI Business Operators)

1. AI business operators that supply high-impact AI or AI-based products and services shall implement the following measures to secure the safety and reliability of high-impact AI:

   a) Establishment and operation of a risk-management plan;

   b) Development and implementation, to the extent technically feasible, of a plan for explaining AI outputs — including the principal criteria used to derive them and an overview of the learning data used;

   c) Establishment and operation of user-protection measures;

   d) Human management and supervision of high-impact AI;

   e) Preparation and retention of documentation evidencing the measures taken to ensure AI safety and reliability.

2. The Minister of Science and ICT may set and publish detailed content for these measures and recommend that AI business operators comply.

### Article 35 (High-Impact AI Impact Assessment)

AI business operators that supply products or services using high-impact AI shall make efforts to assess in advance the impact on fundamental human rights. Where National Institutions intend to use high-impact AI products or services, they shall give priority to those for which an impact assessment has been carried out.

### Article 36 (Designation of a Domestic Representative)

AI business operators that have no address or place of business in the Republic of Korea, and that meet user or revenue thresholds set by Presidential Decree, shall appoint a domestic representative and report the appointment to the Minister of Science and ICT.

## Chapter 5 — Supplementary Provisions

### Article 40 (Fact-Finding Surveys)

1. The Minister of Science and ICT may require AI business operators to submit relevant data or carry out investigations where a violation of Article 31(2) or (3), Article 32(1) or (2), or Article 34(1) is found or suspected, or where a report or complaint of such violation is received.

2. Where the Minister concludes that an AI business operator has violated this Act, the Minister may order the operator to cease or remedy the violation.

---

## Chapter 6 — Penalties

### Article 42 (Punishments)

A person who discloses confidential Committee information or uses it for purposes other than their duties shall be punished by imprisonment of up to three years or a fine of up to 30 million won.

### Article 43 (Administrative Fines)

An administrative fine of up to 30 million won shall be imposed on any person who: fails to provide notification under Article 31(1); fails to designate a domestic representative under Article 36(1); or fails to comply with a cease or corrective order under Article 40(3).
