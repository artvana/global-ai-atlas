## CHAPTER ONE — GENERAL PROVISIONS

### Article 1 — Definitions

In applying this Law, the following terms shall have the meanings indicated unless context requires otherwise:

- **Ministry**: The Ministry designated by decree.
- **Minister**: The Minister designated by decree.
- **Person**: Any natural or legal person.
- **Artificial Intelligence**: A set of characteristics distinguishing computer programs, robots, and intelligent machines that make them simulate human mental capabilities and modes of operation. Key characteristics include the ability to learn, understand, think, listen, speak, move, deduce, predict, react, and provide solutions to situations not programmed into the machine.
- **AI Technologies**: The methods and tools used in developing and implementing AI applications and systems.
- **AI Systems and Applications**: Software based on AI technologies to perform data analysis, information extraction, decision-making, and problem-solving in an automated manner.
- **Robot**: A movable, programmed machine that performs repetitive tasks and executes specific instructions simulating human behavior.
- **Dangerous Robots and Intelligent Machines**: Any machine whose natural path cannot be controlled, and whose use results in damage or security, economic, social, or environmental disasters.
- **Algorithms**: A specific series of steps and instructions used to solve a problem or complete a task, guiding computers and intelligent systems in performing complex tasks. The efficiency and effectiveness of an AI system depends on the algorithms it uses.
- **Processing**: Any operation performed on data and information by automated or non-automated means — including collection, recording, organizing, classifying, copying, storing, altering, modifying, retrieving, using, disclosing, broadcasting, publishing, transferring, making available, merging, blocking, erasing, or destroying.
- **Processor**: The person who undertakes data processing, whether personally or by controlling a machine.
- **Programming**: Writing instructions and directing commands to computer or other devices, or receiving sound and image in electronic communications systems.
- **Programmer**: The person who programs the computer, develops its software, or introduces various software types.
- **Developer**: Any person who develops AI software and systems for the purpose of placing them on the market or putting them into service in their name or trademark, whether for compensation or free of charge.
- **User**: Any person who uses AI systems subject to their authority and control.
- **Cyber Attacks**: Any attack on computer devices or stored information to access or attempt to access this data with intent to alter, disable, destroy, control, or steal it.
- **AI Unit**: The unit concerned with implementing this Law, affiliated with the Ministry designated by decree.

### Article 2 — Purpose

This Law aims to regulate AI technologies and their uses for peaceful purposes.

### Article 3 — Scope

This Law applies to programming, processing, or development operations of all AI systems and technologies carried out by any person residing in the Kingdom of Bahrain or having a place of business therein.

**The following are excluded:**

a) Operations related to national security undertaken by the Ministry of Defense, Ministry of Interior, National Guard, National Security Agency, or other security agencies and government bodies.

b) Operations carried out by Kingdom bodies under international agreements for law enforcement and judicial cooperation.

c) Any entity within the Kingdom working in AI exempted by Council of Ministers decision.

d) Operations carried out by individuals for personal or family purposes, used peacefully.

### Article 4 — National Strategy

The Council of Ministers shall develop an integrated strategy to support AI through:

1. Encouraging AI use across all sectors — education, health, engineering, tourism, sports, media, law, justice, sciences.
2. Transferring and localizing AI technology in cooperation with the private sector.
3. Encouraging AI investment.
4. Facilitating licensing and company formation in AI.
5. Encouraging national talent in AI.
6. Protecting AI-related intellectual property rights.
7. Encouraging scientific research and organizing training programs.
8. Including AI subjects in school curricula.

## CHAPTER TWO — DUTIES AND PROHIBITIONS

### Article 5 — Conditions and Standards

AI programs, processes, applications, and uses must:

1. Not infringe upon constitutional rights and freedoms.
2. Not affect the foundations of Islamic faith and the unity of the people, nor provoke division, sectarianism, or violate public order and morals.
3. Be used for peaceful purposes.
4. Have no harmful effect on public health or the environment.
5. Not infringe upon legal rights established and acquired by children.
6. Not violate national legislation and international agreements related to AI, except matters under reservation.
7. Be accompanied by necessary solutions to prevent cyber attacks.
8. Be of high accuracy and security, and capable of update and development.

The Minister may add further conditions based on AI Unit study.

### Article 6 — Design Requirements

The design, programming, processing, and development of AI must be:

1. Clear and appropriate, so that service recipients and users can understand and operate the systems reasonably.
2. Conducted such that those in charge can supervise its performance and control its paths.
3. Free from bias or discrimination among people. Where bias arises, necessary measures must be taken — modifying algorithms or retraining the machine using clearer data — to ensure integrity.

### Article 7 — Training Obligations

Developers, programmers, and AI providers must educate and train their employees and subordinates who deal with AI systems, and follow up on transferring expertise and the latest technologies.

### Article 8 — Sectoral Use

AI may be used in surgical operations, radiology, early disease prediction, examination, diagnosis, and treatment. **Patient prior consent is required when AI is used in surgical operations.**

The Supreme Council for Health, in coordination with the Minister of Health and the National Health Regulatory Authority, shall issue decisions regulating AI use in the medical sector.

AI may also be used in education, engineering, tourism, sports, media, legal, judicial, scientific, and other sectors, with the relevant sector Minister issuing regulating decisions.

### Article 9 — Prohibited Programs

The following are prohibited:

1. Compositing personal images affecting reputation, honor, or standing.
2. Manipulating official speeches, statements, and declarations.
3. Manipulating textual, audio, or visual content of persons without their consent.
4. Manipulating personal, health, and professional data.
5. Any other cases determined by Ministerial decision based on AI Unit proposal.

### Article 10 — Dangerous Robots

The use of dangerous robots or intelligent machines whose paths cannot be controlled is prohibited. The Executive Regulations shall set out tables of such robots and machines.

### Article 11 — Conditions for Robot Use

Using robots in services, manufacturing, or medical intervention requires:

1. Modern and high quality.
2. Advanced and safe in use and results.
3. Under the control of their operators.
4. Use must not result in unusual risks.

The Executive Regulations shall determine specifications and any additional conditions.

### Article 12 — Decisions Affecting Human Life

It is prohibited to make decisions related to a person's life, freedom, or bodily integrity by **exclusively** relying on AI systems.

### Article 13 — Service Provision

AI may be used in providing services, concluding contracts, and issuing regulatory decisions and operational instructions.

**Total reliance on AI is prohibited for individual decisions requiring selection, evaluation, and harmonization.**

### Article 14 — Prohibited Purposes

AI use is prohibited for:

1. Inciting sedition, unrest, sabotage, calling for terrorist acts, or overthrowing or changing the political system.
2. Harming others or their data, deception, manipulation, or misleading.

---

## CHAPTER THREE — THE AI UNIT

### Article 15 — Establishment and Functions

The Ministry shall establish an AI Unit with the following tasks:

1. Monitoring compliance with this Law.
2. Establishing sustainable frameworks for AI safety and security.
3. Tightening control over AI systems to limit misuse risks.
4. Establishing strong security to protect confidential information and personal data.
5. Developing training plans on AI systems, including how to add new data and develop self-purification and encryption for harmful data.
6. Granting licenses for programming, processing, and the introduction and development of modern systems.
7. Protecting intellectual property rights in AI.
8. Proposing AI use standards consistent with international ethics and standards, presented to the Minister for decision after Council of Ministers review.
9. Cooperating with competent authorities using AI, and issuing written opinions and recommendations.
10. Investigating reports and complaints related to violations.

### Article 16 — Reporting

Developers, programmers, and users must inform the AI Unit of any incidents, violations, or breaches.

The Unit must handle reports, information, or complaints with sufficient speed to mitigate harm, while respecting the confidentiality of information obtained.

### Article 17 — Judicial Officers

AI Unit employees seconded by decision of the Minister of Justice in agreement with the Minister shall have the status of judicial law enforcement officers for crimes under this Law related to their duties. They have the right to:
- Establish such crimes
- Search, gather evidence, seize violations, and write reports
- Reports are referred to the Public Prosecution by Ministerial decision

These officers may not enter residential places without permission from the Public Prosecution or investigating judge.

---

## CHAPTER FOUR — LICENSING, GRIEVANCE AND APPEAL

### Article 18 — Licensing Requirement

Engaging in business related to programming, processing, or developing AI systems, or selling them, **without a license is prohibited.**

### Article 19 — License Application

Submitted to the AI Unit, which must decide within thirty days. Lapse of this period is implicit rejection.

The Executive Regulations shall set out application conditions, required documents, and procedures.

### Article 20 — Grievance and Appeal

A rejected applicant may submit a grievance to the Minister within thirty days of notification or implicit rejection. The Minister must decide within thirty days, otherwise the grievance is rejected.

The Minister's rejection may be appealed before the competent court within sixty days.

The Executive Regulations shall set out grievance procedures.

---

## CHAPTER FIVE — CIVIL LIABILITY

### Article 21 — Damages from Programming

A person harmed by programming, processing, or development work may demand compensation from the programmer, processor, or developer (jointly if multiple).

### Article 22 — Owner Liability

The owner of a robot or electronically programmed machine is liable for damage caused by it, unless the damage arises from a foreign cause beyond their control.

---

## CHAPTER SIX — ADMINISTRATIVE SANCTIONS AND PENALTIES

### Article 23 — Administrative Sanctions

Without prejudice to penalties under this Law, the AI Unit may impose:

1. **Warning** to stop the violation and remove its causes immediately or within a set period.
2. **License withdrawal** for AI programming, processing, or development.
3. **Daily administrative fine** to compel cessation:
   - Up to **BD 100/day** for first-time violation
   - Up to **BD 200/day** for repeated violation within three years
4. **Cap**: total administrative fines may not exceed **BD 10,000**.

### Article 24 — Penalty for Article 14(2) Violation

Without prejudice to harsher penalties under the Penal Code or other laws, violators of Article 14(2) face a fine **not exceeding BD 1,000**.

### Article 25 — Penalty up to BD 2,000

Fine **up to BD 2,000** for:

1. Processing, programming, or introducing AI systems infringing personal liberty, private life sanctity, or contradicting societal customs and traditions.
2. Designing programs and systems leading to discrimination among persons of equal legal status.

### Article 26 — Penalty BD 2,000–5,000

Fine **between BD 2,000 and BD 5,000** for:

1. Granting AI systems exclusive authority to make decisions on a person's life, freedom, or bodily integrity.
2. Using dangerous robots whose paths cannot be controlled.
3. Using AI systems for illegal purposes.

### Article 27 — Operating Without License

Fine **between BD 1,000 and BD 10,000** for engaging in programming, processing, introducing, or developing systems without a license.

### Article 28 — Manipulation Offenses

**Imprisonment and a fine between BD 5,000 and BD 20,000**, or either, for:

1. Manipulating official speeches, statements, and declarations.
2. Manipulating textual, audio, or visual content of persons without consent, with intent to harm.
3. Using AI systems for deception, manipulation, or misleading.

**Aggravated penalty** if offender is a public employee or person entrusted with a public service: imprisonment **at least one year** and fine **between BD 10,000 and BD 20,000**, or either.

### Article 29 — Sedition and Terrorism

**Imprisonment** for anyone who intentionally uses AI to incite sedition, political unrest, sabotage, call for terrorist acts, or overthrow or change the political system.

---

## CHAPTER SEVEN — FINAL PROVISIONS

### Article 30 — Crimes Committed via AI

Except where this Law has special provisions, anyone committing a crime under the Penal Code or any other law **by means of AI** shall receive the penalty prescribed for that crime.

### Article 31 — Attempts

Attempting any crime under this Law is punishable by **no more than half** the maximum penalty for the completed crime.

### Article 32 — Confiscation

Without prejudice to bona fide third party rights, the court may, upon conviction, confiscate devices, programs, tools, or systems used in committing crimes under this Law.

### Article 33 — Liability of Legal Persons

Without prejudice to natural person liability, a legal person faces a fine **double** the prescribed amount if a crime is committed in its name, on its account, or for its benefit, resulting from action, omission, agreement, concealment, or gross negligence by its subordinates.

In case of recurrence, the court may order **dissolution** of the legal person or **closure** (permanent or temporary) of the headquarters where the crime occurred.

### Article 34 — Compliance Period

Persons engaged in AI processing, programming, or development must adjust their situation within **six months** of entry into force.

### Article 35 — International Treaties

This Law does not prejudice rights established under international agreements and treaties in force in the Kingdom.

### Article 36 — Risk Classification Tables

The Executive Regulations shall set tables classifying high-risk programs, processes, and systems.

### Article 37 — Executive Regulations

The Minister shall issue the Executive Regulations within **three months** of publication in the Official Gazette.

### Article 38 — Implementation

The Prime Minister and Ministers shall implement this Law, which enters into force **six months after publication** in the Official Gazette.

*Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa*
*King of the Kingdom of Bahrain*

# EXPLANATORY MEMORANDUM

In accordance with Article 92 of the Internal Regulations of the Shura Council (Decree-Law 55/2002), this proposed law is based on the following:

## I. Constitutional Texts Relating to the Proposal

**Article 6** of the Constitution provides that "The State preserves the Arab and Islamic heritage, contributes to the march of human civilization, works to strengthen ties between Islamic countries, and achieves the aspirations of the Arab nation for unity and progress."

**Article 7** provides that "(a) The State sponsors the sciences, literature, and arts, encourages scientific research, and ensures educational and cultural services for citizens... (d) The State guarantees the inviolability of houses of learning."

**Article 23** provides that "Freedom of opinion and scientific research is guaranteed. Every person has the right to express their opinion and publish it by speech, writing, or otherwise, in accordance with the conditions and procedures specified by law, without prejudice to the foundations of Islamic faith and the unity of the people, and in a manner that does not provoke division or sectarianism."

**Article 92(a)** provides that "Fifteen members of the Shura Council or the Council of Representatives have the right to request a constitutional amendment proposal, and any member of the two councils has the right to propose laws..."

## II. Basic Principles of the Proposal

1. **Strengthening constitutional values** of the reform project of His Majesty the King — encouraging scientific research and contributing to human civilization, which requires using modern AI technologies in service of all scientific, political, economic, and social fields.

2. **Implementing royal directives** from His Majesty's speech opening the Sixth Legislative Term, particularly regarding the development of services for citizens. AI use in fast and accurate citizen service represents a sound path to implementing those directives.

3. **Achieving fast, beneficial results** in healthcare, agriculture, education and training, infrastructure, energy, transport and logistics, public services, security, justice, resource and energy efficiency, and climate change mitigation and adaptation through prediction and digital solutions.

4. **Addressing AI misuse risks** — such as manipulation of images, data, and political statements, making it difficult to distinguish real from fake; the risks of robots, autonomous vehicles, and programmed machines that humans may lose control over, leading to disasters and environmental damage. Hence the urgent need for legislative intervention to regulate AI programming, processing, and uses for benefit, prohibit harmful uses, and restrict the use of dangerous robots and machines whose paths cannot be controlled — with deterrent penalties.

5. **The proposal was prepared considering:**

   - Drawing inspiration from the European Parliament's AI Act, and the Arab Guiding Law in AI.
   - Including in the preamble related legislation.
   - Flexibility and adaptability rather than rigidity, taking into account the privacy, culture, and ethics of Arab societies.
   - Setting restrictions on AI technologies and uses.
   - Prohibiting all processes, programs, and uses violating public order, morals, public health, or environment; prohibiting dangerous robots and machines whose paths cannot be controlled.
   - Granting authorities to the AI Unit for guidance, oversight, inspection, and investigation.
   - Compensating damage from errors in programming, processing, or misuse, unless caused by foreign cause.
   - Graduating criminal penalties to achieve specific and general deterrence.
   - Referring to Executive Regulations for tables of dangerous robots, classification of high-risk programs, and conditions for implementation.
   - Issuing the Executive Regulations within three months of Gazette publication, with entry into force six months after publication, allowing the government, private sector, and individuals time to prepare.

## III. Objectives

To regulate AI technologies, systems, and uses for peaceful purposes, in accordance with public order and morals; achieving the public interest of the State; preserving the rights, security, freedoms, and ethical principles of individuals; prohibiting dangerous robots and machines whose paths cannot be controlled — with deterrent penalties for violators.

## IV. Contents of the Proposal

The proposed law consists of 38 articles distributed across seven chapters, plus the preamble.

**Chapter One** (4 articles) — Article 1 defines key terms; Article 2 sets the purpose; Article 3 defines scope; Article 4 addresses localization, support, and encouragement of AI technology.

**Chapter Two** (Articles 5–14) — Duties and prohibitions. Article 5 sets conditions for programming, processing, and development; Article 6 details system design conditions; Article 7 requires training of employees and operators; Article 8 permits AI in medical and other sectors with patient consent for surgical operations; Article 9 sets prohibited programs; Article 10 prohibits dangerous robots; Article 11 sets conditions for robot use; Article 12 prohibits exclusive AI decision-making over a person's life, freedom, or bodily integrity; Article 13 prohibits total reliance on AI for selection, comparison, and evaluation; Article 14 prohibits using AI for manipulation, harm, misleading, sedition, sabotage, terrorism, or political overthrow.

**Chapter Three** (Articles 15–17) — Establishes the AI Unit. Article 15 sets its monitoring, planning, training, oversight, and investigation competencies; Article 16 addresses incident reporting; Article 17 grants Unit employees judicial officer status with inspection and seizure rights.

**Chapter Four** (Articles 18–20) — Licensing, grievance, and appeal. Article 18 prohibits unlicensed AI commercial activity; Article 19 regulates license applications; Article 20 permits grievance and appeal of rejection decisions.

**Chapter Five** (Articles 21–22) — Civil liability.

**Chapter Six** (Articles 23–29) — Article 23 provides administrative sanctions; Articles 24–29 regulate criminal penalties.

**Chapter Seven** (Articles 30–38) — Final provisions. Article 30 applies Penal Code penalties when AI is the means; Article 31 regulates attempts; Article 32 addresses good faith use; Article 33 covers legal person liability; Article 34 grants compliance period; Article 35 preserves international treaty rights; Article 36 refers to Executive Regulations for high-risk classification; Article 37 requires Executive Regulations within three months of Gazette publication; Article 38 sets entry into force six months after publication.
