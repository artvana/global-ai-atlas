# NIST Artificial Intelligence 600-1: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile

**NIST AI 600-1**
*NIST (United States) — July 26, 2024*

---

## Overview

NIST AI 600-1 is a companion document to the NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0 (AI RMF 1.0), specifically tailored to the unique risks posed by generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) systems. Published on July 26, 2024, it was developed pursuant to a mandate in President Biden's Executive Order 14110 (October 2023) directing NIST to produce guidance on managing GenAI risks. The profile identifies 12 categories of risk that are unique to or significantly amplified by generative AI, and maps over 200 suggested actions to the four core functions of the AI RMF (GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE).

The document is entirely voluntary. It does not impose binding obligations on any entity but serves as the primary technical reference benchmark used by federal agencies implementing AI governance policies under OMB guidance, and is widely adopted by the private sector as a compliance baseline. It addresses the full lifecycle of generative AI systems including foundation models, fine-tuned models, and deployed applications.

## Key Provisions

### 12 Generative AI Risk Categories

NIST AI 600-1 identifies the following unique or amplified risk categories for GenAI:

- **CBRN Information or Capabilities**: Risk that GenAI systems provide uplift to actors seeking to develop chemical, biological, radiological, or nuclear weapons or capabilities.
- **Confabulation (Hallucination)**: Risk of GenAI generating false, fabricated, or misleading outputs presented with apparent confidence — a fundamental limitation of current large language model architectures.
- **Data Privacy**: Risks arising from GenAI systems trained on or processing personal data, including inadvertent memorization and reproduction of training data.
- **Data Poisoning**: Adversarial manipulation of training data to corrupt model behavior, induce backdoors, or degrade accuracy.
- **Harmful Bias and Homogenization**: Risk that GenAI systems encode and amplify social biases, and that widespread adoption of similar models reduces diversity in outputs, perspectives, and information.
- **Human-AI Configuration**: Risks from miscalibrated trust in AI outputs — including over-reliance (automation bias) and under-reliance — and from inappropriate human oversight arrangements.
- **Information Integrity**: Risks related to GenAI-enabled creation of disinformation, influence operations, and large-scale synthetic misinformation.
- **Information Security**: Risks of GenAI systems being exploited as attack vectors (prompt injection, jailbreaking, model extraction) or used to enhance offensive cyber capabilities.
- **Intellectual Property**: Risks of GenAI systems generating outputs that infringe on copyrights, trademarks, or other intellectual property rights, or reproducing proprietary training data.
- **Obscene, Degrading, and Abusive Content (ODAC)**: Risks of GenAI generating non-consensual intimate imagery, child sexual abuse material (CSAM), or other harmful content.
- **Societal Risks**: Broader risks to labor markets, access to information, democratic institutions, and societal resilience from large-scale GenAI deployment.
- **Value Chain and Component Integration**: Risks arising from the complex supply chains of GenAI systems, including third-party datasets, pre-trained models, APIs, and fine-tuning processes.

### Mitigation Mapping

- Over 200 suggested actions are mapped to the GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE functions of the AI RMF 1.0.
- Actions are categorized by which AI actors are responsible: AI developers, deployers, operators, and users.
- Mitigations address technical controls (red-teaming, adversarial testing, output filtering), governance controls (policies, contracts, audit), and operational controls (human oversight, incident response).

### Relationship to AI RMF 1.0

- AI 600-1 is explicitly a profile of AI RMF 1.0, not a standalone framework.
- Organizations using AI 600-1 should first implement the core AI RMF governance structures.
- The profile supplements rather than replaces existing risk management practices.

### Federal Agency Application

- Federal agencies were directed by OMB Memorandum M-24-10 (March 2024) to use the AI RMF, and AI 600-1 by extension, as a reference in managing GenAI risks in agency operations.
- NIST AI 600-1 is referenced in agency AI use case inventories and safety evaluations conducted under the Biden administration's AI governance directives.
- Many provisions of AI 600-1 remain operative as voluntary benchmarks independent of any particular administration's executive orders.

### Notable Scope and Limitations

- The profile covers all types of generative AI: text, image, video, audio, code, and multimodal systems.
- It explicitly acknowledges that GenAI risk management is an evolving field and the document will be updated as knowledge matures.
- NIST explicitly states the document is not a compliance checklist and must be adapted to organizational context.
