1. INTRODUCTION & OVERVIEW
================================================================================

1.1 HISTORY OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

The notion of machine intelligence existed since Greek mythology (the robot Talos).
Key milestones:

- 1950: Alan Turing introduces the Turing Test ("Can machines think?")
- 1956: Term "Artificial Intelligence" coined by John McCarthy at Dartmouth Conference
- 1964: Joseph Weizenbaum develops ELIZA (MIT)
- 1974-80: First AI winter — reduced government funding and interest
- 1980-87: AI revival through expert systems; British government funding to compete
  with Japanese AI efforts
- 1987-93: Second AI winter — expert systems become unfeasible and expensive
- 1997: IBM Deep Blue defeats world chess champion
- 2009-11: Andrew Ng uses GPUs to train deep-learning models more efficiently;
  IBM Watson wins Jeopardy
- 2012: Google reintroduces deep learning for image recognition
- 2014: Amazon Echo introduced
- 2019: OpenAI team defeats five professional players in Dota 2


1.2 WHAT IS ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE?

AI defines a collection of technologies enabling a machine or system to comprehend,
learn, act, and sense like a human. Abilities include: planning, learning, reasoning,
problem-solving, knowledge representation, perception, motion, manipulation, social
intelligence and creativity.

Types of AI:
1. Narrow AI (Weak AI): Specialist systems that can only handle a narrowly defined set
   of tasks. Most existing AI applications are narrow AI (spam filters, recommendation
   systems, Google Translate, Siri).
2. General AI (Strong AI / AGI): A machine that can successfully perform any
   intellectual, physical or emotional human task. Does not yet exist.
3. Super AI: Machines with cognitive abilities surpassing human intelligence. Does not
   yet exist; appears in science fiction.


1.3 MACHINE LEARNING

Machine learning (ML) is an application of AI that teaches machines how to perform
specific tasks by learning from data. Used in speech recognition, spam filtering, and
targeted advertisement.

Three main types:

1.3.1 Supervised Learning
Learns a function mapping input to output by recognizing patterns in labeled examples.
- Classification: maps input to finite set of outputs (e.g., spam detection)
- Regression: output consists of continuous values (e.g., house price prediction)
Examples: support vector machines, logistic/linear regression, decision trees, neural
networks.

1.3.2 Unsupervised Learning
Recognizes patterns in unlabeled data. Used for clustering and dimensionality reduction.
Example: K-means clustering for customer segmentation.
Dimensionality reduction: transforms datasets into lower-dimensional space.
Examples: Principal component analysis, autoencoders.

1.3.3 Reinforcement Learning
Teaches the agent to interact with its environment to maximize a reward. Used to teach
AI to master Atari games and autonomous driving in simulated environments.
Example: Q-learning.


1.4 NEURAL NETWORKS AND DEEP LEARNING

Artificial neural networks consist of connected nodes that transform input using
non-linear activation functions, inspired by the human brain. As hidden layers increase,
the network becomes a "deep" neural network — deep learning. Deep learning is the
most widely adopted approach to ML since Google reintroduced it for image recognition
in 2012.

Popular architectures:
- Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs): mainly used for image classification
- Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs): used for processing temporal data


1.5 DATA SCIENCE

Data science is a multi-disciplinary field involving pre-processing and mining structured
or unstructured data. Most development time in ML applications is spent on data
pre-processing (cleaning, removing missing/incorrect values, transforming formats).


1.6 ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE VS. AUTOMATION

Robots are not artificially intelligent by nature. Common robots (ATMs, cooking robots)
do not use AI. Some automation (robocalls, early chatbots) is hardcoded and not AI.

Key distinction:
- RPA (Robotic Process Automation): keyword-based interaction, rule-based decision
  making, process execution, data collection — "the brawns"
- AI: speech/video/image recognition, NLP, data preparation, predictive analytics,
  emotional recognition, autonomous computing, deep learning, machine learning
  — "the brains"

3. KEY CONSIDERATIONS & EFFORTS OF THE UAE
================================================================================

3.1 AI GOVERNANCE

The UAE Artificial Intelligence and Blockchain Council:
- Oversees implementation of AI technology in society and across government
- Proposes policies to create an AI-friendly ecosystem
- Advances research; promotes collaboration between public and private sectors
- Encourages knowledge exchange; integrates AI in education
- Oversees privacy of user data, data security, data integrity, and efficient data sharing

Federal RegLab (reference to Federal Decree Law No. 25/2018):
Enables the UAE Cabinet to grant temporary licenses for testing innovations using
future technologies such as AI. Similar regulatory sandboxes exist at ADGM and DIFC.

The Data Committee (under the UAE AI and Blockchain Council):
Tasks include: (1) identifying main challenges of collecting data for AI and blockchain,
(2) making recommendations for standardization, collection and inventory of data,
(3) designing specialized courses, policies, systems and standards for all data.

The Cybersecurity and Risk Management Committee (under the UAE AI and Blockchain
Council):
Tasks include: (1) providing technical advice and proposing knowledge initiatives,
(2) making recommendations, (3) conducting surveys on vulnerabilities and security
threats, (4) maintaining a unified and modern record, (5) ensuring effectiveness of
cybersecurity protection systems, (6) combating cybersecurity crimes in AI and
blockchain fields.


3.2 DATA GOVERNANCE

Data plays a pivotal role in developing ML algorithms but raises data privacy challenges.
UAE Government collects customer happiness data through "Happiness Meters" to
improve service delivery.

UAE as data resource: home to more than 200 nationalities — a rich and diverse source
of data for globally relevant use cases.


3.3 CYBERSECURITY

UAE Government treats cyber safety and digital security as a national priority.
- Electronic and biometric identity cards and single-sign-on logins
- Systems regularly monitored with AI-powered programs under the UAE's National
  Cyber Security Strategy
- AI cybersecurity applications: fingerprint/retina scanning for biometric logins,
  malware/virus detection, conditional access with real-time global authentication
- Dubai and Abu Dhabi police use AI to protect sensitive data systems and prevent crime


3.4 ETHICS

The Dubai Data Establishment within the Smart Dubai Office has published an Ethical
AI Toolkit defining guiding principles across four domains:
1. Ethics: Fairness, Transparency, Accountability, Understandability
2. Security: Safe and secure systems
3. Humanity: Serving and protecting humanity; aligned with human values long and
   short term
4. Inclusiveness: Benefiting all people in society; respect for dignity and rights

Smart Dubai has also built a self-assessment tool for government entities, private
sector and individuals to evaluate AI solutions from an ethical perspective.

Dubai AI Ethics Guidelines — Fairness Principle application:
- Consider effect of diversity on development and deployment
- Evaluate whether data ingested is representative of affected population
- Test algorithm for bias prior to deployment
- Consider accessibility and usability across user groups
- Continually evaluate fairness of significant AI-informed decisions


3.5 BIAS

AI is only as accurate as the data it is trained on. Examples of algorithmic bias:
- US criminal risk profiling algorithm that assigned higher risk scores to people of color
  due to historical bias in prosecution data
- Amazon's AI recruiting tool that systematically downgraded female candidates due
  to historical male-dominated tech industry data

UAE approach to bias: following the Fairness Principle in the Dubai AI Ethics Guidelines
(see 3.4 above).


3.6 EMPLOYMENT

AI's impact on the workforce is threefold:
1. Replacing employees in roles with repetitive tasks following strict rules (factory work,
   dangerous roles)
2. Augmenting employees in roles requiring precision with human intuition or empathy
   (medicine)
3. Assisting employees where large data volumes need evaluation for decisions (law,
   finance)

UAE context:
- 22% of graduates study STEM subjects (vs 16% in US)
- UAE has seen second highest AI investment in the region over past decade —
  estimated more than AED 7.9 billion
- 94% of UAE companies report AI involvement at executive management level —
  highest percentage of any surveyed country in the Middle East
- Emerging AI job titles: algorithm manager, AI data analyst, machine learning engineer,
  data curator, digital knowledge manager, cognitive copywriter, AI integration designer


4. BUILDING AI CAPABILITIES AT YOUR ORGANIZATION
================================================================================

A two-stage AI Adoption Framework:

Stage A — Classify: Identify in which category AI adds value:
- Automate: repetitive tasks with clearly defined inputs and definitive output
  → supervised machine learning
- Classify: large volumes of data with anomaly detection (e.g. security footage)
  → computer vision / unsupervised learning
- Predict: real-time inputs compared against historical data
  → supervised ML / predictive modeling

Stage B — Identify: 5-step process:
1. Take Caution: Identify specific business needs and ensure AI is the right fit
2. Assess: Determine best AI solution for the problem
3. Select: Choose appropriate AI type (supervised, unsupervised, NLP, etc.)
4. Refine: Continuously evaluate need/value of AI
5. Review and Reflect: AI is evolving; review ongoing performance

Organizational readiness assessment dimensions:
- People: in-house data science / ML expertise; senior data/technology leadership;
  executive support
- Data: clean and organized data; data governance processes in place
- Process: well-defined and stable business processes; data valued as corporate asset
- Technology: cloud-based technologies; integrated systems with effective data sharing
- Culture: cross-functional collaboration; R&D initiative funding

Key guidance:
- Prior to beginning AI, data should be valued as a corporate asset
- Ensure corporate policy guidelines are in place for ethical, fair, accountable,
  transparent and explainable AI before deployment
- Most time in ML development is spent on data pre-processing
- Continuously review whether AI is performing as intended

7. GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS
================================================================================

Artificial Intelligence: The theory and development of computer systems able to
perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence, such as visual perception,
speech recognition, decision-making, and translation between languages.

General AI: A machine that can perform any intellectual, physical and even emotional
task that a human being could.

Narrow AI: Artificial intelligence focused on one narrow task.

Machine Learning: An application of AI that provides systems the ability to automatically
learn and improve from experience without being explicitly programmed.

Deep Learning: A subset of machine learning where artificial neural networks,
algorithms inspired by the human brain, learn from large amounts of data.

Neural Network: A computer system modeled to simulate the human brain and nervous
system.

Supervised Learning: The ML task of learning a function that maps an input to an
output based on example input-output pairs, using labeled training data.

Unsupervised Learning: A type of ML algorithm used to draw inferences from datasets
consisting of input data without labeled responses.

Reinforcement Learning: A field of ML concerned with how software agents ought to
take actions in an environment so as to maximize cumulative reward.

Federated Learning: A machine learning setting where the goal is to train a high-quality
centralized model with training data distributed over a large number of clients, each
with unreliable and relatively slow network connections.

Bias: Inclination or prejudice for or against one person or group, especially in a way
considered to be unfair.

Ethics: Moral principles that govern a person's behavior or the conducting of an activity.

Automation: The use or introduction of automatic equipment in a manufacturing or
other process or facility.

Robotics: Branch of technology that deals with the design, construction, operation, and
application of robots.

Autonomous Cars: A vehicle that can guide itself without human conduction.
