# New Mexico House Bill 182 — Prohibition on Materially Deceptive AI-Generated Content in Political Advertising

**HB 182 | New Mexico Legislature, 2024 Regular Session**
*New Mexico — Enacted March 5, 2024 | Effective May 15, 2024*

---

## Overview

New Mexico House Bill 182 prohibits the distribution of political advertising that uses materially deceptive AI-generated depictions of candidates or public officials within 90 days of an election, without a prominent disclaimer identifying the AI-generated nature of the content. The law covers all forms of political advertising and campaign communications directed at New Mexico voters, and provides for both criminal and civil enforcement. It is one of the earlier state enactments specifically targeting AI-generated deepfakes in the political advertising context.

The law explicitly exempts satire and parody, recognizing the importance of these forms of political commentary to the First Amendment. Civil enforcement may be initiated by the Attorney General or by affected candidates within 90 days of the election during which the violation occurred.

## Key Provisions

### Prohibition on Materially Deceptive AI-Generated Political Content

- Prohibits any person or entity from distributing or causing to be distributed any political advertisement or campaign communication that contains a materially deceptive AI-generated depiction of a candidate for public office or a current public official.
- Applies within 90 days before any New Mexico primary, general, or special election.
- "Materially deceptive" means the AI-generated content depicts the candidate or official saying, doing, or appearing in a manner that did not occur and that is likely to deceive a reasonable viewer.
- Applies to all distribution channels: broadcast, cable, print, digital, social media, and direct mail.

### Required Disclaimer

- Political advertising or communications that include AI-generated content — even content that is not materially deceptive — must include a clear and prominent disclaimer stating: "This [advertisement/communication] contains content generated or substantially altered by artificial intelligence."
- Disclaimer must appear in a size and manner visible to a reasonable person.
- Audio-only content must include a verbal disclaimer.

### Criminal Enforcement

- Knowing violation of the prohibition on materially deceptive AI-generated political content constitutes a criminal offense under New Mexico election law.
- Penalty: misdemeanor criminal charge with fines and potential short-term incarceration as provided under New Mexico election code.
- The New Mexico Attorney General and District Attorneys have concurrent prosecution authority.

### Civil Enforcement

- The Attorney General may bring a civil action for injunctive relief and civil penalties against any person or entity that violates the Act.
- Candidates and public officials who are the subject of materially deceptive AI-generated political content may bring a private civil action within 90 days after the election at which the violation occurred.
- Available civil remedies include injunctive relief (including take-down orders), actual damages, and attorney's fees.

### Exemptions

- Satire and parody are explicitly exempted, provided the content is presented in a context that makes clear it is not a factual depiction of the candidate's actual statements or conduct.
- News organizations reporting on AI-generated political content as a matter of public interest are not subject to liability for that reporting.
- Historical or archival content used for educational purposes is exempted.

### Scope and Application

- Applies to both in-state and out-of-state persons who distribute content targeting New Mexico voters.
- Applies to campaigns for all state and local offices in New Mexico.
- Preemption: no express preemption clause; state law operates independently of federal campaign finance and election law.
