# Pre-market Guidance for Machine Learning-Enabled Medical Devices

Health Canada
Published: February 5, 2025
Document Type: Guidance

## Overview

Health Canada released its Pre-market Guidance for Machine Learning-Enabled Medical Devices (MLMDs) on February 5, 2025. This guidance document provides direction to manufacturers submitting new or amendment applications for Class II, III, and IV machine learning-enabled medical devices (MLMDs) under the Medical Devices Regulations.

This guidance represents Health Canada's current thinking on the topic and may be revised as the technology matures and regulatory oversight is optimized. The guidance applies to medical devices that use machine learning (ML) methods as part of their software, where the ML component contributes to the device's intended purpose or claims.

## Key Topics Covered

### 1. Product Lifecycle Information

Health Canada considers product lifecycle information to be essential in demonstrating the safety and effectiveness of an MLMD. Manufacturers must provide information about how the device will evolve over its lifecycle, including planned updates to the ML component.

### 2. Good Machine Learning Practice (GMLP)

Good machine learning practice is important when designing, developing, evaluating, deploying, and maintaining an MLMD. Health Canada expects manufacturers to demonstrate that they have followed GMLP principles, which include:

- Using relevant and representative data for training, testing, and validation
- Managing data quality and integrity throughout the ML lifecycle
- Applying appropriate methods for model development and evaluation
- Documenting the ML development process thoroughly
- Implementing appropriate cybersecurity measures

### 3. Predetermined Change Control Plans (PCCP)

The guidance introduces the concept of a Predetermined Change Control Plan (PCCP), which provides a mechanism for Health Canada to address cases where regulatory pre-authorization of planned changes to ML systems is needed to address a known risk. A PCCP:

- Describes anticipated modifications to the MLMD
- Specifies the methodology for implementing and validating each modification
- Defines performance metrics and testing protocols
- Establishes criteria for when a modification requires a new submission versus implementation under the PCCP

Unlike static software, MLMDs evolve over time, necessitating a structured approach to managing updates. The PCCP allows for a more streamlined regulatory pathway for planned, pre-authorized changes.

### 4. Risk-Based Classification

Health Canada follows a risk-based approach, classifying MLMDs into Class II, III, and IV under the Medical Devices Regulations, based on the intended use and associated risks of the device.

### 5. Transparency

Transparency describes the degree to which appropriate information about a device is communicated to stakeholders, and is an important aspect of the device's safety and effectiveness. Manufacturers should:

- Provide clear information about the ML model's intended use, inputs, and outputs
- Disclose the training data characteristics, including any known limitations
- Communicate the performance characteristics of the ML model
- Provide information about how the device's performance may change over time

### 6. Bias and Representativeness

The guidance addresses bias in ML models and the representativeness of training data. Manufacturers must:

- Identify and assess potential sources of bias in training and validation data
- Demonstrate that the training data is representative of the intended patient population
- Implement measures to mitigate identified bias
- Monitor for bias in post-market surveillance

### 7. Cybersecurity

MLMD manufacturers must address cybersecurity considerations specific to ML components, including:

- Protection of the ML model from adversarial attacks
- Security of data pipelines and model updates
- Integrity verification of the ML model

## Development Background

After releasing a draft guidance in August 2023 for public consultation, Health Canada finalized this Pre-Market Guidance in February 2025. The guidance was developed in consultation with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and other international regulatory bodies, reflecting international harmonization efforts.

## Application Scope

This guidance applies to:
- New device licence applications for Class II, III, and IV MLMDs
- Amendment applications for existing device licences where changes involve ML components
- Class I MLMDs are not subject to pre-market review but should follow the guidance as best practice

## Implementation

The guidance became effective upon publication on February 5, 2025. Health Canada has indicated that this guidance may be updated as the field of ML in medical devices evolves.
