## Act LXXV of 2025 on the Domestic Implementation of the EU AI Act
### (A mesterséges intelligenciáról szóló uniós szabályozás végrehajtásáról szóló törvény)

**Hungarian National Assembly (Országgyűlés)**
**Magyar Közlöny No. 127, 31 October 2025**
**Enacted and effective:** October 31, 2025

---

## Overview

Act LXXV of 2025 makes Hungary the first Central and Eastern European country to enact a standalone national law implementing the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689). The Act designates national competent authorities, establishes the Hungarian Artificial Intelligence Council (Magyar Mesterséges Intelligencia Tanács), and creates domestic administrative procedures for market surveillance, conformity assessment, regulatory sandboxes, and enforcement of EU AI Act obligations.

As EU Regulation 2024/1689 is directly applicable in all EU member states, Act LXXV does not re-enact the substantive prohibitions, risk classifications, or obligations of the EU AI Act — these apply automatically as a matter of EU law. Instead, Act LXXV fills the discretionary gaps left to member states: principally, the designation of national authorities, the establishment of domestic governance structures, and the procedural framework for national enforcement.

---

## Legislative Background

### EU AI Act Requirements for Member States

The EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689), which entered into force on August 1, 2024, requires each EU member state to:
- Designate one or more national competent authorities responsible for supervising application and implementation of the Regulation (Article 70);
- Designate a national supervisory authority for GPAI (general-purpose AI) models;
- Establish a national market surveillance authority;
- Create a single point of contact for international cooperation;
- Establish procedures for regulatory sandboxes (Article 57).

Member states had until August 2, 2025 to designate their national competent authorities under Article 70. Hungary met this deadline and used Act LXXV to put those designations on a statutory footing.

### Hungary's AI Policy Context

Hungary adopted a National AI Strategy in 2020 (updated 2022), establishing AI as a priority area. The Hungarian government had designated the National Media and Infocommunications Authority (NMHH — Nemzeti Média- és Hírközlési Hatóság) as the interim contact point for AI-related matters even before Act LXXV. The Act formalizes and extends NMHH's role as the primary national supervisory authority under the EU AI Act.

## Key Provisions

### Chapter I — Purpose and Scope

1. Act LXXV establishes the domestic institutional and procedural framework necessary for Hungary to implement and enforce the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).

2. The Act applies to all providers, deployers, importers, distributors, and authorized representatives of AI systems operating in Hungary, in accordance with the territorial scope of the EU AI Act.

3. The substantive rules governing AI systems — including prohibited practices (Article 5), obligations for high-risk AI systems (Articles 8–27), transparency obligations (Article 50), and GPAI obligations (Articles 51–56) — are those set out in the EU AI Act, which applies directly as EU law. Act LXXV does not modify or duplicate these provisions.

### Chapter II — Hungarian Artificial Intelligence Council (Magyar Mesterséges Intelligencia Tanács)

1. **Establishment** — Act LXXV establishes the Hungarian Artificial Intelligence Council (HMAIC) as the overarching national coordination body for AI governance in Hungary.

2. **Composition** — The HMAIC consists of:
   - The Minister responsible for digitalization (Chair);
   - Representatives from the NMHH;
   - Representatives from sector-specific ministries (health, finance, justice, education);
   - Two independent expert members appointed by the Parliament;
   - A representative from the Hungarian Academy of Sciences.

3. **Functions** — The HMAIC:
   - Coordinates the activities of national competent authorities designated under the EU AI Act;
   - Develops national AI policy guidance and implementation recommendations;
   - Reports annually to the Parliament on the state of AI governance in Hungary;
   - Represents Hungary in the European Artificial Intelligence Board (Article 65 of the EU AI Act);
   - Issues non-binding guidance on the application of EU AI Act obligations in Hungary.

4. **Secretariat** — The NMHH provides the secretariat and administrative support for the HMAIC.

### Chapter III — National Competent Authorities

Act LXXV designates the following national competent authorities for purposes of Article 70 of the EU AI Act:

#### Primary National Supervisory Authority
**National Media and Infocommunications Authority (NMHH)** is designated as:
- The primary national competent authority under Article 70 of the EU AI Act;
- The national market surveillance authority for AI systems in Hungary;
- The single national point of contact for coordination with the European AI Office and other member state authorities.

The NMHH has authority to:
- Conduct market surveillance and investigate complaints regarding AI systems;
- Request technical documentation from providers and deployers of high-risk AI systems;
- Order corrective actions, market withdrawal, or recall of non-compliant AI systems;
- Issue administrative fines within the penalty structure of the EU AI Act;
- Refer matters to the European AI Office where required.

#### Sector-Specific Competent Authorities

The following sector-specific authorities are designated as competent authorities for AI systems deployed in their respective sectors:

| Sector | Designated Authority |
|---|---|
| Healthcare | National Public Health Centre (NNK) |
| Financial services | Hungarian National Bank (Magyar Nemzeti Bank, MNB) |
| Labour and employment | Labour Inspectorate (Munkavédelmi Hatóság) |
| Education | National Education Authority (OH) |
| Law enforcement and justice | Ministry of Interior; Ministry of Justice |
| Transport and infrastructure | Hungarian Transport Authority (NKH) |

Sector-specific authorities cooperate with the NMHH in their respective domains and may conduct joint investigations.

#### Data Protection Authority
The National Authority for Data Protection and Freedom of Information (NAIH — Nemzeti Adatvédelmi és Információszabadság Hatóság) retains jurisdiction over data protection aspects of AI systems under GDPR and the Hungarian data protection law, in coordination with the NMHH.

### Chapter IV — Market Surveillance Procedures

1. **Complaint handling** — Any natural or legal person may submit a complaint to the NMHH regarding a suspected violation of the EU AI Act by an AI system deployed in Hungary. The NMHH must acknowledge receipt within 15 days and conclude its preliminary assessment within 90 days.

2. **Investigation powers** — The NMHH may:
   - Request technical documentation, conformity assessment records, and risk management documentation from providers and deployers;
   - Commission independent technical expertise;
   - Conduct on-site inspections of premises where AI systems are developed, tested, or deployed;
   - Issue interim orders to suspend or restrict use of an AI system where an urgent risk is identified.

3. **Corrective measures** — Following investigation, the NMHH may order:
   - Correction of non-compliance within a specified timeframe;
   - Restriction of use of the AI system;
   - Withdrawal of the AI system from the Hungarian market;
   - Recall of the AI system from deployers.

4. **Appeal** — Decisions of the NMHH may be appealed to the competent administrative court within 30 days of notification.

### Chapter V — Penalties and Enforcement

Act LXXV adopts the EU AI Act penalty structure directly:

| Violation | Maximum Penalty |
|---|---|
| Prohibited AI practices (Article 5) | €35,000,000 or 7% of global annual turnover |
| High-risk AI obligations violations | €15,000,000 or 3% of global annual turnover |
| Supply of incorrect information | €7,500,000 or 1.5% of global annual turnover |

For SMEs and startups, the NMHH must apply proportionality criteria before imposing fines, including the entity's market position, financial capacity, and prior compliance history.

Penalties are collected as national budget revenue and may be used to fund NMHH's AI supervision activities.

### Chapter VI — Regulatory Sandbox

1. Act LXXV establishes the legal framework for AI regulatory sandboxes in Hungary, as required by Article 57 of the EU AI Act.

2. The NMHH operates the national AI regulatory sandbox program, through which innovative AI systems may be tested in a controlled environment under regulatory supervision.

3. Participants in the sandbox are granted limited exemptions from certain conformity assessment requirements during the testing period, subject to conditions set by the NMHH.

4. The sandbox operates for defined periods not exceeding 12 months, extendable by 12 months in exceptional cases.

5. Sandbox participants must report findings to the NMHH and, where applicable, contribute to NMHH guidance on the application of EU AI Act requirements.

### Chapter VII — Transitional and Final Provisions

1. Act LXXV enters into force on October 31, 2025, the date of its publication in the Magyar Közlöny.

2. Transitional provisions align with the EU AI Act's phased implementation timeline:
   - Prohibited practices (Article 5): applied from February 2, 2025 (EU AI Act timetable);
   - GPAI obligations (Articles 51–56): applied from August 2, 2025;
   - High-risk AI obligations (Annexes III and IV): applied from August 2, 2026;
   - Embedded high-risk AI systems: applied from August 2, 2027.

3. The NMHH must publish national implementation guidance within 6 months of the Act's entry into force.

## Relationship to EU Law

Act LXXV is a member state implementing measure under Article 70 of the EU AI Act. It does not — and by EU law cannot — contradict, modify, or expand the substantive obligations, prohibitions, or rights established by the EU AI Act as directly applicable EU regulation. Where Act LXXV and the EU AI Act appear to address the same matter, the EU AI Act prevails.

The Act also operates alongside Hungary's existing framework of:
- GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) — for data protection aspects of AI systems;
- Hungarian data protection law (Act CXII of 2011);
- Sector-specific regulation in healthcare, finance, labour, and other domains.

---

## Significance

Hungary's Act LXXV of 2025 is historically significant as the **first standalone national AI Act implementation law in Central and Eastern Europe**. While all EU member states are subject to the EU AI Act directly, Hungary is among the earliest to enact a comprehensive domestic implementing statute — providing legal clarity on national governance structures, authority designations, and enforcement procedures.

The creation of the Hungarian AI Council, the designation of the NMHH as primary supervisory authority, and the establishment of a national regulatory sandbox framework position Hungary to be a proactive AI governance actor within the EU's broader regulatory ecosystem.
