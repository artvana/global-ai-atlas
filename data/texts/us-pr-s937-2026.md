                                            INTRODUCTION

            Senate Bill 937 proposes to create the "Act for the Ethical Use of Artificial
     Intelligence in Sustainable Agriculture of Puerto Rico", with the purpose of establishing a
     regulatory framework for the use of artificial intelligence tools in the
     agricultural sector, as well as to implement a certification program administered by the
     Department of Agriculture. The measure arises in a context that recognizes the
     potential of emerging technologies to address structural challenges
     of the agricultural sector, such as the optimization of resources, crop forecasting
     and the reduction of the use of chemical inputs.
            However, as part of the legislative evaluation process, the Committee on
     Science, Technology and Artificial Intelligence of the Senate of Puerto Rico
     requested and examined

          the explanatory memoranda of the agencies concerned, namely, the Puerto Rico
          Innovation and Technology Service (PRITS) and the Department of Agriculture. From the

          of the proposal and the implications its approval would have on government management
          and on the agricultural sector of Puerto Rico.

                                  DEPARTMENT OF AGRICULTURE

                The Department of Agriculture of the Government of Puerto Rico appears to
        express its position on Senate Bill 937, which proposes to create an ethical
        regulatory framework for the use of artificial intelligence in sustainable agriculture and
        to establish a certification program administered by said agency. The
        Department recognizes the importance of promoting technological innovation and the
        responsible use of emerging tools in the agricultural sector; however, it maintains that
        the measure, as drafted, presents fiscal, operational and technical limitations that
        make its effective implementation difficult.
                In its analysis, the Department points out that the measure is based on the premise that
        it will not generate initial fiscal impact and that it could be financed through federal funds,
        private alliances and donations. However, it warns that there is no guarantee of
        availability or recurrence of these funds, particularly those subject to
        federal competitive processes, and that alliances or donations do not constitute
        structural or predictable sources to sustain a continuous regulatory program.
                Furthermore, the Department states that it currently operates under significant
        budgetary limitations and with limited human and technical resources, which
        makes it impossible for it to assume the responsibilities imposed by the measure, such as ethical
        audits, technical evaluations of algorithms, certification of tools, mass training
        of farmers and continuous oversight. It adds that the technical functions
        proposed by the measure require specialized expertise in areas such as data science,
        cybersecurity and technological auditing, capabilities that are not part of its
        current operational structure.

       The agency also raises that artificial intelligence systems present
practical limitations in the local agricultural context, including climate variability,
the lack of uniformity in results and the difficulty of establishing rigid metrics
applicable to all agricultural scenarios. Likewise, it warns that a rigid regulatory framework
could quickly become obsolete given the constant evolution of these
technologies and could affect the competitiveness of the agricultural sector compared to other
jurisdictions.
      Consequently, the Department concludes that the measure lacks real and recurrent
budgetary allocation, imposes technical responsibilities that exceed its
institutional capacity and could generate adverse effects on agricultural innovation and
competitiveness. For this reason, it recommends that Senate Bill 937 not be
approved in its current form, although it expresses its willingness to collaborate in the development
of viable alternatives that promote agricultural innovation in a gradual and
fiscally responsible manner.



                                         CONCLUSION
        FOR ALL OF THE FOREGOING, the Committee on Science, Technology and
Artificial Intelligence and the Committee on Agriculture of the Senate of Puerto Rico, after
study and consideration, see fit to present before this High Body the
Negative Report on Senate Bill 937, recommending that the bill not be
approved.
