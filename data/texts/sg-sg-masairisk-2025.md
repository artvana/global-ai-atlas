# MAS Guidelines for Artificial Intelligence (AI) Risk Management

**Issuing Authority:** Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS)
**Document Type:** Consultation Paper on Proposed Guidelines on Artificial Intelligence Risk Management (AIRG)
**Date Published:** November 13, 2025

## Overview

On November 13, 2025, the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) released a consultation paper proposing Guidelines on Artificial Intelligence Risk Management (AIRG). The proposed Guidelines set out MAS's supervisory expectations on the responsible use of AI in the financial sector and apply to all financial institutions (FIs) — whether the AI systems are internally developed or supplied by third-party vendors.

The Guidelines build on MAS's 2024 thematic review of major banks' AI use and prior stakeholder consultations. They cover a wide range of AI applications including traditional quantitative models, generative AI systems, decision-support tools (copilots, analytics platforms), and emerging AI agents.

MAS recognises that AI risks may vary based on the scale, scope, and business models of FIs, and that the Guidelines are intended to be applied proportionately based on institution size, complexity, and the materiality of AI deployment.

---

## Key AI Risks Identified

MAS explicitly recognises several critical risk categories associated with AI use in financial services:

- **Model unpredictability**: AI's probabilistic behaviour can lead to inaccurate outputs affecting risk assessments
- **Operational failures**: Automation breakdowns disrupting core business functions
- **Bias and discrimination**: Discriminatory outcomes harming customer segments or protected groups
- **Financial crime gaps**: Detection systems missing suspicious patterns or criminal activity
- **Reputational exposure**: Customer-facing tools producing inappropriate or harmful responses
- **Data breaches**: Sensitive information exposure, including through third-party AI models

---

## Scope and Application

The Guidelines apply to all financial institutions regulated by MAS and may be implemented proportionately based on:
- The size and nature of the FI's activities
- The extent and materiality of AI usage
- The FI's overall risk profile

Coverage spans both traditional AI/ML models and emerging technologies including generative AI and AI agents.

---

## Four Main Focus Areas

### 1. Governance and Oversight

Board and senior management must establish frameworks, policies, and processes for AI risk management while cultivating appropriate organisational culture. This includes:

- Establishing a board-approved AI governance framework and risk appetite
- Creating cross-functional oversight bodies (a formal AI governance committee is recommended where AI exposure is material)
- Developing clear ethical guidelines covering fair, ethical, accountable, and transparent use of AI
- Defining roles, responsibilities, and escalation procedures for AI risk incidents
- Building internal AI expertise to support both innovation and risk controls

### 2. Risk Management Systems and Procedures

Financial institutions must create systematic approaches to identify and monitor all AI deployment:

- **AI Identification**: Consistent processes for locating and classifying all AI usage across the institution
- **Comprehensive Inventory**: A documented catalogue of all AI systems in use, including approved usage scopes
- **Materiality Assessment**: Structured evaluation covering three dimensions:
  - **Impact**: Effect on customers, compliance, financial risk, or critical operations
  - **Complexity**: Technical sophistication and degree of automation
  - **Reliance**: Extent to which decisions depend on AI outputs without human review

### 3. AI Lifecycle Controls

MAS expects proportionate, end-to-end controls across the full AI lifecycle, with stronger safeguards required where AI affects customer outcomes, regulatory compliance, financial risk, or critical operations:

- **Data management**: Data quality, lineage, governance, and bias identification
- **Fairness and bias mitigation**: Controls to detect and address discriminatory model outcomes
- **Transparency and explainability**: Standards enabling audit and supervisory review
- **Human oversight protocols**: Defined human-in-the-loop requirements, especially for higher-risk applications
- **Third-party and vendor governance**: Independent testing, contractual audit rights, performance guarantees, notification requirements regarding AI integration, contingency plans for vendor discontinuation
- **Pre-deployment testing and evaluation**: Validation requirements scaled to risk level (independent reviews for higher-risk models; peer reviews for lower-risk)
- **Cybersecurity safeguards**: Including data security via private infrastructure for generative AI
- **Audit trail maintenance**: Logging and recordkeeping enabling regulatory review
- **Ongoing monitoring and change management**: Continuous performance assessment and structured processes for model updates

### 4. Organisational Capacity

Financial institutions must ensure they have:
- Adequate AI risk management capabilities commensurate with the scope and complexity of their AI deployment
- Sufficient skilled personnel with AI literacy and risk awareness
- Training programs for staff with supervisory or oversight responsibilities over AI systems

---

## Generative AI Considerations

Given that generative AI adoption in financial services remains at a relatively early stage, MAS recommends:

- Limiting current deployment primarily to human-assisting, non-customer-facing operational uses
- Implementing risk control checkpoints throughout the generative AI lifecycle
- Requiring human oversight for consequential decision-making outputs
- Deploying technical safeguards including input/output filtering
- Using private infrastructure where sensitive customer or financial data is involved

---

## Implementation Timeline

MAS proposes a **12-month transition window** following final guideline issuance, recognising that the maturity of AI risk management practices varies among financial institutions.

---

## Key Quote

Ms Ho Hern Shin, Deputy Managing Director (Financial Supervision), MAS, stated that the guidelines are intended to be "proportionate, risk-based" and designed to enable "responsible innovation" through appropriate safeguards that address key AI risks in the financial sector.
