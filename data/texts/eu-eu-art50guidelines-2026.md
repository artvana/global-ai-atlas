# European Commission Guidelines on the implementation of the transparency obligations for certain AI systems under Article 50 of the AI Act

**Commission Guidelines under Article 96 EU AI Act**
*European Union — adopted 20 July 2026 (Article 50 obligations applicable from 2 August 2026)*

---

## Overview

The European Commission adopted these Guidelines on 20 July 2026 (press release IP/26/1653), less than two weeks before the transparency obligations in Article 50 of the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) become applicable on 2 August 2026. The final document runs to 51 pages and follows a draft circulated for consultation in May 2026.

The stated purpose is to help people recognise when they are interacting with an AI system and when content has been generated or altered by AI, so as to reduce the risk of deception and manipulation.

Unlike the Commission's Article 53 GPAI Guidelines, these Guidelines are expressly **non-binding**: only the Court of Justice of the European Union can give an authoritative interpretation of the AI Act. In practice, however, national market surveillance authorities — and the AI Office where it has exclusive competence under Article 75(1) — are expected to follow them. The underlying Article 50 obligations themselves are enforceable, with fines of up to EUR 15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover under Article 99(4)(g).

The Guidelines are complemented by the voluntary [Code of Practice on marking and labelling AI-generated content](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content), published on 10 June 2026. Adherence to the Code is presented as one way of demonstrating compliance — a facilitating tool rather than a formal legal presumption of conformity.

## Key Provisions

### The four transparency scenarios

The Guidelines organise Article 50 into four scenarios, split between provider-level and deployer-level duties:

1. **Direct interaction with an AI system (Art. 50(1))** — providers must design systems so that natural persons are informed they are interacting with an AI system, unless this is obvious to a reasonably well-informed person. Chatbots and comparable real-time conversational tools are the paradigm case.
2. **Machine-readable marking of synthetic content (Art. 50(2))** — providers of generative AI systems must mark outputs in a machine-readable format so that synthetic audio, image, video and text is detectable as artificially generated or manipulated. Marking must be automatically detectable rather than relying only on human recognition.
3. **Deep fakes and AI-generated text on matters of public interest (Art. 50(4))** — deployers must disclose that image, audio or video content is a deep fake, and must disclose AI-generated or AI-manipulated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest where the text has not been subject to human review or editorial control.
4. **Emotion recognition and biometric categorisation (Art. 50(3))** — deployers must inform natural persons exposed to such systems of their operation.

### Scope clarifications

- "Directly interactive AI system" covers chatbots and comparable tools that engage users in real time.
- Synthetic content is read broadly, including partially generated material and manipulated imagery.
- Disclosures must be provided clearly and distinguishably at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure, and must be accessible to persons with disabilities.

### Exemptions and limits

- **Ordinary editing operations** — assistive edits such as spelling and grammar correction do not trigger the marking obligation where they do not substantially alter the informational substance of the content.
- **No retroactive labelling** — for image, audio and video deep fakes under Article 50(4), the relevant date is the date the content was *generated*, not the date it was published. Content generated before the obligations apply does not acquire a labelling duty on later publication.

### Interaction with the Digital Omnibus on AI

The Guidelines were adopted while Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (the Digital Omnibus on AI) was awaiting publication in the Official Journal. The Omnibus deferred parts of the AI Act timetable — including a transition for systems already placed on the market — while leaving the 2 August 2026 transparency and GPAI enforcement dates intact.

## Enforcement

- **EU AI Office** — coordination, and exclusive competence in the cases set out in Article 75(1).
- **National market surveillance authorities** — primary enforcement of Article 50 against providers and deployers.
- **Penalties** — up to EUR 15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover for infringement of Article 50 (Art. 99(4)(g)).

## Legal citation

EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), Articles 50, 96 and 99(4)(g).
