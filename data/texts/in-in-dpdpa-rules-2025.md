# Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025

## Full Title and Citation

**Full Title:** Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025
**Gazette Notification:** Published in the Gazette of India (Extraordinary), Part II — Section 3(i)
**Enacted under:** Section 40 read with various sections of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act 22 of 2023)
**Notifying Ministry:** Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY)
**Short Citation:** DPDP Rules 2025 or DPDPA Rules 2025

---

## Background and Enabling Legislation

The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 (DPDP Rules) are the subordinate legislation made under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDPA 2023), which received Presidential assent on August 11, 2023. The DPDPA 2023 is India's first comprehensive personal data protection statute, replacing a fragmented legal landscape in which data protection was addressed through a patchwork of sector-specific laws, the Information Technology Act 2000 and its rules, and judicial interpretations of constitutional rights to privacy (particularly the Supreme Court's landmark 2017 ruling in K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India).

The DPDPA 2023 established the framework: it defined key terms, stated foundational principles, created the Data Protection Board of India, and empowered the central government to make rules specifying the operational details. The Rules operationalize this framework, specifying the mechanics of notice, consent, data fiduciary obligations, the Consent Manager system, breach notification, cross-border data transfer, children's data protection, and enforcement.

The Rules were notified in November 2025 following a prolonged drafting process that included public consultations and revisions to an initial draft released in January 2025. The Rules came into force on the date of their notification for most structural provisions, with an 18-month phased implementation timeline for core operational obligations (consent, security, breach notification, etc.), with full compliance required by approximately May 2027.

---

## Core Principles (as operationalized by the Rules)

The Rules implement the seven foundational principles of the DPDPA 2023:

1. **Lawfulness and consent:** Personal data may only be processed with the consent of the Data Principal or on other lawful grounds specified in the Act.
2. **Purpose limitation:** Personal data must be collected only for specified, clear, and lawful purposes, and may not be processed for incompatible purposes.
3. **Data minimisation:** Only personal data that is necessary for the specified purpose may be collected and processed.
4. **Accuracy:** Data Fiduciaries must take reasonable steps to ensure personal data is accurate and kept up to date.
5. **Storage limitation:** Personal data must not be retained for longer than is necessary for the purpose for which it was processed.
6. **Security safeguards:** Data Fiduciaries must implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to protect personal data.
7. **Accountability:** Data Fiduciaries are responsible for demonstrating compliance with the Act and these Rules.

---

## Key Definitions (Rule 2, incorporating DPDPA 2023 definitions)

- **Data Principal:** The individual to whom personal data relates; in the case of a child, includes their parent or legal guardian.
- **Data Fiduciary:** Any person who, alone or in conjunction with other persons, determines the purpose and means of processing personal data.
- **Data Processor:** Any person who processes personal data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary.
- **Consent Manager:** A person registered under these Rules who acts as a single point of contact to enable a Data Principal to give, manage, review, and withdraw consent through an interoperable platform.
- **Significant Data Fiduciary:** A Data Fiduciary designated by the central government as such, based on volume and sensitivity of data processed, risk to rights of Data Principals, potential impact on sovereignty and integrity of India, risk to electoral democracy, security of the State, or public order.
- **Child:** Any person under the age of 18 years.
- **Personal data breach:** Any unauthorized processing of personal data or accidental disclosure, acquisition, sharing, use, alteration, destruction, or loss of access to personal data.

---

## Rule 3 — Notice Requirements

Data Fiduciaries must provide a notice to the Data Principal before seeking consent or as soon as reasonably practicable where personal data was not directly collected from the Data Principal. The notice must:

- Be provided in clear and plain language;
- Be available in English and in any of the 22 official languages of India listed in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution, if the Data Principal indicates a preference;
- State the specific personal data proposed to be collected and the purpose for which it will be processed;
- State the manner in which the Data Principal may exercise their rights under the DPDPA;
- State the manner in which the Data Principal may contact the Data Fiduciary's Data Protection Officer (or nominated person) for grievance redressal;
- Not be combined with other content in a way that obscures or diminishes its clarity.

The notice must be a standalone document — it may not be embedded within general terms and conditions. This requirement addresses the historic practice of burying consent in lengthy terms-of-service documents.

---

## Rule 4 — Consent

**Mechanics of Consent:**
Consent under the DPDP Rules must be:
- **Free:** Not obtained through coercion, undue influence, fraud, or misrepresentation;
- **Specific:** Granular and specific to the purpose for which data is being collected, not blanket consent for unspecified future uses;
- **Informed:** Based on the notice described in Rule 3;
- **Unconditional:** Not bundled with conditions unrelated to the purpose of data collection;
- **Unambiguous:** Signified by a clear affirmative action (ticking a box, clicking a button, or similar); silence, pre-ticked boxes, and inactivity do not constitute consent.

**Consent through Consent Managers:**
Data Principals may give, manage, review, and withdraw consent through registered Consent Managers. A Consent Manager must operate an interoperable platform accessible to Data Principals from any device.

**Withdrawal of Consent:**
Data Principals have the right to withdraw consent at any time. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing based on consent before withdrawal. Data Fiduciaries must make withdrawal as easy as giving consent. Upon withdrawal, the Data Fiduciary must cease processing within a reasonable time and, unless subject to retention obligations, delete the personal data.

**Deemed Consent:**
The Rules specify circumstances in which personal data may be processed without consent (deemed consent), including: compliance with legal obligations, performance of a function by the State for provision of services or benefits, response to medical emergencies, employment purposes, and public interest research with appropriate safeguards.

---

## Rule 5 — Consent Manager Framework

**Registration:**
Consent Managers must be registered with the Data Protection Board of India. Registration requirements include:

- Incorporation in India as a company under the Companies Act, 2013;
- Minimum net worth of INR 2,00,00,000 (approximately USD 240,000);
- Demonstrable technical, operational, and financial capacity to operate an interoperable consent management platform;
- Certification that the platform adheres to specified interoperability standards and data protection requirements;
- Absence of conflicts of interest with any Data Fiduciary.

**Obligations of Consent Managers:**
- Act in the best interests of Data Principals and fiduciary-free of conflicts of interest;
- Maintain comprehensive records of all consents given, managed, and withdrawn, and of all data sharing activities facilitated, for at least 7 years;
- Not sub-contract consent management obligations to third parties;
- Operate an accessible, user-friendly interface available in multiple languages;
- Provide Data Principals with a unified view of all their consents across different Data Fiduciaries through a single platform;
- Comply with technical standards and interoperability requirements issued by the Data Protection Board.

---

## Rule 6 — Data Fiduciary Obligations

**Data Quality:**
Data Fiduciaries must take reasonable steps to ensure that personal data is accurate, complete, and consistent with its stated purpose. Where personal data is used to make decisions about a Data Principal, the standard of accuracy must be commensurate with the impact of the decision.

**Data Minimisation:**
Data Fiduciaries must collect and retain only the personal data that is necessary for the specified purpose and must not seek personal data beyond what is necessary.

**Storage Limitation:**
Data Fiduciaries must erase personal data (and cause Data Processors to erase it) when the purpose for which it was collected has been fulfilled, when the Data Principal withdraws consent, or when instructed by the Data Principal to erase. An exception applies where retention is required by applicable law.

**Retention of Traffic Data and Logs:**
The Rules introduce a specific requirement that all personal data, traffic data, and logs generated from data processing activities be retained for at least one year after the purpose has been fulfilled or after the user account is deleted, for specified purposes listed in the Seventh Schedule (primarily law enforcement, national security, and fraud prevention purposes).

**Data Processor Agreements:**
Data Fiduciaries may engage Data Processors only on the basis of a valid contract. The contract must require the Data Processor to process personal data only in accordance with the Data Fiduciary's instructions and to implement security safeguards equivalent to those required of the Data Fiduciary.

**Data Protection Officer:**
Data Fiduciaries that are bodies corporate must appoint a Data Protection Officer (DPO) based in India who reports directly to the highest management level of the organization. The DPO must be the point of contact for Data Principals for grievance redressal.

---

## Rule 7 — Security Safeguards

Data Fiduciaries must implement appropriate technical and organizational security measures to prevent personal data breaches. Required measures include:

- Encryption of personal data, particularly during transmission and when stored in portable media;
- Access controls limiting access to personal data to personnel who need it for authorized purposes;
- Pseudonymisation of personal data where feasible without impairing the purpose of processing;
- Regular security testing, including vulnerability assessments and penetration testing;
- Incident response plans and breach response protocols;
- Training of personnel on data protection obligations and security practices;
- Vendor risk management procedures for Data Processors.

The specific technical measures required scale with the sensitivity of the personal data processed and the volume of Data Principals affected.

---

## Rule 8 — Personal Data Breach Notification

**Notification to the Data Protection Board:**
Upon becoming aware of a personal data breach, a Data Fiduciary must:

1. **Without delay:** Provide the Data Protection Board with an initial notification including a description of the nature of the breach, the categories and approximate number of Data Principals affected, the categories and approximate quantity of personal data records affected, the likely consequences of the breach, and the measures taken or proposed to mitigate the breach.

2. **Within 72 hours:** Provide a detailed report to the Board including all information available at that time, updated as information becomes available.

**Notification to Affected Data Principals:**
Data Fiduciaries must also notify each affected Data Principal without delay, providing:
- A description of the personal data breach;
- The likely consequences of the breach for the Data Principal;
- The measures taken or proposed to mitigate the adverse effects;
- The contact information of the Data Protection Officer or other point of contact.

Notification to Data Principals may be delayed if it would impede a law enforcement investigation, subject to approval by the Data Protection Board.

---

## Rule 9 — Rights of Data Principals

**Right to Access Information:**
Data Principals have the right to obtain from the Data Fiduciary:
- Confirmation of whether their personal data is being processed;
- A summary of the personal data being processed;
- A summary of the processing activities undertaken by the Data Fiduciary;
- Identities of all Data Fiduciaries and Processors with whom personal data has been shared.

Data Fiduciaries must respond within a reasonable time period as specified by the Board (expected to be 30 days in standard cases).

**Right to Correction and Erasure:**
Data Principals may request correction of inaccurate or misleading personal data, completion of incomplete data, and erasure of personal data that is no longer necessary for the purpose of processing.

**Right to Grievance Redressal:**
Data Principals may contact the Data Fiduciary's DPO or nominated person with grievances, and must receive a response within a specified time period.

**Right to Nominate:**
Data Principals may nominate another individual to exercise their rights in the event of their death or incapacity.

**Right to Complain to the Data Protection Board:**
If a Data Fiduciary fails to address a grievance satisfactorily, the Data Principal may complain to the Data Protection Board.

---

## Rule 10 — Processing of Children's Data

**Definition of Child:**
A child is any individual under 18 years of age.

**Verifiable Parental Consent:**
Before processing any personal data of a child, a Data Fiduciary must obtain verifiable consent from the child's parent or legal guardian. The Data Fiduciary must take reasonable technical and procedural steps to verify that the consenting individual is the child's parent or legal guardian.

**Prohibition on Tracking and Behavioral Monitoring of Children:**
Data Fiduciaries may not track or monitor the behavior of children, profile them for targeted advertising, or engage in other data processing activities that are detrimental to the wellbeing of children.

**Exemptions from Parental Consent (Fourth Schedule, Part A):**
The Rules specify classes of Data Fiduciaries and processing activities that are exempt from the parental consent requirement, including:
- Processing by healthcare providers for the provision of healthcare or health-related services to a child;
- Processing for the purpose of determining the real-time location of a child, limited to tracking in the interest of the child's safety and protection;
- Processing for education-related purposes under specified conditions;
- Processing by childcare service providers.

The exemption for real-time location tracking for child safety allows service providers to track a child's location for safety purposes without parental consent, subject to strict limitations on the use of that data for any other purpose.

---

## Rule 12 — Significant Data Fiduciaries

The central government may designate any Data Fiduciary as a **Significant Data Fiduciary** based on:
- Volume and sensitivity of personal data processed;
- Potential risk to the rights of Data Principals;
- Potential impact on sovereignty and integrity of India;
- Potential risk to electoral democracy;
- Security of the State or public order;
- National security considerations.

**Additional Obligations for Significant Data Fiduciaries:**

1. **Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA):** Every Significant Data Fiduciary must conduct and document a comprehensive DPIA at least once every 12 months, evaluating: the nature, scope, and context of processing; the purpose and necessity of processing; risks to rights and freedoms of Data Principals; and measures to mitigate those risks.

2. **Independent Audit:** Every Significant Data Fiduciary must arrange for an independent data protection audit at least once every 12 months by a certified auditor. The audit must assess compliance with the DPDPA and these Rules and the adequacy of the Data Fiduciary's security measures and governance practices.

3. **Algorithmic Accountability:** Significant Data Fiduciaries must maintain technical and organizational documentation of any algorithmic systems used to make significant decisions about Data Principals, and must be able to demonstrate to the Board that such systems operate consistently with the Act's principles.

4. **Senior Management Responsibility:** A senior official of the Significant Data Fiduciary, designated by its highest management body, must be personally responsible for oversight of data protection compliance.

---

## Rule 13 — Cross-Border Data Transfers

The central government may specify, by notification, categories of personal data that may not be transferred to other countries or territories, or conditions on which such transfers may be made. Pending such notifications, personal data may generally be transferred outside India subject to compliance with the Act's other requirements.

Where the government restricts cross-border transfers, Data Fiduciaries must store and process the restricted categories of personal data exclusively within India (data localization).

The Rules contemplate an adequacy-style framework under which the government may notify countries or territories to which personal data may be freely transferred, based on their data protection standards.

---

## Rule 14 — Data Protection Board of India

The Rules specify the organizational and procedural aspects of the Data Protection Board of India (the Board), including:

**Composition:** The Board consists of a Chairperson and other Members appointed by the central government. Members must have expertise in areas including technology, data protection, law, and public administration.

**Functions:** The Board adjudicates complaints filed by Data Principals against Data Fiduciaries, inquires into alleged breaches of the Act and Rules, imposes penalties, and issues directions to Data Fiduciaries and Processors.

**Powers:** The Board has the powers of a civil court for certain purposes, including the power to summon and examine witnesses, require production of documents, and take evidence on affidavit.

**Penalties:** The Board may impose financial penalties for violations of the Act and Rules, on a graduated scale based on the severity of the violation. Maximum penalties under the Act are:
- Up to INR 250 crore (approximately USD 30 million) for failure to implement security safeguards and notify breaches;
- Up to INR 200 crore for failure to observe obligations regarding children's data;
- Penalties for other violations on a progressively lower scale.

---

## Implementation Timeline

**At notification (November 2025):** Structural provisions (Board establishment, consent manager registration framework, definitions) in effect.

**After 18 months (approximately May 2027):** Core operational provisions in force, including:
- Consent requirements and notice obligations;
- Security safeguards and breach notification;
- Data minimisation and storage limitation;
- Children's data protection and parental consent;
- Remaining Board powers;
- Significant Data Fiduciary obligations.

**Regulations and notifications:** The central government continues to issue sector-specific regulations, lists of Significant Data Fiduciaries, data localization notifications, and other subordinate instruments to complete the regulatory framework.

## Relationship to Global Frameworks

The DPDP Rules draw on multiple international frameworks while reflecting India's distinctive legal and policy context:

- **GDPR influence:** The Rules borrow conceptually from the EU's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), including principles-based regulation, a consent framework, data subject rights, and supervisory authority enforcement. However, the Rules are generally considered less prescriptive than GDPR on specific technical requirements.

- **Distinctions from GDPR:** The DPDP Act and Rules do not include a right to object to processing for legitimate interests, do not mandate a Data Protection Officer for all organizations (only for bodies corporate that are Data Fiduciaries), and place stronger emphasis on deemed consent (legitimate interests processing equivalent) for state and certain other purposes.

- **Adequacy implications:** India and the European Commission have engaged in discussions about whether the DPDP Act meets GDPR adequacy standards, which would facilitate data transfers from the EU to India without additional safeguards.
