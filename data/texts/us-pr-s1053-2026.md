                     SENATE OF PUERTO RICO

                             S. B. 1053
                                  February 3, 2026
                          Introduced by Mrs. Soto Aguilú
                                        Referred to

                                          ACT

    To regulate advertising through the use of Artificial Intelligence in marketing;
require clear and mandatory disclosure when advertising content is generated
by AI; protect consumers from deceptions and confusions; and for other purposes
related thereto.


                              STATEMENT OF MOTIVES

   In the current digital era, Artificial Intelligence (AI) has revolutionized the field of
marketing and advertising, allowing the creation of highly
personalized, realistic and attractive content at a reduced cost. Tools such as
generators of synthetic images, videos, texts and audio have facilitated the production
of advertising campaigns that simulate authentic human experiences, which
represents a significant technological advance. However, this progress brings with it
ethical and transparency challenges that directly affect consumers in Puerto
Rico, an insular jurisdiction with a diverse population that includes a high percentage
of older persons and vulnerable communities.

   One of the main problems presented by this new aspect is the confusion
between what is real and what is generated by AI. For example, the hyperrealistic images created by
algorithms such as DALL-E or Midjourney can represent scenarios, testimonies or
                                           2


representations that appear captured from real life, but which in reality are
digitally fabricated. This may lead consumers to make
informed decisions based on erroneous perceptions, believing that they are seeing authentic
evidence of veracity, effectiveness or genuine representations. At specific
moments, such as during high-volume promotional campaigns (for example, in
shopping seasons such as Christmas or Black Friday), people may confuse
reality, assuming that a video of a satisfied user is real when in fact
it is a "deepfake" or an AI simulation.

   Particularly concerning is the impact on vulnerable populations, such as
older persons. In Puerto Rico, where 23% of the population is 65 years or older
according to data from the United States Census of 2020, older adults often
face digital barriers and less familiarity with technology. These individuals
can be easily deceived by AI-generated images or videos that simulate
attractive offers, false celebrity endorsements or idealized representations. That is to
say, an advertisement of a financial service with AI images of "before and after" could
induce an elderly person to believe in impossible results, leading to impulsive decisions
that compromise their economic or physical well-being.

   International studies, such as the report of the Organization for Economic
Cooperation and Development (OECD) of 2023 on AI and consumers, highlight that 45%
of respondents over 60 years of age do not distinguish between real and synthetic content,
increasing the risk of fraud and disinformation. In addition, the lack of regulation in
this area fosters unfair practices in the market. Unscrupulous companies could
use AI to create campaigns that exaggerate benefits without real evidence, violating
principles of advertising truthfulness established in existing laws such as the Act on
Monopolistic Practices and Unfair Competition of Puerto Rico (Act No. 77 of June 25,
1964).
                                              3


   This not only erodes confidence in the market, but also harms
ethical competitors who invest in authentic content. In local contexts, such as
tourism advertising on the Island, where beaches or cultural experiences are promoted, the
undisclosed use of AI could distort the perception of visitors and residents,
affecting the tourism economy that represents 7% of the GDP of Puerto Rico according to the
Economic Development Bank.

   Another relevant matter is that, in the absence of truthful information, without clear disclosure,
consumers are exposed to subtle manipulations that exploit cognitive
biases, such as the anchoring effect on decisions. For example, an AI advertisement of a
cultural event with ideal scenarios could influence low-income audiences,
leading them to participate unnecessarily. Additionally, in the area of health and
safety, regulated campaigns such as those of medications or services could be
promoted with false allegations generated by AI, putting public health at risk.

   This measure proposes an innovative and balanced regulation, since it does not impose
significant additional costs on companies. The disclosure requirement is limited to
clear and visible labels, such as "AI-Generated Content", which can be integrated
easily on digital platforms without requiring massive investments. This contrasts with
more onerous regulations in other jurisdictions, such as the European Union under the "AI Act
of 2024", which includes complex audits.

   In Puerto Rico, this Act would be pioneering, since there is no specific legislation on AI
in marketing, complementing existing frameworks such as the Consumer
Protection Act (Act No. 5 of April 23, 1973) without unnecessary overlaps. The
objective is to foster technological innovation while protecting consumers,
promoting an ethical advertising ecosystem that benefits all parties
involved. By requiring transparency, Puerto Ricans are empowered to make
informed decisions, reducing litigation and complaints before agencies such as the Department
                                                 4


     of Consumer Affairs (DACO). This initiative aligns with global trends, such as
     the recommendations of the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) of the U.S. on
     disclosure in AI, adapted to the insular context of Puerto Rico.



     BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY OF PUERTO RICO:

 1          Article 1. - Title.

 2          This Act shall be known as the "Act for Transparency in Advertising

 3   Generated by Artificial Intelligence in Puerto Rico".

 4          Article 2. - Definitions.

 5          For the purposes of this Act, the following terms shall have the meanings hereby

 6   expressed:

 7          a) Artificial Intelligence (AI): Any system, software or algorithm that simulates

 8   processes of human intelligence, including but not limited to the generation of text,

 9   images, audio, video or any synthetic multimedia content.

10          b) AI-Generated Content: Any advertising material created totally or

11   partially through AI, that is not derived exclusively from real human inputs

12   without algorithmic alteration.

13          c) Advertising: Any commercial communication intended to promote the sale,

14   use, acquisition or dissemination of goods, services, ideas, events or any other concept,

15   directed to the general public through digital, printed, audiovisual media or

16   any other channel.

                                                  5


 1          d) Clear Disclosure: A visible, legible and audible (according to the medium) notification,

 2   placed prominently on the advertising content, that explicitly indicates

 3   the use of AI.

 4          e) Consumer: Any natural or juridical person who receives, uses or is exposed to

 5   advertising as the final addressee.

 6          f) Advertising Company: Any entity, natural or juridical person, that creates,

 7   distributes or publishes advertising in Puerto Rico.

 8          Article 3. - Scope of Application.

 9          This Act shall apply to all advertising directed to consumers in the territory of

10   Puerto Rico, regardless of the origin of the advertising company. It shall not apply to

11   non-commercial content, such as pure artistic expressions or non-profit educational

12   communications. This regulation is new and does not modify existing laws,

13   except as necessary for its implementation.

14          Article 4. - Disclosure Requirement.

15          Every advertising company that uses AI to generate content in advertising

16   shall disclose said use in a clear and mandatory manner. The disclosure shall:

17          a) Include phrases such as "Content Generated by Artificial Intelligence" or "AI

18   Generated", in Spanish and English if the advertisement is bilingual.

19          b) Be placed in a prominent position: at the beginning of videos (for at least

20   10 seconds), at the top of images or texts, or as an overlay on audio.

21          c) Use a font size no smaller than 10% of the main size of the advertisement,

22   in a contrasting color for visibility.

                                                   6


 1          d) On digital platforms, include metadata or digital tags that allow

 2   automatic detection by browsers or apps.

 3          e) Not require additional actions from the consumer, such as clicks or scrolls, to

 4   visualize it.

 5          This requirement shall not impose significant additional costs, since it is integrated into

 6   existing advertising design processes.

 7          Article 5. - Prohibitions.

 8          The following are prohibited:

 9          a) Omit the disclosure in any AI-generated content.

10          b) Use AI to simulate real human testimonies without authentic evidence,

11   even with disclosure.

12          c) Direct deceptive AI advertising at vulnerable populations, such as

13   minors or older persons, with unverifiable allegations or interpretations.

14          d) Alter or remove disclosures in redistributions of advertisements.

15          Article 6. - Consumer Protection.

16          Consumers shall have the right to:

17          a) Report violations before the Department of Consumer Affairs

18   (DACO), which must investigate said complaint.

19          b) Receive refunds or compensation if a decision was induced by

20   undisclosed AI advertising that is deceptive.

21          c) Access governmental educational campaigns on AI in marketing, financed

22   by fines collected.

                                                 7


 1         Article 7. - Sanctions.

 2         Violations of this Act shall be sanctioned by DACO with:

 3         a) Administrative fines of $500 to $5,000 for the first infraction, escalating to

 4   $10,000 for repeat offenses.

 5         b) Cease and desist orders, including immediate withdrawal of advertisements.

 6         c) Civil sanctions for damages to consumers, up to triple the affected value.

 7         d) In serious cases, referral to the Prosecutor's Office for criminal charges if they involve

 8         intentional fraud.

 9         Article 8. - Implementation and Regulation.

10         DACO shall adopt regulations within 180 days of the approval of this Act,

11   including guidelines for disclosures in different media and monitoring mechanisms.

12   A public registry of violations shall be created for transparency.

13         Article 9.- Severability Clause.

14         If any provision, word, sentence or subsection of this Act is challenged

15   for any reason before a court and declared unconstitutional or null, such judgment shall not

16   affect, impair or invalidate the remaining provisions of this Act.

17         Article 10.- Effective Date.

18         This Act shall take effect immediately upon its approval.
