UNICEF Innocenti – Guidance on AI and Children, Version 3.0
December 2025

OVERVIEW

This is the third edition of UNICEF's guidance on AI and children, updated to reflect rapid advances in AI technologies such as generative AI, increased adoption of AI systems by children, emergent opportunities and risks with regard to children's rights, and changes in the AI governance landscape.

The guidance establishes a child rights-based framework for developing and deploying artificial intelligence systems used by or affecting children. It contains 10 requirements and 48 recommendations for AI policies and systems that uphold child rights.

Version 3.0 was informed by a range of inputs: the guidance of a diverse expert advisory group; an expert consultation with civil society, governments and academia; internal and external peer review; and emergent evidence, including a twelve-country UNICEF study with children and caregivers.

THE 10 CORE REQUIREMENTS

1. REGULATORY FRAMEWORKS AND OVERSIGHT
Establish governance structures with compliance mechanisms enforcing child rights standards throughout AI lifecycles. Governments must establish binding legal frameworks ensuring AI systems used by or affecting children comply with children's rights standards. Frameworks should include independent oversight mechanisms with genuine enforcement powers.

2. SAFETY FOR CHILDREN
Protect children from physical and psychological harm, addressing threats like synthetic child exploitation material and unsafe AI companions. New areas addressed in version 3.0 include AI-generated child sexual abuse material (CSAM), non-consensual intimate images, and the unique risks posed by AI companion applications used by children.

3. DATA AND PRIVACY PROTECTION
Implement enhanced protections using privacy-by-design principles recognizing children's vulnerability to data exploitation. Children's data requires enhanced protections beyond those afforded adults due to children's particular vulnerability and the long-term implications of data collected during childhood.

4. NON-DISCRIMINATION AND FAIRNESS
Prevent AI systems from perpetuating biases against children, ensuring equitable access across diverse populations. AI systems must not discriminate against children on any grounds including race, ethnicity, gender, disability, religion, nationality, or socioeconomic status.

5. TRANSPARENCY AND ACCOUNTABILITY
Make AI systems understandable to children, parents, and guardians with clear accountability for AI-driven decisions. Children, parents, and guardians have the right to know when AI systems are being used, how they work, and what decisions are being made. Clear lines of accountability must exist for harm caused by AI systems affecting children.

6. HUMAN AND CHILD RIGHTS RESPECT
Align development with international human rights standards, conducting child rights impact assessments. AI systems used by or affecting children must be developed and deployed in compliance with international human rights standards, including the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child.

7. DEVELOPMENT AND WELL-BEING SUPPORT
Design AI contributing positively to cognitive, social, and emotional development, prioritizing learning and health applications. AI systems must be designed to support rather than undermine children's holistic development, including cognitive, social, emotional, and physical dimensions.

8. INCLUSION OF AND FOR CHILDREN
Include diverse children in design processes, creating accessible tools for children with disabilities and marginalized communities. Children must be meaningfully included in the design, development, and governance of AI systems that affect them. AI systems should be accessible to all children including those with disabilities and from marginalized communities.

9. AI SKILLS AND PREPARATION
Equip children with digital literacy understanding AI capabilities and limitations through educational programs. All children should have access to age-appropriate AI literacy education that equips them to engage critically and safely with AI systems.

10. ENABLING ENVIRONMENT CREATION
Foster multi-stakeholder collaboration among governments, companies, and civil society supporting child-centered innovation. Governments, companies, civil society, academia, and international organizations must collaborate to create conditions in which child-centered AI can flourish.

VERSION 3.0 NEW CONTENT AREAS

New Digital Threats: Addresses synthetic child abuse material and non-consensual intimate imagery with prevention and response guidance, including specific requirements for AI systems that generate or disseminate such content.

Supply Chain Examination: Considers ethical concerns throughout AI lifecycles, including child labor in hardware production and harmful training datasets. Organizations must conduct due diligence on their AI supply chains to identify and address child rights risks.

Environmental and Conflict Impacts: Addresses energy-intensive AI's disproportionate effects on children's futures and AI applications in armed conflict, including autonomous weapons systems.

Positive Inclusion Applications: Highlights AI's potential for accessibility and personalized learning benefiting children with disabilities, including speech recognition, alternative communication tools, and customized educational approaches.

AI Companions: Provides specific guidance on AI companion applications increasingly used by children, addressing risks related to emotional dependence, data collection, and inappropriate content or interactions.

TARGET AUDIENCES

The guidance targets:
- Governments creating AI policies and legislation
- Companies developing child-facing AI products or whose AI systems indirectly affect children
- Educators using AI tools in learning environments
- Parents and caregivers seeking to understand AI's impact on children
- Civil society organizations advocating for children's rights in AI governance
- International organizations setting AI standards and norms

DEVELOPMENT PROCESS

The guidance has been developed since 2020 through:
- Pilot programs testing the framework in multiple countries
- Expert advisory group guidance from diverse global experts
- Expert consultations with civil society, governments, and academia
- Internal and external peer review
- Twelve-country research study with children and caregivers

KEY IMPLEMENTATION MESSAGE

"Successful implementation requires collaboration across sectors—governments must establish regulations, companies must embed ethics in design, educators must guide critical use, and civil society must provide oversight."
