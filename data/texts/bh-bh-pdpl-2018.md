# Law No. 30 of 2018 on the Protection of Personal Data
**Law No. 30 of 2018 (Bahrain)**
*Kingdom of Bahrain — Enacted July 12, 2018; Effective August 1, 2019*

---

## Overview / Background

Law No. 30 of 2018 on the Protection of Personal Data (the Bahrain PDPL) is the Kingdom of Bahrain's comprehensive personal data protection statute, enacted on July 12, 2018 and entering into force on August 1, 2019 (following a one-year implementation period). Bahrain was one of the first Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) states to enact comprehensive data protection legislation, establishing a framework that has influenced subsequent regional laws in Oman, Jordan, and other Middle Eastern jurisdictions.

The PDPL established the Personal Data Protection Authority (PDPA) as Bahrain's independent data protection supervisory authority. The PDPA has powers to issue licenses, receive and investigate complaints, conduct audits, issue guidance, and impose administrative penalties. The Minister responsible for information and communications technology oversees the PDPA's operations.

The PDPL applies to any organization (public or private sector) that controls the collection, processing, or use of personal data in Bahrain, or that processes personal data outside Bahrain on behalf of a controller established in Bahrain. The law applies regardless of the nationality of the data subjects. Certain exemptions apply to personal or family use, journalistic or artistic purposes, and processing for statistical or scientific research purposes under appropriate safeguards.

The PDPL is structured around principles broadly similar to the EU GDPR, though with features reflecting Bahrain's civil law tradition and GCC regulatory context.

## Key Provisions / Chapters

### Lawful Basis for Processing
- Processing of personal data requires one of the following lawful bases:
  - Consent of the data subject: must be free, specific, informed, and unambiguous; consent forms may not be bundled with general terms and conditions
  - Performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party, or at the data subject's request prior to entering into a contract
  - Compliance with a legal obligation applicable to the controller
  - Protection of vital interests of the data subject or another natural person
  - Performance of a task in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority
  - Legitimate interests pursued by the controller, provided not overridden by the fundamental rights and interests of the data subject

### Sensitive Personal Data
- The PDPL provides heightened protection for special categories of sensitive personal data:
  - Health and medical data
  - Genetic data
  - Biometric data used for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person
  - Racial or ethnic origin
  - Political opinions
  - Religious or philosophical beliefs
  - Trade union membership
  - Criminal convictions and offenses
  - Sexual orientation or behavior
  - Financial information (bank accounts, credit records)
- Processing of sensitive data requires explicit consent or falls within strictly defined statutory exceptions
- Biometric data identification systems (facial recognition, fingerprint systems) require explicit legal authorization or consent

### Data Subject Rights
- **Right of access:** Data subjects may request confirmation of whether their data is held and receive a copy within 30 days of the request.
- **Right to correction:** Data subjects may request correction of inaccurate or incomplete data; controllers must respond within 15 days.
- **Right to deletion:** Data subjects may request deletion of data no longer necessary for its original purpose, or where consent is withdrawn; controllers must respond within 15 days.
- **Right to objection:** Data subjects may object to processing of their data for direct marketing or on grounds relating to their particular situation (where processing is based on legitimate interests).
- **Right to withdraw consent:** Data subjects may withdraw consent at any time; withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of prior processing.

### Data Protection Officer (DPO)
- Organizations that process personal data on a large scale, or that process special categories of data as a core activity, must appoint a Data Protection Officer.
- The DPO must have expertise in data protection law and practice.
- The DPO is responsible for advising on compliance, monitoring adherence to the PDPL, cooperating with the PDPA, and serving as a contact point for data subjects.

### Cross-Border Transfer Restrictions
- Transfer of personal data outside Bahrain is permitted only where:
  - The destination country or territory provides equivalent data protection
  - The controller implements appropriate safeguards (e.g., binding corporate rules, contractual clauses)
  - One of the enumerated derogations applies (consent, contract, vital interests, legal claims)
- The PDPA maintains a list of approved jurisdictions

### Data Breach Notification
- Controllers must notify the PDPA and affected data subjects of personal data breaches that are likely to result in harm to data subjects.
- Notification must occur without undue delay following discovery of the breach.
- Notification must include: nature of the breach; data categories and approximate number of individuals affected; likely consequences; and measures taken or proposed.

### Implementing Regulations
- The PDPL was supplemented by implementing regulations issued by the PDPA between 2019 and 2021, covering: consent requirements; DPO appointment procedures; cross-border transfer mechanisms; breach notification timelines; and sector-specific guidance (healthcare, financial services).

### Licensing Requirement
- Certain categories of personal data processing (particularly large-scale processing of sensitive data) require a license from the PDPA before commencement.
- License applications require controllers to demonstrate appropriate governance, security, and data subject rights mechanisms.

## Enforcement & Penalties

- **Administrative penalties:** The PDPA may impose fines of up to BHD 20,000 (approximately $53,000 USD) per violation for non-compliance with the PDPL.
- **Criminal penalties:** Individuals who unlawfully process sensitive personal data, intentionally breach security obligations, or obstruct PDPA investigations face imprisonment of up to 1 year and/or fines.
- **PDPA enforcement powers:** The PDPA may issue binding corrective orders, suspend or revoke licenses, and publish enforcement decisions.
- **Civil liability:** Data subjects whose rights are violated may seek compensation for material and non-material damages through Bahraini courts.
- **Aggravated penalties:** Repeat violations or particularly serious breaches involving health data or financial data may attract enhanced penalties.
- **Sector coordination:** The PDPA coordinates with the Central Bank of Bahrain (CBB) and other sector regulators to ensure consistent enforcement across financial services, telecommunications, and healthcare.
