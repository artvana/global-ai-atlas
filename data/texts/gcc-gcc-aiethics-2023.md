INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

The term Artificial Intelligence (AI) refers to computer systems and devices
that simulate human intelligence. They exhibit a special behaviour that makes
them mimic human cognitive abilities and modes of operation. Among the most
important of these abilities are analysis, inference, cumulative learning, and
the making of decisions and reactions in changing situations for which the
machine has not been pre-programmed — and this is the main principle of how AI
operates.

The ability of AI to simulate and surpass the way humans understand and
interact with the world around us — through the various forms of machine
learning with which it has been equipped to recognise patterns in data — has
led to many beneficial outcomes. At the same time, however, it has also posed
risks to human societies, the environment, ecosystems, human life, and even
the human mind itself.

The use of AI technologies affects many sectors and fields of life, such as
the economy, work, upbringing and education, culture, health, social welfare,
and others. It is no secret that AI can offer great opportunities to advance
the welfare of society in many areas of life. For example, AI has contributed
to facilitating the educational process and enabling access to knowledge for
various societal groups, especially during the period of the pandemic crisis,
which was very difficult to overcome without resorting to AI technologies.
Nevertheless, several issues and challenges should be taken into consideration
when dealing with AI, as it can undermine the protection of human rights and
personal data in general or in particular. For example, the use of AI may
threaten the right to equality and the prevention of discrimination, which is
one of the most important pillars on which the human rights system as a whole
rests. In addition, AI technologies may threaten and violate special rights,
such as individuals' right to privacy, or, conversely, may strengthen them in
a systematic and organised way — as when these technologies operate as a
safety valve for the enjoyment of fundamental rights and personal and
political freedom. Accordingly, we are speaking here of a double-edged weapon,
and it must be handled with caution.

Accordingly, the risks and threats arising from the spread of AI use — which
are expected to increase in the coming years — may affect governments,
economies, and societies locally, regionally, and internationally. This
underscores the importance of an urgent national and international response to
reduce the likelihood of their occurrence or to limit them and address their
effects.

Based on the foregoing, this Guiding Document on the Ethics of AI Use was
developed in order to support innovation while protecting the vital interests
of the GCC member states and their national security from risks and threats
that may arise from AI or other technologies. This contributes to achieving
optimal benefit from technological developments and enables the GCC states to
lead globally on all fronts. Trustworthy AI was our fundamental aspiration in
creating this document, since it is important to build AI systems that are
worthy of trust — because humans will not be able to fully and confidently
reap their benefits unless the technology, including the processes and the
people behind it, is trustworthy. This document was created on the basis of
the "Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence" issued by the
United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO) in
2021.


================================================================================
VALUES AND PRINCIPLES
================================================================================

The aim of these guiding values and principles is to promote AI technology in
the GCC states across all dimensions — technical, social, and even political.
The presence of good intentions for use does not preclude the possibility that
AI systems may cause unintended harm. Accordingly, the GCC states should be
guided by this document in order to achieve trustworthy AI that is consistent
with the United Nations recommendations on AI ethics.

These guiding values and principles set out a general framework for developing
AI systems that revolves around protecting human dignity and rights,
preserving the environment and coexistence with all living beings, and is
based on a commitment to using this technology in the service of humanity and
the public good — with the aim of improving human welfare and freedom. While
AI systems offer great opportunities, they also give rise to certain risks
that must be handled appropriately and proportionately.

This is the approach that the GCC states should adopt in order to become a
leading region for advanced and ethical technology, since safe AI will benefit
the citizens of the GCC states in line with fundamental values represented by
respect for Islamic Sharia, human rights, and the rule of law.

In this context, the GCC states, through this document, seek to increase
investment opportunities for producers of AI systems and to enable them to
gain a competitive advantage by integrating AI into products and services that
serve humanity. They also seek to improve users' trust in AI products, since
the absence of a clear and comprehensive ethical framework supporting the safe
application of this technology hinders the trust of people and societies in
the development of the technology and its applications.

Accordingly, the values and principles mentioned in this document must be
respected and, where necessary, strengthened by drafting new legislation,
regulations, and guidelines, and by amending existing legislation, regulations,
and guidelines in a manner consistent with the local laws and regulations of
the member states.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VALUES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

First Value: Respect for Human Dignity, Freedom, and Autonomy — Their
              Protection and Promotion
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Human dignity rests on the idea that every human being possesses an
"intrinsic value," which should never be diminished or compromised by others
in any form whatsoever — including by new technologies such as AI systems. In
this context, respect for human dignity means treating all people with the
respect due to them as inviolable moral subjects, and not as mere objects to
be sorted, recorded, bred, or conditioned. Accordingly, AI systems should be
developed in a way that respects, serves, and protects the physical and
mental safety of human beings and their personal and cultural sense of
identity, meets their basic needs, and grants the humans who interact with AI
systems the ability to maintain full and effective self-determination of
themselves, including the choice and determination of their own desires.


Second Value: Respect for Islamic Sharia and the Constitution and the
               Strengthening of Gulf Cohesion
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
AI systems should work to preserve the principles of the tolerant Islamic
Sharia, the constitution and national legislation, the prevailing laws, and
the local standards and procedures in force in the GCC states, and should
strengthen them and respect the plurality of values and life choices of
individuals. Such systems must not undermine the application of these
principles and laws or limit their effectiveness. Where appropriate, these
systems should also support strengthening cooperation and integration among
the GCC states, and should include a commitment to ensuring that they do not
operate in ways that undermine the fundamental commitments on which the rule
of law is based, the mandatory laws and regulations, and that they ensure due
process and equality before the law for all members of society.


Third Value: Environmental Protection and Promotion of Sustainability
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Digital transformation initiatives face challenges, the most important of
which is the negative impact on the environment and on the ecosystems of
human beings and other living creatures. The GCC states must therefore take
into account the environmental risks generated by operating and training AI
systems using electrical energy — for example, carbon emissions or the use of
fresh water for cooling — so as to ensure that ecosystems are not degraded,
and thereby ensure their sustainability for future generations.


Fourth Value: Promoting Peaceful Use to Achieve the Welfare of Gulf Citizens
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Human welfare means that AI systems should work to improve the means of life
for individuals and the community. When AI systems are fed a massive flow of
data, these systems will undertake repetitive tasks with a high level of
accuracy without boredom or fatigue. The entities responsible for deploying AI
systems must verify that these systems are not used in fields that threaten
human coexistence, security, or safety within the societies of the GCC states.
AI systems must not cause or exacerbate harm or negatively affect humans. This
means that AI systems and the environments in which they operate must be safe
and secure, must be technically robust, and that they must not be vulnerable
to harmful use. This document also recommends that vulnerable persons —
including persons with disabilities, the elderly, and others — be given
greater attention, and that their needs be incorporated into the development
of AI systems.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRINCIPLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

First Principle: Decisions Are for Humans
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
AI systems should support human autonomy and decision-making, as set out in
the values mentioned previously. This requires that AI systems function as
enablers of a democratic, prosperous, and just society by supporting the
individual and the community, promoting fundamental rights, and allowing
human oversight of AI systems.

Like any technology, AI technologies can both enable and impede fundamental
rights at the same time. They can benefit people, for example, by helping
them track their personal data, by increasing access to education, by
speeding up the diagnosis of diseases, or by providing appropriate treatments
— thereby supporting them and granting them the right to education and to
treatment. However, given the extent of AI systems' access to available data
and their analytical capabilities, they can also have a negative impact on
fundamental rights. In cases where such risks exist, a fundamental rights
impact assessment should be carried out. This should be done before the
system is developed and should include an assessment of the level of harm
and risk to all sections of society, in order to ensure human safety and
security before deployment.

Accordingly, AI systems should support individuals' freedom to make better
and more informed choices in line with their goals, and these systems should
avoid shaping and influencing human behaviour through mechanisms that may be
difficult to detect — because they may exploit unconscious processes,
including various forms of unfair manipulation, deception, and conditioning,
all of which may threaten individual autonomy. User autonomy should be the
fundamental principle in the system's functions, and the key to this is to
grant the user the right not to be subjected to a decision based solely on
automated processing if this would have legal effects on users or
significantly affect them.

Recommendations and Suggestions for Achieving This Principle:

- Activating Governance and Human Oversight: This document recommends
  activating human oversight to ensure that AI systems do not undermine human
  autonomy or cause other negative effects. Oversight can be achieved through
  governance mechanisms which may include the decision not to use an AI
  system in a particular situation, defining levels of human discretion
  during use of the system, or ensuring the ability to override the decision
  taken by the system. Furthermore, it must be ensured that the public
  implementing entities have the capacity to exercise oversight in line with
  their authorities. Oversight mechanisms with varying degrees may be needed
  to support safety measures and other controls, depending on the field of
  application of the AI system and the potential risks. The less oversight a
  human can exercise over an AI system, the greater the need for more
  comprehensive evaluation tests and stricter governance.


Second Principle: Safety and Prevention of Harm
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
One of the features that AI possesses is the technical power to generate
decisions and control outputs, which requires that AI systems be developed
within a precautionary approach toward risk and in a manner that makes them
behave reliably as intended, while minimising unintended and unforeseen harm
and preventing unacceptable harm. This should also apply to potential changes
in the operation of these systems within an environment that may interact
with the system in a way that threatens the physical and mental safety of
humans and all living beings.

AI systems share characteristics with all software systems: they may suffer
from vulnerabilities that can allow system hackers to exploit them, and
attacks may target the data, the AI model, or even its infrastructure. Such
attacks may result in changes in the system's behaviour, prompting it to make
decisions that differ from what was expected, or causing it to shut down in a
way that threatens the safety of humans and other living beings. This
document therefore recommends ensuring adequate security protection for the
data used by AI systems and for the training and operation models, and
protecting the system from extremist or terrorist groups that may exploit the
system's use for purposes harmful to society. Steps should be taken to
prevent and mitigate this.

Recommendations and Suggestions for Achieving This Principle:

- Establishing a Fall-Back Plan: This document recommends establishing a
  fall-back point for AI systems in the event of high-severity damage that
  threatens humans, living creatures, or the environment. This includes
  reducing unintended consequences and errors. In addition, processes should
  be established to clarify and assess the potential risks associated with
  the use of AI systems across different fields of application, and to
  identify what is known as a Rollback system as a measure for safety and to
  prevent the continuation of harm. When the development process or the
  system itself is expected to pose particularly high risks, it is essential
  to develop and test rollback and safety measures proactively.

- Assessing the Accuracy of Predictions, Recommendations, and Decisions:
  This document recommends ensuring a high level of accuracy in an AI
  system's ability to make correct judgements, or its ability to provide
  correct predictions, recommendations, or decisions based on the available
  data or models. A clear and well-designed evaluation process can support,
  mitigate, and correct the unintended risks resulting from inaccurate
  predictions. In cases where inaccurate predictions are difficult to avoid,
  it is essential to estimate the potential error rate to help decision-makers
  determine the fate of the system and whether it should be released or
  prevented based on the error rate — particularly in situations where the AI
  system directly affects human life.

- Testing and Verifying Reliability: This document recommends verifying the
  reliability of AI systems and their ability to reproduce results. An AI
  system is considered reliable if it works correctly with a set of inputs
  and in a range of situations. This is necessary during the AI system's
  performance evaluation phase in order to prevent unintended damage.
  Developers should ensure during AI testing that the system's behaviour does
  not act anomalously when the same inputs are repeated within the same range
  of conditions.


Third Principle: Justice, Equity, and Non-Discrimination
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
All stakeholders should be involved throughout the lifecycle of AI systems,
and everyone should be empowered to participate in their development. In
addition, individuals and communities should be enabled to obtain the
benefits of AI system applications equally and fairly, which means ensuring
equal access through inclusive design processes as well as equal treatment.
This requirement is closely linked to the principle of justice. Member states
should also adopt the principle of access for all during the system design
stages in a way that allows all persons to use AI products or services,
regardless of their age, gender, abilities, or characteristics. Access to
this technology should also be made available to persons with disabilities,
vulnerable elderly persons, and others.

Recommendations and Suggestions for Achieving This Principle:

- Auditing Datasets Used in Training Systems: This document recommends
  ensuring that the datasets used by AI systems for training and operation
  are free of any unintended biases that may affect certain categories and
  create direct bias or discrimination against specific groups or persons,
  which may lead to the exacerbation of bias and marginalisation in outputs.
  Harm may also result from the deliberate exploitation of biases or by
  engaging in unfair competition. Bias or discrimination is not limited to
  shortcomings in the data; the way AI systems are developed (such as
  algorithm programming) may also suffer from unfair bias.

- Establishing Oversight Processes to Ensure Justice, Equity, and
  Non-Discrimination: This document recommends establishing oversight
  processes to analyse, address, and assess the purpose, restrictions,
  requirements, and decisions of an AI system in a clear and transparent
  manner before launching the system. Furthermore, recruiting from diverse
  backgrounds, cultures, and disciplines can ensure a diversity of views and
  should be encouraged.

- Addressing Digital Divides: This document recommends addressing digital
  and knowledge divides within and between countries throughout the
  lifecycle of any AI system, particularly with regard to access to
  technology and data — and the quality of access — in accordance with
  relevant national legal frameworks, as well as with regard to knowledge,
  skills, connectivity and communication, and the effective participation of
  the relevant local communities in the efforts made in this regard, in
  order to treat each person equitably.

- Engaging Stakeholders: This document recommends engaging stakeholders in
  advance and consulting with them throughout the lifecycle of AI systems,
  given that they are most likely to be affected by these systems, whether
  directly or indirectly. It is also useful to seek stakeholder feedback
  regularly, even after AI systems have been launched, throughout the
  implementation and operation process of AI systems.

- Ensuring Diversity in the Means of Making AI Systems Available: This
  document recommends ensuring diversity in the means of making AI systems
  available, and not limiting them to a narrow set of methods, technologies,
  or channels. Design principles that address the broadest possible range of
  users should be taken into account, in accordance with accessibility
  standards and access for all. This will enable equitable access and the
  active participation of all people.


Fourth Principle: Privacy Protection and Data Protection
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Privacy is closely linked to the principle of harm prevention and is a
fundamental right that is particularly affected by AI systems. The GCC states
should prevent harm to privacy through governance of data use that preserves
the quality and integrity of the data used and its relevance in light of the
field in which the AI systems will be deployed, along with their access
protocols, and the ability to process data in a privacy-preserving manner.

Recommendations and Suggestions for Achieving This Principle:

- Regulating the Use of Digital Records: This document recommends regulating
  the use of digital records by which AI systems profile human behaviour to
  infer the preferences or orientations of individuals — such as the use of
  age, gender, or religion — so that they are not used for unlawful or
  unfair discrimination against them. AI systems should ensure the
  protection of privacy and data throughout the entire lifecycle of the
  system, including the information initially provided by the user, as well
  as the information generated about the user during their interaction with
  the system.

- Restricting Absolute Use of Data: This document recommends following best
  principles and practices in handling data and the appropriate mechanisms
  for encrypting it, which entails the necessity of restricting the absolute
  use of data — particularly personal data — by AI systems. The purpose of
  use, expected outcomes, and risk assessment should be specified for each
  system separately.

- Regulating Absolute Access to Data: This document recommends adhering to
  data governance principles in a way that ensures security and provides
  accountability for those who violate data privacy. Absolute access to data
  — and especially personal data intended for use by AI systems — must be
  regulated. Protocols governing access to data should be established,
  specifying who can access the data and under what circumstances. Only
  qualified personnel who have the competence and the need to access
  individuals' data should be permitted to do so, with the necessary measures
  taken for the optimal use of audit trails relating to access to data and
  its use.

- Monitoring the Quality and Integrity of Data: This document recommends
  monitoring the quality and integrity of the data used by AI systems and
  carrying out adequate testing to verify the quality of the datasets used,
  which is a matter of paramount importance for the performance of AI
  systems. Work should be carried out to develop an internal automated system
  for monitoring and alerting that incorporates human oversight in the data
  collection processes, since the data may contain social biases,
  inaccuracies, errors, and harmful data that may cause damage during the
  training or operation of AI models — for example by changing their
  behaviour, particularly with self-learning systems. Tests should therefore
  be carried out according to clear criteria for the processes and datasets
  used and should be documented at every step — such as planning, training,
  testing, and deployment. This should also apply to AI systems that have
  not been developed internally but have instead been obtained from external
  institutions. Appropriate channels should also be provided for feedback to
  AI technologies in a way that does not allow them to be tampered with by
  other parties.

- Establishing Appropriate Frameworks and Mechanisms for Data Protection:
  This document recommends establishing appropriate frameworks and
  mechanisms for data protection based on national principles and standards
  for the protection of personal data with respect to its collection, use,
  processing, disclosure, and the exercise by data subjects of their rights.
  These frameworks and mechanisms should ensure the existence of a
  legitimate purpose or objective and a sound legal basis for handling
  personal data, including the requirements for obtaining informed consent
  from the data subjects concerned. These frameworks and mechanisms should
  also enjoy the protection of judicial systems and should be guaranteed to
  be applied throughout the lifecycle of AI systems. Practical examples of
  this include granting users of metaverse technology, or those acting on
  their behalf, the full right to manage and control their data in
  accordance with personal data protection policies and legislation.


Fifth Principle: Transparency and Explainability
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Efforts must be made to increase the transparency of AI systems and to
strengthen their explainability throughout their lifecycle. However, the
level of transparency and explainability should always be appropriate to the
context and consequences, since explainability is a matter of paramount
importance for building and maintaining users' trust in AI systems. People
should be fully aware of any decision being taken based on information
derived from AI systems and on the basis of AI algorithms, including
decisions affecting their safety or human rights. In such cases, they have
the right to ask the relevant AI actors or public sector institutions for
explanatory information about the reasons for taking those decisions that
affect their rights and freedoms. This may be achieved by submitting appeals
or objections to a designated officer responsible for receiving such requests
in order to reconsider and correct the decisions taken — provided that the
decision-making mechanism is not treated as a "black-box" algorithm in such
circumstances. AI actors should inform users in a sound and timely manner
about products or services provided directly and about products or services
provided through the use of AI systems. It should also be noted that it may
be necessary to strike a balance between the principle of transparency and
explainability and other principles such as the right to privacy and the
principle of safety and security.

Recommendations and Suggestions for Achieving This Principle:

- Developing Traceability of How Decisions Are Reached: This document
  recommends developing traceability so that the datasets and processes
  leading to an AI system's decision are documented — including the
  collection and classification of data, as well as the algorithms used —
  according to the best possible standards to allow for traceability and to
  increase transparency. This also applies to the decisions made by an AI
  system. Such a mechanism should make it possible to identify the reasons
  why an AI decision was incorrect, which in turn can help prevent future
  errors.

- Developing Explainable AI Systems: This document recommends developing AI
  systems in a way that is as explainable as possible. Explainability
  relates to the ability to explain both the technical processes of an AI
  system and the related human decisions, such as the system's fields of
  application. Explanation or interpretation should be provided in a timely
  manner and in a manner appropriate to the intellectual level of the
  expertise of the relevant stakeholders (such as the layperson, the
  regulator, or the researcher).

- Informing Users of Their Interaction with AI Systems: This document
  recommends informing users that they are interacting with an AI system,
  and AI systems should be identifiable as such. In addition, the option
  should be provided to make a decision against this interaction in favour
  of human interaction when needed, in order to ensure compliance with
  fundamental rights. AI practitioners or end users should also be informed
  of the capabilities and limitations of the AI system in a manner
  appropriate to the current use case. This may include reporting on the
  level of accuracy of the AI system, as well as its limits or even the
  risks associated with its use.


Sixth Principle: Responsibility, Accountability, and Awareness
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The requirements of responsibility and accountability are closely linked to
the principle of justice. They require putting in place mechanisms to ensure
the assumption of responsibility for the system's performance and to make
accountability available for AI systems and their outcomes — before, during,
and after their development, deployment, and use. This requires a focus on
the awareness of individuals and their moral responsibility for the practices
and acts they undertake in the virtual environment, given the lack of clarity
of the legal authority that governs it so far. Efforts should also be made to
promote awareness and knowledge of AI technology and its ethics among the
general public through education, instruction, and training, taking into
account the linguistic, social, and cultural diversity of the local
community.

Recommendations and Suggestions for Achieving This Principle:

- Developing Legislation that Ensures Legal Accountability: This document
  recommends developing legislation that affirms that responsibility for the
  performance of AI systems lies with entities that can be held legally
  accountable. Accordingly, responsibility should not be left on the system,
  but should be clearly defined, with the entities concerned with developing
  AI systems bearing responsibility for the performance of the system
  throughout its lifecycle.

- Developing Legislation that Ensures Auditing and Evaluation: This document
  recommends developing legislation and procedures relating to the technical
  auditing of system performance, with the possibility of auditing and
  evaluating algorithms, data, and design processes. The system should
  always be available to internal and external auditors independently for
  any authorised evaluation process, with evaluation reports being provided
  in a manner that contributes to improving the credibility of the
  technology.

- Developing Mechanisms for Reporting on the Outputs of AI Systems: This
  document recommends developing mechanisms for reporting on the actions or
  decisions resulting from AI systems, with response to such reports being
  ensured in an appropriate manner that satisfies users. Reports should also
  be documented and analysed by specialised entities in order to assess and
  reduce the potential negative impacts of AI systems, and due protection
  should be provided to whistleblowers when they legitimately report on the
  performance of an AI system.

- Promoting Awareness and Disseminating Knowledge: This document recommends
  promoting awareness of AI systems by creating educational materials for
  various age groups in society. These educational materials should be made
  openly available to all in order to raise awareness of the fields of AI,
  its impact on society, the pros and cons of its uses, and the importance
  of data throughout the AI lifecycle. It is also appropriate to increase
  media campaigns, taking into account linguistic, social, and cultural
  diversity to ensure that the benefit reaches everyone and to help
  individuals make appropriate decisions about the use of AI systems. Joint
  trainings can also be organised between the GCC states to achieve
  knowledge integration on how AI systems work.


Seventh Principle: Integrity and Non-Falsification
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The principle of integrity in the use of AI should be adopted, which is
embodied in the keenness to avoid falsifying facts and avoid exaggerating,
amplifying, or distorting the abilities and capabilities of AI for ulterior
goals or purposes — such as making profits, achieving competitive advantages,
or influencing public opinion in a way that negatively affects the primary
beneficiaries. The fundamental interests of individuals and the principal
parties dealt with should be taken into account in the first place.

Recommendations and Suggestions for Achieving This Principle:

- Establishing Mechanisms for Verifying the Application of Specific
  Foundations and Standards on the Introduction or Generalisation of AI
  Systems: This document recommends establishing clear mechanisms that make
  it possible to verify the application of specific foundations and
  standards on which decisions concerning the introduction or generalisation
  of AI systems are based. These foundations and standards should also be
  clear, documented, and available for any authorised evaluation process by
  internal and external auditors.


================================================================================
USE, BENEFIT FROM, AND APPLICATION OF THIS GUIDE
================================================================================

The member states and other concerned entities should respect, promote,
disseminate, and protect the values, principles, and ethical standards set
out in this document with regard to AI; they should also take all possible
measures to apply the provisions of this Guide.

The member states should also seek to expand the scope of their work on this
Guide and to complement this work by cooperating with all relevant national
and international governmental and non-governmental organisations, as well as
with transnational corporations and with scientific organisations whose
activities fall within the scope of this Guide and within its purposes and
objectives. Establishing a methodology for assessing ethical consequences and
establishing national committees for AI ethics may be two important means to
this end.


================================================================================
PROMOTING AI ETHICS
================================================================================

The Cooperation Council for the Arab States of the Gulf will promote this
Guide by publishing it on the official website of the General Secretariat of
the Council and on the e-Government portal of the Council, as well as by
disseminating it through the e-Government portals and official social media
sites of the member states, as the member states see fit.

This document should be taken into account when developing new policies or
legislation, or when preparing national strategies for e-government, digital
transformation, the digital economy, and information security — including the
programmes, initiatives, and projects emerging from them.


================================================================================
FINAL PROVISIONS
================================================================================

This document is a single and indivisible document, and the fundamental
values and principles contained therein shall be considered integrated and
interrelated.

No provision of this document may be interpreted as intending to substitute
any of its provisions for the duties or rights of states or to be interpreted
in a manner that would alter or otherwise prejudice these duties and rights.
