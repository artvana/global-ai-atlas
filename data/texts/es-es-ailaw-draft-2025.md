# Anteproyecto de Ley para el Buen Uso y la Gobernanza de la Inteligencia Artificial
## (Draft Law for the Proper Use and Governance of Artificial Intelligence)

**Issuing Authority:** Government of Spain (Consejo de Ministros / Council of Ministers)
**Document Type:** Anteproyecto de Ley (Preliminary Draft Bill)
**Date of Council of Ministers Approval:** March 11, 2025 (first reading, "aprobación en primera lectura")
**Legal Basis:** Article 26.4 of Law 50/1997 on the Government
**Legislative Procedure:** Urgent (procedimiento urgente)
**Public Consultation Period:** March 18–26, 2025
**Official Source:** https://www.lamoncloa.gob.es/consejodeministros/resumenes/Paginas/2025/110325-resumen_consejo-ministros.aspx
**Regulations.ai Entry:** https://regulations.ai/regulations/RAI-ES-NA-DADLGXX-2025

## Overview

The Spanish Government approved on March 11, 2025 the preliminary draft of the "Anteproyecto de Ley para el Buen Uso y la Gobernanza de la Inteligencia Artificial" (Draft Law for the Proper Use and Governance of Artificial Intelligence). The draft is designed to transpose and operationalise the EU AI Regulation (Regulation 2024/1689, the EU AI Act) into Spanish national law, establishing concrete legal obligations, a domestic enforcement framework, a comprehensive sanctions regime, and detailed governance mechanisms for AI systems deployed in Spain.

Spain frames the draft as aligning domestic law with European AI regulation timelines and obligations, not introducing an entirely independent legal framework. The bill will be submitted to the Cortes Generales (Spanish Parliament) for parliamentary debate after completing the preliminary draft consultation process.

---

## Key Definitions (Adopted from EU AI Act)

- **AI system**: Produces outputs such as content, predictions, recommendations, or decisions influencing real or virtual environments
- **Provider**: Develops or markets AI systems
- **Operator/Deployer**: Deploys AI systems in professional contexts
- **High-risk AI**: Systems affecting safety-critical sectors or fundamental rights
- **Prohibited practice**: AI uses creating unacceptable risk

---

## Governance Structure

### Spanish Agency for the Supervision of Artificial Intelligence (AESIA)
**AESIA** (Agencia Española de Supervisión de la Inteligencia Artificial) serves as the default national competent authority for AI supervision for cases not assigned to specialised regulators. AESIA was established by Royal Decree 729/2023 and became operational in June 2024 — the first dedicated AI supervisory agency in the EU.

AESIA's powers include:
- Market surveillance and enforcement
- Management of national regulatory sandboxes for testing high-risk AI systems
- Publication of compliance guidance (16 practical guides released December 2025)
- Coordination of cross-sector AI governance

### Sectoral Competent Authorities
The draft establishes a tiered supervisory architecture distributing oversight across specialised regulators:

- **AEPD (Agencia Española de Protección de Datos)**: Supervises biometric systems, border management AI applications, and data protection-related AI uses
- **CGPJ (Consejo General del Poder Judicial)**: Handles AI systems used in the justice sector and judicial administration
- **JEC (Junta Electoral Central)**: Monitors AI systems affecting democratic processes and elections
- **Banco de España**: Supervises AI in credit assessment and banking
- **CNMV (Comisión Nacional del Mercado de Valores)**: Supervises AI in securities markets
- **AESIA**: Covers all remaining sectors not assigned to specialised regulators

### Joint Commission
A joint commission for market surveillance coordination is established, with AESIA leading operational coordination across all national authorities.

## Prohibited AI Practices

The following AI practices are banned, effective from the EU AI Act's February 2025 application date for prohibitions:

- **Subliminal manipulation**: Techniques that manipulate persons covertly through subliminal means beyond their consciousness to impair their autonomous decision-making
- **Exploitation of vulnerabilities**: Targeting vulnerable populations such as children, the elderly, or persons with disabilities to distort their behaviour
- **Discriminatory biometric classification**: Profiling or classifying individuals by race, ethnicity, political beliefs, religion, trade union membership, or sexual orientation
- **Social scoring**: Evaluating or rating the trustworthiness of natural persons based on social behaviour or personal characteristics
- **Predictive criminality systems**: AI systems used by law enforcement for risk assessment of individuals based solely on profiling
- **Real-time remote biometric identification**: In public spaces for law enforcement (with limited exceptions)
- **Emotion inference**: In workplace and educational institutions (with certain exceptions)

## High-Risk AI Systems

High-risk AI systems include applications in:
- Critical infrastructure (energy, water, transport)
- Education and vocational training (access, admission, grading)
- Employment, HR management, and access to work (recruitment, performance monitoring)
- Essential private and public services (creditworthiness, insurance, social benefits)
- Law enforcement
- Migration, asylum, and border control
- Administration of justice and democratic processes
- Biometric identification and categorisation

### Requirements for High-Risk Systems
Providers and operators of high-risk AI systems must:
- Implement comprehensive risk management systems throughout the AI lifecycle
- Ensure mandatory human oversight and intervention capability
- Produce and maintain technical documentation
- Implement robust data governance measures
- Conduct conformity assessments (internal or third-party)
- Affix CE marking where applicable
- Register deployments in the national AI database
- Establish and maintain quality management systems
- Report serious incidents to competent authorities

---

## Transparency Obligations

- AI-generated or AI-manipulated content — including images, audio, and video (deepfakes) — must carry "clear and distinguishable identification" at the point of initial exposure to users
- AI systems interacting with humans must disclose their AI nature
- Content generated by general-purpose AI must be labelled as machine-generated

---

## Novel Consumer Right: Digital Right to Withdraw

The draft introduces a **"digital right to withdraw"** — a novel provision not found in the EU AI Act itself — enabling citizens and consumers to remove AI systems from the market if those systems cause serious incidents. This allows individuals to trigger regulatory action against harmful AI products similarly to product safety withdrawal mechanisms.

---

## Innovation Support: Regulatory Sandboxes

The draft establishes a national regulatory sandbox framework allowing AI developers to test high-risk AI systems in controlled environments under AESIA oversight before full commercial deployment. Key features:
- Controlled testing with regulatory monitoring
- Reduced compliance burdens during testing phases
- AESIA manages sandbox selection and oversight
- 12 sandbox projects were active as of April 2025
- Sandbox hosting deadline for required availability: August 2, 2026

---

## Enforcement and Penalty Framework

The draft adopts the EU AI Act's three-tier sanction structure, applied domestically:

### Prohibited Practices
- Maximum penalty: **€35 million or 7% of prior year's global annual turnover** (whichever is higher)

### Very Serious Violations
- Include: unreported serious incidents, non-compliance with competent authority orders, inadequate data governance
- Range: **€7.5 million–€15 million or up to 3% of global turnover**

### Serious Violations
- Include: inadequate human oversight, missing quality management systems, failure to register high-risk systems
- Range: **€500,000–€7.5 million or up to 2% of global turnover**

### Minor Violations
- Include: CE marking violations, labelling failures, minor documentation deficiencies
- Lower fines; SMEs receive differentiated treatment with warnings for first minor violations

### Additional Enforcement Powers
- Corrective orders requiring compliance measures
- Provisional market withdrawal measures during enforcement proceedings
- Anonymous complaint-based initiation mechanisms

---

## Enforcement Procedures

- Market surveillance may be initiated by complaint or ex officio
- Anonymous reporting mechanisms for AI system violations
- Precautionary/provisional product withdrawal available during proceedings
- SMEs receive differentiated treatment: warnings for minor violations before fines

## Relationship to Existing Spanish Law

Spain already has several relevant legal frameworks that the draft law interacts with:
- **Organic Law 3/2018 (LOPDGDD)**: Data protection, complementing GDPR
- **Law 15/2022**: Non-discrimination, addressing algorithmic bias and transparency
- **Royal Decree-Law 9/2021**: Algorithmic transparency for platform work
- **AI Strategy 2024**: Spain's broader AI policy framework (three pillars: strengthening AI levers, facilitating sector expansion, promoting transparent and responsible AI)

---

## AESIA's Enforcement Posture

As of the publication date, AESIA has adopted a "guidance-first, enforcement-second" approach during the initial implementation phase, prioritising compliance assistance over penalties. As of March 2026, no formal fines had been issued by any EU member state under the EU AI Act.
