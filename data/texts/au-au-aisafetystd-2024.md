# Australian Voluntary AI Safety Standard — 10 Mandatory Guardrails for Responsible AI
**Department of Industry, Science and Resources**
*Australia — Published September 5, 2024*

---

## Overview / Background

The Australian Voluntary AI Safety Standard was published by the Australian Department of Industry, Science and Resources on September 5, 2024, as part of the government's Safe and Responsible AI in Australia consultation process. It articulates ten voluntary guardrails for the responsible development and deployment of AI systems, intended to apply to organizations across all sectors.

The standard was developed in response to public consultation (conducted in 2023-2024) on Australia's approach to AI governance. It serves as a bridge between Australia's existing legal framework — which covers AI-related harms through existing consumer protection, privacy, anti-discrimination, and product liability laws — and a future proposed mandatory regime for high-risk AI systems. As of May 2026, the mandatory regime had not yet been enacted; the voluntary standard represents the primary operational guidance available to Australian organizations.

The Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) retain enforcement authority over AI-related harms under their respective mandates. The voluntary standard is intended to help organizations demonstrate compliance with these existing obligations and prepare for future mandatory requirements.

The standard explicitly draws on the EU AI Act's risk-based approach, OECD AI Principles, and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, while calibrating expectations to Australia's primarily small and medium enterprise (SME) economy and its regulatory culture. Organizations that adopt the standard may use it to demonstrate responsible AI governance to regulators, customers, and stakeholders.

## Key Provisions / Chapters

### Guardrail 1 — Accountability and Governance
- Establish clear governance structures with defined accountability for AI systems throughout their lifecycle
- Appoint responsible individuals or teams with authority and resources to implement and maintain AI governance
- Maintain documentation of AI system decisions, including the rationale for deployment choices
- Ensure boards and senior leadership are informed of material AI risks

### Guardrail 2 — Transparency
- Be transparent with users and affected parties about when and how AI is being used in products and services
- Disclose the general nature and purpose of AI systems in consumer-facing contexts
- Publish AI policies and governance frameworks that are accessible to stakeholders
- Avoid deceptive practices that misrepresent the nature, capabilities, or limitations of AI systems

### Guardrail 3 — Human Oversight and Control
- Implement mechanisms to enable human review, intervention, and override of AI-generated decisions or recommendations
- For high-stakes decisions (affecting employment, credit, healthcare, legal matters), ensure meaningful human oversight before final determinations
- Maintain the ability to shut down or roll back AI systems in the event of unacceptable behavior
- Log AI system actions to enable post-hoc audit and accountability

### Guardrail 4 — Risk Assessment
- Conduct structured risk assessments before deploying AI systems, proportionate to the potential impact and scale of the system
- Risk assessments should address: harm to individuals; discrimination or bias risks; privacy impacts; security vulnerabilities; and broader societal risks
- Review risk assessments when material changes are made to AI systems or their deployment context
- Document risk assessment findings and the measures taken to address identified risks

### Guardrail 5 — Security
- Implement appropriate cybersecurity measures to protect AI systems from adversarial attacks, data poisoning, model theft, and unauthorized access
- Apply security controls to training data, model weights, inference infrastructure, and output channels
- Conduct regular security testing including adversarial robustness assessments
- Have incident response procedures for AI security incidents

### Guardrail 6 — Testing and Evaluation
- Test AI systems before deployment to assess performance, reliability, and safety characteristics
- Include diverse and representative test cases, including edge cases and adversarial scenarios
- Conduct ongoing evaluation of deployed systems to detect performance degradation or unexpected behavior
- Use red-teaming and independent evaluation for high-risk AI applications

### Guardrail 7 — Privacy Protection
- Implement privacy by design principles in AI system development and deployment
- Comply with the Privacy Act 1988 (as amended) and the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) for personal data processing
- Minimize collection of personal data for AI training; use anonymization or synthetic data where possible
- Conduct Privacy Impact Assessments for AI systems involving significant personal data processing

### Guardrail 8 — Data Quality
- Ensure training data is accurate, representative, and free from systematic biases that could lead to discriminatory or unreliable AI outputs
- Document data provenance, including sources, collection methods, and quality assessment procedures
- Implement data governance processes to maintain data quality throughout the AI lifecycle
- Address known biases in training data before deployment

### Guardrail 9 — Explainability
- Design AI systems to be explainable to an appropriate degree, given their use context and the potential consequences of their decisions
- Provide explanations of AI outputs to users and affected individuals where required for transparency or redress
- Maintain internal documentation enabling technical explanation of AI system behavior to regulators and auditors
- Distinguish between explanation needs for technical audiences (regulators, auditors) and lay audiences (users, consumers)

### Guardrail 10 — Fairness and Non-Discrimination
- Design and test AI systems to avoid unlawful discrimination on the basis of protected attributes (race, sex, age, disability, religion, national origin) under Australian anti-discrimination law
- Conduct bias testing using appropriate metrics across demographic groups before deployment
- Monitor deployed systems for evidence of disparate impact and take corrective action
- Comply with the Racial Discrimination Act 1975, Sex Discrimination Act 1984, Age Discrimination Act 2004, Disability Discrimination Act 1992, and other applicable laws

### Relationship to Future Mandatory Regime
- The ten guardrails are intended to become mandatory for "high-risk AI" under the proposed regime
- High-risk AI is anticipated to include AI used in: employment decisions; credit and financial services; healthcare; law enforcement; critical infrastructure; and education assessment
- The mandatory regime will include third-party conformity assessment for the highest-risk applications
- Consultation on the mandatory regime design is ongoing as of May 2026

## Enforcement & Penalties

The standard is voluntary and carries no direct enforcement mechanism or penalty structure. However:
- Existing Australian law — including the Australian Consumer Law (ACL), Privacy Act 1988, and anti-discrimination statutes — applies to AI-related harms regardless of voluntary standard adoption
- The ACCC may take action for misleading or deceptive conduct involving AI under the ACL
- The OAIC may investigate and make determinations for privacy breaches involving AI systems
- The Australian Human Rights Commission may investigate discrimination complaints arising from AI decisions
- Maximum penalty for serious breaches of the Australian Consumer Law: AUD 50 million per contravention for corporations
- Organizations demonstrating alignment with the voluntary standard may receive regulatory credit in ACCC or OAIC proceedings
