20th	Assembly

SENATE OF PUERTO RICO
S. B. 870
December 11, 2025
Introduced by Mrs. Soto Aguilú
Referred to the Committee on Judiciary

ACT

To amend Act No. 146-2012, as amended, known as the "Penal Code of Puerto Rico", specifically to add subsections (jj) and (kk) to Article 14; create Title VII with a Sole Chapter and include Articles 310 through 317; and add subsection (k) to Article 64, in order to prevent and penalize the malicious use of artificial intelligence."

STATEMENT OF MOTIVES

The Legislative Assembly of Puerto Rico has the constitutional and moral duty to always remain at the vanguard in the defense of the fundamental rights of our citizens, anticipating the challenges imposed by technological evolution and adapting the legal system to the realities of each era. It is not enough to react when the harm has already been consummated; it is the duty of this Legislative Body to lead with foresight, legislate with audacity and proactively protect Puerto Rican society against the emerging threats brought about by the digital era.
The exponential development of artificial intelligence (AI) has radically transformed the way we interact, communicate, work and consume information. While this technology offers extraordinary opportunities for human progress, it has also opened the door to unprecedented forms of criminality: the massive and credible impersonation of identities through deepfakes, the non-consensual commercial exploitation of the image and voice of persons, automated and industrial-scale cyber harassment, algorithmic discrimination, the manipulation of financial markets and the intentional dissemination of disinformation capable of undermining democracy and public health.
These risks are neither hypothetical nor distant. Today in Puerto Rico, cases are already reported of victims whose faces have been used without authorization in false advertising, minors who suffer harassment through bots and AI-generated accounts, and bank fraud perpetrated with synthetic voices that perfectly imitate family members. The absence of specific criminal types and clear aggravating circumstances in our Penal Code leaves victims in a legal limbo and disincentivizes the effective prosecution of these crimes.
The Legislative Assembly cannot remain passive while the rest of the world advances. Jurisdictions such as California, New York, New Jersey and the European Union have already approved specific legislation against non-consensual deepfakes, AI-generated disinformation and commercial exploitation of digital likeness. At the federal level, the TAKE IT DOWN Act (2025) and the NO FAKES Act (pending final approval in 2025) establish clear precedents that Puerto Rico must observe, but also surpass with its own response, adapted to our cultural, social and legal reality.
This bill responds to that historical urgency. It creates precise definitions, typifies new conducts, establishes proportional and deterrent penalties, and enshrines artificial intelligence as a general aggravating circumstance. With this, Puerto Rico positions itself as one of the first Spanish-speaking jurisdictions in the hemisphere to have a comprehensive and specific criminal framework against the misuse of AI, sending an unequivocal message: on our Island, technological innovation will never be above the dignity, privacy and security of persons.
For all of the foregoing, this Legislative Assembly, faithful to its tradition of leadership and anticipation, must approve the present measure without reservation.
BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY OF PUERTO RICO:
Section 1.- Article 14 of Act No. 146-2012, as amended, known as the "Penal Code of Puerto Rico", is hereby amended to add the following new subsections (jj) and (kk), which shall read as follows:
"Article 14. - Definitions.
...
(jj) Artificial Intelligence (AI): Any digital system or tool that simulates human intelligence to generate, manipulate or analyze data, including but not limited to the creation of false images, videos, audios or texts (deepfakes), identity impersonation, or automation of cyber actions.
(kk) Malicious use of AI: Use of Artificial Intelligence with the intent to harm, defraud, discriminate, harass or violate rights, without the consent of the affected parties."
Section 2.- A new Title VII is added to the Penal Code of Puerto Rico, titled "Crimes Related to the Malicious Use of Artificial Intelligence", with a Sole Chapter, which shall read as follows:
"TITLE VII CRIMES RELATED TO THE MALICIOUS USE OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE
SOLE CHAPTER
Article 310. - Unauthorized use of image or personal attributes with artificial intelligence for commercial purposes.
Any person who, without the express written consent of the affected person or of their legal representative, uses artificial intelligence to generate, alter or manipulate the image, voice or any digital representation of the physical features or identifiable personal characteristics of another person for commercial purposes—including advertising, promotion, sale of products or services, or any lucrative activity—shall incur in a fourth-degree felony, punishable by a fixed term of imprisonment of three (3) years or a fine of up to ten thousand dollars ($10,000), or both penalties at the discretion of the court.
If the conduct causes economic damage greater than five thousand dollars ($5,000) or duly documented severe emotional damage, it shall be classified as a felony, with a fixed term of imprisonment of six (6) years.
Article 311. - Cyberbullying through Artificial Intelligence.
Any person who uses Artificial Intelligence to harass, intimidate or pester another person through social media or electronic means (including automated bots, deepfakes or messages generated by Artificial Intelligence), shall incur in a misdemeanor, punishable by a term of imprisonment not to exceed six (6) months or a fine of up to $5,000, or both penalties. If it involves minors or results in severe psychological damage, it shall be classified as a felony (fixed imprisonment of three (3) years). This provision complements the definition of cyber violence in Act No. 54-1989, as amended.
Article 312. - Fraud with Artificial Intelligence.
Any person who uses Artificial Intelligence to impersonate identity, create false information or perpetrate scams, shall incur in the penalties established in Article 202 (Fraud), aggravated by one degree if the malicious use of Artificial Intelligence as a facilitating element is proven.
Article 313. - Cybercrime with Artificial Intelligence.
The use of Artificial Intelligence to execute cyberattacks, such as automated phishing or algorithm-assisted hacking, shall aggravate the penalties of Article 2184 (Computer crimes), with additional imprisonment of up to two (2) years.
Article 314. - Discrimination with Artificial Intelligence.
If Artificial Intelligence is used to intentionally discriminate in decisions of employment, housing or credit based on race, gender, ethnic origin or other categories protected by Act No. 100-1959, as amended, it shall be penalized in accordance with said anti-discrimination laws, with criminal aggravation under the Code that elevates the classification if it causes demonstrable economic or social damage.
Article 315. - Violation of privacy with Artificial Intelligence.
The collection, analysis or disclosure of personal data without consent through Artificial Intelligence shall aggravate the penalties of Article 197 (Violation of privacy), with an additional fine of up to $15,000 per violation.
Article 316. - Disinformation with Artificial Intelligence.
Any person who creates and disseminates false news or disinformation through Artificial Intelligence, with the intent to cause public harm or alter democratic processes, shall incur in a misdemeanor (fine of up to $5,000 or imprisonment for a term not to exceed six (6) months). If it affects elections, public safety or collective health, it shall be aggravated to a felony (fixed imprisonment of one (1) year).
Article 317. - Market manipulation with Artificial Intelligence.
Using Artificial Intelligence to manipulate financial markets through misleading information or automation of fraudulent trading shall aggravate the penalties under Article 248 (Swindle), and shall be coordinated with applicable federal regulations of the SEC."
Section 3.- Article 64 of Act No. 146-2012, as amended, is hereby amended to add a new subsection (k), which shall read as follows:
"Article 64. - Aggravating circumstances.
(a) ...
(j) ...
(k) Committing the crime through the malicious use of AI, which shall increase the penalty by 25% and shall require restitution to the affected victims, in accordance with the provisions of Article 80 of this Code."
Section 4.- Severability Clause.
If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional, null or invalid by a competent court, said declaration shall not affect the other provisions of this Act, which shall continue in full force and effect.
Section 5.- This Act shall take effect immediately upon its approval.
