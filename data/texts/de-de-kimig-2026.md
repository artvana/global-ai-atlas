# Germany — Gesetz zur Durchführung der europäischen KI-Verordnung (KI-Marktüberwachungs- und Innovationsgesetz — KI-MIG)

**KI-Marktüberwachungs- und Innovationsgesetz (KI-MIG)**
*Germany — Cabinet adopted 11 February 2026; before Bundestag as of May 2026*

---

## Overview

The KI-Marktüberwachungs- und Innovationsgesetz (KI-MIG) — formally the Gesetz zur Durchführung der europäischen KI-Verordnung — is Germany's national implementing legislation for the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689). The federal cabinet adopted the bill on 11 February 2026. As of May 2026, it was before the Bundestag for first and second readings, with passage expected during 2026.

Germany, as the EU's largest economy and a major AI developer and deployer, has significant choices in how it structures AI Act implementation — particularly regarding which existing federal authority becomes the primary market surveillance authority and how enforcement is resourced. The KI-MIG makes those structural choices and provides national-level procedural rules supplementing the EU AI Act's direct applicability.

## Key Provisions

### Designation of Market Surveillance Authorities

**Federal Network Agency (Bundesnetzagentur — BNetzA) as Primary Authority.** The Federal Network Agency is designated as Germany's primary market surveillance authority under the EU AI Act. BNetzA, which already regulates telecommunications, energy, postal, and railway networks, has cross-sectoral enforcement experience and technical capacity. Its designated AI Act competence covers: general-purpose AI models and GPAI providers; horizontal AI systems not falling within sector-specific regulator jurisdiction; AI regulatory sandboxes; and coordination with the EU AI Office.

**BaFin for Financial Sector AI.** The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht — BaFin) retains jurisdiction over AI systems used in financial services, including credit scoring, algorithmic trading, insurance underwriting, and investment advice, aligning with BaFin's existing sectoral authority.

**Other Sectoral Authorities.** The Law anticipates that sector-specific authorities (Federal Motor Transport Authority for transport AI, Federal Office for Information Security (BSI) for cybersecurity-critical AI, Federal Institute for Drugs and Medical Devices for healthcare AI) will exercise jurisdiction over AI systems within their respective sectors, coordinated through BNetzA as lead authority.

**Proposed Federal Office for Artificial Intelligence Safety (BAIS).** The bill includes provisions enabling the future establishment of a Federal Office for Artificial Intelligence Safety (Bundesamt für Sicherheit in der künstlichen Intelligenz — BAIS), potentially housed within or alongside BSI. As of May 2026, BAIS had not been formally established pending Bundestag approval of the KI-MIG.

### National Enforcement Procedures

The KI-MIG establishes national procedural rules for:
- Receiving and investigating complaints about AI systems placed on the German market
- Conducting market surveillance inspections, including powers of entry, document access, and technical testing
- Issuing corrective orders, including market withdrawal, recall, and access restriction orders
- Coordination protocols between BNetzA and sector-specific authorities for multi-sector AI systems
- Appeals procedures before administrative courts

### Penalty Structures

Germany mirrors the EU AI Act maximum penalties at the national level:
- Up to **€35 million or 7% of worldwide annual turnover** for prohibited AI practice violations
- Up to **€15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover** for GPAI provider obligation failures
- Up to **€7.5 million or 1% of worldwide annual turnover** for providing false or misleading information to supervisory authorities

The KI-MIG provides for a differentiated penalty scale based on: the severity and duration of the violation; the size and financial capacity of the operator; cooperation during investigation; and whether the operator proactively disclosed and remediated the non-compliance.

### AI Regulatory Sandbox Program

The bill establishes a national AI regulatory sandbox program, operated by BNetzA in coordination with relevant sector authorities. The sandbox allows AI developers to test systems against EU AI Act requirements in a supervised environment. SME participation is prioritized. The sandbox program is funded through the federal budget for an initial three-year period.

### GPAI Model Provisions

The KI-MIG includes national provisions for handling GPAI model enforcement, reflecting Germany's importance as a host for major AI infrastructure. BNetzA is empowered to: request technical documentation from GPAI model providers operating in Germany; conduct investigations into alleged systemic-risk model non-compliance; and coordinate with the EU AI Office for enforcement actions that require cross-border coordination.

### Legislative Status

Cabinet adopted: 11 February 2026. Bundestag first reading: March 2026. Committee hearings: April–May 2026. Bundesrat (upper house) review pending. Final passage expected: second half of 2026.
