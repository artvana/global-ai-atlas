(Act implementing the Regulation on Artificial Intelligence)

A. Problem and Objective
On 1 August 2024, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 June 2024 laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence and amending Regulations (EC) No 300/2008, (EU) No 167/2013, (EU) No 168/2013, (EU) 2018/858, (EU) 2018/1139 and (EU) 2019/2144 as well as Directives 2014/90/EU, (EU) 2016/797 and (EU) 2020/1828 (Regulation on Artificial Intelligence) (OJ L, 2024/1689, 12.07.2024) entered into force.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 establishes a uniform legal framework for the development, placing on the market, putting into service and use of artificial intelligence systems (AI systems) in the European Union. The aim is to create a uniform internal market for AI-supported goods and services, to promote innovation, and at the same time to ensure a high level of protection with regard to health, safety and the fundamental rights enshrined in the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union, including democracy, the rule of law and environmental protection.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 follows a risk-based approach and contains, in particular, prohibitions of certain practices in the AI field, special requirements for high-risk AI systems and obligations for actors in relation to such systems, transparency rules for certain AI systems, and measures to promote innovation with particular focus on small and medium-sized enterprises and start-ups.

The provisions of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 apply, in principle, directly from 2 August 2026. For the purposes of implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, every Member State of the European Union must, by 2 August 2025, establish or designate as competent national authorities at least one notifying authority and at least one market surveillance authority, including one market surveillance authority that acts as the single point of contact for Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

B. Solution
The present draft Act serves to implement Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. The implementing legislation must be completed by the Member States of the European Union by 2 August 2025. Article 1 designates the authorities competent for the implementation of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, regulates their tasks and adopts cooperation rules and the necessary provisions for the administrative fine procedure. Articles 2 to 4 amend relevant laws to align them with Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

C. Alternatives
The implementation of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 is mandatory. To that extent, there are no alternatives. Various implementation variants were examined; however, none was preferable.

D. Budgetary Expenditure without Compliance Costs
1. Federal Network Agency (Bundesnetzagentur)
As a result of the new rules of this Act, the Federal Network Agency will incur annual individual personnel costs for the performance of the specialist tasks amounting to a total of EUR 3,678,000, individual material costs of EUR 1,137,000 and overhead costs of EUR 1,416,000; these costs apply from the year 2026 and remain — subject to a separate evaluation — constant for the following years.

According to the results determining and presenting the compliance costs, a total of 33.2 posts are required for the performance of the specialist tasks (23.0 in the higher service (hD), 7.7 in the upper service (gD) and 2.5 in the intermediate service (mD)); a further 9.8 posts are required for the cross-cutting area (6.8 hD, 2.3 gD and 0.7 mD). One-time material costs of EUR 3,000,000 and ongoing material costs of EUR 6,300,000 will arise, in particular for the operation and further development of the necessary IT procedures, the operation of an AI regulatory sandbox and the provision of the interface to the EU database.

The individual personnel and material costs for the cross-cutting area are included in the overhead surcharge on the annual individual personnel and material costs for the specialist tasks. The calculations of the additional personnel requirements are to be seen against the background of the evaluation clause provided for in section A point VIII of the explanatory memorandum, which may make a needs-based readjustment of capacities necessary. The costs were determined on the basis of the Federal Ministry of Finance's circular on cost-benefit analyses and cost calculations of 23 June 2025 (file ref.: II A 3 – H 1012/00236/007/015).

2. Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht)
For costs that arise for the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority from its competence under this draft Act, the provisions of the Act on the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Financial Services Supervision Act – FinDAG, see Article 3) apply.

3. Federal Office for Information Security (Bundesamt für Sicherheit in der Informationstechnik)
As a result of the new rules of this Act and the associated transfer of new tasks, the Federal Office for Information Security will incur additional personnel costs. For the performance of the new specialist tasks, 13 posts/established posts (11 hD, 1 gD, 1 mD) are required. This results in expected annual personnel costs of EUR 1,511,000, individual material costs of EUR 445,000 and overhead costs of EUR 575,000.

4. Central Office for Information Technology in the Security Sector (Zentrale Stelle für Informationstechnik im Sicherheitsbereich)
As a result of the new rules of this Act and the associated transfer of new tasks, the Central Office for Information Technology in the Security Sector will incur additional personnel costs. For the performance of the new specialist tasks, a total of 8 established posts (5 hD, 3 gD) are required. This results in expected annual personnel costs of EUR 872,000, individual material costs of EUR 274,000 and overhead costs of EUR 337,000.

In addition, one-time material costs of EUR 850,000 will arise.

The one-time material expenditure is necessary for the procurement of technical equipment (e.g., high-performance workstations) including specialised analysis and documentation tools.

5. Federal Commissioner for Data Protection and Freedom of Information (Bundesbeauftragte für den Datenschutz und die Informationsfreiheit)
As a result of the new rules of this Act and the associated transfer of new tasks, the Federal Commissioner for Data Protection and Freedom of Information will incur additional personnel costs of EUR 1,033,000. These consist of individual personnel costs of EUR 596,000, individual material costs of EUR 158,000 and overhead costs of EUR 279,000. The costs apply from the year 2026 and remain — subject to a separate evaluation — constant for the following years.

6. Federal Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (Bundesanstalt für Arbeitsschutz und Arbeitsmedizin)
As a result of the new rules of this Act and the associated transfer of new tasks under § 7(1) and (2), the Federal Institute for Occupational Safety and Health will incur additional personnel costs. For the performance of the new specialist tasks, according to the results determining and presenting the compliance costs, a total of 5 established posts (1 hD, 4 gD) are required. This results in expected annual total personnel costs of EUR 773,052 and total material costs of EUR 221,598. This results in total costs of EUR 994,649. These costs apply from the year 2027 and remain — subject to a separate evaluation — constant for the following years.

7. Cost Bearing
The additional staffing requirements in the area of the Federal Network Agency under the technical supervision of the Federal Ministry for Digital Affairs and State Modernisation (BMDS) shall be offset within the budget of the competent department. The additional financial requirements shall be offset within the budget chapter 24 of the BMDS.

For the rest, the additional needs for material and personnel resources arising in the federal sphere from the implementation of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 shall be offset financially and in terms of posts within the respective budget chapter concerned. The details will be decided as part of the respective budget preparation procedures of the coming years.

E. Compliance Costs
Obligations for industry as well as for citizens result from the directly applicable Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. This Implementing Act therefore creates no new obligations for them. Compliance costs based on this draft therefore arise neither for industry nor for citizens.

E.1 Compliance Costs for Citizens
None.

E.2 Compliance Costs for Industry
None.

E.3 Compliance Costs for Administration
For the administration, the annual compliance costs increase by approximately EUR 48,997,000. The one-time compliance costs amount to approximately EUR 4,076,000. Of this, EUR 15,898,000 of annual compliance costs are attributable to the Federal Government and EUR 33,099,000 to the Länder (including municipalities). Of the one-time compliance costs, EUR 4,048,000 are attributable to the Federal Government and EUR 28,000 to the Länder (including municipalities).

F. Further Costs
None.

6. Mixed financial holding companies within the meaning of § 1(35) of the Banking Act in conjunction with Article 4(1) point 21 of Regulation (EU) No 575/2013,
7. Credit servicing institutions within the meaning of § 2(2) of the Secondary Credit Market Act of 22 December 2023 (BGBl. 2023 I No 411, p. 2),
8. Payment institutions within the meaning of § 1(1), first sentence, point 1, of the Payment Services Supervision Act of 17 July 2017 (BGBl. I p. 2446; 2019 I p. 1113), as last amended by Article 11 of the Act of 28 February 2025 (BGBl. 2025 I No 69),

Under the conditions of the first sentence, the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority is also the competent market surveillance authority for AI systems directly connected with a regulated financial activity to the extent that supervisory competence pursuant to Article 43(7) or Article 44(4) of Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 in respect of an issuer of a significant asset-referenced token has been transferred to the European Banking Authority and the issuer continues to have its place of business in the Federal Republic. As regards Deutsche Welle, supervision of compliance with Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 in the area referred to in the first sentence shall, by way of derogation from the first sentence, be governed by the Deutsche Welle Act and the supervisory provisions of Deutsche Welle.

(9) The authorities competent under paragraphs 1 to 7 may consult experts of the scientific panel (Article 68 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) pursuant to Article 69 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.


                                           § 3
           Authorities Competent for the Notification and Accreditation of
                          Conformity Assessment Bodies
(1) The authorities that have been designated by federal or Land law as notifying authorities for the implementation of the harmonisation legislation referred to in Annex I, Section A, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 shall also perform the tasks of competent notifying authorities pursuant to Article 28(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 where AI systems are concerned that are connected with such products to which the provisions referred to in Annex I, Section A, are to be applied. For AI systems connected with such products to which Regulation (EU) 2016/424 is to be applied, the Länder shall designate the competent notifying authorities.

(2) For high-risk AI systems pursuant to Article 6(2) in conjunction with Annex III, point 1, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the market surveillance authority to be designated pursuant to Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 shall be the notifying authority competent pursuant to Article 28(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. Until the designation of the market surveillance authority pursuant to Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, the Federal Office for Information Security shall perform the task referred to in the first sentence.

(3) Where the notifying authority pursuant to paragraph 1 or 2 has established that a conformity assessment body fulfils the requirements pursuant to Article 31 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, it shall grant that body the authorisation to carry out conformity assessments pursuant to Article 3, point 20, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and shall notify it to the Commission and to the other Member States pursuant to Article 30(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. The authorisation may be granted subject to conditions or requirements. It may be granted for a limited period and subject to revocation, as well as subject to subsequent requirements. It shall be granted under the suspensive condition that neither the European Commission nor the other Member States of the European Union raise objections to the notification.

(4) The assessment and supervision of conformity assessment bodies pursuant to Article 3, point 21, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 that have their seat in Germany shall be the responsibility of the respective bodies that already perform this task in the framework of the European Union harmonisation legislation referred to in Annex I, Section A, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. The assessment and supervision of conformity assessment bodies pursuant to Article 3, point 21, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 that have their seat in Germany and carry out conformity assessments for the high-risk AI systems referred to in Annex III, point 1, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 shall, by way of derogation from the first sentence, be the responsibility of the German Accreditation Body (Deutsche Akkreditierungsstelle).

(5) The authorities competent under paragraphs 1, 2 and 4 and the German Accreditation Body may consult experts of the scientific panel (Article 68 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) pursuant to Article 69 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.


                                            § 4
                               AI Market Surveillance Chamber
(1) The AI Market Surveillance Chamber shall have three members. The chair of the Chamber shall be the President of the Federal Network Agency. The Vice Presidents of the Federal Network Agency shall be associate members. The decisions of the AI Market Surveillance Chamber shall be taken by a simple majority of its members. In the event of a tie, the vote of the Chair shall be decisive.

(2) The AI Market Surveillance Chamber shall be supported by a secretariat. To perform its tasks, it may dispose of personnel and material resources of the Federal Network Agency. While working for the AI Market Surveillance Chamber, the staff of the Federal Network Agency shall be subject exclusively to the members of the AI Market Surveillance Chamber.

(3) The AI Market Surveillance Chamber shall act in complete independence. It shall not be subject to any direct or indirect external influence and shall neither seek nor accept instructions.

(4) The AI Market Surveillance Chamber shall submit an annual activity report to the Bundestag. The report shall be drawn up for the first time for the year 2026 and transmitted to the Bundestag by 31 March of the year following the reporting year.

(5) The tasks of the AI Market Surveillance Chamber shall not extend to reviewing the formal and substantive requirements set out in Article 5(2) and (3) and Article 26(10) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 for the ordering of the use of high-risk AI systems and for the use of those systems in individual cases.


                                           § 5
      Coordination and Competence Centre for the Implementation of Regulation
                                  (EU) 2024/1689
A central Coordination and Competence Centre for Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 shall be established at the Federal Network Agency. The Centre shall have the task of:
1. providing expert support, on request, to the market surveillance authorities, notifying authorities and the German Accreditation Body that are competent under this Act in complex decisions within the scope of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689,
2. coordinating the cooperation among the authorities competent under this Act and with other authorities affected within their scope of competence and working towards uniform answers to horizontal legal questions,
3. facilitating the drawing up of codes of conduct within the meaning of Article 95(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, and
4. ensuring an appropriate, transparent and regular involvement of civil society, trade unions, industry, science and research as well as of the Länder.

To perform its tasks under the second sentence, points 1 to 3, the Centre may, in individual cases, involve federal authorities affected in their competence and consult external expertise. To perform its task under the second sentence, point 2, the Centre may set up suitable committees, in particular consisting of representatives of the competent authorities.


                                           § 6
                                   Single Point of Contact
(1) The single point of contact within the meaning of Article 70(2), third sentence, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 shall be the Federal Network Agency.

(2) The market surveillance authorities and the notifying authorities shall provide the single point of contact with information about their tasks and their electronic contact address as well as the contact details of a contact person and a person deputising for them. The market surveillance authorities and the notifying authorities shall inform the single point of contact without undue delay of any changes to this information.

(3) The single point of contact shall make publicly available the electronic contact addresses pursuant to paragraph 2 as well as its own electronic contact address. The single point of contact shall communicate to the European Commission the names and tasks of the market surveillance authorities and the notifying authorities, the electronic contact addresses referred to in the first sentence as well as any subsequent changes to those contact addresses.

(4) The single point of contact shall receive submissions from the AI Office established under Article 64 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, from the public and from other contact points at the level of the Member States of the European Union and the European Union itself, and shall forward them to the competent national bodies.

(5) Unless otherwise provided in this Act, the authorities competent under §§ 2 and 3 shall fulfil the reporting obligations resulting from Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 via the single point of contact, by making available to it in electronic form the documents required to fulfil the respective reporting obligation. The single point of contact shall provide the competent authorities with electronic forms for that purpose, which are to be used. The first sentence shall not apply to the reporting obligation pursuant to Article 70(6) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the fulfilment of which shall be the responsibility of the Federal Ministry for Digital Affairs and State Modernisation. The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority may, by way of derogation from the first sentence, fulfil its reporting obligations under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 directly vis-à-vis the other Member States and the institutions of the European Union, in so far as legal grounds preclude their fulfilment by the single point of contact.


                                            § 7
            Notification of Non-Compliance or Risks of an AI System
(1) The notification of the European Commission and other Member States of the European Union in the case of non-compliance of an AI system that is not limited to Germany pursuant to Article 79(3), (5), second sentence, and (7), and pursuant to Article 81(2), first and second sentences, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 shall be carried out by the competent market surveillance authority without undue delay via the Federal Institute for Occupational Safety and Health.

(2) The notification of risks despite compliance of an AI system pursuant to Article 82(3) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 shall be carried out by the competent market surveillance authority without undue delay via the Federal Institute for Occupational Safety and Health.

(3) The Federal Institute for Occupational Safety and Health shall, within its tasks, support the market surveillance of AI systems that are integrated into a product covered by one of the legal acts of the European Union under Annex I, Section A, points 1, 4, 5, 7, 9 or 10 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, in particular with evidence-based specialised knowledge for deployers, providers and standardisation bodies.


                                            § 8
                                 Central Complaints Body
(1) Complaints concerning an infringement of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 may, without prejudice to the requirements of Article 85 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, be submitted to the Federal Network Agency as the central complaints body.

(2) The central complaints body shall forward complaints as follows:
1. a complaint that falls within the competence of a market surveillance authority pursuant to § 2(2) to (8) shall be forwarded to the competent authority or to the competent single contact point, or
2. a complaint that also falls within the competence of an authority or public body pursuant to Article 77(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 shall be forwarded to that authority or body.

Without prejudice to the duties to provide information on personal data under Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the complainant shall, in the case of the first sentence, point 1, be informed by the central complaints body which authority pursuant to § 2(2) to (8) is competent for their complaint and that the complaint has been forwarded to that authority or to the competent single contact point.

(3) To perform its tasks as central complaints body, the Federal Network Agency shall set up a complaints management system in accordance with the requirements of the Barrier-Free Information Technology Regulation.

(4) Where a complaint concerning an infringement of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 is submitted to a market surveillance authority pursuant to § 2(2) to (8), that authority shall forward the complaint to the Federal Network Agency, in so far as it does not fall within its own competence. Otherwise, the market surveillance authority shall provide the Federal Network Agency with a copy of the complaint in textual form.


                                     Section 2
                                  Cooperation


                                                § 9
                       Cooperation of the Competent Authorities
(1) The market surveillance authorities and the notifying authorities shall cooperate to perform their tasks within the framework of their respective competences in a cooperative and trustful manner. They shall communicate to one another observations and findings that may be of importance for the performance of their respective tasks.

(2) Where their respective competences are concerned in specific market surveillance activities or in checks and measures pursuant to Article 79 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the market surveillance authorities, the authorities and public bodies pursuant to Article 77(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and the other authorities shall mutually inform each other of measures they intend to take. In carrying out measures, they shall communicate to one another observations and findings that they need for the performance of their respective tasks under this Act or under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

(3) Where the market surveillance authorities reach the conclusion that public bodies within the meaning of § 2(1) and (2) of the Federal Data Protection Act are infringing Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, this shall be communicated to the legal or technical supervisory authority competent for the respective public body, and that authority shall be given the opportunity to comment within an appropriate period before measures are taken against the public body. The opportunity to comment may be dispensed with where an immediate decision appears necessary by reason of imminent danger or in the public interest, or where a compelling public interest would be opposed thereto. The statement of the legal or technical supervisory authority should also include a description of the supervisory measures taken on the basis of the notification by the authority or public body pursuant to paragraph 2, first sentence. The second sentence shall not apply where the public bodies are law enforcement authorities within the meaning of Article 3, point 45, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

(4) In performing their tasks under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the market surveillance authorities shall in particular also involve the following other authorities, in so far as their respective scope of competence is affected:
1. the data protection supervisory authorities of the Federal Government and of the Länder,
2. the market surveillance authority to be designated pursuant to Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, and
3. the Federal Cartel Office (Bundeskartellamt).

(5) The authorities referred to in paragraphs 1 to 4 may, within their competence and irrespective of the type of procedure chosen, exchange information among themselves, including personal data and trade and business secrets, in so far as this is strictly necessary for the performance of their respective tasks pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and this Act. They may use this information in their proceedings. Prohibitions on the use of evidence remain unaffected. The provisions on mutual legal assistance in criminal matters and on mutual administrative and legal assistance agreements remain unaffected.

(6) Persons employed by, or commissioned by, the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority must not, without authorisation, disclose or use facts that have come to their knowledge in the course of their activity, the confidentiality of which is in the interest of a person subject to obligations under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, of the competent authorities or of a third party, in particular trade and business secrets, even after they are no longer in service or their activity has ended. Unauthorised disclosure or use shall in particular not be deemed to occur where:
1. facts are passed on to authorities or other public bodies and the passing on of the information is necessary for the performance of the tasks of those authorities or bodies under this Act or Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, or

Explanatory Memorandum

A. General Part

I. Objective and Necessity of the Provisions
On 1 August 2024, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 June 2024 laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence and amending Regulations (EC) No 300/2008, (EU) No 167/2013, (EU) No 168/2013, (EU) 2018/858, (EU) 2018/1139 and (EU) 2019/2144 as well as Directives 2014/90/EU, (EU) 2016/797 and (EU) 2020/1828 (Regulation on Artificial Intelligence) (OJ L, 2024/1689, 12.07.2024) entered into force.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 establishes a uniform legal framework for the development, placing on the market, putting into service and use of artificial intelligence systems (AI systems) in the European Union. It follows a risk-based approach and contains, in particular, prohibitions of certain practices in the AI field, special requirements for high-risk AI systems and obligations for actors in relation to such systems, transparency rules for certain AI systems, as well as measures to promote innovation with particular focus on small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and start-ups, as well as application-oriented research. The aim is to create a uniform internal market for AI-supported goods and services, to promote innovation, and at the same time to ensure a high level of protection with regard to health, safety and the fundamental rights enshrined in the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union, including democracy, the rule of law and environmental protection.

The provisions of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 apply, in principle, directly from 2 August 2026. For the purposes of implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, every Member State must, by 2 August 2025, establish or designate as competent national authorities at least one notifying authority and at least one market surveillance authority (including one market surveillance authority that acts as the single point of contact for Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) and adopt, in accordance with the requirements of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, rules for sanctions and other enforcement measures.

According to Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the notifying authorities are responsible for the establishment and operation of the necessary procedures for the assessment, designation and notification of conformity assessment bodies and for their supervision. Member States may decide that the assessment and supervision are to be carried out by a national accreditation body within the meaning of, and pursuant to, Regulation (EC) No 765/2008.

The market surveillance authorities carry out the activities and take the measures provided for in Regulation (EU) 2019/1020. Even if deviations are possible under certain conditions, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 provides that, for high-risk AI systems and products related thereto to which the European Union harmonisation legislation listed in Annex I, Section A, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 applies, the authority designated for market surveillance in those legal acts shall be the market surveillance authority. For high-risk AI systems placed on the market, put into service or used by financial institutions regulated on the basis of Union law in the area of financial services, the national authority designated in those provisions for the financial supervision of those institutions shall be the market surveillance authority, in so far as the placing on the market, putting into service or use of the AI system is in direct connection with the provision of those financial activities and no other competent authority is designated as market surveillance authority. Furthermore, for the high-risk AI systems referred to in Annex III, point 1, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, where these systems are used for law enforcement purposes, border management and justice and democracy, and for the high-risk AI systems referred to in Annex III, points 6, 7 and 8, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the Member States shall designate as market surveillance authorities either the supervisory authorities competent for data protection under Regulation (EU) 2016/679 or Directive (EU) 2016/680, or any other authority pursuant to the same conditions as those laid down in Articles 41 to 44 of Directive (EU) 2016/680. Market surveillance activities must in no way affect the independence of judicial authorities or otherwise influence their actions in the framework of their judicial activities. Furthermore, market surveillance activities must not violate fiscal secrecy or affect or otherwise influence the actions of the fiscal authorities in the framework of their fiscal activities (Article 108(1) and (2) of the Basic Law). Coordination among the market surveillance authorities designated on the basis of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and other national authorities and bodies should be facilitated (cf. Article 74(10) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, as a product regulation, fits into the general system of market surveillance. II. Essential Content of the Draft
The present draft Act serves to implement Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. The deadline for this was 2 August 2025. Article 1 designates the authorities competent for the implementation of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, regulates their tasks and adopts cooperation rules and the necessary provisions on the administrative fine procedure. Articles 2 to 4 carry out the necessary amendment of relevant laws.

The aim is an innovation-friendly and low-bureaucracy implementation of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. In this context, the determination of the national supervisory and authority structure in Article 1 is of essential importance. A coordination centre is therefore being established at the Federal Network Agency (BNetzA) to support all market surveillance authorities and notifying authorities in their tasks resulting from Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. This bundles AI expertise centrally and makes it available to existing authorities, where needed, in a resource-efficient manner.

Authorities that are already competent in fully harmonised areas of product regulation as market surveillance authorities and notifying authorities are also to become the authorities competent for market surveillance and notification in the area of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. To that extent, the existing structures should be used, since otherwise duplicate structures would be created at the expense of the affected undertakings. If, in this area of fully harmonised products, relevant European legislative acts are adopted, the structures created there shall accordingly also be competent for Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (future clause). This could in future affect cases of Annex I, Section B, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (including in the area of motor vehicles). This makes use of existing sector-specific expertise and the existing authority and supervision structures remain in place for undertakings.

The same applies in those areas to which the system of market surveillance is extended by Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and in which systematically different supervisory structures already partly exist (e.g. the area of media supervision). Here too, the authorities from the existing structures become competent for the market surveillance in the area of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

In areas in which existing structures in the area of product regulation or other existing supervisory structures cannot or must not be relied upon, the Federal Network Agency shall become the competent market surveillance authority and notifying authority.

Where AI systems are placed on the market, put into service or used by public bodies of the Länder, market surveillance shall be the responsibility of the competent authorities to be designated under Land law.

In addition, the Federal Network Agency shall be competent for innovation promotion in the field of artificial intelligence (in particular the establishment and operation of an AI regulatory sandbox).

The expertise of other authorities (in particular the Federal Office for Information Security, the Federal Cartel Office and the Federal Commissioner for Data Protection and Freedom of Information) is involved within their respective scopes of competence.

The competent authorities shall, in performing their tasks under this Act, ensure innovation-friendly and resource-efficient processes. Processes for which artificial intelligence is technically available and economically reasonable, trustworthy, secure and ethically acceptable should, within the legal requirements, be automated by AI systems.

III. Executive Footprint
The Federal Government has evaluated and taken into account the publicly available statements of stakeholders and those directly submitted to it. No statement that essentially determined the content of the draft Act was submitted.

IV. Alternatives
The implementation of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 is mandatory. To that extent, there are no alternatives.

For market surveillance, it has been examined whether a central authority with competence for all AI systems should be created, possibly through an interstate treaty, as well as whether supervision for all areas could take place at Land level. Reasons of time speak in particular against the first alternative. Such a process could not be completed within the statutory implementation deadline. Against the second alternative, it must be said that the most uniform application of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 is a precondition for legal certainty, cross-border uniform application of the law and innovation promotion. As a result, these aims are best achieved through an approach that combines the use of existing structures with the creation of a new central competence at the Federal Network Agency, which includes further bundling with other digital topics in the future course. For example, it is planned to also use the Federal Network Agency as the central supervisory authority for the enforcement and monitoring of Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 December 2023 on harmonised rules on fair access to and use of data and amending Regulation (EU) 2017/2394 and Directive (EU) 2020/1828 (Data Regulation) and — alongside the Federal Statistical Office — as the competent authority for the implementation of Regulation (EU) 2022/868 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 30 May 2022 on European data governance and amending Regulation (EU) 2018/1724 (Data Governance Act).

For the area of complete independence of market surveillance required by Article 74(8) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, various alternatives have been examined. It would, for example, be conceivable to transfer market surveillance to an already established authority (such as the Federal Commissioner for Data Protection and Freedom of Information). However, weighty reasons speak against this:

In dividing competences among different authorities, demarcation difficulties arise that may lead to unclear competences and to different points of contact for undertakings and administrations.

Different interpretations of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 by different authorities should be avoided. However, the risk of different interpretation would exist if an authority other than the Federal Network Agency were designated, since the data protection authorities focus primarily on the protection of fundamental rights and have no experience in the field of product regulation. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 has, alongside the protection of fundamental rights, the main aim of creating uniform market rules and legal certainty in order to promote innovation.

Finally, there is a foreseeable shortage of AI specialists. If market surveillance were divided among different authorities, these would compete for scarce resources and would each have to build up competences separately. This would be inefficient and uneconomical.

Various models have also been examined for the design of complete independence within the Federal Network Agency, including ensuring the independence of the authority as a whole or the creation of a completely independent department within the Federal Network Agency. In the balance between legal requirements and practical feasibility, the solution found, which is based on the principle of the existing ruling chambers, is the most appropriate.

V. Legislative Competence
The federal legislative competence is based primarily on Article 74, point 11, of the Basic Law (GG; law of the economy) in conjunction with Article 72(2) GG and, with regard to the administrative fine provisions (Part 5 of the AI Market Surveillance and Innovation Promotion Act in Article 1 of the draft), on Article 74(1), point 1, GG (criminal law). The present Act serves to implement Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, which, primarily as a product regulation, contains economy-related requirements. The conditions of Article 72(2) GG are met. A federal statutory regulation is necessary in the overall national interest for the implementation of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 in order to ensure a uniform approach to the application of the EU rules within the Federal Republic of Germany and thus also to safeguard legal and economic uniformity. A uniform federal arrangement of the implementing provisions ensures the application of uniform standards in the supervision and enforcement with regard to the measures that providers and deployers of AI systems must in particular take pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 in order to place AI systems on the market, put them into service and use them in conformity with the Regulation. This is served in particular by the Coordination and Competence Centre created by this Act, which is intended to support all market surveillance authorities and notifying authorities in their tasks resulting from Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. In addition, the Federal Network Agency shall, under this Act, be competent on a federal level for innovation promotion in the field of artificial intelligence. The regulatory aim of an innovation-friendly implementation of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 makes a federal statutory regulation necessary.

The federal legislative competence for Article 2 results from Article 74(1), point 1, (criminal law, judicial procedure), point 11 (law of the economy), of the Basic Law (GG) in conjunction with Article 72(2) GG, Article 74(1), point 12, (labour law) GG, and additionally as an annex competence of the Federation to the respective legislative competences of the Federation under Articles 73 and 74 GG affected by the substantive scope of the draft Act.

The federal legislative competence for Article 3 results, by way of factual connection, from Article 74(1), point 12 (labour law, social insurance), or Article 74(1), point 7, GG (public welfare) in conjunction with Article 72(2) GG.

VI. Compatibility with European Union Law and International Treaties
The present Act is compatible with European Union law and with international treaties concluded by the Federal Republic. It serves to implement Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

VII. Consequences of the Act
1. Simplification of Law and Administration
No existing rules are simplified or repealed. The creation of an authority structure for the implementation of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and the formulation of provisions on the cooperation and collaboration of authorities pursue the aim of achieving the most uniform and practicable application of the law possible and thus keeping the administrative burden for the enforcement of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 at all competent authorities as low as possible. The maximum possible bundling of AI competence and resources also serves this aim.

2. Sustainability Aspects
The Act is in line with the Federal Government's Sustainability Strategy. It serves to implement Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. The purpose of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 is to improve the functioning of the internal market and to promote the introduction of human-centric and trustworthy artificial intelligence (AI) and innovations connected therewith. At the same time, a high level of protection with regard to health and safety and the fundamental rights enshrined in the Charter is to be ensured.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 promotes the smooth functioning of the internal market by creating a horizontal legal framework for AI models and systems. The provisions of the draft Act serve the goals of the German Sustainability Strategy. In particular, the draft is intended to contribute to steady and adequate economic growth (cf. German Sustainability Strategy of the Federal Government, indicator 8.4) by strengthening the conditions for effective competition. The establishment of reliable legal framework conditions also promotes innovations that make it possible to shape the future sustainably with new solutions (Sustainability Strategy, goal 9).

The draft thus follows in particular the sustainability principles "Maintaining and improving social cohesion in an open society" and "Using education, science and innovation as drivers of sustainable development".

4. Compliance Costs
a. Brief Description
The obligations for industry and citizens result from the directly applicable Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. This Implementing Act creates no new obligations for them but merely designates the competent authorities and regulates their tasks and cooperation. Compliance costs therefore do not result from this Act for industry and citizens.

Against the background of the broadly framed horizontal regulatory approach, the complex regulatory subject-matter as well as the dynamically growing market environment in the field of artificial intelligence, the determination of the resources needed for the application and enforcement of the AI Regulation is subject to numerous uncertainties, possibly making a later needs-based readjustment of capacities necessary. The assessment of necessary additional needs is in particular possible after the implementation of the planned evaluations by the European and national legislator and should be linked with these (cf. section A point VIII of the explanatory memorandum).

b. 1:1 Implementation of EU Law
According to the polluter-pays principle, the burden is attributed to the level that first introduced the rule (according to the date of decision). This means that the mere transposition of a rule from an EU regulation into federal law does not cause a change in burden for the addressees of the norm. In addition to the addressee industry (here in its entirety), burdens also arise for the administration through the AI Regulation, which are to be allocated to this 1:1 implementation. For these requirements, which are covered by the framework of the EU regulation, no compliance costs arise on the basis of the federal legislation. Consequently, these requirements are not listed in the estimate. This concerns, for example, many burdens for AI regulatory sandboxes (Articles 57 et seq. AI Regulation) or in the context of high-risk AI systems (Articles 16, 26, 27, 49, 60, 84 in conjunction with the annexes of the AI Regulation).

Compliance costs within the meaning of this estimate arise only where the national legislator makes specifications and, for example, designates an authority (e.g., the Federal Network Agency in § 2(1) KI-MIG). In such a case, the Federal Government is to be regarded as the cause and the rule is associated with a change of compliance costs.

c. Costs of the Länder
The compliance costs of the Länder shown below are based on an extrapolation. In the field phase, a large part of the Länder could be surveyed. These surveyed Länder represent 76.5% of all market surveillance activities of all Länder. On this basis, the costs were extrapolated to the population of all Länder. Therefore, the case number here is 1. The costs shown in the upper service and in the higher service are thus equally equivalent to the relation of the surveyed Länder.

In addition, there is also the Central Office of the Länder, which is listed in Requirement 2. This performs the tasks in this area for all Länder. Accordingly, this burden was not extrapolated.

d. Costs of the Federal Authorities
In many cases, the costs of the federal authorities have been shown with a case number of 1. This is due to various factors. On the one hand, it was difficult for the authorities to estimate the costs concretely, since often no comparable predecessors are established to which the new AI area is comparable, and therefore prior experience for assessments was lacking. Here, processes were often estimated that may also include several cases (e.g., parallel processing). In addition, there is great heterogeneity between the cases, which makes a valid representation of a typical individual case impossible. Furthermore, the material costs mostly arise as a whole (e.g., IT infrastructure) and not for the respective individual cases.

A representation of the individual case would only be apparently precise in these cases.

VIII. Time Limit; Evaluation
A time limit of the Act implementing the Regulation on Artificial Intelligence does not come into consideration, since the rules implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 are designed to be permanent.

An evaluation is provided for at the level of the European Union in Article 112 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. Article 112 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 contains various evaluation mandates to the European Commission. Pursuant to paragraph 2(c), this includes, among other things, that the European Commission shall, by 2 August 2028 and every four years thereafter, evaluate amendments to improve the effectiveness of the supervisory and governance system and shall report thereon to the European Parliament and the Council. The reports should in particular also address the following aspects: the state of play with regard to the financial, technical and human resources of the competent national authorities with a view to their ability to perform effectively the tasks entrusted to them on the basis of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, and the state of sanctions, in particular the administrative fines under Article 99(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, that Member States have imposed for infringements of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (cf. Article 112(4)(a) and (b) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689). Pursuant to Article 112(13) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the European Commission shall, in addition, by 2 August 2031, taking into account the first years of application of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, carry out an evaluation of the enforcement of the Regulation and report thereon to the European Parliament, the Council and the European Economic and Social Committee. On the basis of the results, the report shall, where appropriate, be accompanied by a proposal to amend Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 concerning the structure of the enforcement and the necessity of a Union agency for the resolution of identified shortcomings. To the extent that any need for amendment results from the evaluations at European Union level, the implementing Act is to be adapted accordingly.

A comprehensive evaluation of the national supervisory and authority structure laid down with this Act shall take place no later than three years after enactment. Therein, an evaluation shall be made of whether, with the supervisory and authority structure, including the cooperation rules, an innovation-friendly and low-bureaucracy implementation of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 has been achieved. Criteria could be assessments of innovation-friendliness from the perspective of undertakings as well as assessments of the competent authorities regarding their financial, technical and human resources and their cooperation with the Coordination and Competence Centre as well as the low-bureaucracy implementation of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. For the purposes of the evaluation, in particular the Federal Network Agency as well as further competent authorities representatively selected from the various areas affected by Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 shall be surveyed and the information transmitted to the European Commission for the preparation of the report under Article 112(2)(c) and (13) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 shall be evaluated.


B. Specific Part

On Article 1 (Act on Market Surveillance and on Ensuring the Conformity of Artificial Intelligence Systems – AI Market Surveillance and Innovation Promotion Act)

On Part 1 (General Provisions)

On § 1 (Scope of Application)
§ 1 regulates the substantive scope of the Act. This results from those provisions of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 that require implementation by national provisions. According to Article 2(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the Regulation applies under the conditions stated therein, among others, to providers, deployers, importers and distributors of AI systems pursuant to Article 3, point 1, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as well as to authorised representatives of providers that are not located in the European Union, and to product manufacturers who place such AI systems on the market or put them into service together with their product under their own name or trademark. Supplementing the Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 applicable to that extent, the present Act lays down the competent national authorities, makes procedural determinations regarding the measures to be taken by these authorities, regulates the details requiring implementation regarding innovation promotion, and makes the necessary determinations regarding sanctions for infringements of the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

The supervision and enforcement of the requirements for general-purpose AI models regulated in Chapter V of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 lies exclusively with the European Commission, which has, in Article 88(1), second sentence, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, transferred the performance of these tasks to the AI Office.

On Part 2 (Competent Authorities and Cooperation)

On Section 1 (Competent Authorities)
§§ 2 and 3 serve to implement Article 70(1), first sentence, and Article 28(1) and (2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. According to these provisions, every Member State must, for the purposes of this Regulation, establish or designate at least one notifying authority and at least one market surveillance authority as competent national authorities. These authorities shall exercise their powers, pursuant to Article 70(1), second sentence, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, in an independent, impartial and unbiased manner in order to ensure the objectivity of their activities and tasks and to ensure the application and implementation of the Regulation.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 is, at its core, a product regulation and, with the system of post-market surveillance and conformity assessment and notification, draws on familiar regulatory concepts of market surveillance authorities, notifying authorities and notified bodies.

The system of market surveillance is also extended by Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 to new areas that were not previously covered by it. This applies in particular to areas in which systematically different supervisory structures exist (including the financial services area and the area of media supervision) and areas such as those mentioned in Annex III (high-risk AI systems in the areas of biometrics, critical infrastructure, AI in the workplace and in educational institutions, granting of essential public services, law enforcement, migration, asylum, border control and justice), in which existing supervisory structures cannot be relied upon.

Against this background, §§ 2 and 3, on the one hand, designate the already existing authorities as market surveillance authorities and notifying authorities pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, or transfer the market surveillance over AI systems of public bodies of the Länder to the authorities competent under Land law. On the other hand, the Federal Network Agency is additionally designated as a central authority, which, in addition to its tasks as market surveillance authority (§ 2(1)) and notifying authority (§ 3(1)), shall also have a coordination and competence function (§ 5), thereby significantly reducing the risk of inconsistent interpretation and application of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and addressing the limited availability of AI specialists.

For the supervision of certain high-risk AI systems, an Independent AI Market Surveillance Chamber is in addition established at the Federal Network Agency pursuant to Article 74(8) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (§ 4).

The Federal Network Agency shall also become the Single Point of Contact (§ 6) and the Central Complaints Body (§ 8).


On § 2 (Market Surveillance Authorities)
§ 2 lays down the respectively competent market surveillance authority.

Substantively, the tasks of market surveillance include the supervision of compliance with the provisions on prohibited practices in the AI field (Chapter II of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), on high-risk AI systems (Chapter III of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), and on transparency obligations for providers and deployers of certain AI systems (Chapter IV of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).

The competence of the market surveillance authorities under this provision also extends to the examination of an AI system with regard to its classification as a high-risk AI system on the basis of the conditions laid down in Article 6 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and the guidelines of the Commission.

On Paragraph 1
§ 2(1) designates the Federal Network Agency as market surveillance authority pursuant to Article 70(1), first sentence, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, in so far as this task is not assigned to another authority. This concerns the areas in which already existing authorities are not designated (cf. on this paragraphs 2 to 7) and thus, above all, the areas referred to in Annex III of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

The designation of the Federal Network Agency as market surveillance authority for the areas referred to in Article 74(8), first sentence, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 also has, pursuant to Article 43(1), second subparagraph, second sentence, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the consequence that the Federal Network Agency takes over the function of the notified body where a high-risk AI system within the meaning of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 is to be put into service by law enforcement, immigration or asylum authorities. To that extent, however, the Federal Network Agency does not require recognition as a notified body.

For AI systems that fall within the scope of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 (Cyber Resilience Regulation) and are classified as high-risk AI systems pursuant to Article 6 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the market surveillance authorities designated for the purposes of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 are also competent for the market surveillance activities required under Regulation (EU) 2024/2847. This results from Article 52(14) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847. This provision regulates the case in which there are different market surveillance authorities under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, on the one hand, and under Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, on the other. If a product falls under both Regulations, the Federal Network Agency under § 2(1), or any other market surveillance authority competent under this Act, is in such cases, by reason of the rule in Article 52(14), first sentence, of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, also competent for the supervision of the obligations under Regulation (EU) 2024/2847. In this case, it cooperates, pursuant to Article 52(14), second sentence, of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, with the market surveillance authority to be designated under Regulation (EU) 2024/2847. The market surveillance authority competent under this Act in particular informs the market surveillance authority to be designated under Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 of all findings that are relevant for the performance of its tasks in connection with the implementation of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847. A legislative decision in connection with the designation of a market surveillance authority pursuant to Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 is not pre-empted.

On Paragraph 2
§ 2(2) designates the competent market surveillance authorities for the scope of Annex I, Section A, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

For this, § 2(2) is based on Article 74(3), first subparagraph, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. According to this, for high-risk AI systems and products related thereto, to which the European Union harmonisation legislation listed in Annex I, Section A, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 applies, the authority designated for market surveillance in those legal acts shall be the market surveillance authority for the purposes of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. The deviation option referred to in Article 74(3), second subparagraph, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 is not exercised.

Thus, the authorities designated as market surveillance authorities in the European Union harmonisation provisions referred to in Annex I, Section A, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 are also designated as such authorities under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. This takes account of the fact that Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 is, at its core, a product regulation, by which familiar regulatory concepts are relied upon. Existing authority structures are used. The limited availability of AI specialists is addressed through the Coordination and Competence Centre for the AI Regulation established in § 5.

The market surveillance by an authority designated under § 2(2) covers all AI systems that, as products, fall under a harmonisation legislation listed in Annex I for which this authority is already a market surveillance authority, and all AI systems used as a safety component of a product falling under that Union harmonisation legislation (cf. Article 6(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).

On Paragraph 3
In order to ensure a coherent application and enforcement of the obligations under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 concerning AI systems and the relevant requirements of all sectoral supervisory laws in the financial market area, the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority shall, for the institutions referred to in § 2(3), become the market surveillance authority within the meaning of Article 70(1), first sentence, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, in so far as the placing on the market, putting into service or use of the AI system is in direct connection with the provision of the regulated financial activity. AI systems that are placed on the market, put into service or used in direct connection with the provision of a regulated financial activity are in particular those pursuant to Annex III, point 5(b) and (c), of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, i.e. AI systems that are intended to be used for creditworthiness assessment and credit scoring or for risk assessment and pricing in the case of life and health insurance. In addition, there may be other AI systems that have a direct connection to the provision of a regulated financial activity.

The competence of the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority shall, beyond Article 74(6) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, also be extended to undertakings that are supervised by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority on a purely national level and thus not on the basis of Union law in the financial market area.

On Paragraph 5
For certain high-risk AI systems pursuant to Article 74(8) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, an independent body within the Federal Network Agency is designated as the competent market surveillance authority, since particular requirements pursuant to Articles 41 to 44 of Directive (EU) 2016/680 apply with regard to its independence.

A transfer of this partial task to the data protection authorities, as Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 provides as an alternative, appears to be hardly appropriate. In dividing competences among different authorities, demarcation difficulties arise that may lead to unclear competences and to different points of contact for undertakings and administrations.

Different interpretations of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 by different authorities should be avoided. However, the risk of different interpretation would exist if an authority other than the Federal Network Agency were designated, since the data protection authorities focus primarily on fundamental rights protection and have no experience with product regulation. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 has, alongside the protection of fundamental rights, the main aim of creating uniform market rules and legal certainty in order to promote innovation.

Finally, there is a foreseeable shortage of AI specialists. If market surveillance were divided among different authorities, these would compete for scarce resources and would each have to build up competences separately. This would be inefficient and uneconomical.

It is therefore appropriate to create an independent body within the Federal Network Agency that becomes the competent market surveillance authority for this sub-area. The term "authority" is to be interpreted broadly here; the English language version uses the word "authority". A purely organisational integration of the independent body into an already existing state institution is unproblematic, provided that it is ensured that no instructions and supervision are possible.

In order to enable this independent unit to draw on experience, personnel and competences from the existing market surveillance activities of the Federal Network Agency, and thus to ensure effective supervision, this body is to be modelled on the ruling chambers already existing at the Federal Network Agency under other laws.

§ 2(5) regulates the establishment of the independent body. This body shall be competent for the entire procedure, not only final decisions on market surveillance, in order also to be able to act on its own initiative to initiate proceedings.

To delineate journalistic from non-journalistic purposes, the respective interpretive practice in the area of Article 85 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 may be drawn upon. According to that practice, actions are covered that take place with the objective of disclosing information, opinions or ideas to an indeterminate group of persons, by whatever means of transmission (CJEU, judgment of 14.2.2019, C-345/17, Sergejs Buivids/Datu valsts inspekcija, para. 53, NJW 2019, 2451; CJEU, judgment of 16.12.2008, C-73/07, Tietosuojavaltuutettu/Satakunnan Markkinapörssi Oy and Others, para. 61, EuZW 2009, 108). All phases of journalistic action are covered: journalistic research, editing, publication, documentation and archiving with the intention of reporting. However, a minimum level of own substantive editing of the information made available is required (cf. BGH, judgment of 2025, VI ZR 426/24, para. 51, NJW 2025, p. 3219, 3225). Private or commercial uses where the profit-making intention is in the foreground and the information of the public is to be classified as a side effect — such as publications by undertakings or associations for the purpose of public relations and advertising (Higher Regional Court Koblenz, decision of 12.4.2021 – 4 W 108/21) or individual communication for private purposes — are to be distinguished from journalistic purposes.

An AI system is used for journalistic purposes if its use in the specific case serves the creation, dissemination or support of journalistic content. It is irrelevant that the system in question can also be used for other purposes; what matters is solely the actual purpose of use in each case. In these cases, the supervision of the state-independent market surveillance authorities competent under Land law is limited to the operation of the AI system, in so far as this takes place for journalistic purposes. This ensures that the protection of journalistic freedom is preserved and at the same time a clear demarcation from other areas of use is made. Where, on the other hand, an AI system is used for several purposes that are not exclusively of a journalistic nature, the competence vis-à-vis the provider shall be governed by the other paragraphs of § 2. However, this does not apply where the provider itself acts for journalistic purposes; in this case, supervision under paragraph 1 remains in place.

In the case where that authority is competent, it must at least provide the Federal Network Agency with a copy of the complaint, so that the Federal Network Agency obtains an overview of all complaints.


On Section 2 (Cooperation)

On § 9 (Cooperation of the Competent Authorities)
Since §§ 2 and 3 designate various authorities as competent authorities and, beyond that, further authorities are also affected within their respective scopes of competence by Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, a rule on cooperation is needed in order to ensure an effective and efficient implementation of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 in Germany.

On Paragraph 1
Paragraph 1 regulates the relationship of the authorities competent under §§ 2 and 3 among each other. They are required to cooperate in a cooperative and trustful manner. In addition, paragraph 1 takes up the idea, contained in Article 70(7) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, of an exchange of experience between the competent national authorities.

On Paragraph 2
Through market surveillance measures by the competent market surveillance authorities, the competences of authorities and public bodies under Article 77(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as well as of other authorities may be affected (e.g. in the area of occupational safety). For better coordination of any measures, it is necessary that the market surveillance authorities exchange information with the affected authorities or public bodies and cooperate in carrying out market surveillance measures.

On Paragraph 3
On the first sentence
The competent legal or technical supervisory authority of a federal public body shall, before the market surveillance authority issues a measure against an authority subordinated to it for an infringement of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, be given by the market surveillance authority the opportunity to comment. This is intended to avoid contradictory decisions of the market surveillance authority and the legal or technical supervisory authority. In order not to interfere with the procedural law of the Länder, this does not apply to the competent legal or technical supervisory authority of a public body of the Länder.

On the second sentence
The opportunity to comment is not necessary where there is imminent danger, the immediate measure appears necessary in the public interest, or a compelling public interest stands in the way.

On the third sentence
The statement of the legal or technical supervisory authority should also include a description of the supervisory measures that have been taken on the basis of the notification by the authority or public body pursuant to paragraph 2, first sentence.

On the fourth sentence
The second sentence does not apply where the public body is a law enforcement authority within the meaning of Article 3, point 45.

On Paragraph 4
Paragraph 4 regulates the relationship of the authorities competent under §§ 2 and 3 with other authorities to which Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 confers an independent role. These are — in an exemplary, non-exhaustive list — in particular the data protection supervisory authorities of the Federal Government and the Länder, the Federal Anti-Discrimination Agency and, where designated under Article 77(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the Anti-Discrimination Agencies of the Länder, the Federal Office for Information Security, the Federal Cartel Office, the Federal Ministry for Research, Technology and Space, and the Federal Centre for Children and Youth Media Protection. The authorities competent under §§ 2 and 3 must involve the said authorities in the performance of their tasks under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

The cooperation of the market surveillance authorities competent under this Act with the authority designated pursuant to Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 (and, until its designation, with the Federal Office for Information Security) is specified in § 10.

Concerning the involvement of the Federal Cartel Office, in particular pursuant to Article 74(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the market surveillance authorities are required to report annually to the Federal Cartel Office all information that they have obtained in the course of their market surveillance activities and that may be of interest for the application of Union law in the area of competition rules (which, pursuant to § 6(5) of this Act, is done via the Federal Network Agency as the single point of contact). However, beyond this requirement of an annual report, the market surveillance authorities and the Federal Cartel Office should also be able to exchange information, in so far as this is necessary for the performance of their tasks. Such exchange takes place between the Federal Cartel Office and the Federal Network Agency in the framework of the relevant § 50f(1) of the Act against Restraints of Competition (GWB). Pursuant to § 50f GWB, regardless of the type of procedure chosen, information including personal data and trade and business secrets may be exchanged and used in the respective proceedings. The provisions of the Act against Restraints of Competition and the rules on the tasks, powers and competences of the cartel authorities remain unaffected.

In cases where the market surveillance authorities consider the protection of children and young persons in the media to be affected, they should involve the Federal Centre for Children and Youth Media Protection. Through reference to Article 24 of the Charter and to the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC) and General Comment No 25 of the UNCRC in recital 48 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the importance of the protection of young people in the media in the framework of AI systems is emphasised. It is emphasised that children have rights that go beyond the rights protected in the Charter. The Federal Centre for Children and Youth Media Protection is the authority competent for compliance with these rights. For this reason, and although the Federal Centre for Children and Youth Media Protection has no independent role in the framework of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, it is among the authorities to be involved by the market surveillance authorities.

As one possible forum for cross-authority coordination, cooperation still to be formalised and further design within the framework of the existing Digital Cluster Bonn comes into consideration.

On Paragraph 5
In cooperation, confidentiality pursuant to Article 78 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 must be observed. Therefore, paragraph 5 provides that the authorities referred to in paragraphs 1, 2 and 4 may exchange information, in so far as this is strictly necessary for the performance of their tasks. The rules included in the third and fourth sentences serve clarification and are an expression of the rule made in Article 78(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 that only "strictly necessary" data shall be exchanged. The wording is based on the wording from § 47i GWB.

On Paragraph 6
The provision permits, within the area of competence of the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority, the exchange of information with the relevant bodies, where they are needed for the performance of tasks under this Act or under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. These bodies are added as further regular examples of exceptions to the duties of confidentiality in § 9(1), first sentence, KWG, § 12(1), first sentence, WpIG, § 8(1), first sentence, KMAG, § 309(5) VAG as well as § 5, third sentence, of the Secondary Credit Market Act, § 6, third sentence, ZAG, § 8, second sentence, KAGB, each in conjunction with § 9(1), first sentence, KWG.

For domestic recipients, a duty of confidentiality is in turn ordered under the second sentence. The rule is modelled on § 9(1), fifth sentence, KWG.

On Paragraph 7
The provision is based on the requirements of § 9(1), sixth sentence, KWG, according to which the recipients must be subject to "largely corresponding duties of confidentiality". This is ensured in the European Economic Area within the scope of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 by Article 78 thereof.


On § 10 (Cooperation of the Market Surveillance Authorities with the Authority Designated under Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847)
On Paragraph 1
For AI systems that fall within the scope of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 and are classified as high-risk AI systems pursuant to Article 6 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the market surveillance authorities designated for the purposes of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 are, pursuant to Article 52(14), first sentence, of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, also competent for the market surveillance activities required under Regulation (EU) 2024/2847. Article 52(14), second and third sentences, of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 regulates the cooperation between the market surveillance authorities competent under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and the market surveillance authority competent pursuant to Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, in so far as their tasks overlap. § 10(1) of this Act specifies this cooperation. The aim is in particular to ensure effective and efficient enforcement of the requirements of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, which also ensures an appropriate level of cybersecurity in products with digital elements and at the same time avoids divergent application of cybersecurity requirements under Annex I of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847. Decisive for this is a cooperative and trustful cooperation of the authorities among each other, which takes account of the expertise and competences in the various subject areas. The rule of § 10(1), second sentence, therefore relates, beyond the competences of the respective competent market surveillance authority pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, also to the communication of observations that may be of importance for the work of the other authority. Since products under Regulation (EU) 2017/745 and Regulation (EU) 2017/746 do not fall within the scope of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, § 10 does not apply to these products.

The requirements to be fulfilled and demonstrated by the addressees are laid down by law by Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, in a manner that is uniform across Europe. The requirements for high-risk AI systems result from Articles 9 to 15 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, including the cybersecurity requirements in Article 15 of that Regulation. At the same time, high-risk AI systems are deemed, pursuant to Article 12(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, to comply with the cybersecurity requirements pursuant to Article 15 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 where they fulfil the cybersecurity requirements referred to in Annex I of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847. Moreover, conformity with Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 is presumed pursuant to Article 42(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 in so far as the requirements of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 are complied with and overlap. This circumstance underlines the importance of cooperation among the market surveillance authorities referred to in § 10(1).

On Paragraph 2
In the framework of cooperation, the market surveillance authority to be designated pursuant to Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 informs the market surveillance authority competent in each case under this Act about suspected cases regarding non-compliance with cybersecurity requirements in high-risk AI systems. A reasoned assessment is generally provided, where possible at the same time as the information about a suspected case, by the market surveillance authority to be designated pursuant to Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 to the market surveillance authority competent under this Act. The market surveillance authority competent in each case under this Act then examines the possibility of measures in the area of cybersecurity of high-risk AI systems in light of the indications received from the market surveillance authority pursuant to Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847. The examination is carried out on the basis of examination criteria (specifications) jointly developed by the Federal Network Agency and the market surveillance authority competent pursuant to Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847. This is intended to ensure that the requirements described in Annex I of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 are applied uniformly nationally.

However, the already existing competences of sectoral authorities for cybersecurity issues must be observed. An exception therefore exists for the market surveillance vis-à-vis financial undertakings. For Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (DORA Regulation) contains comprehensive independent cybersecurity rules specifically for the financial sector. The Federal Network Agency and the market surveillance authority to be designated pursuant to Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 therefore draw up, in the framework of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, joint examination criteria (specifications) for financial undertakings within the meaning of Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 in agreement with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority. The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority remains free, in the framework of its tasks and powers under the DORA Regulation, to derive, as lex specialis, independent cybersecurity requirements from Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, in particular also without the agreement of the Federal Network Agency or the market surveillance authority to be designated pursuant to Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847. This expresses the existing division of tasks in the area of cybersecurity, according to which only the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority is competent for the financial sector, while the Federal Office for Information Security is competent for the other economic areas and public administration.

The details of cooperation may be regulated in an administrative agreement.

On Paragraph 3
Article 96(1)(a) in conjunction with Article 15(5) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 provide for the drawing up of European guidelines by the European Commission. Through these European guidelines, the harmonisation idea is given effect.

In order to ensure consistent and smooth enforcement of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and to achieve synergies through the use of the specific expertise of the authority designated pursuant to Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, that authority's contribution to the drawing up of the guidelines of the European Commission is to be ensured. To accompany this process at an early stage, the Federal Office for Information Security should perform this task already before the designation of a market surveillance authority pursuant to Article 52(2) of Regulation 2024/2847.

The participation in the development of the European harmonised standards within the meaning of Article 40 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and in the laying down of common specifications within the meaning of Article 41 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 on the cybersecurity requirements pursuant to Article 15(5) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 should also be performed by the authority to be designated pursuant to Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847. Here too, the Federal Office for Information Security should, with a view to early involvement, be involved already before the designation of a market surveillance authority pursuant to Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847.

The authority referred to in § 2(3) cooperates to the same extent, in so far as the scope of Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 is concerned.

On Paragraph 4
Since this Act enters into force in time before a designation of the market surveillance authority pursuant to Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 — that is, the market surveillance authorities competent under this Act begin enforcing Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 before such a market surveillance authority can perform the tasks under paragraphs 1 to 3 — a transitional rule is created in paragraph 4. Since this rule is not intended to pre-empt the decision under Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, it is limited in time to the period until the application of Article 52(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 by designation. At the same time, the Federal Office for Information Security, as the central body for information security at national level pursuant to § 1, second sentence, of the Act on the Federal Office for Information Security, is the authority competent at federal level.


On Part 3 (Powers)

On § 11 (Powers of the Competent Authorities)
On Paragraph 1
On the first and second sentences
With § 11(1), first sentence, all market surveillance authorities designated under this Act and the persons commissioned by them are conferred the powers under Article 14(4) and (5) and Article 16 of Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 as well as the powers under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (cf. Article 74(1), first sentence, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).

This includes the power to require the submission of relevant documents, technical specifications, data or information about conformity. The market surveillance authorities are also empowered, within the scope of the Act, to require economic operators to take measures to terminate the non-compliance or the risk emanating from an AI system as well as to prohibit or restrict the making available of a non-compliant AI system or of an AI system from which a risk emanates. Where there is a case of prohibited practices under Article 5 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the affected AI system must be withdrawn from the market without undue delay. Where the economic operator does not take any suitable measures or where the non-compliance or risk persists, the market surveillance authorities may, within the scope of the Act, take all necessary measures to terminate the non-compliance or risk. This does not, however, include the power of the market surveillance authorities to interfere with the official/sovereign action of the actors supervised by them. The powers of the market surveillance authorities are limited to checking the requirements arising from Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

The other supervisory powers in the sectoral supervisory laws, such as those of the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority in the Banking Act, the Insurance Supervision Act, the Investment Firms Act, the Payment Services Supervision Act or the Capital Investment Code, also remain unaffected.

On Paragraph 2
On the first sentence
The first sentence makes clear that the market surveillance authorities, in exercising their powers, may engage third persons as administrative assistants.

On the second sentence
The second sentence serves to clarify that the market surveillance authorities may exercise the powers referred to in Article 14(4)(d) and (j) of Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 remotely. A clarification is necessary because Article 74(5) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 only provides that these powers may be exercised "where appropriate".

On Paragraph 3
Pursuant to Article 14(4)(e) of Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, the market surveillance authorities are also empowered, among other things, to carry out unannounced on-site inspections, premises and grounds as well as means of transport that the economic operator uses for purposes connected with its commercial activity. § 11(3), first sentence, provides that this may only take place during normal operating and business hours. The second sentence takes account of the citation requirement under Article 19(1), second sentence, of the Basic Law.

On Paragraph 4
For the European Union harmonisation legislation listed in Annex I of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, § 7 of the Market Surveillance Act (MüG) already applies (cf. § 1(1) MüG in conjunction with Article 2(1) of Regulation (EU) 2019/1020). In these cases, the high-risk AI systems are connected with products within the meaning of the harmonisation legislation referred to in Annex I of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. In order to extend the existing market surveillance powers by the aspects of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, a corresponding application of the rules in § 7(2) to (4) MüG is necessary.

On Paragraph 5
In so far as public bodies, in particular security authorities, have particularly protection-worthy interests of secrecy under applicable law, paragraph 5 makes clear, for the protection of the functioning of these bodies, that the data covered thereby may, in principle, not be collected or further processed within the meaning of Article 4, point 2, of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.

There are, however, cases in which the interest of the market surveillance authority, in the framework of its competence regulated under this Act, in the processing of the data prevails and the interest of secrecy must therefore exceptionally yield. This is, for example, the case where there are concrete indications of a particularly serious infringement of the requirements of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

On Paragraph 6
Paragraph 6 regulates the disclosure of data subject to fiscal secrecy by the fiscal authority to the market surveillance authority. According to § 30(4), point 2, of the Fiscal Code, disclosure is permissible where it is expressly authorised by federal law. Paragraph 6 contains such a power of disclosure. The disclosure of the data takes place only at the request of the market surveillance authority and must be limited to the necessary extent. It is permissible only where the disclosure of the data is necessary in order for the market surveillance authority to perform its powers under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and the market surveillance authority demonstrates the necessity to the fiscal authority on a reasoned basis.

On Paragraph 7
Paragraph 7 regulates the procedural provisions for the objection and action procedure.

First sentence
For medical devices (§ 45(5) of the Medical Devices Implementing Act) and radio equipment (§ 36(1) of the Radio Equipment Act), it is provided in the sector-specific legal acts to which Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 refers in Article 6(1) in conjunction with Annex I that objection and action against decisions of market surveillance shall not have suspensive effect, while in other sector-specific legal acts this is not the case. Divergence between the procedural provisions for measures under those sector-specific legal acts and measures under the present Implementing Act should, however, be avoided.

Second sentence
Measures of the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority under this Act are also immediately enforceable. In the area of financial supervision, there is high public sensitivity to the integrity and stability of the financial market and to the protection of creditors, investors, policyholders, consumers and other market participants. Measures of the Federal Authority on the basis of its sectoral specialist laws are therefore mostly immediately enforceable (cf. e.g. § 49 KWG, § 310(2) VAG, § 7 KAGB, § 6 WpIG, § 9 ZAG, § 51(2) GwG). Mirroring this, it is necessary that BaFin must be able to take measures quickly and with legal certainty also for the examination of compliance with the requirements of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and for their enforcement.

In addition, deviating from the regular case under § 80(1) VwGO, it is necessary expressly to extend the immediate enforceability also to the threat and imposition of coercive measures. This corresponds also to the usual norms in financial market regulation. For the immediate enforceability of the coercive measures serves the rapid and effective enforcement of supervisory measures. This is intended in particular to ensure uniform enforceability of decisions of the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority as market surveillance authority and supervisory decisions of the Federal Authority.

For the rest, the legislative decision of § 80 of the Code of Administrative Court Procedure remains in the scope of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. The market surveillance authorities retain the possibility of ordering immediate enforcement pursuant to § 80(2), point 4, of the Code of Administrative Court Procedure.


On Part 4 (Innovation Promotion Measures)
Part 4 of the Act serves to implement the innovation-promoting measures of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

On § 12 (Innovation-Promoting Measures)
§ 12 regulates the implementation of the innovation-promoting measures provided for under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 that go beyond the establishment of AI regulatory sandboxes (cf. § 13) or tests under real-world conditions outside AI regulatory sandboxes (cf. § 14). These include an AI service desk for the providers and deployers of AI systems as well as the other addressees of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (point 1), the performance of certain advisory services (point 2), the carrying out of awareness-raising and training measures (point 3), the promotion of knowledge build-up and exchange on AI (point 4), the networking of relevant actors of the AI ecosystem regarding AI systems (point 5), and the participation in standardisation and norm-setting (point 6).

On Point 1
Point 1 confers on the Federal Network Agency the task of establishing a central AI service desk. The tasks of the BeKI currently being established remain unaffected (point 2).

On Point 2
Public bodies such as authorities and corporations under public law can perform their statutory tasks more effectively and efficiently with the use of artificial intelligence. With this provision, the federal legislator addresses legal uncertainty in the classification of borderline cases as AI system or high-risk AI system. The public body should receive a legally non-binding assessment within a foreseeable period. The essential considerations are to be published by the Federal Network Agency in order also to provide other providers, prospective providers or deployers of AI systems dealing with comparable borderline cases with low-threshold and practice-oriented orientation. In the assessments, the Federal Network Agency is guided by the relevant guidelines of the European Commission.

A precondition for the performance of the advisory service provided for by this provision is the prior conclusion of an administrative agreement between the Federal Network Agency and the respective competent federal or Land ministry. The subject of this administrative agreement must include, among other things, the staffing and financial offsetting of the additional burdens arising at the Federal Network Agency through the advisory work, by funds of the respective competent federal or Land ministry from its respective budget chapter.

On Point 3
The carrying out of awareness-raising and training measures provided for in Article 62(1)(b) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 for the application of the Regulation is assigned to the BNetzA as a task. The target group of these measures to be carried out by the Federal Network Agency are primarily the actors of the AI ecosystem, in particular SMEs and start-ups. Cooperation with business-oriented networks will also play an important role here.

The Federal Academy of Public Administration remains competent for the carrying out of awareness-raising and training measures for the federal administration. The BeKI currently being established is to take over, in the area of the federal administration, among other things, the task of innovation promotion by supporting the use of artificial intelligence there. The Federal Network Agency cooperates closely with the Federal Academy of Public Administration and the BeKI currently being established. Thus, in this area of tasks too, tried-and-tested structures are relied upon, which supports the goals of this legislative project, maximises efficiencies and minimises the compliance burden.

On Point 4
The Federal Network Agency is conferred the task of promoting the build-up and exchange of knowledge on artificial intelligence.

On Point 5
The Federal Network Agency is conferred the task of promoting the networking and cooperation of the relevant actors of the AI ecosystem with regard to AI systems, in so far as this serves the performance of its tasks under this Act. In performing its tasks under point 5, § 9 must be observed.

On Point 6
The Federal Network Agency shall make its expertise available also for the performance of further tasks from Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 to promote innovation, in particular through cooperation in the area of technical standardisation of artificial intelligence in national and international standardisation bodies.


On § 13 (AI Regulatory Sandboxes; Authorisation to Issue Regulations)
Pursuant to Article 57(1), first sentence, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Member States are required to establish at least one AI regulatory sandbox at national level which must be operational by 2 August 2026. The objectives are referred to in Article 57(9) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

On Paragraph 1
On the first sentence
Paragraph 1, first sentence, confers on the Federal Network Agency the task of establishing and operating this AI regulatory sandbox.

On the second sentence
The second sentence makes clear that additional AI regulatory sandboxes by other competent national authorities pursuant to Article 3, point 48, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 remain permissible.

On Paragraph 2
Paragraph 2 requires the Federal Network Agency to cooperate pursuant to Article 57(4), second sentence, first half-sentence, and Article 57(4), fourth sentence, pursuant to Article 57(10) and pursuant to Article 58(4) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. In the interest of effective operation of the AI regulatory sandbox, the Federal Network Agency is also dependent on the cooperation of other affected authorities of the Federation and the Länder. The provision therefore makes clear that the cooperation of all authorities should be cooperative and trustful.

The duty to cooperate includes in particular close cooperation with the competent data protection supervisory authority, which also takes over the examination of the data protection requirements pursuant to Article 59 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. The guidelines to be drawn up pursuant to Article 57(7) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 are drawn up jointly by the Federal Network Agency and the data protection supervisory authorities concerning data protection; the data protection supervisory authorities decide on the final version of the data protection guideline.

The early and comprehensive involvement of the competent data protection supervisory authority should also enable a uniform proof under Article 57(7) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. In doing so, the implementing acts to be issued by the Commission under Article 58 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 must be taken into account.

It is also clarified that the supervisory and monitoring powers of further authorities under other laws remain unaffected.

On Paragraph 3
Pursuant to Article 62(1)(a) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the Federal Network Agency is required to grant priority access to the AI regulatory sandbox under paragraph 1, first sentence, to SMEs, including start-ups, that have their seat or a branch in the European Union and meet the conditions and selection criteria. These conditions are still to be laid down in delegated EU legal acts pursuant to Article 58(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

Paragraph 3 additionally requires the Federal Network Agency to grant priority access to the AI regulatory sandbox also to research institutions as well as universities and their spin-offs that have their seat or a branch in the Union. This requires, on the one hand, that the research institutions as well as universities and their spin-offs meet the conditions and selection criteria to be laid down pursuant to the implementing acts within the meaning of Article 58(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. On the other hand, the access to the AI regulatory sandbox under paragraph 1, first sentence, must facilitate or accelerate the access of AI systems to the Union market, e.g. because the AI systems are the subject of applied research and development projects, thereby facilitating the transfer of research results into practice. This limits the priority access and takes account of the character of AI regulatory sandboxes as an instrument of economic promotion.

Article 2(6) and (8) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 apply without restriction. According to these, AI systems that exclusively serve scientific research and development are excluded from priority access to the AI regulatory sandbox.

On Paragraph 4
Articles 57 to 59 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 regulate the Union-law requirements for the establishment of AI regulatory sandboxes by the competent authorities of the Member States. This is intended to ensure responsible innovation and the integration of suitable safeguards and risk mitigation measures and to facilitate the development and trialling of innovative AI systems under strict regulatory supervision before these systems are placed on the market or otherwise put into service.

If a competent national authority establishes an AI regulatory sandbox under § 13(1) to (3) of this Act, it must observe the requirements of Articles 57 and 59 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as directly applicable law. In addition, there are the requirements that may result from implementing acts of the European Commission within the meaning of Article 58 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. This in particular includes provisions on establishment, development, implementation, operation, supervision of AI systems in the AI regulatory sandbox as well as cooperation with other affected authorities.

Pursuant to Article 58(1), first sentence, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the implementing acts lay down common principles, which represent only minimum requirements that are to be observed Union-wide. These leave open possibilities for national detailed regulations. Therefore, the Federal Ministry for Digital Affairs and State Modernisation is empowered to lay down further details of the establishment and operation of the AI regulatory sandboxes under paragraph 1, first sentence. This includes in particular conditions and selection criteria for general as well as priority access, cost-setting, environment design and involvement of, and cooperation with, other actors within the AI ecosystem, access to infrastructure and testing environment, build-up of own testing infrastructure, possibility of guidance, supervision and support, drawing up and adaptation of the sandbox plan, and duration and termination of tests in AI regulatory sandboxes, as well as cooperation with other affected federal authorities. This power takes account in particular of the fact that the AI regulatory sandbox, as an instrument of innovation, is to be able to develop further and adapt rapidly to market conditions.


On § 14 (Tests of High-Risk AI Systems Under Real-World Conditions Outside AI Regulatory Sandboxes)
On Paragraph 1
With paragraph 1, the market surveillance authorities are also conferred the power pursuant to Article 60(6) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

Excepted from this are tests carried out within the meaning of Article 2(3) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. This concerns AI systems where, and in so far as, these are placed on the market, put into service or, with or without modifications, used exclusively for military, defence or national security purposes, regardless of the type of entity carrying out these activities.

On Paragraph 2
With paragraph 2, the deemed authorisation pursuant to Article 60(4)(b) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 is created.


On Part 5 (Administrative Offences)

On § 15 (Administrative Fine Provisions for Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)
§ 15 serves to transpose Article 99(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, according to which Member States must adopt rules on sanctions and other enforcement measures applicable to infringements of this Regulation by actors. According to that Article, Member States must ensure that, in addition to the infringements enumeratively named in Article 99(3), (4) and (5) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, further infringements of the Regulation are also subject to sanctions. Against this background, the paragraph lists all infringements of all enforceable obligations of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 that are not already subject to sanctions under Article 99 of the Regulation — provided that the infringements can be subject to administrative fines.


On § 16 (Application of the Provisions on Administrative Fine and Criminal Procedure)
On Paragraph 1
Pursuant to § 2 of the Act on Administrative Offences (OWiG), the OWiG applies to administrative offences under federal and Land law. § 15(1), first sentence (sic — § 16(1), first sentence), of this Act declares that the OWiG is, in principle, also correspondingly applicable to infringements pursuant to Article 99(3) to (5) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, which is directly applicable national law. In the corresponding application of § 10 OWiG, it is to be taken into account that Article 99(3) to (5) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 covers both intentional and negligent action. This results from Article 99(7)(i) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, according to which the intentional or negligent character of the infringement is a relevant circumstance to be taken into account in setting the administrative fine. It is thus required by the Regulation that negligent action is sanctioned and that mere negligent commission is taken into account only in the context of the assessment of the administrative fine.

According to the second sentence, § 17 OWiG, also in conjunction with § 30(3) OWiG, as well as § 30(1) and (2) OWiG, do not apply, since the European sanctions law regulated in Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 contains exhaustive and prevailing rules in this respect.

On Paragraph 2
Since the Regulation itself does not regulate the procedure for imposing the administrative fine, § 16(2), first sentence, provides that the provisions of the OWiG, in principle, apply correspondingly, and expressly clarifies that this also applies to the provisions of the general statutes on criminal procedure that are applicable mutatis mutandis under § 46(1) OWiG.

Paragraph 2, second sentence, provides that the public prosecutor's office in the intermediate proceedings may discontinue the proceedings only with the consent of the market surveillance authority that issued the administrative fine notice. This takes account of the importance of the administrative fines in Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and of the primary-law-anchored independence of market surveillance.


On § 17 (Authorities)
On Paragraph 1
Paragraph 1 lays down which administrative authorities are competent for the administrative fine procedure.

On Paragraph 2
Paragraph 2 makes clear, with a view to Article 99(8) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, that no administrative fines are imposed against public bodies within the meaning of § 2(1) and (2) of the Federal Data Protection Act.


On Part 6 (Retention Obligations)

On § 18 (Retention Obligation under Article 18(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)
Pursuant to Article 18(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, a rule on the retention of documentation in the case of bankruptcy or cessation of business is to be made. In the case of insolvency, this obligation passes by law to the insolvency administrator pursuant to § 80 of the Insolvency Code; upon termination thereof pursuant to § 215(2) of the Insolvency Code, it returns to the company owner.

§ 5 of the Medical Devices Implementing Act remains unaffected.


On Article 2 (Amendment of the Whistleblower Protection Act)
Through Article 2, it is ensured that the Whistleblower Protection Act applies to the reporting and disclosure of infringements of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. This takes account of the requirement in Article 87 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, according to which Directive (EU) 2019/1937 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 23 October 2019 on the protection of persons who report breaches of Union law (OJ L 305, 26.11.2019, p. 17), as last amended by Directive (EU) 2024/1760 (OJ L 2024/1760, 5.7.2024), applies to the reporting of infringements of this Regulation and to the protection of persons reporting such infringements.


On Article 3 (Amendment of the First Book of the Social Code)
The amendment takes account of the conclusive character of social data protection and supplements the directly applicable Union-law rules on the processing of social data with scopes for the protection during the processing of personal data of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. The permissibility of the processing of social data is regulated in the Tenth Book of the Social Code in conjunction with the First Book of the Social Code as well as in the other Books of the Social Code, whereby the direct primacy of Union law must be taken into account. In addition to the rules of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the data protection provisions in Article 10(5) and Article 59 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 now have direct primacy. Pursuant to Article 2(7) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the Union law on the protection of personal data, privacy and the confidentiality of communications applies to the processing of personal data in connection with the rights and obligations laid down in Regulation (EU) 2024/1689; the data protection provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 or (EU) 2018/1725 and Directive 2002/58/EC or (EU) 2016/680 remain unaffected by Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, without prejudice to Article 10(5) and Article 59.


On Article 4 (Amendment of the Financial Services Supervision Act)
On Number 1
The amendment of § 15(1), first sentence, supplements the catalogue of measures that are subject to separate reimbursement, namely with regard to the introduction of the AI Market Surveillance and Innovation Promotion Act.

On Number 2
The amendment of § 16b takes account of the establishment of the competence of the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority as competent market surveillance authority within the meaning of Article 70(1), first sentence, of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 in § 2(4) of the AI Market Surveillance and Innovation Promotion Act, with regard to the determination of costs by area of responsibility, by allocating these costs, in so far as they are costs relating to credit, financial services, securities institutions, payment services, crypto or domestic investment, to the area of responsibility "Banks and Other Financial Services" and, in so far as they are costs of the insurance sector, to the area of responsibility "Insurance" correspondingly.

On Number 3
This is an editorial adjustment. The supplement inserted by the Secondary Credit Market Promotion Act with regard to the keeping of crypto-securities registers was, due to an editorial oversight, not retained in the adjustment by the Financial Market Digitalisation Act, so that re-insertion is necessary.

On Number 4
This is an editorial adjustment. The inserted addition serves to clarify the version of the provisions referred to.


On Article 5 (Entry into Force)
For the purposes of implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, every Member State must, by 2 August 2025, establish or designate the competent national authorities and, in accordance with the requirements of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, adopt rules on the administrative fine procedure. The provisions in Chapters I and II of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 already apply from 2 February 2025. Even though certain requirements for providers and deployers of AI systems only enter into force at a later point in time, this Implementing Act should therefore enter into force without undue delay.
