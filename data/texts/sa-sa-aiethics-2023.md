## Introduction

The Saudi Data & Artificial Intelligence Authority (SDAIA) published the "Principles and Controls of AI Ethics" in September 2023 as a national, principle-based framework for ethical AI development and deployment across the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.

### Scope of Application

This AI Ethics Framework applies to all AI stakeholders designing, developing, deploying, implementing, using, or being affected by AI systems within the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, including:
- Public sector entities;
- Private sector companies;
- Non-profit organisations;
- Researchers and academic institutions;
- Public services and infrastructure operators;
- Civil society organisations; and
- Individuals and workers involved in AI systems.

The framework applies across the complete AI system lifecycle: conception and design, development and testing, deployment and operation, monitoring, maintenance, and retirement.

---

## Risk Classification Framework

The framework employs a four-tier risk model to calibrate the level of controls required:

| Risk Level | Description | Requirements |
|------------|-------------|--------------|
| **Little/No Risk** | Minimal ethical concerns; limited potential for harm | Baseline documentation and good practices |
| **Limited Risk** | Standard potential for harm; manageable risks | Standard controls apply; documentation required |
| **High Risk** | Significant potential for harm to individuals or society | Enhanced documentation, testing, human oversight, and ethics impact assessments required |
| **Unacceptable Risk** | Prohibited applications | Not permitted; development and deployment banned |

## The Seven Core Principles

### Principle 1: Fairness

AI systems must be designed and operated to treat individuals equitably, without unlawful discrimination or unjustified bias.

**Key controls:**
- Implement data governance measures to identify and mitigate biases in training data;
- Apply model-level controls to detect and reduce discriminatory outputs;
- Ensure equitable treatment across demographic groups, use cases, and operating environments;
- Conduct ongoing testing for bias throughout the AI system lifecycle; and
- Maintain documentation of bias assessment results and remediation actions.

### Principle 2: Privacy and Security

AI systems must respect the personal privacy of individuals and protect data against unauthorised access, misuse, and disclosure.

**Key controls:**
- Comply with Saudi Arabia's Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) and applicable regulations;
- Minimise data collection to what is strictly necessary for the AI purpose;
- Implement privacy-by-design approaches in AI system architecture;
- Apply strong technical security controls, including encryption and access management;
- Conduct regular security assessments and penetration testing;
- Establish procedures for responding to personal data breaches; and
- Ensure personal data is not retained beyond its necessary period.

### Principle 3: Humanity

AI systems must respect human dignity and safeguard fundamental human rights, with meaningful human oversight maintained in critical decisions.

**Key controls:**
- Ensure AI systems do not undermine fundamental human rights as recognised under Saudi law and applicable international instruments;
- Maintain meaningful human control and oversight for high-risk decisions affecting individuals;
- Prevent uses of AI that exploit psychological vulnerabilities or manipulate individuals against their interests;
- Design AI systems with mechanisms enabling human intervention, override, and correction; and
- Document how human oversight is exercised in each deployment context.

### Principle 4: Social and Environmental Benefits

AI systems must be designed and deployed in ways that generate net positive value for society and the environment, aligned with Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 objectives.

**Key controls:**
- Assess the broader societal impacts of AI systems before deployment;
- Promote AI applications that address national priorities including economic diversification, healthcare improvement, and educational advancement;
- Consider environmental impacts of AI systems, including energy consumption;
- Avoid AI applications whose aggregate social harms outweigh their benefits; and
- Support sustainability objectives in AI system design and operation.

### Principle 5: Reliability and Safety

AI systems must be reliable, safe, and resilient, performing consistently and predictably across diverse operating conditions.

**Key controls:**
- Conduct rigorous testing and validation before deployment;
- Verify system performance across diverse and representative datasets;
- Test for robustness against adversarial inputs and edge cases;
- Establish monitoring mechanisms to detect performance degradation after deployment;
- Implement fail-safe mechanisms for high-risk applications; and
- Maintain plans for system failure, degraded performance, and emergency shutdown.

### Principle 6: Transparency and Explainability

AI systems must be sufficiently transparent to enable stakeholders to understand how decisions are made and to provide accountability.

**Key controls:**
- Document datasets used for training, including data sources, characteristics, and known limitations;
- Document model design, architecture, and key design decisions;
- Document the decision logic of AI systems to the extent technically feasible and appropriate;
- Maintain model cards and dataset records for accountability purposes;
- Provide affected individuals with accessible explanations of AI-driven decisions that significantly affect them; and
- Disclose to users when they are interacting with an AI system rather than a human.

### Principle 7: Accountability and Responsibility

Clear ownership, governance structures, and redress mechanisms must be established for AI systems.

**Key controls:**
- Define clear lines of accountability for each AI system, including identification of the responsible organisation and individual;
- Establish incident reporting protocols enabling timely detection, escalation, and reporting of AI-related harms;
- Implement redress mechanisms enabling affected individuals to seek correction or remedy;
- Designate organisational roles for AI ethics governance (see Governance Structure below);
- Conduct ethics impact assessments for high-risk AI systems; and
- Cooperate with SDAIA and relevant sectoral regulators in compliance assessments.

## Governance Structure

### Organisational Roles

| Role | Responsibilities |
|------|-----------------|
| **Head of Entity** | Overall accountability for AI ethics compliance within the organisation |
| **Chief Data Officer (CDO)** | Mandatory for public sector bodies; oversees data governance in AI |
| **Responsible AI Officer** | Designated to oversee day-to-day AI ethics implementation |
| **Chief Compliance Officer** | Ensures adherence to applicable standards and regulations |
| **AI System Assessor** | Conducts risk evaluations and ethics impact assessments |
| **AI Ethics Governance Committee** | Internal oversight body reviewing high-risk AI systems |

### SDAIA's Supervisory Role

SDAIA serves as the national authority responsible for:
- Promulgating the AI Ethics Framework and associated guidance;
- Overseeing compliance across public and private sectors;
- Conducting periodic assessments and audits;
- Coordinating with sectoral regulators in healthcare, finance, telecommunications, and other specialised domains; and
- Measuring AI ethics maturity across Saudi organisations using SDAIA's Maturity Model.

## Implementation Requirements

### Mandatory Activities for All Entities

- Risk classification mapping for all AI systems in development or operation;
- Ethics impact assessments for high-risk AI systems;
- Documented evidence of compliance (including model cards, test results, and audit logs);
- Maturity-based adoption using SDAIA's AI Ethics Maturity Model (ranging from Emerging to Advanced); and
- Redress mechanisms enabling affected individuals to seek correction or remedy.

### Lifecycle Application

Controls apply across the complete AI system lifecycle:
1. **Conception and Design** — embedding ethical principles from the outset;
2. **Development and Testing** — bias assessment, safety testing, documentation;
3. **Deployment and Monitoring** — ongoing performance monitoring, incident detection;
4. **Maintenance** — updating systems in response to identified issues; and
5. **Retirement** — secure decommissioning and data deletion.

---

## Monitoring and Enforcement

SDAIA conducts periodic assessments, audits, and targeted investigations of AI systems against these Principles. Non-compliance may:
- Escalate to sectoral regulators;
- Trigger enforcement under existing statutory instruments, including the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) and cybersecurity regulations; and
- Result in compliance directions and corrective measures.

The framework does not prescribe fixed financial penalties; enforcement operates as a normative supervisory structure, with sanctions determined by the applicable statutory instruments under which a violation falls.

## International Alignment

The Principles align conceptually with:
- The **EU AI Act's** risk-based methodology;
- **OECD Recommendations** on Trustworthy AI;
- **UNESCO Recommendation** on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence; and
- Saudi Arabia's **Vision 2030** digital transformation and economic diversification objectives.
