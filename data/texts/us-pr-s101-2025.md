ACT
To add a new subsection 39 and renumber the current subsections 39 to 74 as the new subsections 40 to 75 of Article 2.004; amend Articles 7.007 and 7.009 of Chapter II of Act 222-2011, as amended, better known as the "Act for the Oversight of Political Campaigns in Puerto Rico," and amend Article 12.7 of Chapter XII of Act 58-2020, as amended, better known as the "Electoral Code of Puerto Rico of 2020"; for the purposes of including as a right of the voter to know whether any propaganda, advertisement, or communication was generated partially or completely through the use of, or by means of, Artificial Intelligence; and to add the definition of "Artificial Intelligence," as well as broaden said definition to include related concepts such as Generative Artificial Intelligence, content generated by Artificial Intelligence, and "deepfake," and for other related purposes.
STATEMENT OF MOTIVES
We live in an era of great technological advances that, day by day, open new doors to digital integration, oriented to facilitating people's lives in physical and cyber space. One of these great advances has been the appearance of Artificial Intelligence, which, although not a new concept, has been present since the beginning of computer science. Since then, the possibility that machines may make decisions with the same rigor and complexity as a human being has been discussed, bringing with it debates about their artistic or intellectual capacity.
Artificial Intelligence (AI), as a phenomenon, has become quite popular recently, causing both public and private interest in the development of these technologies to have increased exponentially in just a couple of years. The appearance on the market of software such as ChatGPT or Dall-E, both owned by the company OpenAI, has driven public interest in the future possibilities of systems supported by AI and the various uses that could be given to them. However, in just a few years of activity, we have been able to witness how cutting-edge the integration of Artificial Intelligence is into our daily routines and its use, as well as the lack of understanding regarding the impact it implies on our society. We observe examples such as the alarming number of schools and universities that have reported the use of ChatGPT by their students for the production of essays and to generate the answers to the most important examinations. Likewise, there are cases where digitally generated images circulated showing public figures in surreal and defamatory scenes that ended up going viral, leading to thousands of press releases being issued to clarify the truth and indicate that the image was made with AI.
In Puerto Rico, the phenomenon of Artificial Intelligence software has been received with open arms, forming part of the objectives to be achieved within the public policy of the Executive Branch. The goal is to implement systems that streamline bureaucratic processes for both citizens and public officials. According to a report offered by the Puerto Rico Innovation and Technology Service (PRITS), five agencies of the Government of Puerto Rico have already implemented software that uses some kind of Artificial Intelligence modality for operational and citizen service purposes. However, at the time of drafting this Act there is no type of regulation for its correct implementation at the governmental, state, or federal level, opening the door to uncertainty and misuse of these software programs.
The breadth of the AI programs currently available allows any user to take full advantage of their capabilities for any purpose, but a positive end cannot always be expected. We can already observe the political use of Artificial Intelligence with cases such as the projection of a Deep Fake type video during the Convention of the Republican National Committee that was generated almost entirely with Artificial Intelligence systems, with the aim of criticizing the management of Democratic President Joe Biden. Likewise, the use of digitally generated presenters used to disseminate propaganda on social networks was seen, as was the case with the announcements scheduled during the live broadcasts of the 2023 Caribbean Series held in Venezuela, with the aim of making propaganda in favor of the Venezuelan regime.
Like a technology capable of driving progress, Artificial Intelligence represents a considerable risk to national security when it is not employed carefully and with understanding. The integrity of persons can be threatened by defamation with artificially generated images or videos; in the same way, the integrity of democratic institutions can be hampered by the possibility of transmitting false messages and communications to the population. The democratic structure suffers a severe threat in the face of the possibility of creating a false truth where "fake news" and the defamation of persons are a constant danger.
This Legislative Assembly seeks to establish the foundations for a series of legislation that must be in constant updating due to the novelty of the legislative objective; however, keeping in mind the benefit that technologies such as this and many others can provide to the common welfare of Puerto Ricans in the future. By amending the Act for the Oversight of Political Campaigns in Puerto Rico, we anticipate the possibility that the use of these technologies will not be oriented to undermining the right to vote of Puerto Ricans, and that they additionally have the right to know the origin, human or digital, of the multimedia content they consume.
However, the breadth of the concept of Artificial Intelligence and the diversity of available technological tools makes it necessary to delimit with greater precision its scope in the electoral context. In particular, the development of Generative Artificial Intelligence systems, capable of producing original content in the form of text, images, audio, or video, has increased the risks associated with disinformation and manipulation of the electorate. Likewise, the proliferation of content known as "deepfakes," which appears to be authentic but represents events or expressions that did not occur, poses significant challenges to the integrity of the democratic process.
In light of this reality, this Legislative Assembly deems it necessary to complement the general definition of Artificial Intelligence by incorporating additional concepts that allow for delimiting with greater precision the scope of the legislation, facilitating its implementation, and reducing ambiguities in its application.
BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY OF PUERTO RICO:
Section 1.- A new subsection 39 is added to Article 2.004 of Chapter II of Act 222-2011, as amended, better known as the "Act for the Oversight of Political Campaigns in Puerto Rico," to read as follows:
"Article 2.004.- Definitions.
 For the purposes of this Act, the following terms shall have the meaning set forth below, unless from the text itself another meaning is derived:
…
…
39) "Artificial Intelligence" – Defined as a field of computer science that focuses on the creation of systems and programs capable of performing tasks that require human intelligence. These systems may include capabilities such as machine learning, natural language processing, logical reasoning, and decision-making, among others. The objective of artificial intelligence is to develop machines that can simulate human cognitive processes and perform tasks autonomously, adaptively, and efficiently.
39(a) "Generative Artificial Intelligence" or "generative AI" – shall be understood as any artificial intelligence system that has the capacity to autonomously produce or generate original content in the form of text, images, audio, video, multimedia material, or other digital content, from instructions, specific requests,
or data supplied by a person.
39(b) "Content generated by Artificial Intelligence" – any material in digital format created or altered, in whole or in part, by means of artificial intelligence systems.
39(c) "Deepfake" – content generated by means of artificial intelligence in the form of an image, video, audio, or multimedia material that appears to be authentic, representing a person as if saying or doing something that he or she did not in fact say or do.
…"
Section 2.- The current subsections 39 to 74 of Article 2.004 of Chapter II of Act No. 222-2011, as amended, better known as the "Act for the Oversight of Political Campaigns in Puerto Rico," are renumbered as the new subsections 40 to 75.
Section 3.- Article 7.007 of Act 222-2011, as amended, better known as the "Act for the Oversight of Electoral Campaigns," is amended to read as follows:
"Article 7.007. - Publication and Distribution of Communications; Prohibition of Discrimination by the Print Press.
Whenever a political action committee makes a disbursement to finance any communication through any radio or television station, cable or satellite television, via the Internet, by computers, newspaper, magazine, "billboard," mailing to five hundred (500) or more persons of the same type of message, or any other type of political advertisement to the general public, or when any person makes a disbursement to finance a communication for electoral purposes or to finance an electoral communication as the term is defined in Article 2.004 of this Act, said communication shall contain the following:
(a) … .
(b) … .
(c) … .
(d) If the communication was generated or altered, in whole or in part, by Artificial Intelligence systems, including Generative Artificial Intelligence, in addition to complying with the previous subsections, the communication shall clearly indicate that it was generated or altered, in whole or in part, as the case may be, by Artificial Intelligence systems.
 (e) No person who sells space on television, radio, cable or satellite television, newspaper, magazine, internet, or billboard to a party, aspirant, or candidate, or their committees, or agent of the party, aspirant, or candidate, for the purposes of their campaign, may charge a rate for such space that exceeds the rate charged for the use of such space for other purposes."
Section 4.- Article 7.009 of Act 222-2011, as amended, better known as the "Act for the Oversight of Electoral Campaigns," is amended to read as follows:
"Article 7.009.— Communications made by candidates or authorized persons.
By radio, television, or any other audiovisual medium. Any communication described in Article 7.007 and that is transmitted by radio, in addition to complying with the requirements of said Article, shall include the following statement in audio: "name of the person or committee that paid for the communication and the name of any organization related to said person or committee that is responsible for the content of this message." These requirements shall apply equally to any proselytizing communication transmitted via the Internet. In addition to the foregoing, if the communication was generated or altered, in whole or in part, as the case may be, with Artificial Intelligence systems, then the announcement shall state so by audio statement."
Section 5.- Article 12.7 of Chapter XII of Act 58-2020, as amended, better known as the "Electoral Code of Puerto Rico of 2020," is amended to read as follows:
"Article 12.7.- Falsity of Data or Images in Electronic Systems.
Any person who maliciously, on a second or more occasions, includes, maintains, or transmits by any means information, data, documents, forms, and/or images that are false, altered, or created with artificial intelligence systems without a clear and legible notice or that do not correspond to reality and the truth in any electronic system provided and operated by the Commission, shall incur a misdemeanor and, upon conviction, shall be punished by imprisonment of not more than one (1) year or a fine of five hundred dollars ($500) for each false data, information, or image, or both penalties at the discretion of the Court."
Any person who, by any electronic application or artificial intelligence system, creates or disseminates realistic imitations of a person — whether face, voice, body, data, documents, forms, or images — without that person's consent, may incur a felony, with imprisonment of up to three (3) years and a fine of five hundred dollars ($500) for each false data, information, or image, or both penalties at the discretion of the Court. The following cases shall be considered aggravating circumstances when the previously described conduct involves any of the following:
The dissemination of the falsified image, audio, or voice recording through social networks or telematic media.
The use of the falsified image, audio, or voice recording to commit extortion, threats, obtain money, or affect the rights or property interests of third parties or of the State.
The use of the face or body of a falsified person in photographs or videos of sexual conduct.
The viral dissemination of falsified images, audios, or voice recordings. In the event of aggravating circumstances, a sentence of imprisonment of up to eight (8) years shall be imposed.
Section 6.- If any clause, paragraph, subparagraph, sentence, word, letter, article, provision, section, subsection, title, chapter, subchapter, heading, or part of this Act is annulled or declared unconstitutional, the resolution, ruling, or judgment to such effect shall not affect, prejudice, or invalidate the remainder of this Act.
