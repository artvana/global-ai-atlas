# Florida SB 262 — Digital Bill of Rights and AI Provisions

## Full Title and Citation

**Full Title:** CS for CS for Senate Bill 262 (2023 Legislature)
**Short Title:** Florida Digital Bill of Rights
**Enacted as:** Chapter 2023-100, Laws of Florida
**Signed:** June 6, 2023
**Effective Date:** July 1, 2024 (most consumer-facing provisions)
**Codified at:** Chapter 501, Part V, Florida Statutes (Florida Digital Bill of Rights); various other Florida Statutes titles

**Note on Scope:** Florida SB 262 enacted in the 2023 legislative session is the primary vehicle for Florida's Digital Bill of Rights, which took effect July 1, 2024. Related AI-specific legislation in the 2024 session includes CS/HB 919 (AI in Political Advertising, ch. 2024-126, L.O.F.), which is treated together with SB 262 in this entry as together they constitute Florida's 2024 AI and digital rights framework.

---

## Overview

Florida Senate Bill 262 and its companion 2024 AI legislation represent Florida's comprehensive approach to regulating the intersection of artificial intelligence, digital privacy, and consumer protection. The framework has two principal components:

1. **The Florida Digital Bill of Rights (SB 262/Ch. 501, Part V, F.S.):** Establishes consumer data privacy rights and places special obligations on large technology companies and platforms, with specific provisions addressing AI-driven profiling, facial recognition, voice recognition, and chatbot disclosure.

2. **AI in Political Advertising (CS/HB 919 / ch. 2024-126):** Requires disclosure of AI-generated content in political advertising and prohibits the use of certain synthetic media to deceive voters about candidates and ballot issues.

Governor Ron DeSantis signed SB 262 on June 6, 2023, making Florida the second U.S. state (after California) to enact comprehensive consumer privacy legislation. The Florida Digital Bill of Rights went into effect on July 1, 2024.

## Part I: Florida Digital Bill of Rights (Chapter 501, Part V, F.S.)

### Scope and Applicability

The Florida Digital Bill of Rights applies to "controllers" — entities that, alone or jointly with others, determine the purposes and means of processing personal data. However, it applies only to controllers that:

- Conduct business in Florida or produce products or services targeted to Florida residents; **AND**
- Satisfy **one of the following thresholds:**
  - Control or process personal data of 100,000 or more consumers (excluding personal data processed solely to complete a payment transaction); **OR**
  - Control or process personal data of 25,000 or more consumers **AND** derive more than 50% of global gross revenue from the sale of personal data.

Notably, the Florida Digital Bill of Rights applies a significantly higher threshold than the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) and other state privacy laws, effectively targeting large technology platforms rather than mid-size businesses.

**Exemptions:** The Act contains extensive exemptions, including for:
- Government entities and their contractors acting on behalf of those entities;
- Financial institutions subject to the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act;
- Covered entities and business associates under HIPAA;
- Nonprofit organizations;
- Higher education institutions;
- Air carriers;
- Certain businesses covered by COPPA;
- Data processed for employment purposes.

### Consumer Rights

The Florida Digital Bill of Rights grants Florida consumers the following rights:

**1. Right to Know / Right of Access**
Consumers have the right to confirm whether a controller is processing their personal data and to access that data. Controllers must respond to verified requests within 45 days, with a possible 45-day extension for good cause.

**2. Right to Correct**
Consumers have the right to correct inaccurate personal data held by a controller.

**3. Right to Delete**
Consumers have the right to request deletion of personal data they have provided to or that has been obtained about them by the controller. The right to delete is subject to exceptions for legal obligations, contract performance, public interest research, free speech, and other specified purposes.

**4. Right to Opt-Out of Sale / Targeted Advertising**
Consumers have the right to opt out of:
- The sale of their personal data to third parties;
- The processing of their personal data for targeted advertising purposes;
- Profiling in furtherance of decisions that produce legal or similarly significant effects.

**5. Right to Data Portability**
Consumers have the right to receive their personal data in a portable, readily usable format that allows them to transmit the data to another controller.

**6. Right to Non-Discrimination**
Controllers may not discriminate against consumers for exercising their rights, including by denying goods or services, charging different prices, or providing a different quality of goods or services.

### Special Protections for Sensitive Data

The Act establishes heightened protections for "sensitive data," which includes:

- Racial or ethnic origin, religious beliefs, mental or physical health diagnosis, sexual orientation, or citizenship or immigration status;
- Genetic or biometric data processed for the purpose of uniquely identifying an individual;
- Personal data collected from a known child (under 18);
- Precise geolocation data.

Processing of sensitive data requires **consumer consent** (opt-in), rather than merely the opportunity to opt-out.

### AI-Specific Provisions: Profiling and Automated Decision-Making

**Profiling with Legal Effects:**
The Act requires controllers to provide consumers with a meaningful opportunity to opt out of profiling in furtherance of solely automated decisions that produce legal or similarly significant effects, such as decisions concerning employment, credit, housing, insurance, education, or access to essential services.

**Profiling Transparency:**
Controllers that engage in automated profiling must provide clear disclosures about the fact of profiling, the categories of personal data used, and the logic involved, in plain and accessible language.

### Special Obligations for Large Online Platforms

The Act imposes heightened obligations on "controllers that operate a search engine, a social media platform, an email service, an instant messaging service, a major cloud computing service, or a consumer electronics operating system" with global annual revenues exceeding $100 billion — effectively targeting a small number of the largest technology companies. These include:

- **Consent for targeted advertising to minors:** Such controllers must obtain affirmative consent before processing personal data of consumers under 18 for targeted advertising.
- **Prohibition on certain data practices toward minors:** Heightened restrictions on the collection and sale of personal data from minors.
- **Transparency reports:** Annual transparency reporting on content moderation practices, algorithmic curation, and data practices.

### AI Chatbot and Voice/Facial Recognition Provisions

**Section 501.706 — Chatbot Disclosure**
Businesses subject to the Act that use chatbots or other automated systems capable of simulating human conversation must:
- Clearly and conspicuously disclose at the beginning of any consumer interaction that the consumer is communicating with an automated system, not a human;
- Not design or deploy chatbots in ways specifically intended to deceive consumers into believing they are communicating with a human;
- Maintain audit trails of chatbot interactions sufficient to demonstrate compliance.

**Biometric Data — Voice and Facial Recognition**
Controllers that use voice recognition, facial recognition, or other biometric identification features in consumer devices or services are subject to heightened data protection requirements:
- Consumers must be affirmatively notified of voice or facial recognition collection;
- Biometric data collected through these systems is classified as "sensitive data" and requires explicit consent before processing;
- Biometric data collected through voice or facial recognition features may not be shared with third parties without separate explicit consent.

### Enforcement

**Florida Attorney General:**
The Florida Attorney General has exclusive enforcement authority over the Digital Bill of Rights. There is no private right of action for most provisions.

**Notice and Cure:** Before bringing an enforcement action, the AG must provide written notice to the controller specifying the alleged violation and giving 45 days to cure. If the controller provides written representation that the violation has been cured and will not recur, the AG may not bring an action based on the original noticed violation.

**Civil Penalties:** Controllers that violate the Act may be subject to civil penalties of up to $50,000 per violation. Willful violations may be subject to treble penalties (up to $150,000 per violation).

**Injunctive Relief:** The AG may seek injunctive relief to stop ongoing violations.

## Part II: AI in Political Advertising (CS/HB 919 / ch. 2024-126, L.O.F.)

### Overview

Florida enacted separate legislation in 2024 directly addressing the use of AI-generated synthetic media in political advertising. CS/HB 919 was approved by Governor DeSantis on April 26, 2024, and took effect on July 1, 2024.

### Key Definitions

**Generative AI:** A machine-based system that can, for a given set of human-defined objectives, emulate the structure and characteristics of input data in order to generate derived synthetic content, including images, videos, audio, text, and other digital content.

**Synthetic media:** An image, audio, or video that has been created or substantially altered using AI techniques in a way that depicts a real person performing an action or making a statement that did not actually occur.

**Political advertisement:** Any written or graphic communication that expressly advocates for or against a candidate or ballot issue.

**Electioneering communication:** A communication that refers to a clearly identified candidate and is distributed close in time to an election.

### Disclosure Requirements

Any political advertisement, electioneering communication, or other political communication **created in whole or in part using generative AI** must bear a clear and conspicuous disclaimer stating: "CREATED IN WHOLE OR IN PART WITH THE USE OF GENERATIVE AI."

The disclaimer requirement is triggered when the generated content:
- Depicts a real person performing an action that did not actually occur; **AND**
- Was created with the intent to influence a candidate or ballot issue, or to injure a candidate's reputation.

**Format of Disclaimer:**
- For visual media (video): the disclaimer must be displayed continuously throughout the advertisement in a font size at least as large as any other text in the ad, and in a contrasting color.
- For audio media: the disclaimer must be read aloud at the beginning and end of the advertisement in a clear and audible manner.
- For printed or digital graphic communications: the disclaimer must be displayed prominently.

### Prohibition on Deceptive Synthetic Media

Beyond disclosure, the Act prohibits the publication or distribution of synthetic media depicting a candidate that:
- Represents the candidate making statements or engaging in conduct that did not occur; **AND**
- Is designed or intended to cause voters to have a false impression about the candidate's character, qualifications, statements, or actions; **AND**
- Is published or distributed within 90 days of an election.

Prohibited content must be removed from digital platforms and broadcast media within 48 hours of receiving notice from the depicted candidate or their authorized representative.

### Enforcement

**Candidates' Rights:** A candidate depicted in prohibited synthetic media may seek injunctive relief in circuit court to compel removal of the content.

**Civil Liability:** Persons who knowingly publish or distribute prohibited synthetic media are liable to the depicted candidate for damages, including actual damages, legal fees, and punitive damages for willful violations.

**Criminal Penalties:** Willful violations by candidates or their agents constitute a misdemeanor of the first degree.

**Platforms:** Online platforms that receive valid removal notices and fail to remove prohibited synthetic media within 48 hours may be liable for damages.

## Part III: Related Florida AI Provisions (Other 2023-2024 Legislation)

### Children's Online Safety

Florida enacted separate legislation in 2023-2024 addressing children's online safety, including age verification requirements for social media platforms, restrictions on minors' access to social media, and enhanced protections for children's personal data.

### Florida AI in Government

Florida's executive branch has developed AI use policies for state agencies, building on the Digital Bill of Rights framework, that include requirements for transparency, human oversight of AI decision-making in government services, and prohibitions on using AI in ways that violate Florida residents' civil rights.

---

## Interaction with Federal Law

The Florida Digital Bill of Rights does not preempt or supersede federal privacy laws such as HIPAA, COPPA, or GLBA. Where federal law provides equivalent or stronger protections, federal law controls. The Act's provisions that exceed federal requirements (such as the enhanced chatbot disclosure requirements and the biometric data protections) create Florida-specific compliance obligations for covered entities.

The AI in political advertising provisions supplement but do not supersede federal campaign finance law, including FEC regulations on political advertising disclosures.

## Compliance Implications

For businesses subject to the Florida Digital Bill of Rights, key compliance steps include:

1. **Data mapping:** Identifying all personal data collected from Florida consumers and the purposes for which it is processed.
2. **Privacy notice update:** Publishing a comprehensive privacy notice that discloses data processing practices, consumer rights, and how to exercise them.
3. **Consumer request mechanisms:** Establishing accessible mechanisms for consumers to submit rights requests (access, deletion, correction, opt-out).
4. **Consent mechanisms:** Implementing opt-in consent mechanisms for sensitive data processing and targeted advertising to minors.
5. **Chatbot disclosures:** Auditing all consumer-facing chatbots and automated communication systems to ensure they include required disclosures.
6. **Biometric data compliance:** Reviewing facial recognition and voice recognition data collection practices and implementing required consent flows.
7. **Vendor agreements:** Updating data processing agreements with vendors and processors to ensure compliance with the Act's requirements.

For political campaigns and media producers in Florida, compliance with the AI political advertising disclosure requirements requires reviewing all AI-assisted content production workflows and implementing disclosure mechanisms for qualifying content.
