# Abu Dhabi Global Market Data Protection Regulations 2021
**ADGM Board of Directors Resolution No. 3 of 2021**
*Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) Financial Free Zone — In force February 14, 2021*

---

## Overview / Background

The Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) Data Protection Regulations 2021 (DPR 2021) establish a comprehensive GDPR-aligned data protection framework applicable within the ADGM financial free zone on Al Maryah Island in Abu Dhabi. The DPR 2021 replaced the ADGM Data Protection Regulations 2015, introducing a substantially modernized regime reflecting developments in EU data protection law, the emergence of artificial intelligence in financial services, and lessons from the 2015 regulations.

ADGM is an international financial centre and free zone operating under its own legal system (English common law), regulatory framework, and court system, distinct from both the UAE federal legal system and the Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC). The DPR 2021 is administered by the ADGM Registration Authority, in coordination with the ADGM Financial Services Regulatory Authority (FSRA) for financial sector entities.

The DPR 2021 applies to all personal data processing by entities established or operating in ADGM — including financial institutions, law firms, technology companies, and other professional service providers — regardless of where the data subjects are located. It is distinct from the UAE Federal PDPL (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021) which does not apply within the ADGM free zone.

The regulation is structurally and conceptually modeled on the EU GDPR and aligns with its core architecture: lawful bases, data subject rights, controller-processor relationships, DPO requirements, impact assessments, cross-border transfer mechanisms, and enforcement powers with significant financial penalties.

## Key Provisions / Chapters

### Lawful Basis for Processing
- Controllers must identify and document a valid legal basis before processing personal data:
  - Consent: freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous; may be withdrawn at any time
  - Performance of a contract with the data subject
  - Compliance with a legal obligation
  - Protection of vital interests of the data subject or another person
  - Performance of a task carried out in the public interest
  - Legitimate interests of the controller or a third party (subject to balancing test)
- Special categories of sensitive data (health, genetic, biometric, racial/ethnic origin, political opinions, religious beliefs, sexual orientation, criminal records) require explicit consent or a specific enumerated legal basis

### Data Subject Rights
- **Right of access:** Individuals may request and receive a copy of their personal data and processing information within one month (extendable to three months in complex cases).
- **Right to rectification:** Individuals may request correction of inaccurate or incomplete data.
- **Right to erasure:** Individuals may request deletion of data where no longer necessary, consent withdrawn, or data unlawfully processed.
- **Right to restriction of processing:** Individuals may request limitation of processing pending dispute resolution.
- **Right to data portability:** Where processing is based on consent or contract and conducted by automated means, individuals may receive their data in structured, machine-readable format.
- **Right to object:** Individuals may object to processing based on legitimate interests, direct marketing, or research/statistical purposes.
- **Automated decision-making rights:** (see dedicated section below)

### Automated Decision-Making and Profiling
- Data subjects have the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing — including profiling — that produces legal effects on them or significantly affects them.
- Exceptions apply where the automated decision is: (1) necessary for the performance of a contract; (2) authorized by ADGM law; or (3) based on the individual's explicit consent.
- Where decisions are made under an exception, the controller must implement suitable safeguards including: the right to obtain human intervention; the right to express a point of view; and the right to contest the decision.
- These provisions have direct application to AI-driven credit decisions, fraud detection, investment recommendations, and employment screening systems — common use cases in ADGM's financial services environment.

### Data Protection Officer (DPO) Requirement
- Controllers and processors must appoint a DPO where they:
  - Are a public authority or body
  - Carry out large-scale systematic monitoring of data subjects as a core activity
  - Carry out large-scale processing of special category data as a core activity
- The DPO must have expert knowledge of data protection law and practice
- DPO responsibilities include advising on compliance, monitoring adherence to the DPR, cooperating with the ADGM Registration Authority, and serving as a contact point for data subjects

### Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs)
- DPIAs are mandatory before commencing processing likely to result in high risk to individuals, including:
  - Systematic and extensive automated processing of personal data (including AI-driven profiling)
  - Large-scale processing of special categories of data
  - Systematic monitoring of publicly accessible areas
- DPIAs must assess: necessity and proportionality; risks to rights and freedoms; and measures to mitigate risks
- Where a DPIA identifies residual high risk, the controller must consult the ADGM Registration Authority before proceeding

### Cross-Border Data Transfers
- Personal data may be transferred outside ADGM to:
  - Countries or territories with an ADGM adequacy decision
  - Recipients under standard contractual clauses (SCCs) issued by the ADGM Registration Authority
  - Recipients covered by binding corporate rules approved by the Authority
  - Recipients under approved codes of conduct or certification mechanisms
- Derogations for specific situations (consent, contract, legal claims, vital interests) are available but narrowly construed

### Breach Notification
- Controllers must notify the ADGM Registration Authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of a personal data breach that is likely to result in risk to individuals
- Where the breach is likely to result in high risk to individuals, affected data subjects must also be notified without undue delay
- Breach notifications must include: nature of the breach; categories and number of data subjects affected; categories and number of records affected; likely consequences; and measures taken or proposed

### Records of Processing Activities
- Controllers and processors with 250 or more employees (or whose processing is likely to result in risk) must maintain records of processing activities
- Records must be made available to the ADGM Registration Authority on request

## Enforcement & Penalties

- **ADGM Registration Authority:** Primary enforcement body with powers to investigate complaints, conduct audits, issue binding orders, and impose administrative fines
- **ADGM FSRA:** Coordinates enforcement for regulated financial services entities; DPR violations may also trigger FSRA regulatory action
- **Administrative fines — Tier 1:** Up to USD 14 million (or 2% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher) for violations of organizational and technical obligations
- **Administrative fines — Tier 2:** Up to USD 28 million (or 4% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher) for violations of core processing principles, data subject rights, or unlawful cross-border transfers
- **Civil liability:** Data subjects may bring civil claims for material and non-material damages resulting from DPR violations
- **Criminal penalties:** Certain willful violations may be referred for criminal prosecution under ADGM criminal law
- The penalty structure mirrors the GDPR's tiered approach and represents among the most stringent data protection enforcement regimes in the Middle East
