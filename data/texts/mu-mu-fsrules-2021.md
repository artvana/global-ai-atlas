## Summary and Analysis

### Purpose and Scope

The Financial Services (Robotic and AI Enabled Advisory Services) Rules 2021 (the "Robo-Advisory Rules" or "the Rules") were issued by the Financial Services Commission (FSC) of Mauritius pursuant to the Financial Services Act 2007. They came into effect in June 2021 and represent a pioneering regulatory instrument in the African and Indian Ocean region specifically targeting the provision of financial advisory services through automated, algorithmic, and AI-driven systems — commonly known as robo-advisors.

The Rules apply to any holder of a license under the Financial Services Act 2007 who provides or proposes to provide investment advice, financial planning, portfolio management, or other regulated financial advisory services through an automated or AI-enabled system — whether fully automated (robo-advisory) or partially automated (hybrid advisory combining algorithmic outputs with human adviser oversight). The Rules do not apply to the use of algorithmic tools solely for back-office processing, risk management, or regulatory compliance where no direct client-facing advice is generated.

The policy rationale for the Rules is to ensure that the deployment of automation in financial advisory services in Mauritius does not erode the investor protection standards, fiduciary standards, and disclosure obligations that apply to human financial advisers, while also facilitating financial inclusion by enabling lower-cost digital advisory services to reach a broader population. Mauritius, as a major financial services jurisdiction and regional hub for African investment, has positioned itself as an early mover in providing a clear, predictable regulatory framework for fintech and AI-driven financial services.

The FSC is responsible for administering and enforcing the Rules. Licensees operating robo-advisory services must notify the FSC prior to commencing such services and must comply with the Rules on an ongoing basis. The FSC may conduct inspections and issue guidance to supplement the Rules.

### Key Definitions

**Robotic advisory services** (or robo-advisory services) means financial advisory services delivered to clients through an automated digital platform that uses algorithms, decision trees, machine learning models, or other AI techniques to generate investment recommendations, portfolio allocations, or financial plans, without the continuous involvement of a human financial adviser.

**AI-enabled advisory services** means advisory services that use artificial intelligence — including machine learning, natural language processing, and predictive analytics — as a significant component in generating recommendations for clients, whether or not human advisers also review those recommendations.

**Hybrid advisory model** means a model in which automated algorithmic tools generate recommendations that are reviewed and potentially modified or supplemented by a human financial adviser before being communicated to the client.

**Algorithm** means the set of rules, instructions, or mathematical model embedded in the advisory system that processes client data and generates recommendations.

**Significant change to the algorithm** means any modification to the algorithm that would materially affect the nature, risk profile, or outputs of the recommendations generated.

### Disclosure Requirements

Transparency and disclosure are central pillars of the Rules. Licensees operating robo-advisory services must provide clients with clear, complete, and non-misleading disclosures that address the following:

**Nature of the Service.** Clients must be told, before engaging with the service, that advice is being generated by an automated system. The disclosure must be prominent and must not be buried in lengthy terms and conditions. Clients must understand that they are not receiving personalized advice from a human adviser (in the case of fully automated services) or that human involvement is limited to oversight or review (in hybrid models).

**Algorithm Description.** The Rules require that clients receive a plain-language description of how the algorithm works, what inputs it uses (such as risk tolerance questionnaires, market data, or client financial information), and what types of recommendations it generates. While full technical disclosure of proprietary models is not required, clients must have sufficient understanding of the decision-making process to make an informed choice about whether to accept recommendations.

**Limitations of the Service.** Licensees must clearly disclose the limitations of the automated advisory service, including: the types of financial situations or needs the algorithm is not designed to address; the scenarios in which the algorithm may produce suboptimal recommendations; and the circumstances in which clients should seek additional human advice.

**Fees and Conflicts of Interest.** Disclosures must include a clear explanation of all fees, charges, and any conflicts of interest that may affect the recommendations generated, including any arrangements under which the algorithm may favor particular products due to distribution agreements.

**Model Risk.** Clients must be informed that all algorithmic models carry model risk — the risk that the model may produce incorrect or sub-optimal outputs due to flawed assumptions, data quality issues, or changes in market conditions not anticipated by the model.

### Client Suitability Assessment

The Rules impose obligations on licensees to conduct rigorous client suitability assessments before providing robo-advisory services. The suitability assessment must gather sufficient information about the client's: financial situation and objectives; investment experience and knowledge; risk tolerance and capacity for loss; time horizon; and any specific financial needs or constraints. The algorithm must be designed to use this suitability information to generate recommendations that are appropriate for each individual client. Licensees must periodically update client suitability profiles and must ensure that recommendations remain suitable as the client's circumstances change.

Where the algorithm is unable to make a suitable recommendation for a client (for example, where the client's circumstances are too complex or unusual for the algorithm's scope), the licensee must inform the client and, where appropriate, refer the client to a human adviser.

### Human Oversight Requirements

A central requirement of the Rules is the maintenance of meaningful human oversight over AI-enabled advisory services. Licensees must designate a responsible individual — typically a senior compliance or technology officer — who is accountable for the ongoing operation and performance of the robo-advisory system. This responsible person must:

- Understand the algorithm's logic and limitations;
- Oversee the quality and accuracy of outputs on an ongoing basis;
- Ensure that the system is operating within approved parameters;
- Escalate concerns to senior management and the FSC where the system produces anomalous outputs or where model integrity is in question.

In hybrid advisory models, the human adviser who reviews algorithmic outputs must be able to genuinely assess and, if appropriate, override the algorithm's recommendations. The Rules emphasize that human oversight must be substantive rather than merely nominal: a compliance review that rubber-stamps algorithmic outputs without genuine assessment does not satisfy the oversight standard.

Licensees must also establish a client support mechanism whereby clients who have received robo-advisory recommendations can contact a human representative to ask questions, raise concerns, or obtain additional guidance. This requirement ensures that fully automated services do not leave clients without access to human assistance when needed.

### Algorithm Transparency and Change Management

**Algorithm Documentation.** Licensees must maintain comprehensive internal documentation of the algorithm, including its design philosophy, the data inputs used, the model logic (at a level sufficient for internal review and regulatory inspection), the testing methodology, performance metrics, and the version history of the algorithm. This documentation must be made available to the FSC upon request.

**Approval for New Algorithms and Significant Changes.** Before deploying a new algorithm or making a significant change to an existing algorithm, licensees must conduct internal validation and testing and must notify the FSC. The FSC may require additional information or impose conditions before approving deployment of a materially changed algorithm. This prior notification requirement is designed to prevent the deployment of untested or poorly validated models in client-facing contexts.

**Ongoing Monitoring.** Licensees must implement ongoing monitoring of the algorithm's performance, including: regular back-testing of recommendations against actual market outcomes; analysis of client outcomes to identify patterns suggesting systematic bias or error; review of client complaints related to robo-advisory recommendations; and assessment of the algorithm's performance across different client segments to identify potential disparate impacts.

**Bias and Fairness.** The Rules require licensees to take steps to identify and mitigate algorithmic bias — the risk that the algorithm may systematically produce different quality recommendations for clients based on characteristics such as age, gender, wealth level, or other protected attributes. Licensees must document their bias assessment methodology and must take remedial action when bias is identified.

### Fiduciary Duties and Conduct Standards

Licensees operating robo-advisory services remain subject to the full range of conduct of business obligations applicable under their license and under Mauritius financial services law, including fiduciary duties to act in clients' best interests. The automation of advice does not diminish these obligations. Specifically:

- The duty to provide suitable advice applies equally to algorithm-generated recommendations as to human-generated advice.
- The prohibition on misleading or deceptive conduct applies to both the design of the algorithm and to disclosures made to clients about the service.
- Best execution obligations apply to portfolio management systems that implement robo-advisory recommendations by executing trades on behalf of clients.
- Record-keeping obligations require retention of client data inputs, the recommendations generated, and the basis for those recommendations, for a minimum period specified by the FSC.

### Cybersecurity and Data Protection

Given that robo-advisory platforms process significant volumes of sensitive financial data, the Rules require licensees to implement robust cybersecurity measures and to comply with applicable data protection obligations under the Mauritius Data Protection Act 2017. Cybersecurity measures must include: encryption of client data in transit and at rest; access controls limiting employee access to client data on a need-to-know basis; penetration testing of the platform; incident response procedures for data breaches; and business continuity and disaster recovery arrangements.

### Client Rights in Relation to Automated Recommendations

Consistent with Mauritius's obligations under the Data Protection Act 2017, clients receiving AI-generated financial advice have the right to: request an explanation of why a particular recommendation was made; request that a human adviser review the recommendation; object to the use of certain personal data in the algorithm's processing; and withdraw consent for the processing of their personal data for robo-advisory purposes. Licensees must establish accessible procedures for clients to exercise these rights.

### Enforcement and Penalties

The FSC is responsible for enforcing the Rules and has broad supervisory and enforcement powers under the Financial Services Act 2007. The FSC may conduct on-site inspections of licensees' robo-advisory operations, require production of algorithm documentation and testing records, require suspension of a robo-advisory service pending investigation, revoke or impose conditions on a license, and impose civil penalties.

Violations of the Rules may result in: fines imposed by the FSC under the Financial Services Act; suspension or revocation of the relevant financial services license; public censure; and referral to law enforcement authorities for criminal prosecution where fraudulent conduct is involved. The FSC has indicated that it takes a proportionate approach to enforcement, prioritizing supervisory engagement and remediation for first-time or minor violations while applying stricter sanctions for repeated, systemic, or deliberate failures.

### Significance

The Mauritius Robo-Advisory Rules are notable as one of the few pieces of dedicated financial regulatory guidance in Africa and the Indian Ocean region specifically designed for AI-driven financial services. They have been referenced in discussions of best practice in financial technology regulation in COMESA and other regional forums. The Rules demonstrate that effective, principles-based regulation of AI in financial services can be achieved without either prohibiting innovation or abandoning investor protection standards, and provide a useful template for jurisdictions across the region developing their own approaches to the regulation of AI in financial services.
