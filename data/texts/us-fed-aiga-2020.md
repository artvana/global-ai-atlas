# National Artificial Intelligence Initiative Act of 2020

## Full Title and Citation

**Full Title:** National Artificial Intelligence Initiative Act of 2020
**Enacted as:** Division E of the William M. (Mac) Thornberry National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021
**Public Law:** 116-283
**Date of Enactment:** January 1, 2021
**Codified at:** 15 U.S.C. Chapter 119 (§§ 9401–9461) and related provisions
**Short Citation:** NAIIA 2020 or National AI Initiative Act

---

## Overview and Purpose

The National Artificial Intelligence Initiative Act of 2020 (NAIIA) establishes a comprehensive, coordinated federal program to accelerate artificial intelligence research, development, demonstration, and education in the United States. It is the primary federal statute governing the U.S. government's approach to AI research and development investment, interagency coordination, standards development, and workforce preparation.

The NAIIA was enacted following years of bipartisan concern that the United States risked losing its technological leadership in AI to China and other competitors. It built on prior executive action — notably the February 2019 Executive Order 13859, "Maintaining American Leadership in Artificial Intelligence," which established the American AI Initiative — by providing a statutory foundation and congressional authorization for sustained federal investment.

The Act was introduced in the House as H.R. 6216 (116th Congress) and incorporated into Division E of the FY2021 National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA), H.R. 6395, which was enacted over a presidential veto on January 1, 2021. It establishes 15 U.S.C. Chapter 119 as the permanent statutory home for federal AI coordination.

### Statement of Purposes

The Act enumerates the following core purposes of the National Artificial Intelligence Initiative:

1. Ensuring continued U.S. leadership in AI research and development;
2. Leading the world in the development and use of trustworthy AI systems in the public and private sectors;
3. Preparing the present and future U.S. workforce for the integration of AI across all sectors of the economy and society;
4. Coordinating ongoing AI research, development, demonstration, and education activities across all federal agencies;
5. Supporting long-term investment in fundamental AI research and development, including foundational research on AI safety and security;
6. Expanding public-private partnerships to accelerate advances in AI;
7. Developing and maintaining a risk-based approach for the evaluation and governance of AI applications; and
8. Ensuring that AI technologies are developed and deployed in ways that are consistent with U.S. values and national security interests.

## Title I: National AI Initiative and Governance Structure

### Section 5101 — National AI Initiative Office

The Act establishes the **National Artificial Intelligence Initiative Office** (NAIIO) within the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP). The NAIIO serves as the central coordination point for the federal government's AI activities. Its responsibilities include:

- Overseeing and implementing the National AI Initiative;
- Serving as the primary point of contact for coordination between federal agencies, the private sector, academia, nonprofit organizations, professional societies, state and local governments, and appropriate interagency committees;
- Conducting outreach to U.S. and international AI stakeholders on AI policy issues;
- Identifying opportunities to leverage federal investments in AI for economic and national security benefits;
- Transmitting an annual report to Congress on the Initiative's activities, funding, and progress.

### Section 5102 — Interagency Committee

The Act requires the Director of OSTP, acting through the National Science and Technology Council (NSTC), to **establish or designate an Interagency Committee on Artificial Intelligence** to coordinate federal programs and activities in support of the Initiative. Key provisions:

- The Committee shall be co-chaired by the Director of OSTP and, on an annual rotating basis, a representative from one of the following: the Department of Commerce, the National Science Foundation, or the Department of Energy.
- The Committee's membership includes representatives from: NSF, NIST, NIH, DARPA, DOE, DOD, NASA, USDA, DOT, DHS, EPA, and other agencies as appropriate.
- The Committee must meet at least quarterly and must transmit annual reports to Congress identifying federal AI research priorities, investment levels, interagency coordination mechanisms, and progress metrics.
- The Committee is responsible for maintaining a roadmap for federal AI R&D activities.

### Section 5103 — National AI Advisory Committee

The Act establishes the **National Artificial Intelligence Advisory Committee** (NAIAC) — a federal advisory committee composed of external experts that advises the President and the Interagency Committee on AI policy. Key provisions:

- **Membership:** The Secretary of Commerce, in consultation with the OSTP Director, appoints 12–25 members representing diverse expertise across: AI research, AI applications in industry, civil liberties and privacy, economics and labor, national security, education, law and ethics, and other relevant domains. Members serve 2-year terms, renewable once.
- **Qualifications:** Members must be leading experts in their fields, including representation from AI companies of varying sizes, academia, and civil society.
- **Functions:** The NAIAC advises on: the current state of U.S. AI competitiveness; the social, ethical, legal, and economic implications of AI; workforce impacts and education needs; AI and national security; privacy and civil liberties concerns; and the effectiveness of the Initiative.
- **Reporting:** The NAIAC must submit annual reports to the President and Congress with findings and recommendations.
- **AI Safety subcommittee:** The Act requires the establishment of a standing subcommittee on AI safety.
- **Meetings:** The Committee must meet at least twice annually and may hold additional meetings as needed.

### Section 5104 — National Academies AI Workforce Study

The Act directs the Director of OSTP, in consultation with the Secretary of Labor and the Secretary of Education, to contract with the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine to conduct a study on the impact of AI on the U.S. workforce. The study must examine:

- Current and projected job displacement and creation from AI adoption;
- Demographic disparities in AI workforce impacts;
- Current federal workforce development programs and their adequacy;
- Recommendations for new federal investments in AI education, retraining, and workforce development.

## Title II: National AI Research Institutes

### Section 5201 — AI Research Institutes Program

The Act authorizes the Director of the National Science Foundation, in coordination with other agency heads, to establish a competitive grant program to award financial assistance to eligible entities — including universities, research institutions, and public-private partnerships — to establish and support **National Artificial Intelligence Research Institutes**.

Key provisions of the AI Research Institutes program:

- **Eligible recipients:** Public or private institutions of higher education, nonprofit research organizations, and consortia thereof, as well as partnerships with private sector entities.
- **Grant amounts and terms:** Awards are structured as multi-year cooperative agreements, intended to provide sustained support for long-term foundational research. Individual institutes may receive substantial funding to support infrastructure, personnel, and research programs.
- **Focus areas:** The Act identifies research priority areas including AI safety, human-AI interaction, explainability, robustness, privacy-preserving AI, AI for scientific discovery, AI applications in sectors of national importance (healthcare, agriculture, manufacturing, transportation, education, energy), and AI workforce development.
- **Geographic distribution:** The Act requires consideration of geographic diversity in awards, to avoid concentration of AI research capacity in a small number of metropolitan areas.
- **Collaboration requirements:** Institutes must engage in partnerships with industry, non-profits, and other academic institutions, including historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) and minority-serving institutions (MSIs).
- **Technology transfer:** Institutes are expected to facilitate the transfer of research results to commercial applications and to develop training programs for AI researchers and practitioners.

### Section 5202 — NSF-DOE Coordination

The Act requires NSF and DOE to coordinate their AI Research Institute programs to avoid duplication, leverage complementary strengths (NSF's breadth across disciplines; DOE's unique scientific facilities), and maximize the return on federal investment.

## Title III: NIST AI Activities

### Section 5301 — NIST AI Leadership

The Act designates the **National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)** as the lead federal agency for AI standards development and AI risk management frameworks. Key NIST responsibilities under the Act include:

**AI Risk Management Framework:**
- NIST must develop a voluntary risk management framework for trustworthy AI, drawing on input from the private sector, academia, civil society, and other stakeholders.
- The framework must be technology-neutral, sector-agnostic, and applicable across the full AI system lifecycle.
- NIST must update the framework as AI technology evolves.
- (This provision led to the publication of the NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0 in January 2023.)

**Standards and Metrology:**
- NIST must participate in the development of international AI standards through ISO, IEC, IEEE, and other bodies.
- NIST must develop measurements and benchmarks for evaluating AI trustworthiness characteristics including safety, security, robustness, explainability, and fairness.
- NIST must establish a program to assist U.S. AI developers in adopting relevant standards.

**Data Sharing Guidance:**
- NIST must develop guidelines for voluntary sharing of data among private and public entities to facilitate AI research and development, while protecting privacy and intellectual property rights.

**Dataset Best Practices:**
- NIST must develop best practices for the creation, management, and use of datasets used to train and test AI systems, with particular attention to quality, diversity, representativeness, and documentation.

**Coordination:**
- NIST must coordinate its AI standards and framework activities with other federal agencies and with international standards bodies to promote global interoperability.

### Section 5302 — AI Testbeds

The Act authorizes NIST to establish AI testbed infrastructure to support the evaluation of AI systems across a range of applications and to facilitate technology transfer from research to practice.

## Title IV: National Science Foundation AI Activities

### Section 5401 — NSF AI Research and Education

The Act establishes a comprehensive NSF program for AI research and education. Key elements include:

**Fundamental Research:**
- NSF must sustain and strengthen its support for fundamental AI research across all relevant disciplines, including computer science, mathematics, statistics, cognitive science, social science, and engineering.
- Priority research areas include: machine learning theory and algorithms, computer vision, natural language processing, robotics, planning and reasoning, AI safety and robustness, privacy-preserving AI, and human-AI interaction.

**Interdisciplinary Research:**
- NSF must support interdisciplinary AI research that integrates technical AI development with insights from social sciences, humanities, ethics, law, and other fields.

**Education:**
- NSF must support AI education programs at all levels, including K-12, community colleges, undergraduate, graduate, and continuing education.
- Programs must specifically target underrepresented groups to diversify the AI workforce.
- NSF must support the development of AI curricula, educational tools, and faculty development programs.

**Graduate Fellowships:**
- The Act authorizes NSF to increase the number of Graduate Research Fellowships in AI and related fields.

### Section 5402 — National AI Research Resource

The Act directs the Director of NSF and the Director of OSTP to jointly establish a **National AI Research Resource (NAIRR) Task Force** to develop a concept for a shared computing and data infrastructure that would provide researchers at universities and other institutions with access to the computational resources, high-quality datasets, and AI tools needed to conduct AI research. The Task Force must:

- Assess the current and future needs of the U.S. AI research community for computational resources and datasets;
- Develop a roadmap for establishing a national AI research resource;
- Identify governance models, funding mechanisms, and access policies;
- Report to Congress with findings and recommendations.

(This Task Force provision led to the establishment of the NAIRR Task Force, which reported in January 2023, and subsequently to the pilot NAIRR program launched in 2024.)

## Title V: Department of Energy AI Activities

### Section 5501 — DOE AI Research Program

The Act establishes a dedicated AI research program at the Department of Energy, reflecting DOE's unique assets in high-performance computing, large-scale scientific facilities, and national laboratories. Key elements:

- The Secretary of Energy must carry out a cross-cutting AI research and development program to advance AI for DOE missions, including energy security, scientific discovery, environmental management, and national security.
- The program must leverage DOE's national laboratories (Argonne, Brookhaven, Lawrence Berkeley, Oak Ridge, Pacific Northwest, etc.) as national resources for AI research.
- DOE must develop AI-specific computing infrastructure and tools optimized for scientific AI applications.
- DOE must support the development of AI applications for energy systems, including smart grids, energy efficiency, and clean energy technologies.
- DOE must participate in interagency coordination on AI research through the NSTC Interagency Committee.

### Section 5502 — AI for Scientific Discovery

The Act specifically directs DOE to invest in AI applications for scientific discovery, including AI methods for materials science, climate modeling, genomics, particle physics, and other DOE science mission areas. DOE must ensure that AI-generated scientific insights are validated against experimental evidence.

## Definitions (15 U.S.C. § 9401)

The Act establishes the following key definitions:

- **Artificial intelligence:** Any artificial system that performs tasks under varying and unpredictable circumstances without significant human oversight, or that can learn from experience and improve performance, or that uses a rule-based system, search, optimization, natural language processing, signal processing, computer vision, or any combination of these approaches.

- **Machine learning:** An application of AI that provides systems the ability to automatically learn and improve from experience without being explicitly programmed.

- **Initiative:** The National Artificial Intelligence Initiative established by the Act.

- **Initiative Office:** The National Artificial Intelligence Initiative Office established within OSTP.

- **Interagency Committee:** The Interagency Committee on Artificial Intelligence established or designated under the Act.

---

## Appropriations and Funding

The Act authorizes (but does not appropriate) funding for the Initiative. Authorization levels include multi-year authorizations for NSF, NIST, DOE, and other participating agencies. Actual funding depends on annual appropriations acts. In the years following enactment, Congress appropriated significant increases in federal AI R&D spending, with the total federal AI R&D budget exceeding several billion dollars annually.

## Implementation Highlights

Key actions taken under the NAIIA since enactment:

- **NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0** (January 2023): Published by NIST pursuant to its mandate under the Act, providing voluntary guidance for organizations developing and deploying AI systems.
- **NAIRR Task Force Final Report** (January 2023): Recommending creation of a national shared AI research infrastructure.
- **NAIRR Pilot** (2024): Launched as a proof-of-concept for the National AI Research Resource.
- **National AI Research Institutes:** NSF, in partnership with DOE and USDA, has awarded funding for more than 25 National AI Research Institutes across 40+ states.
- **NAIAC Reports:** The NAIAC has issued multiple annual reports with recommendations to the President and Congress on AI policy.

## Relationship to Executive Actions

The NAIIA operates alongside and reinforces a series of executive actions on AI, including:

- **Executive Order 13859** (February 2019): American AI Initiative — preceded the NAIIA and established initial interagency coordination.
- **Executive Order 14110** (October 2023): "Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy Development and Use of Artificial Intelligence" — significantly expanded federal AI policy requirements, building on the foundation established by the NAIIA.
- **National AI Strategy** and associated implementation plans developed by OSTP.

The NAIIA's institutional infrastructure — particularly the NAIIO, Interagency Committee, and NAIAC — provided the organizational capacity to implement subsequent executive AI policy directives.
