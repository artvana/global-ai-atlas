# Alabama House Bill 172 — Prohibition on Materially Deceptive AI-Generated Media in Elections

**HB 172 | Alabama Legislature, 2024 Regular Session**
*Alabama — Enacted April 24, 2024 | Effective October 1, 2024*

---

## Overview

Alabama House Bill 172 makes it a criminal offense to knowingly distribute materially deceptive AI-generated media in connection with an election campaign within 90 days before an election, with intent to influence the outcome of that election. The law requires that any AI-generated media used in political advertising and communications carry a prominent disclosure identifying it as AI-generated content. Alabama was among the first Southern states to enact criminal penalties specifically targeting AI deepfakes in the electoral context.

The law distinguishes between the general use of AI in political communications (which remains lawful with disclosure) and the distribution of materially deceptive AI-generated content without disclosure or with intent to deceive (which is criminalized). First-time violations are classified as Class A misdemeanors; repeat violations within a five-year period are elevated to Class C felonies.

## Key Provisions

### Prohibition on Materially Deceptive AI-Generated Media

- Makes it a criminal offense to distribute, with intent to influence an election, any AI-generated media that materially deceives a reasonable viewer as to a candidate's or public official's statements, conduct, or appearance.
- Applies within 90 days before any primary, runoff, general, or special election in Alabama.
- "Materially deceptive" means the AI-generated content would likely cause a reasonable viewer to have a false impression about a candidate's or official's actual statements, positions, or conduct.

### Required Disclosure

- Any AI-generated content used in political advertising or political communications must include a prominent, clearly legible or audible disclosure stating that the content was generated or substantially altered using artificial intelligence.
- Disclosure must be visible or audible to a reasonable person and placed so as to be noticed prior to or during engagement with the content.
- Failure to include required disclosure is an element of the criminal offense.

### Criminal Penalties

- First violation: Class A misdemeanor — punishable by up to one year in county jail and/or a fine up to $6,000.
- Subsequent violation within five years of the first: Class C felony — punishable by one to ten years in state prison.
- Prosecution may be initiated by the Alabama Attorney General or by local district attorneys.

### Exemptions

- Satire and parody are explicitly exempted, provided the content is clearly presented as satirical or comedic and not reasonably likely to be mistaken for factual reporting.
- News organizations reporting on AI-generated deepfakes as a matter of public interest are not subject to liability for that reporting.
- Political advertising that uses AI-generated content with proper disclosure does not violate the prohibition on materially deceptive media.

### Enforcement

- Alabama Attorney General has primary enforcement authority.
- District Attorneys may independently prosecute violations occurring within their jurisdictions.
- No private right of action — enforcement is exclusively governmental.
