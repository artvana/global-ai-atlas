Israel National Policy on Artificial Intelligence — Responsible Innovation
Ministry of Innovation, Science and Technology / Ministry of Justice
ISRAEL | 2023

Source: https://www.gov.il/en/pages/ai_2023

Non-binding national AI policy published in 2023, jointly developed by Israel's Ministry of Innovation, Science and Technology and the Ministry of Justice. The policy was developed following public consultation and inter-ministerial coordination. It establishes Israel's approach to AI governance, favoring a sectoral, risk-based, and innovation-friendly regulatory framework over comprehensive binding AI legislation.

Key Elements:

1. Regulatory Philosophy
   Israel's AI policy explicitly rejects a comprehensive AI law in favor of a risk-based, sectoral approach. The government prefers to adapt existing regulatory frameworks to address AI risks sector by sector. The policy emphasizes maintaining Israel's competitive advantage as a technology innovator.

2. Seven Key Challenge Areas
   The policy identifies seven principal ethical and governance challenges posed by AI:
   - Discrimination and bias: AI systems must not produce discriminatory outcomes
   - Human oversight: Meaningful human control must be maintained over consequential AI decisions
   - Explainability: AI systems should be able to explain their outputs and decision processes
   - Disclosure: Users should be informed when they are interacting with AI systems
   - Safety: AI systems must meet appropriate safety standards before deployment
   - Accountability: Clear responsibility frameworks must exist for AI harms
   - Privacy: AI systems must comply with Israeli privacy law and data protection requirements

3. Regulatory Tools
   The policy favors soft regulatory instruments including:
   - Regulatory sandboxes for AI innovation
   - Voluntary codes of conduct and ethics guidelines
   - Industry self-regulation with government oversight
   - Sector-specific guidance from existing regulators
   - Guidance and clarifications on how existing laws apply to AI

4. Government AI Use
   The policy includes provisions on responsible AI use by government agencies, including requirements for transparency, accountability, and impact assessment for government AI systems.

5. International Coordination
   Israel commits to coordinating its AI governance approach with OECD AI Principles, G7 Hiroshima AI Process, and other international frameworks. Israel is an active participant in OECD AI policy development.

6. Data Governance
   The policy addresses the intersection of AI and Israel's Privacy Protection Law, clarifying how existing data protection requirements apply to AI systems and identifying areas requiring legislative update.

7. Research and Innovation
   The policy supports investment in AI research through the Israel Innovation Authority and academic partnerships to strengthen Israel's position as an AI hub.
