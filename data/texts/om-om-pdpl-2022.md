# Personal Data Protection Law — Royal Decree No. 6/2022
**Royal Decree No. 6/2022**
*Sultanate of Oman — Enacted February 9, 2022; Effective February 9, 2023*

---

## Overview / Background

The Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL), promulgated by Royal Decree No. 6/2022, is the Sultanate of Oman's first comprehensive personal data protection statute. Enacted on February 9, 2022, and entering into force one year later on February 9, 2023 (following a mandatory transition period), the PDPL establishes the legal framework for the protection of personal data in Oman and is administered by the Information Technology Authority (ITA).

Oman's PDPL reflects the influence of regional and international data protection frameworks, including Bahrain's PDPL 2018 and elements of the EU GDPR. Its enactment positions Oman as a credible digital economy participant in the Gulf Cooperation Council and enables cross-border data flows with jurisdictions requiring equivalent protection standards. The PDPL is supplemented by implementing regulations issued by the ITA in 2023.

The PDPL applies to any person (natural or legal, public or private sector) that processes personal data within the Sultanate of Oman, or that processes personal data of Omani residents outside Oman. The ITA is empowered to investigate violations, issue guidance and orders, and impose administrative penalties. Implementing regulations issued in 2023 provide procedural detail on consent requirements, data subject rights, cross-border transfers, and the DPO requirement.

## Key Provisions / Chapters

### Core Processing Principles
- **Lawfulness:** Personal data must be processed on a valid legal basis; processing without legal basis is prohibited.
- **Purpose limitation:** Data may only be collected for specific, explicit, and legitimate purposes and may not be processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes.
- **Data minimization:** Only data that is adequate, relevant, and necessary for the stated purpose may be collected.
- **Accuracy:** Controllers must take reasonable steps to ensure data is accurate and up to date.
- **Retention limitation:** Data must not be kept in identifiable form longer than necessary for its processing purpose.
- **Integrity and confidentiality:** Appropriate security measures must protect personal data against unauthorized processing, accidental loss, destruction, or damage.

### Lawful Bases for Processing
- Processing of personal data requires one of the following legal bases:
  - Consent of the data subject (freely given, informed, specific, and unambiguous)
  - Performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party
  - Compliance with a legal obligation
  - Protection of vital interests of the data subject or another person
  - Performance of a task in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority
  - Legitimate interests of the controller or third party (with balancing test)

### Special Categories of Sensitive Data
- The PDPL designates the following as sensitive data requiring heightened protection:
  - Health and medical data
  - Genetic data
  - Biometric data used for identification purposes
  - Racial or ethnic origin
  - Religious or philosophical beliefs
  - Criminal convictions and offenses
  - Financial data
- Processing of sensitive data requires explicit consent or falls within specific statutory exceptions (e.g., vital interests, legal proceedings, healthcare)
- Biometric identification systems deployed in public or employment contexts require specific authorization

### Data Subject Rights
- **Right of access:** Data subjects may request confirmation of whether their data is held and receive a copy of it, along with information about processing.
- **Right to correction:** Data subjects may request correction of inaccurate or incomplete data; controllers must respond within 30 days.
- **Right to deletion:** Data subjects may request erasure of personal data in defined circumstances (no longer necessary; consent withdrawn; unlawfully processed).
- **Right to restriction:** Data subjects may request limitation of processing pending resolution of disputes about accuracy or lawfulness.
- **Right to object:** Data subjects may object to processing of their data for direct marketing purposes, or on grounds relating to their specific situation.
- **Right to withdraw consent:** Consent may be withdrawn at any time; withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of prior processing based on consent.

### Data Protection Officer (DPO)
- Controllers and processors that conduct large-scale processing of personal data, large-scale processing of sensitive data, or systematic monitoring of data subjects must appoint a Data Protection Officer.
- The DPO must possess expert knowledge of data protection law and practice.
- The DPO is responsible for: advising the controller on PDPL obligations; monitoring compliance; conducting internal audits; cooperating with the ITA; and serving as a contact point for data subjects.

### Mandatory Data Breach Notification
- Controllers must notify the ITA of personal data breaches without undue delay and within a period specified by implementing regulations.
- Where a breach is likely to result in significant harm to data subjects, affected individuals must also be notified.
- Breach notifications must include: description of the breach; categories and approximate number of data subjects affected; categories and number of records affected; likely consequences; and remedial measures.

### Cross-Border Transfer Restrictions
- Personal data may not be transferred outside Oman unless:
  - The destination country or territory provides equivalent data protection (ITA adequacy determination)
  - Appropriate safeguards are in place (standard contractual clauses, binding corporate rules, approved codes of conduct)
  - A specific derogation applies (consent, contract performance, vital interests, legal claims)
- The ITA may maintain and publish lists of adequate jurisdictions and approved safeguard mechanisms

### Implementing Regulations 2023
- ITA implementing regulations issued in 2023 provide detailed procedures for:
  - Registration of processing activities with the ITA
  - Consent collection and record-keeping
  - Data breach notification timelines (72 hours to ITA; without undue delay to individuals)
  - Cross-border transfer approval processes
  - DPO appointment and registration

## Enforcement & Penalties

- **Administrative fines:** The ITA may impose administrative fines of up to OMR 500,000 (approximately $1.3 million USD) for violations of the PDPL.
- **Criminal penalties:** Intentional violations of the PDPL — particularly involving sensitive data, fraudulent processing, or obstruction of ITA investigations — may result in criminal prosecution with imprisonment and additional fines.
- **ITA enforcement powers:** The ITA may issue corrective orders, require suspension of processing activities, and publish enforcement decisions.
- **Civil liability:** Data subjects may seek compensation for material and non-material damages through Omani courts.
- **Aggravated penalties:** Violations involving health data, biometric data, or data of minors attract enhanced penalties.
- **Sector coordination:** The ITA coordinates with sector regulators in telecommunications (TRA), financial services (CMA), and healthcare for consistent enforcement.
