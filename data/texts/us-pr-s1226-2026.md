                    GOVERNMENT OF PUERTO RICO
20th Legislative Assembly                                                              3rd Regular Session

                     SENATE OF PUERTO RICO

                              Senate Bill No. 1226
                                   May 5, 2026

                        Presented by Ms. Moran Trinidad

                                        Referred to

                                           ACT

To establish the "Act of Transparency and Supervision in the Use of Artificial
    Intelligence in Insurance Claims"; to amend Article 1.120 and add a
    new Chapter 55 to Act No. 77 of June 19, 1957, as amended,
    known as the "Insurance Code of Puerto Rico," for the purpose of instituting the public
    policy that shall govern in order to safeguard the fundamental rights of citizens,
    prevent arbitrary or unjust decisions, mitigate the risks inherent to the use of
    artificial intelligence and to algorithmic biases, and promote transparency,
    accountability, and public confidence in the insurance system of Puerto Rico,
    during the claim processes of insurance consumers; and for other
    related purposes.

                              STATEMENT OF MOTIVES

     The Constitution of Puerto Rico establishes in its Article II, Section 7, that no
person shall be deprived of his property without due process of law. In that context, an
insurance claim constitutes a legitimate property interest, to the extent that
it involves economic benefits, access to medical services, and compensations that
directly impact the financial stability, health, and welfare of
citizens. Likewise, Article II, Section 1, of the Constitution provides that the
dignity of the human being is inviolable and that all persons are equal before the law.
These fundamental guarantees impose on the State the unavoidable duty to ensure that
                                            2

every determination affecting substantial rights of individuals be adopted
through fair, reasonable, non-arbitrary processes free of discrimination.
     The insurance industry fulfills an essential function in the economic, medical,
and social protection of persons in Puerto Rico. Through insurance, citizens
seek protection against losses, accidents, illnesses, property damage,
disabilities, medical emergencies, and other events that may directly affect their
stability, health, patrimony, and quality of life. Therefore, the evaluation of an
insurance claim is not a mere administrative procedure, but a process that may
substantially impact the rights, benefits, and fundamental needs of a
person or family.
     In recent years, the accelerated development of automated technologies, including
algorithms, artificial intelligence systems, and machine learning mechanisms,
has transformed the way different industries process information and make
decisions. The insurance industry has not been the exception. These tools may
be useful to streamline processes, organize data, identify patterns, and assist in the
administration of claims. However, their use without adequate
safeguards can generate serious risks for consumers, particularly when
such technologies are used as the exclusive basis for denying, reducing, or
limiting claims.
     An adverse determination on an insurance claim may affect a person's
access to medical treatments, health services, economic compensation,
repair of damages, or benefits necessary for his or her sustenance. When such determination
is produced exclusively through an automated system, without the intervention of a
qualified human professional, there is a risk that the claimant may be subjected to a
decision that is opaque, impersonal, decontextualized, or based on criteria that do
not necessarily reflect the particular circumstances of his or her case.
     An insurance claim may constitute a legitimate property interest, to the extent
that it involves money, services, contractual compensations, medical
benefits, or indemnifications to which a person may be entitled in accordance with a
                                            3

policy or insurance contract. Therefore, any determination that reduces, limits, or
denies such benefits must be subject to a fair, transparent, verifiable,
and reasoned process.
     The exclusive use of artificial intelligence or automated systems in the
adjudication of claims raises particular concerns. These systems
may operate with incomplete data, undisclosed criteria, models difficult to
explain, or historical patterns that reproduce existing inequalities. Likewise,
they may generate incorrect, inconsistent, or unjust results when the
medical, economic, social, or contractual particularities of each
claimant are not adequately considered.
     This Legislative Assembly recognizes that technological innovation must not be
rejected, but it must be regulated responsibly when it impacts fundamental
rights. Administrative efficiency cannot prevail over human dignity,
transparency, due process, or equality before the law. Technology must
serve as a support tool, not as an absolute substitute for human professional
judgment in decisions that affect substantial rights.
     For this reason, this Act establishes minimum safeguards for the use of algorithms,
artificial intelligence, and machine learning systems in the handling of insurance
claims. In particular, it provides that no insurer, health services
organization, or regulated entity may use such systems as the sole basis to
deny in whole or in part a claim, reduce a payment, or limit benefits. Every
adverse determination shall be reviewed and adopted by a qualified human
professional, who shall have the duty to evaluate the pertinent information, consider the
individual circumstances of the claimant, and base the decision in a
reasoned manner.
     In addition, this Act promotes transparency through the obligation to maintain
records, document the use of automated systems, and disclose relevant information
in communications denying or reducing claims. These measures
will allow more effective oversight by regulatory agencies and
                                                 4

    will provide claimants with greater clarity about the reasons for an
    adverse determination.
         The present legislation also seeks to prevent arbitrary, inconsistent,
    or discriminatory decisions, as well as to mitigate the risk of algorithmic biases. By
    requiring mandatory human supervision, adequate documentation, and oversight
    mechanisms, public confidence in the insurance system is strengthened and it is ensured that
    technological modernization occurs within applicable constitutional and legal
    limits.
         Consequently, this Legislative Assembly declares as the public policy of the
    Government of Puerto Rico that the use of automated technologies in the insurance
    industry must be subordinated to the principles of human dignity, equality, due
    process, transparency, reasonableness, and responsibility. As well as to the rights and
    contractual obligations of the insured and the insurer. No citizen should
    lose medical benefits, economic compensation, or contractual protection due to a
    decision made exclusively by a machine.
         For all the foregoing, it is necessary to approve this Act, aimed at ensuring that
    every adverse determination on an insurance claim be subject to effective
    human review, that insurers be accountable for the use of automated
    technologies, and that claimants receive fair, dignified, and lawful treatment.

16      Section 3.- A new Chapter 55 is added to Act No. 77 of June 19, 1957,

17   as amended, to read as follows:

18             "CHAPTER 55.- TRANSPARENCY AND SUPERVISION IN THE USE OF

19           ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE IN INSURANCE CLAIMS

20      Article 55.010. — Purpose.

21      This Chapter shall be known as the "Act of Transparency and Supervision in the Use of

22   Artificial Intelligence in Insurance Claims" and its purpose is to ensure that the use of
                                                       6

 1   automated technologies in the insurance industry is subject to clear limits, mechanisms of

 2   effective supervision, and criteria of human responsibility.

 3      Article 55.020.- Declaration of Public Policy

 4      It is the public policy of Puerto Rico to ensure that every adverse determination in the

 5   processing of insurance claims is made in a manner that is fair, transparent, and

 6   grounded in the independent judgment of a qualified human professional.

 7      The Legislative Assembly recognizes that the insurance industry has incorporated the use of

 8   automated technologies, including algorithms, artificial intelligence systems, and machine

 9   learning, to assist in the evaluation and processing of claims. Although such

10   tools may contribute to administrative efficiency, their use without adequate controls

11   poses significant risks, such as opaque decision-making, the reproduction of algorithmic

12   biases, the absence of individualized criteria, and the dehumanization of processes that

13   impact fundamental rights.

14      The Legislative Assembly recognizes that exclusively delegating to automated systems

15   decisions related to the denial, reduction, or limitation of insurance claims

16   may result in arbitrary, inconsistent, or discriminatory actions, in contravention of

17   the constitutional principles of due process of law, equality before the law, the protection of

18   human dignity, and the rights and obligations established. Consequently, it is the public policy

19   of Puerto Rico to ensure that the use of automated technologies in the insurance industry is

20   subject to clear limits, mechanisms of effective supervision, and criteria of human responsibility.

21   To such effects, it is established that no adverse determination to a claimant, including the

22   total or partial denial of a claim, or the reduction of benefits, may be based
                                                      7

 1   exclusively on automated systems, requiring the intervention of a qualified human

 2   professional who shall carry out an integral, reasoned evaluation in accordance with law.

 3      This public policy has the purpose of safeguarding the fundamental rights of

 4   citizens, preventing arbitrary or unjust decisions, mitigating the risks inherent to algorithmic

 5   biases, and promoting transparency, accountability, and public confidence in the

 6   insurance system. Likewise, it seeks to harmonize the use of emerging technologies with the

 7   constitutional principles that govern our legal system, ensuring that

 8   technological innovation does not impair the essential guarantees of individuals.

 9      In sum, the State reaffirms its commitment that every person who presents an insurance claim

10   be treated with dignity, equity, and justice, and that every determination that affects his

11   rights be subject to effective human review and to the highest standards of legality and

12   reasonableness.

13      Article 55.030.- Definitions

14      For purposes of this Chapter, the terms included below shall have the following

15   meaning:

16      (a) Algorithm: Structured mathematical process designed to generate results through

17   predefined rules.

18      (b) Artificial intelligence system: Automated system capable of generating predictions,

19   recommendations, or decisions based on data.

20      (c) Machine learning system: Subcategory of artificial intelligence that allows the

21   system to learn from data without explicit programming.
                                                     8

 1      (d) Qualified human professional: Person authorized under the Insurance Code of Puerto

 2   Rico to adjust, evaluate, or deny claims.

 3      Article 55.040.- Permitted Use

 4      Insurers, as defined in this Code, may use algorithms or systems of

 5   artificial intelligence solely as support tools in the processing of

 6   claims.

 7      Article 55.050.- Applicability

 8      This Act shall apply to anyone engaged in insurance contracting as defined in Article

 9   1.050 of this Code. Without limiting the general meaning of the foregoing definition, a reciprocal

10   insurance association, a mutual association, a health services organization, or a group of

11   any kind, organized for pecuniary purposes or without them, engaged in the business of granting

12   insurance contracts, is an "insurer."

13      Article 55.060.- Prohibitions

14      No insurer or entity subject to this Code may:

15      (a) Deny, totally or partially, a claim or reduce the payment thereof based

16   exclusively or predominantly on algorithms, artificial intelligence systems, or machine

17   learning systems, without the significant intervention of a qualified human professional.

18      (b) Delegate the making of final decisions on claims to automated systems without

19   carrying out an independent, documented, and reasoned human review.

20      (c) Issue automated decisions without verifying the accuracy, reliability, and pertinence of

21   the data used by such systems.
                                                     9

 1      (d) Omit, alter, or conceal information about the participation of automated systems in

 2   the claim evaluation process.

 3      (e) Refuse to provide the insured with a clear, specific, and substantiated explanation about the

 4   reasons for the denial or reduction of the claim.

 5      (f) Limit, hinder, or condition the right of the insured to request an independent human review

 6   pursuant to this article.

 7      (g) Take retaliation, directly or indirectly, against an insured for exercising his rights

 8   under this Act, including the request for human review or the challenging of a decision.

 9      (h) Maintain internal practices or policies that incentivize or promote the automated

10   denial of claims without due human analysis.

11      (i) Fail to comply with the obligation to maintain complete, truthful, and accessible records on the

12   decisions made pursuant to this Chapter.

13      (j) Use automated systems whose criteria, parameters, or operation cannot

14   be reasonably explained by the insurer or validated by a qualified human

15   professional.

16      Article 55.070.- Mandatory Review

17      Before issuing a denial or reduction, the qualified human professional shall:

18      (a) Independently analyze the facts and the applicable policy.

19      (b) Evaluate the accuracy of any automated recommendation.

20      (c) Determine, by his or her own judgment, the appropriateness of the denial or reduction.

21      Article 55.080.- Mandatory Record
                                                     10

1       Insurers shall maintain detailed records that include: identification of the

2    professional who made the decision, date and time of the determination, justification of the decision, and

3    participation of automated systems in the analysis.

4       Article 55.090.- Right of the Insured to a Mandatory Human Review

5       Every insured shall have the right to:

6       (a) Request a review by an independent human being of any denial or reduction

7    of a claim.

8       (b) Receive said review within a term of no more than fifteen (15) calendar days from

9    submission of the request.

10      (c) Obtain a detailed, clear explanation grounded in law and facts.

11      (d) No insurer may limit, waive, or condition this right.

12      Article 55.100.- Penalties

13      In addition to the denial, revocation, or suspension of the license or in lieu thereof, any

14   insurer that violates the provisions of this Chapter may be subject to any

15   of the following sanctions:

16      (a) Denial of the license in any capacity authorized under this Code for

17   a term not to exceed five (5) years.

18      (b) Administrative fines of no less than $5,000 nor more than $25,000 for each violation.

19      (c) Any of said penalties may be imposed in substitution of or in conjunction with

20   any other provided by Law.

21      Article 55.110.- Penalties Right to Private Action

22      Every insured or insurance consumer affected by a violation of this Chapter may:
                                                     11

 1       (a) Bring a civil action against an insurer if having suffered damages as a consequence of

 2   violations of this Chapter.

 3       (b) Claim actual damages, statutory damages, and attorney's fees.

 4       (c) Request equitable remedies, including the review of the claim.

 5       This right shall be independent of any administrative action before the Office of

 6   the Insurance Commissioner of Puerto Rico.

 7       Article 55.120.- Presumption of Arbitrariness

 8       A denial or reduction of a claim shall be presumed arbitrary and capricious when:

 9       (a) The insurer cannot demonstrate independent human review; or

10       (b) Adequate records do not exist in accordance with this Chapter; or

11       (c) The decision is based predominantly on automated systems without documented

12   human validation.

13       This presumption shall be rebuttable, but it shall impose the burden of proof on the insurer.

14       Article 55.130. - Notification to the Insured

15       Every denial communication shall include:

16       (a) Contact information of the responsible professional.

17       (b) Express statement that the decision was not made exclusively by artificial

18   intelligence.

19       Article 55.140. – Manuals of the Insurers

20       Insurers shall document in their internal manuals the manner in which they use

21   automated systems and the procedures they use to ensure compliance with this

22   Chapter.

 1      Article 55.150.- Power of Regulation

 2      The Insurance Commissioner shall adopt the regulations necessary to ensure the full

 3   implementation of the provisions of this Chapter."
