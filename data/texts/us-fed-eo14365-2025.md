# Executive Order on Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence

**Executive Order 14365**
*United States (Federal) — December 11, 2025*

---

## Overview

Executive Order 14365, signed by President Trump on December 11, 2025, directs federal agencies to establish a unified national policy framework for artificial intelligence with an explicit goal of preempting conflicting state AI regulations. The order reflects the Trump administration's view that a fragmented patchwork of state AI laws threatens American AI competitiveness and innovation, and that a consistent federal approach is necessary to allow AI development to flourish.

The order directs the FTC to issue a policy statement asserting federal preemption of state AI laws that conflict with federal policy, creates a DOJ AI Litigation Task Force to challenge such state laws in court, directs the FCC to consider a national AI content disclosure standard, and conditions certain categories of federal funding on states not enacting laws deemed to conflict with national AI development priorities. The order includes narrow carve-outs for state laws protecting children's safety and state AI procurement rules governing state agencies.

## Key Provisions

### FTC Preemption Policy Statement

- Directs the Federal Trade Commission to issue a policy statement, within 120 days, asserting that certain state AI laws are preempted by federal consumer protection law or constitute unlawful barriers to interstate commerce.
- The statement is intended to establish the administration's position on the scope of federal preemption applicable to state AI regulations that impose disclosure, risk assessment, or liability requirements on AI developers and deployers.
- The FTC statement is a policy document, not a binding legal ruling; preemption determinations require judicial enforcement.

### DOJ AI Litigation Task Force

- Creates within the Department of Justice a dedicated AI Litigation Task Force charged with identifying, challenging, and seeking to enjoin state AI laws that conflict with federal AI policy.
- The Task Force is directed to prioritize states with the most restrictive AI regulations and to file suit where legal grounds for preemption or Commerce Clause challenge exist.
- Coordinates with the FTC preemption statement to provide legal basis for litigation.

### FCC AI Content Disclosure Standard

- Directs the Federal Communications Commission to initiate a rulemaking proceeding within 90 days to consider a uniform federal standard for disclosure of AI-generated content in broadcasting, telecommunications, and related media.
- Intended to supersede a growing number of state-level AI content disclosure requirements (such as those enacted in California, Colorado, and other states).
- FCC rulemaking is not final AI policy; it initiates a notice-and-comment process.

### Federal Funding Conditions

- Conditions certain categories of federal grants, research funding, and technology assistance to states on states not enacting or enforcing AI laws that the OMB Director determines conflict with national AI development priorities.
- Carve-outs: states may enact laws specifically protecting minors from AI-generated harmful content, and states may govern their own AI procurement practices without losing federal funding eligibility.

### OMB Coordination

- Directs the Office of Management and Budget to issue an updated federal AI governance framework within 180 days applicable to federal agency AI acquisition and use.
- Requires federal agencies to inventory AI use cases and identify regulatory barriers to AI adoption in agency operations.

### Scope and Limitations

- The order applies to state laws regulating private-sector AI development and deployment; it does not restrict state government regulation of its own AI use.
- Carve-outs for children's safety laws reflect political consensus that child protection remains a state prerogative.
- The legal basis for federal preemption of state AI laws is contested; the order anticipates litigation.
