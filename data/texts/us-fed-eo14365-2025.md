# Executive Order 14365
## Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence

**December 11, 2025 — President Donald J. Trump**

*Federal Register Doc. 2025-23092, published December 16, 2025*

---

## Section 1: Purpose

The order establishes that U.S. AI leadership serves national and economic security interests. It references Executive Order 14179 from January 23, 2025, which removed barriers to American AI advancement. The administration has updated federal regulatory frameworks to encourage AI adoption across sectors, generating substantial investments.

The order identifies three key problems with state-level AI regulation:

1. Patchwork regulation across 50 states creates compliance burdens for startups and businesses operating nationally.
2. State laws increasingly require ideological bias in AI models (citing Colorado's algorithmic discrimination ban as an example).
3. Some state laws improperly regulate interstate commerce.

The stated goal is establishing "a minimally burdensome national standard — not 50 discordant State ones" while protecting children, preventing censorship, respecting copyrights, and safeguarding communities.

---

## Section 2: Policy

"It is the policy of the United States to sustain and enhance the United States' global AI dominance through a minimally burdensome national policy framework for AI."

---

## Section 3: AI Litigation Task Force

Within 30 days, the Attorney General must establish an AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state AI laws inconsistent with the order's policy. The Task Force will target laws that:

- Unconstitutionally regulate interstate commerce
- Are preempted by existing federal regulations
- Are otherwise unlawful

The Task Force coordinates with the Special Advisor for AI and Crypto and other senior officials.

---

## Section 4: Evaluation of State AI Laws

Within 90 days, the Secretary of Commerce must publish an evaluation of state AI laws identifying those that:

- Conflict with the policy in Section 2
- "require AI models to alter their truthful outputs" or compel disclosure violating the First Amendment
- Should be referred to the Task Force

The evaluation may also identify state laws promoting AI innovation.

---

## Section 5: Restrictions on State Funding

**(a)** Within 90 days, the Secretary of Commerce must issue a Policy Notice specifying conditions for state eligibility under the Broadband Equity Access and Deployment (BEAD) Program. States with onerous AI laws identified in Section 4 become ineligible for non-deployment funds "to the maximum extent allowed by Federal law."

**(b)** Executive agencies must assess discretionary grant programs and determine whether to condition grants on states either:

- Not enacting conflicting AI laws, or
- Entering binding agreements not to enforce existing conflicting AI laws during the funding period

---

## Section 6: Federal Reporting and Disclosure Standard

Within 90 days of Section 4's publication, the Federal Communications Commission Chairman must initiate a proceeding to determine whether to adopt a federal reporting and disclosure standard for AI models that preempts conflicting state laws.

---

## Section 7: Preemption of State Laws Mandating Deceptive Conduct

Within 90 days, the Federal Trade Commission Chairman must issue a policy statement explaining when state laws requiring "alterations to the truthful outputs of AI models" are preempted by the FTC Act's prohibition on deceptive practices.

---

## Section 8: Legislation

**(a)** The Special Advisor for AI and Crypto and Assistant to the President for Science and Technology must jointly prepare legislation establishing a uniform federal AI policy framework that preempts conflicting state laws.

**(b)** The legislation shall NOT preempt state laws relating to:

- Child safety protections
- AI compute and data center infrastructure (except general permitting reforms)
- State government procurement and use of AI
- Other topics as determined

---

## Section 9: General Provisions

**(a)** The order doesn't impair authority granted by law to executive departments or affect OMB functions.

**(b)** Implementation is consistent with applicable law and subject to appropriations availability.

**(c)** The order creates no enforceable rights.

**(d)** Publication costs are borne by the Department of Commerce.
