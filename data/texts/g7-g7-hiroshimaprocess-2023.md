# G7 Hiroshima Process: International Guiding Principles and Code of Conduct for Organizations Developing Advanced AI Systems

**Adopted by G7 Leaders | 30 October 2023 | Hiroshima, Japan**

---

## Overview

The G7 Hiroshima Process produced two interrelated voluntary instruments adopted on 30 October 2023 under Japan's G7 Presidency:

1. **International Guiding Principles for Organizations Developing Advanced AI Systems** — 11 high-level principles
2. **International Code of Conduct for Organizations Developing Advanced AI Systems** — 11 concrete actions

Both instruments are addressed to organizations developing "the most advanced AI systems, including foundation models and generative AI systems." They are voluntary and do not create legally binding obligations. G7 members encouraged non-G7 countries and organizations to endorse and implement them.

---

## Part I: International Guiding Principles

### Principle 1 — Risk identification and mitigation throughout the AI lifecycle

Take appropriate measures throughout the development of advanced AI systems — including prior to and throughout their deployment — to identify, evaluate, and mitigate risks across the AI lifecycle. This includes diverse testing methods such as red-teaming, and enabling traceability regarding datasets and development decisions. Risk categories requiring particular attention: CBRN risks, offensive cyber capabilities, health and safety risks, self-replication and self-proliferation risks, societal and democratic risks, and systemic chain-reaction risks.

### Principle 2 — Post-deployment monitoring and vulnerability management

Identify and mitigate vulnerabilities and, where appropriate, incidents and patterns of misuse after deployment. Organizations must monitor for emerging risks and facilitate third-party discovery and reporting through bounty systems or responsible disclosure mechanisms.

### Principle 3 — Public transparency reporting on capabilities and limitations

Publicly report advanced AI systems' capabilities, limitations, and domains of appropriate and inappropriate use. Publish transparency reports for all significant releases covering: safety evaluations, model limitations, bias and discrimination assessments, and red-teaming results.

### Principle 4 — Responsible information sharing and incident reporting

Work toward responsible information sharing and reporting of incidents among organizations developing advanced AI systems, including with industry, governments, civil society, and academia. This covers sharing evaluation reports, information about security risks, dangerous capabilities, and circumvention attempts.

### Principle 5 — AI governance and risk management policies

Develop, implement, and disclose AI governance and risk management policies grounded in a risk-based approach, including privacy policies, mitigation measures, and accountability mechanisms. Regularly update policies as the technology and risk landscape evolve.

### Principle 6 — Robust security controls across the AI lifecycle

Invest in and implement robust security controls, including physical security, cybersecurity, and insider threat safeguards across the AI lifecycle. Secure model weights, algorithms, servers, and datasets; manage vulnerabilities; implement role-based access controls.

### Principle 7 — Content authentication and provenance mechanisms

Develop and deploy reliable content authentication and provenance mechanisms where technically feasible, such as watermarking or other techniques to enable users to identify AI-generated content.

### Principle 8 — Prioritize safety and security research

Prioritize research to mitigate societal, safety, and security risks, and prioritize investment in effective mitigation measures addressing democratic values, human rights, bias mitigation, and disinformation prevention.

### Principle 9 — Prioritize AI development for global challenges

Prioritize the development of advanced AI systems to address the world's greatest challenges — notably the climate crisis, global health, and education — and support digital literacy initiatives aligned with the UN Sustainable Development Goals.

### Principle 10 — Advance international technical standards

Advance the development and adoption of international technical standards, including standards for watermarking and content authentication interoperability, through collaboration with Standards Development Organizations.

### Principle 11 — Data quality and personal data and IP protection

Implement appropriate data input measures and protections for personal data and intellectual property through managing training data quality, implementing privacy-preserving techniques, and mitigating harmful biases in training data.

## Part II: International Code of Conduct — 11 Actions

The Code translates the 11 Principles into concrete operational actions for organizations developing advanced AI systems, to be followed "in line with a risk-based approach."

**Action 1:** Identify, evaluate, and mitigate risks across the AI lifecycle through diverse testing including red-teaming, with particular attention to CBRN risks, offensive cyber capabilities, self-replication risks, and societal/democratic risks.

**Action 2:** Monitor post-deployment for vulnerabilities, emerging risks, incidents, and patterns of misuse. Consider bounty systems or disclosure mechanisms for third-party reporting. Document incidents in cooperation with stakeholders.

**Action 3:** Publicly report capabilities, limitations, and appropriate/inappropriate use domains. Publish transparency reports for all significant releases covering evaluation results, model capacities, performance limitations, safety effects, and red-teaming results.

**Action 4:** Engage in responsible information sharing with industry, governments, civil society, and academia on evaluation reports, security risks, dangerous capabilities, and circumvention attempts. Work toward shared reporting standards.

**Action 5:** Develop, implement, and disclose AI governance and risk management policies grounded in a risk-based approach, including privacy policies and accountability processes covering the full AI lifecycle.

**Action 6:** Implement robust security controls covering physical security, cybersecurity, insider threat safeguards, model weight protection, and vulnerability management across the AI lifecycle.

**Action 7:** Develop and deploy reliable content authentication and provenance mechanisms where technically feasible, such as watermarking, to enable users to identify AI-generated content.

**Action 8:** Prioritize investment in AI safety, security, and trust research, including mitigation tools for democratic values, human rights, bias, and disinformation.

**Action 9:** Prioritize development of advanced AI systems to address the world's greatest challenges — climate, health, and education — while supporting digital literacy initiatives aligned with UN SDGs.

**Action 10:** Contribute to and adopt international technical standards through collaboration with Standards Development Organizations, particularly for watermarking and content authentication interoperability.

**Action 11:** Implement appropriate data input measures and protections for personal data and intellectual property through training data quality management, privacy-preserving techniques, and mitigation of harmful biases.

---

## Status and Adoption

By 2024, over 50 organizations had voluntarily committed to the Code of Conduct, including major AI labs. The instruments were adopted alongside the UK AI Safety Summit (Bletchley Declaration, November 2023) and complement the OECD AI Principles and EU AI Act. The Hiroshima Process continued in 2024 under Italy's G7 Presidency with further refinements.
