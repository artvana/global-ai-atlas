EXPRESS VETO / S.B. 705

Dear Mr. President Rivera Schatz:

The Legislative Assembly of Puerto Rico recently approved S. B. 705. According to its title, it seeks:

       To add a new subsection (d), redesignate the current (d) through (n) as (e)
       through (o), respectively, add a new subsection (p), redesignate the current
       subsections (p) through (x) as (q) through (r) respectively to Article 3; add a
       new Article 24 and renumber the current Articles 24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29,
       30, 31, 32 as Articles 25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30, 31, 32 and 33 of Act 8-
       2004, as amended, known as the "Organic Act of the Department
       of Recreation and Sports," in order to promote innovation, technology, and
       Artificial Intelligence in sports; and for other related purposes.

After carefully evaluating this legislative measure, I hereby issue an express veto due to the reasons that I express below.

I recognize that the measure pursues a legitimate and laudable objective. However, after carefully evaluating it, I conclude that, in its current form, it presents serious challenges of administrative, operational, and fiscal implementation that make its effective execution unfeasible.

The measure requires the Department of Recreation and Sports (DRD) to do the following:

Encourage the use of advanced technologies, such as Artificial Intelligence and data analysis, for multiple aspects. Establish implementation metrics, including annual investment in technology, number of athletes benefited, equipped facilities, and reduction of injuries. Establish alliances with universities, research centers, private companies, and international organizations to develop innovative projects

Train coaches, athletes, and technical personnel in the use of Artificial Intelligence. Create a research and technological development unit within the Department of Recreation and Sports in coordination with the Puerto Rican Institute for the Development of Sports and Recreation. Submit an annual report to the Legislative Assembly on the advances, results, and proposals for expansion derived from the implementation of this Article. Adopt an ethical governance framework of Artificial Intelligence based on international standards, that includes principles of transparency, fairness, non-discrimination, privacy protection, and human oversight. Establish robust protocols for the protection of biometric and performance data of athletes, including informed consent, minimization of data, rights of access and rectification, and encryption in accordance with international standards. Create an Oversight Committee on Artificial Intelligence in Sports, composed of representatives of the Department of Recreation and Sports, experts in Artificial Intelligence, ethics, data protection, and high-performance athletes, that shall meet quarterly to review and approve implementations.

We recognize that the measure represents a significant advance in the modernization of sports with global trends; and promotes alliances with universities, research centers, and technology companies that have the potential to drive applied research, foster the development of specialized talent, and strengthen the knowledge economy in PR. As regards the Department of Education, the measure assigns it a function of inter-agency collaboration with consultative purpose in the regulation process of public policy. In other words, it would be an advisory entity, not responsible for direct execution. Therefore, the effective implementation of the measure falls upon the Department of Recreation and Sports, so it is said entity of the State that must face the fiscal challenges.

We warn that before thinking about innovation, technology, and artificial intelligence in sports, it is indispensable to correct and maintain the existing sports infrastructure, so that they are in adequate conditions of use and provide safety to all participants, preventing injuries and promoting a healthy environment. We cannot aspire to implement an artificial intelligence model until the basic needs for those who practice sports at all levels are satisfied, which is a goal on which we are working.


On the other hand, we emphasize that the measure contemplates the use of artificial intelligence for the development of high-performance athletes, which are not under the jurisdiction of the DRD, but of COPUR and the sports federations. We recognize that the incorporation of technology and intelligence in sports is an advanced topic that,

in Puerto Rico, has been worked on very little and in isolation. Although through the Puerto Rican Institute for the Development of Sports and Recreation (IPDDER), attached to the DRD, courses could be adopted on various alternatives to adopt mechanisms and technological tools in sports, the human resources with knowledge on the subject are scarce. Even with the knowledge on the subject, it could be insufficient given the lack of centers and equipment that allow the implementation of such skills. In addition, even if the model recommended in the measure of "Train-the-trainer" is followed, the hiring of additional employees would be required. Implementing the measure entails an economic investment that the DRD is not in a position to make. This is because it does not have the budget allocated for such mandate. The measure also does not contemplate the disbursement of the funds that its implementation would require, which conservatively have been estimated at $1,200,000.00 for this fiscal year.

In accordance with the foregoing, we conclude that the use of artificial intelligence tools in sports requires a great investment of funds not available. It requires not only the obtaining of the technology, but the identification and training of human resources prepared in these specialized areas. Certainly the initiative is necessary for the development of athletes in Puerto Rico, but at this time the DRD lacks the fiscal capacity for its execution.

It is estimated that the estimated cost of implementation of the measure amounts to $1,200,000, which is not budgeted and, consequently, would have a negative impact on the certified budget.

For all the foregoing reasons, I hereby issue this express veto to S. B. 705.

Cordially,
