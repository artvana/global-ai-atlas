# European Commission Proposal — Digital Omnibus Regulation Amending the AI Act (COM/2025)

**Commission Legislative Proposal (COM/2025)**
*European Union — Proposed 19 November 2025; in trilogue as of May 2026*

---

## Overview

The European Commission introduced the Digital Omnibus package on 19 November 2025 as part of a broader effort to reduce regulatory compliance burdens across the digital sector and support EU competitiveness in technology. The package comprises two linked proposals: this regulation amending the EU AI Act, and a separate regulation amending other digital laws (GDPR, NIS2, DORA, Data Act, and related instruments).

The AI Act amendment proposal targets several provisions of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 that industry groups and member states had identified as creating disproportionate compliance burdens, particularly for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and academic research institutions. The proposal does not alter the EU AI Act's core risk-tier architecture, prohibited practices list, or GPAI model obligations.

As of May 2026, the proposal is in trilogue negotiations between the European Parliament, the Council of the EU, and the Commission. The second trilogue session (April 2026) failed to reach agreement on the timeline deferral provisions. A third trilogue was scheduled for 13 May 2026. Final text and adoption timeline remain uncertain.

## Key Provisions

### Deferral of High-Risk AI Obligations

The most significant proposed change is a deferral of compliance deadlines for high-risk AI systems listed in Annex III of the EU AI Act. Under the original Regulation, Annex III high-risk system obligations were to apply from **August 2026**. The Commission proposes deferring this deadline to **December 2027**, providing an additional 16 months for operators to achieve compliance.

This deferral would not affect:
- Prohibited AI practices (already applicable since February 2025)
- GPAI model obligations (applicable since August 2025)
- Conformity assessment obligations for AI embedded in regulated products (Annex I)

### AI Literacy Requirement Modifications

Article 4 of the EU AI Act requires deployers to ensure their staff has sufficient AI literacy. The proposal would narrow the scope of this obligation for deployers, requiring AI literacy programs only for personnel directly interacting with high-risk AI systems, rather than all staff who "use" AI systems in a broader sense. The Commission argues this avoids disproportionate training burdens for organizations using AI tools incidentally.

### Expanded SME Exemptions

The proposal extends reduced compliance obligations for micro-enterprises and SMEs operating under Annex III (high-risk) use cases, including:
- Simplified technical documentation requirements
- Extended timescales for conformity assessment preparation
- Reduced post-market monitoring reporting frequency
- Exemptions from certain AI literacy program mandates for micro-enterprises with fewer than 10 employees

Academic and non-commercial research institutions would also benefit from broader exemptions, particularly for testing and development activities.

### Legitimate Interest Legal Basis for AI Training

The proposal adds an explicit legitimate-interest legal basis within the AI Act's framework (complementing existing GDPR provisions) for the use of personal data in AI system training, evaluation, and testing. This addresses a legal uncertainty identified by the AI industry, particularly regarding the use of publicly available data for training GPAI and high-risk AI systems.

### Other Proposed Modifications

- Simplification of the EU database registration requirements for high-risk AI systems
- Clarification that certain AI-enabled software embedded in regulated products (medical devices, machinery) does not trigger duplicate conformity assessment obligations
- Adjustment of the significant modification threshold that triggers re-conformity-assessment obligations for deployed AI systems

## Legislative Status

The proposal was published on 19 November 2025 and submitted to the Council and European Parliament simultaneously. The Council adopted its general approach in February 2026. The Parliament's IMCO and LIBE committees submitted their positions in March 2026. Trilogue negotiations commenced in April 2026.

As of May 2026:
- First trilogue (late March 2026): procedural alignment
- Second trilogue (April 2026): deadlock on deferral provisions; member states divided on December 2027 vs. June 2027 deadline
- Third trilogue scheduled: 13 May 2026

The final text, once agreed, will require formal adoption by Council and Parliament votes before entering into force.
