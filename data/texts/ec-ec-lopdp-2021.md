# Ley Orgánica de Protección de Datos Personales (LOPDP) — Ecuador
**Registro Oficial Suplemento No. 459, 26 de mayo de 2021**
*Ecuador — May 26, 2021*

---

## Overview

Ecuador's Ley Orgánica de Protección de Datos Personales (LOPDP) is the country's first comprehensive data protection law, enacted on May 26, 2021. The LOPDP establishes a GDPR-inspired framework for the protection of personal data, creating the Superintendencia de Protección de Datos Personales (SNPDP) as the independent supervisory authority. Implementing regulations were issued in 2023, providing detailed operational guidance. The law applies to any processing of personal data carried out in Ecuador or by controllers/processors established in Ecuador, and also applies to processing by non-Ecuadorian controllers targeting Ecuadorian residents (extraterritorial reach). The LOPDP is recognized as one of the most modern data protection frameworks in Latin America, drawing heavily on GDPR principles and structure.

## Key Provisions

### Superintendencia de Protección de Datos Personales (SNPDP)
- Independent supervisory authority with investigative, advisory, and enforcement powers
- Issues binding regulations, guidelines, and codes of conduct
- Investigates complaints from data subjects and conducts ex officio investigations
- May conduct audits and order data protection impact assessments
- Imposes administrative sanctions for violations
- International cooperation with peer supervisory authorities

### Seven Core Principles
- Legality: processing must have a lawful basis
- Fairness: processing must be fair and transparent
- Purpose: purposes must be specified, explicit, and legitimate
- Proportionality: data must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary
- Conservation: data not retained longer than necessary
- Responsibility and accountability: controllers demonstrate compliance
- Security: appropriate technical and organizational measures

### Lawful Processing Bases
- Consent: freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous
- Contract performance to which the data subject is party
- Legal obligation applicable to the controller
- Protection of vital interests
- Public interest or exercise of official authority
- Legitimate interests of the controller or third party

### Data Subject Rights (ARCO+)
- Right of Access to personal data held
- Right of Rectification of inaccurate or incomplete data
- Right of Cancellation/Erasure where grounds are met
- Right of Opposition to processing based on legitimate interests or for direct marketing
- Right to Portability: receive data in a structured, machine-readable format and transfer to another controller
- Right to Non-Discrimination: data subject not to be subject to discriminatory treatment for exercising rights

### Sensitive Personal Data
- Special categories: health and medical data, genetic data, biometric data, racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious beliefs, trade union membership, sexual orientation, criminal record
- Processing requires explicit consent or specific statutory authority
- Impact assessment required before processing sensitive data at scale

### Automated Decision-Making
- Data subjects have the right to be informed when subject to decisions based solely on automated processing
- Right to request human review of significant automated decisions
- Controllers must implement measures to detect and prevent discriminatory algorithmic outcomes

### Data Protection Officer (DPO)
- Mandatory for public bodies and organizations processing large-scale or sensitive data
- DPO must have expert knowledge of data protection law
- DPO reports to highest management level and is point of contact for data subjects and SNPDP

### Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs)
- Required for high-risk processing: new technologies, large-scale profiling, systematic monitoring, sensitive data categories, automated decision-making
- Must consult SNPDP for prior authorization where residual high risk remains after mitigation

### Cross-Border Data Transfers
- Transfers to countries with SNPDP adequacy recognition are permitted
- Transfers to non-adequate countries require appropriate safeguards: standard contractual clauses, binding corporate rules, or individual consent
- SNPDP issues adequacy determinations

### Breach Notification
- Controllers must notify SNPDP within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach likely to affect rights
- Notification to affected data subjects required where high risk to their rights and freedoms

## Enforcement & Penalties

- SNPDP may issue compliance orders, warnings, corrective measures, and processing bans
- Administrative fines: up to $700,000 USD or 1% of annual revenue for serious violations; up to $1,000,000 or 2% for very serious violations
- Criminal penalties for certain offenses under Ecuador's Integral Criminal Code
- Civil liability: data subjects may seek compensation through Ecuadorian courts
- Right to lodge complaints directly with SNPDP free of charge
