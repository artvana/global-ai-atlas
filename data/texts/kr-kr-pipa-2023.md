# Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) — 2023 Amendments
**Act No. 19234**
*South Korea — Enacted March 14, 2023; Effective September 15, 2023*

---

## Overview / Background

South Korea's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA, 개인정보 보호법) is the country's primary data protection statute, administered by the Personal Information Protection Commission (PIPC). Originally enacted in 2011 and significantly revised in 2020, the 2023 amendments (Act No. 19234, enacted March 14, 2023, effective September 15, 2023) represent the most substantial overhaul of PIPA since its inception, introducing rights specifically addressing automated decision-making and AI systems, strengthening the cross-border transfer framework, and substantially expanding PIPC's enforcement powers.

South Korea's PIPA framework has long been characterized by its GDPR-adjacent structure — emphasizing lawful basis, data subject rights, and independent supervisory authority — while incorporating Korean-specific elements such as the "pseudonymous information" regime enabling research and AI development uses. The 2023 amendments accelerate the convergence with European standards while introducing novel provisions on automated decisions that are among the strongest in Asia-Pacific.

The PIPA applies to all personal information controllers — public and private sector entities that determine the purposes and means of processing personal data — operating in South Korea or processing data of South Korean residents. The extraterritorial scope was clarified and expanded in 2023 to cover online service providers outside Korea that process data of Korean residents.

## Key Provisions / Chapters

### Right to Explanation for Automated Decisions
- Individuals have the right to request an explanation of how an automated decision was made when that decision: (1) produces legal effects on the individual; or (2) significantly affects the individual's rights or interests.
- This is one of the first statutory rights to explanation for AI-driven decisions in the Asia-Pacific region.
- The explanation must cover the logic, significance, and foreseeable consequences of the automated processing.
- Controllers must establish procedures for receiving and responding to explanation requests within a reasonable timeframe.

### Right to Object to and Refuse Automated Decisions
- Individuals may object to decisions made solely by automated processing (including AI), and may request that a human being review the decision.
- The right to object applies to decisions involving: hiring and employment assessments; credit scoring and financial access; healthcare diagnosis or treatment recommendations; and other significant decisions affecting the individual.
- Where an objection is filed, the controller must pause the effects of the automated decision pending human review, unless the decision is required by law or is necessary for contract performance.
- Controllers must notify individuals in advance of their right to object when deploying automated decision-making systems.

### Restrictions on AI-Driven Profiling for Marketing
- Processing of personal information for behavioral profiling for targeted marketing without explicit consent is restricted.
- Controllers must provide clear opt-out mechanisms for profiling-based marketing.
- Data subjects may withdraw consent for profiling at any time.

### Strengthened Cross-Border Transfer Mechanisms
- The 2023 amendments introduce a comprehensive framework for cross-border data transfers modeled on GDPR Chapter V:
  - Adequacy decisions by PIPC for countries/organizations providing equivalent protection
  - Standard contractual clauses (SCCs) issued by PIPC
  - Binding corporate rules (BCRs) for intra-group transfers
  - Individual consent with full disclosure as a fallback mechanism
- Controllers transferring data internationally must disclose destination countries, recipients, purposes, and retention periods to data subjects.
- Transfers to countries without adequacy decisions require one of the approved supplementary mechanisms.

### 72-Hour Breach Notification
- Data controllers must notify PIPC within 72 hours of becoming aware of a personal data breach (aligning with GDPR Article 33).
- Notification to affected data subjects must follow without undue delay where the breach is likely to result in significant harm.
- Breach notification must include: nature and circumstances of the breach; categories and approximate number of individuals and records affected; likely consequences; measures taken or proposed to address the breach.

### Expanded PIPC Powers
- PIPC gains authority to conduct on-site investigations and audits of controllers without prior notice in urgent circumstances.
- New power to impose corrective orders requiring controllers to suspend processing activities posing imminent risk of harm.
- Enhanced data subject complaint handling powers, with mandatory mediation procedures.
- PIPC may publish investigation findings and enforcement decisions (public disclosure as a compliance incentive).

### Extraterritorial Scope Expansion
- Explicit coverage of foreign online service providers (including app operators and cloud services) that process personal data of Korean residents, even if they have no physical presence in Korea.
- Foreign entities subject to PIPA must designate a domestic representative in Korea.
- PIPC may request cooperation from foreign regulators for cross-border investigations.

### Pseudonymous Information for AI Development
- Pseudonymous information (processed so the individual cannot be re-identified without separate restoration information) may be used for: statistical purposes; scientific research; and preservation of public records.
- This enables AI model training using pseudonymized datasets without individual consent, provided appropriate technical and organizational safeguards are in place.
- Pseudonymous information may not be linked with other data in a way that enables re-identification.

## Enforcement & Penalties

- **Maximum administrative fine:** Up to KRW 30 billion or 3% of global annual turnover (whichever is greater) for serious violations — approximately $22 million USD at current exchange rates.
- **Criminal penalties:** Individuals who unlawfully process sensitive data or breach security obligations face up to 5 years imprisonment or fines up to KRW 50 million.
- **PIPC enforcement:** The PIPC may issue corrective orders, impose administrative fines, and recommend criminal prosecution.
- **Private right of action:** Data subjects may seek civil damages for violations of their rights, including violations of automated decision rights.
- **Public disclosure:** PIPC may publicly disclose the names of non-compliant organizations and the nature of violations.
- **Data subject remedies:** Controllers must provide data subjects with compensation for damages resulting from breaches of PIPA obligations.
