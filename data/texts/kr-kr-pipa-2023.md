# South Korea Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) — 2023 Amendments
**Passed: February 27, 2023 | In force: September 15, 2023**

## Overview
The 2023 amendments to the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) significantly updated South Korea's data protection framework, introducing key provisions on automated decision-making, AI, data portability, and strengthened individual rights.

## Key 2023 Amendments

### Automated Decision-Making (AI) Provisions
Data subjects have the right to:
- **Opt out** of automated decision-making processes (such as AI systems)
- **Request explanation** of automated decisions: data controllers must disclose the standards and procedures for these decisions and how personal information is processed
- **Request human review** of automated decisions affecting them significantly

### Right to Data Portability
Data subjects can transfer their personal data between service providers seamlessly. This applies to data processed by automated means with consent or for contract performance.

### Biometric and Sensitive Data
While PIPA does not explicitly define biometric data, it treats physical, physiological, and behavioral characteristics from 'sensitive data' as data used to identify a person, subject to heightened protections.

### Enforcement Strengthened
The amendments introduce adjusted punitive measures while expanding certain data subject rights.

### Cross-Border Transfer Provisions
Revised rules for cross-border data transfers, including adequacy recognition mechanisms.

## Personal Information Protection Commission (PIPC)
The Personal Information Protection Commission (PIPC) is the independent supervisory authority responsible for enforcement, guidance, and international cooperation.

## Penalties
Significant administrative fines and criminal penalties for violations.

## Context
South Korea is one of the few non-EU countries to have achieved EU adequacy status. PIPA is considered one of Asia's most comprehensive data protection laws.
