# Data Privacy Act of 2012

**Republic Act No. 10173**
*Republic of the Philippines, Fifteenth Congress, Second Regular Session*
*Approved: 15 August 2012*

---

## CHAPTER I — GENERAL PROVISIONS

### Section 1 — Short Title

This Act shall be known as the **"Data Privacy Act of 2012"**.

### Section 2 — Declaration of Policy

It is the policy of the State to protect the fundamental human right of privacy of communication while ensuring free flow of information to promote innovation and growth. The State recognizes the vital role of information and communications technology in nation-building and its inherent obligation to ensure that personal information in information and communications systems in the government and in the private sector are secured and protected.

### Section 3 — Definitions

- **(a) Commission** — the National Privacy Commission created by this Act.
- **(b) Consent** — any freely given, specific, informed indication of will whereby the data subject agrees to the collection and processing of personal information. May be written, electronic or recorded, or given by an authorized agent.
- **(c) Data subject** — an individual whose personal information is processed.
- **(d) Direct marketing** — communication by any means of advertising or marketing material directed to particular individuals.
- **(e) Filing system** — any structured set of information relating to natural or juridical persons where specific information relating to a particular person is readily accessible.
- **(f) Information and Communications System** — a system for generating, sending, receiving, storing or otherwise processing electronic data messages or electronic documents.
- **(g) Personal information** — any information from which the identity of an individual is apparent or can be reasonably and directly ascertained, or when put together with other information would directly and certainly identify an individual.
- **(h) Personal information controller** — a person or organization that controls the collection, holding, processing or use of personal information, including a person who instructs another to collect, hold, process, use, transfer or disclose personal information on their behalf. Excludes: (1) a person performing such functions as instructed by another; and (2) an individual collecting for personal, family or household affairs.
- **(i) Personal information processor** — any natural or juridical person to whom a personal information controller may outsource the processing of personal data.
- **(j) Processing** — any operation performed upon personal information including collection, recording, organization, storage, updating, modification, retrieval, consultation, use, consolidation, blocking, erasure or destruction of data.
- **(k) Privileged information** — any and all forms of data which under the Rules of Court and other pertinent laws constitute privileged communication.
- **(l) Sensitive personal information** — personal information:
  1. About an individual's race, ethnic origin, marital status, age, color, and religious, philosophical or political affiliations;
  2. About an individual's health, education, genetic or sexual life, or any proceeding for any offense committed or alleged to have been committed by such person;
  3. Issued by government agencies peculiar to an individual (e.g., social security numbers, health records, licenses, tax returns); and
  4. Specifically established by executive order or Act of Congress to be kept classified.

### Section 4 — Scope

This Act applies to the processing of all types of personal information and to any natural and juridical person involved in personal information processing, including those not found or established in the Philippines who use equipment located in the Philippines or maintain an office, branch or agency in the Philippines.

**Does not apply to:**
- Information about government officers/employees relating to their position or functions;
- Information about individuals performing services under government contract relating to those services;
- Information relating to discretionary financial benefits (e.g., licenses, permits) given by the government;
- Personal information processed for journalistic, artistic, literary or research purposes;
- Information necessary for public authority functions, including processing by the independent central monetary authority and law enforcement and regulatory agencies;
- Information necessary for banks and financial institutions to comply with anti-money laundering and related laws;
- Personal information originally collected from residents of foreign jurisdictions in accordance with the laws of those jurisdictions.

### Section 6 — Extraterritorial Application

This Act applies to acts done or practices engaged in outside the Philippines by an entity if:
- (a) The act relates to personal information about a Philippine citizen or resident;
- (b) The entity has a link with the Philippines (contract entered in the Philippines; juridical entity with central management in the country; entity with a Philippine branch/agency/subsidiary whose parent has access to personal information); or
- (c) The entity carries on business in the Philippines or the personal information was collected or held by an entity in the Philippines.

## CHAPTER II — THE NATIONAL PRIVACY COMMISSION

### Section 7 — Functions of the National Privacy Commission

The NPC, an independent body, shall:
- (a) Ensure compliance of personal information controllers with this Act;
- (b) Receive complaints, institute investigations, facilitate settlement through ADR, adjudicate, and award indemnity on matters affecting personal information;
- (c) Issue cease and desist orders; impose temporary or permanent bans on processing found detrimental to national security and public interest;
- (d) Compel any entity, government agency or instrumentality to abide by its orders;
- (e) Monitor compliance of government agencies on security and technical measures;
- (f) Coordinate with government agencies and the private sector on data privacy protection;
- (g) Publish a regular guide to all laws relating to data protection;
- (h) Recommend to the DOJ the prosecution and imposition of penalties;
- (i) Review, approve, reject or require modification of privacy codes voluntarily adhered to by personal information controllers;
- (j) Provide assistance on privacy or data protection matters;
- (k) Comment on the implication on data privacy of proposed legislation; issue advisory opinions; interpret the provisions of this Act;
- (l) Propose legislation, amendments or modifications to Philippine laws on privacy or data protection;
- (m) Ensure coordination with data privacy regulators in other countries; participate in international and regional data privacy protection initiatives;
- (n) Negotiate and contract with other data privacy authorities for cross-border application and implementation of privacy laws.

### Section 9 — Organizational Structure

The NPC is attached to the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) and headed by a **Privacy Commissioner**, assisted by two Deputy Privacy Commissioners (one for Data Processing Systems, one for Policies and Planning). All are appointed by the President for three-year terms, renewable once. The Privacy Commissioner must be at least 35 years of age, of good moral character, and a recognized expert in information technology and data privacy.

## CHAPTER III — PROCESSING OF PERSONAL INFORMATION

### Section 11 — General Data Privacy Principles

Processing of personal information shall be permitted subject to compliance with this Act and adherence to the principles of **transparency, legitimate purpose and proportionality**. Personal information must be:

- **(a)** Collected for specified and legitimate purposes determined before or as soon as reasonably practicable after collection, and further processed only compatibly with those purposes;
- **(b)** Processed fairly and lawfully;
- **(c)** Accurate, relevant, and where necessary, kept up to date; inaccurate or incomplete data must be rectified, supplemented, destroyed or further processing restricted;
- **(d)** Adequate and not excessive in relation to the purposes for which collected and processed;
- **(e)** Retained only for as long as necessary for the purpose for which the data was obtained or for establishment, exercise or defense of legal claims, or for legitimate business purposes or as provided by law; and
- **(f)** Kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects for no longer than necessary.

### Section 12 — Criteria for Lawful Processing of Personal Information

Processing is permitted only when at least one of the following exists:
- **(a)** The data subject has given consent;
- **(b)** Processing is necessary for a contract with the data subject or pre-contractual steps at the data subject's request;
- **(c)** Processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation;
- **(d)** Processing is necessary to protect vitally important interests of the data subject;
- **(e)** Processing is necessary to respond to national emergency, comply with public order and safety, or fulfill public authority functions; or
- **(f)** Processing is necessary for legitimate interests of the personal information controller or third parties, except where overridden by the fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject.

### Section 13 — Sensitive Personal Information and Privileged Information

Processing of sensitive personal information and privileged information is **prohibited** except in the following cases:
- **(a)** The data subject has given specific consent prior to processing;
- **(b)** Processing is provided for by existing laws and regulations guaranteeing protection;
- **(c)** Processing is necessary to protect the life and health of the data subject who is unable to express consent;
- **(d)** Processing is necessary for lawful and non-commercial objectives of public organizations limited to bona fide members;
- **(e)** Processing is necessary for medical treatment carried out by a medical practitioner with adequate protection; or
- **(f)** Processing is necessary for protection of lawful rights in court proceedings or legal claims, or when provided to government or public authority.

### Section 14 — Subcontract of Personal Information

A personal information controller may subcontract the processing of personal information, provided that the personal information controller shall be responsible for ensuring proper safeguards are in place. The personal information processor shall comply with all requirements of this Act.

## CHAPTER IV — RIGHTS OF THE DATA SUBJECT

### Section 16 — Rights of the Data Subject

The data subject is entitled to:

- **(a)** Be **informed** whether personal information pertaining to them shall be, are being or have been processed;
- **(b)** Be **furnished information** before entry of their personal information into the processing system (or at the next practical opportunity), including: description of the personal information; purposes for which it is processed; scope and method of processing; recipients or classes of recipients; methods utilized for automated access; identity and contact details of the personal information controller; period for which information will be stored; and existence of their rights;
- **(c)** Reasonable **access** to: contents of processed personal information; sources from which personal information were obtained; names and addresses of recipients; manner of processing; reasons for disclosure; information on automated processes where the data will be made the sole basis for any decision significantly affecting the data subject; dates when personal information were last accessed and modified; and identity of the personal information controller;
- **(d)** **Dispute** inaccuracy or error in personal information and have it corrected immediately; retracted information and corrected information shall both remain accessible; third parties previously receiving the information shall be informed of rectification upon reasonable request;
- **(e)** **Suspend, withdraw or order blocking, removal or destruction** of personal information upon discovery and substantial proof that the information is incomplete, outdated, false, unlawfully obtained, used for unauthorized purposes or no longer necessary; and
- **(f)** Be **indemnified** for any damages sustained due to inaccurate, incomplete, outdated, false, unlawfully obtained or unauthorized use of personal information.

### Section 17 — Transmissibility of Rights

Lawful heirs and assigns of the data subject may invoke their rights after the data subject's death or when the data subject is incapacitated.

### Section 18 — Right to Data Portability

The data subject shall have the right, where personal information is processed by electronic means and in a structured and commonly used format, to obtain from the personal information controller a copy of data undergoing processing in an electronic or structured format commonly used and which allows for further use by the data subject. The Commission may specify electronic formats and technical standards.

## CHAPTER V — SECURITY OF PERSONAL INFORMATION

### Section 20 — Security of Personal Information

**(a)** The personal information controller must implement reasonable and appropriate organizational, physical and technical measures for the protection of personal information against accidental or unlawful destruction, alteration and disclosure.

**(b)** The personal information controller shall implement measures to protect against natural dangers (accidental loss or destruction) and human dangers (unlawful access, fraudulent misuse, unlawful destruction, alteration and contamination).

**(c)** Measures must include: safeguards to protect computer networks; a security policy with respect to processing; a process for identifying and accessing vulnerabilities and for preventive, corrective and mitigating action; and regular monitoring for security breaches.

**(d)** Third parties processing personal information on behalf of the controller must also implement the required security measures.

**(e)** Employees and agents of a personal information controller involved in processing shall operate and hold personal information under **strict confidentiality**, continuing even after leaving employment.

**(f)** The personal information controller shall **promptly notify the Commission and affected data subjects** when sensitive personal information or information that may enable identity fraud is reasonably believed to have been acquired by an unauthorized person and is likely to give rise to a real risk of serious harm. The notification shall describe the nature of the breach, the sensitive personal information possibly involved, and the measures taken to address the breach. Notification may be delayed only to the extent necessary to determine the scope of the breach, prevent further disclosures, or restore reasonable integrity to the system.

---

## CHAPTER VI — ACCOUNTABILITY FOR TRANSFER OF PERSONAL INFORMATION

### Section 21 — Principle of Accountability

Each personal information controller is responsible for personal information under its control or custody, including information transferred to a third party for processing, whether domestically or internationally.

**(a)** The personal information controller is accountable for complying with this Act and shall use contractual or other reasonable means to provide a comparable level of protection while information is processed by a third party.

**(b)** The personal information controller shall designate an individual or individuals accountable for the organization's compliance with this Act. Their identity shall be made known to any data subject upon request.

---

## CHAPTER VII — SECURITY OF SENSITIVE PERSONAL INFORMATION IN GOVERNMENT

### Section 22 — Responsibility of Heads of Agencies

All sensitive personal information maintained by the government shall be secured using the most appropriate standard recognized by the information and communications technology industry. The head of each government agency shall be responsible for complying with security requirements.

### Section 23 — Requirements Relating to Access by Agency Personnel

**On-site and Online Access**: No government employee shall have access to sensitive personal information on government property or through online facilities unless they have received a **security clearance** from the head of the source agency.

**Off-site Access**: Sensitive personal information may not be transported or accessed from a location off government property unless approved by the head of the agency, subject to: 2-business-day approval deadline (unanswered requests are disapproved); limitation to not more than **1,000 records** at a time; and mandatory **encryption** using the most secure standard recognized by the Commission.

### Section 24 — Applicability to Government Contractors

In any contract that may involve accessing sensitive personal information from **1,000 or more individuals**, an agency shall require a contractor and its employees to register their personal information processing system with the Commission and comply with this Act.

## CHAPTER VIII — PENALTIES

### Section 25 — Unauthorized Processing

- Personal information: 1–3 years imprisonment + PHP 500,000–2,000,000 fine
- Sensitive personal information: 3–6 years imprisonment + PHP 500,000–4,000,000 fine

### Section 26 — Accessing Personal Information Due to Negligence

- Personal information: 1–3 years + PHP 500,000–2,000,000
- Sensitive personal information: 3–6 years + PHP 500,000–4,000,000

### Section 27 — Improper Disposal

- Personal information: 6 months–2 years + PHP 100,000–500,000
- Sensitive personal information: 1–3 years + PHP 100,000–1,000,000

### Section 28 — Processing for Unauthorized Purposes

- Personal information: 1½–5 years + PHP 500,000–1,000,000
- Sensitive personal information: 2–7 years + PHP 500,000–2,000,000

### Section 29 — Unauthorized Access or Intentional Breach

1–3 years + PHP 500,000–2,000,000

### Section 30 — Concealment of Security Breaches Involving Sensitive Personal Information

1½–5 years + PHP 500,000–1,000,000

### Section 31 — Malicious Disclosure

1½–5 years + PHP 500,000–1,000,000

### Section 32 — Unauthorized Disclosure

- Personal information: 1–3 years + PHP 500,000–1,000,000
- Sensitive personal information: 3–5 years + PHP 500,000–2,000,000

### Section 33 — Combination or Series of Acts

3–6 years + PHP 1,000,000–5,000,000

### Section 35 — Large-Scale

Maximum penalty in the applicable scale shall be imposed when personal information of at least **100 persons** is harmed, affected or involved.

### Section 36 — Offense Committed by Public Officer

Accessory penalty: disqualification to occupy public office for a term **double** the term of criminal penalty imposed.

---

## CHAPTER IX — MISCELLANEOUS PROVISIONS

### Section 38 — Interpretation

Any doubt in interpretation shall be liberally interpreted in a manner mindful of the rights and interests of the individual about whom personal information is processed.

### Section 45 — Effectivity

This Act shall take effect fifteen (15) days after publication in at least two national newspapers of general circulation.
