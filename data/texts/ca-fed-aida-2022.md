# Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA) — Canada Bill C-27

## Overview

The **Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA)** was Part 3 of **Bill C-27**, the *Digital Charter Implementation Act, 2022*, introduced by Minister of Innovation François-Philippe Champagne in the House of Commons on June 16, 2022. It was Canada's proposed comprehensive federal AI governance framework — the first of its kind at the federal level in Canada — and would have been the most significant AI law in North America had it passed.

Bill C-27 had three parts:
- **Part 1**: *Consumer Privacy Protection Act (CPPA)* — replacing PIPEDA with a modernized private-sector privacy law.
- **Part 2**: *Personal Information and Data Protection Tribunal Act* — creating a new enforcement tribunal.
- **Part 3**: *Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA)* — the AI-specific component.

AIDA died when Parliament was prorogued on **January 6, 2025**, after Prime Minister Justin Trudeau announced his intention to resign as Liberal Party leader. The bill had been in extended committee study and was never brought to a final vote. It was never enacted.

### Scope

AIDA as introduced (and as amended through 2024 committee work) would have applied to:
- **Regulated persons**: Any person or organization that designs, develops, makes available for use, or manages the operation of an AI system in the course of international or interprovincial trade and commerce.
- **High-impact AI systems**: AI systems determined by regulation to present a significant risk of harm to individuals or to their interests.
- **General-purpose AI systems (GPAI)**: Significantly amended versions of the bill introduced in November 2023 added specific obligations for GPAI systems, influenced by EU AI Act developments.

The bill explicitly excluded AI systems used for personal, non-commercial purposes and AI systems regulated under sector-specific federal statutes.

### Core Obligations

**For all regulated persons dealing with high-impact AI systems:**

1. **Risk identification and assessment**: Assess whether the AI system is a high-impact system and identify reasonably foreseeable risks of harm or biased output.
2. **Risk mitigation measures**: Implement measures to mitigate identified risks and monitor for new risks after deployment.
3. **Record-keeping**: Maintain records of risk assessments and mitigation measures.
4. **Plain-language description**: Make available a plain-language description of the AI system and its uses.
5. **Cessation of use**: Cease use of a high-impact AI system if it presents a serious risk of harm.

**For persons making AI systems available for use:**
1. Assess whether the system is high-impact.
2. Establish measures to mitigate risks of harm or biased output.
3. Publish plain-language descriptions of the system.

**For persons managing the operation of high-impact AI systems:**
1. Monitor systems for risks.
2. Notify the Minister of any serious risks.
3. Cease operation if a serious risk cannot be mitigated.

### General-Purpose AI System Obligations (2023 Amendments)

Following EU AI Act negotiations, a significant amendment package proposed in November 2023 added a separate regime for GPAI:
- Developers of GPAI must assess whether the system is general-purpose.
- Maintain technical documentation of the system's capabilities, limitations, and training data sources.
- Publish public summaries of training data.
- Implement measures to prevent GPAI from being used for prohibited purposes.
- Cooperate with downstream deployers' compliance efforts.

### Prohibited Conduct

AIDA would have prohibited:
1. Making available or managing a high-impact AI system that was likely to cause serious harm to individuals.
2. Processing biometric information to infer certain sensitive attributes without authorization.
3. Using AI to produce "synthetic intimate images" (deepfake pornography) without consent.
4. Using AI to manipulate individuals against their interests in ways likely to cause serious harm.

### Enforcement

- **Minister of Innovation, Science and Economic Development (ISED)**: Primary oversight, with power to require audits, access records, and issue compliance orders.
- **Artificial Intelligence and Data Commissioner (AIDC)**: A new Commissioner created by AIDA to assist the Minister with oversight, conduct audits, and publish guidance. The Commissioner would have been appointed by Cabinet.
- **Penalties**:
  - Administrative monetary penalties up to **C$10 million or 3% of global annual revenue** for violations of the compliance provisions.
  - Criminal offences for the most serious violations: up to **C$25 million or 5% of global annual revenue**, and up to **10 years imprisonment** for individuals who knowingly or recklessly engage in prohibited conduct causing serious harm.

### AI and Data Commissioner

AIDA would have created an *Artificial Intelligence and Data Commissioner* within the ISED portfolio:
- Powers to conduct audits of high-impact AI systems.
- Power to order production of records and technical documentation.
- Power to publish audit findings (subject to confidentiality protections).
- Advisory role to the Minister on enforcement decisions.

## Why It Failed

AIDA did not fail due to a veto. It died through **parliamentary prorogation** — the suspension of Parliament — on January 6, 2025.

**Sequence of events:**
1. Bill C-27 (including AIDA) was introduced June 16, 2022, and passed second reading in the House of Commons in April 2023.
2. The bill was referred to the House Standing Committee on Industry and Technology (INDU) in spring 2023.
3. The committee process was prolonged by extensive amendments — particularly following the November 2023 announcement of proposed GPAI provisions — and by significant opposition from privacy experts, civil society, and some industry groups.
4. In October 2024, the committee had completed study of Parts 1 and 2 but AIDA remained under active committee consideration with no clear timeline to report back to the House.
5. On December 16, 2024, Prime Minister Justin Trudeau announced his intention to resign as Liberal Party leader. He prorogued Parliament effective January 6, 2025, to allow time for a Liberal leadership race.
6. Prorogation extinguished all bills that had not received Royal Assent, including C-27 in its entirety.

**Legislative criticism of AIDA:**

Even before prorogation, AIDA faced substantial criticism that undermined its political viability:

**1. Vagueness on "high-impact" definition.** The bill delegated the definition of "high-impact AI system" entirely to future regulations, leaving regulated entities with no certainty about their obligations. Critics — including the Privacy Commissioner of Canada — argued this was a fundamental deficiency.

**2. Lack of rights for individuals.** Unlike the EU AI Act and consumer protection AI laws in other jurisdictions, AIDA as originally drafted did not give individuals any rights against developers or deployers of AI systems. There was no right to explanation, human review, or appeal. Civil society groups argued strenuously for individual rights provisions; limited amendments were eventually proposed but remained insufficient to many critics.

**3. No prohibited categories at introduction.** The original bill contained no list of prohibited AI applications. The 2023 amendments added prohibitions, but critics argued they were too narrow and lacked sufficient detail.

**4. Overlap with CPPA.** Parts 1, 2, and 3 of C-27 were bundled together, meaning AIDA's fate was tied to the more contentious privacy law debate. Several stakeholders who supported CPPA urged the government to split AIDA into a separate bill to allow faster consideration; the government declined.

**5. Inadequate consultation.** Industry groups, academics, and civil society broadly agreed that the government had not conducted adequate pre-legislative consultation before introducing AIDA, resulting in a bill that required extensive amendments before it could be workable. The committee process reflected this, with the government itself tabling major amendments.

**6. Changed political environment.** By late 2024, the Trudeau government faced severe political pressure, minority status, and the departure of its NDP confidence-and-supply partners. The government lacked political capital to prioritize AIDA through an already-contentious committee process.

## Key Provisions (as proposed, including 2023 amendments)

### High-Impact AI Systems

**Core risk management obligations** for persons who make high-impact AI systems available:
- Assess whether the system is a high-impact system.
- Assess reasonably foreseeable risks of physical, psychological, or financial harm to individuals, or of biased outputs that may harm individuals.
- Implement and document measures to mitigate those risks.
- Monitor for new risks after deployment and mitigate accordingly.
- Make available a plain-language description of the system.

### Biased Output

"Biased output" was defined as output that treats individuals or groups of individuals in a way that is discriminatory based on protected grounds under the *Canadian Human Rights Act* or the *Charter of Rights and Freedoms*.

Regulated persons dealing with high-impact systems were required to:
- Assess risks of biased output before making the system available.
- Implement mitigation measures.
- Monitor for and address biased output after deployment.

### Notification and Transparency

- Regulated persons must inform the Minister when a high-impact AI system is made available and provide documentation of risk assessments and mitigation measures upon request.
- A plain-language description of the system must be publicly available.
- Auditors designated by the Minister may require access to AI systems, data, and records.

### Prohibited Conduct (final version)

AIDA prohibited:
1. Processing personal information obtained through unlawful activity to train an AI system.
2. Making available a high-impact AI system where the person knows or is reckless as to whether the system will likely cause serious physical or psychological harm or substantial financial loss.
3. Using an AI system for the purpose of producing content that depicts explicit sexual activity and that depicts real individuals in a manner they have not consented to (deepfake intimate images).
4. Using AI to defraud the public.

### Offences and Penalties

**Administrative monetary penalties (civil):**
- Up to C$10 million or 3% of global annual revenue (whichever is greater) for contraventions of the compliance requirements.

**Criminal offences:**
- Making available a high-impact AI system knowing or being reckless as to the risk of serious harm: up to C$25 million or 5% of global annual revenue and/or 10 years imprisonment.
- Obstruction of the Minister's oversight: up to C$10 million or 3% global revenue and/or 5 years imprisonment.

### Artificial Intelligence and Data Commissioner

- Appointed by the Governor in Council (Cabinet).
- Assisted the Minister in administering AIDA.
- Powers to conduct or order independent audits of high-impact AI systems.
- Power to publish audit findings (subject to confidentiality).
- Could make recommendations to the Minister on enforcement.
- Did not have independent order-making or penalty-issuing powers (unlike the Privacy Commissioner's proposed powers under CPPA Part 1).

## Policy Significance

Despite never being enacted, AIDA was significant in shaping North American AI policy discourse:

1. **Canada's AI governance gap**: AIDA's failure left Canada — a major AI research hub — without any federal AI-specific legislation, relying instead on the 2019 Directive on Automated Decision-Making (government only), Quebec Law 25 (provincial privacy law), and sector-specific guidance. The Liberal leadership race and subsequent 2025 federal election created further uncertainty about when federal AI legislation would be reintroduced.

2. **Individual rights gap highlighted**: The sustained criticism of AIDA's lack of individual rights provisions influenced later proposals and reinforced the EU AI Act model (which included rights) as the global template.

3. **GPAI coverage**: AIDA was one of the first non-EU proposed laws to explicitly address general-purpose AI systems, demonstrating the influence of EU AI Act negotiations on other jurisdictions.

4. **Lessons for Bill C-27 successor**: The successor Liberal government (under PM Mark Carney, elected April 2025) was expected to introduce a new digital governance package addressing the deficiencies identified in AIDA — potentially separating the AI and privacy components, adding individual rights, and clarifying the high-impact definition.

5. **Criminal liability model**: AIDA's use of criminal penalties for the most serious AI harms was notable as a model for AI governance combining regulatory and criminal law tools — similar to approaches later adopted in other jurisdictions.
