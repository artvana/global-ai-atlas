================================================================================
CALIFORNIA — AB 1137 (2025) — Reporting Mechanism: Child Sexual Abuse Material (AI-Generated)
2025–2026 Regular Session
================================================================================

CALIFORNIA ASSEMBLY BILL 1137
Introduced by Assembly Members Krell and Wicks
(Coauthors: Kalra, Macedo, Stefani)
February 20, 2025

An act to amend Sections 3273.65, 3273.66, 3273.67, and 3345.1 of the Civil Code,
relating to social media platforms.

LEGISLATIVE COUNSEL'S DIGEST

AB 1137, as amended, Krell. Reporting mechanism: child sexual abuse material.

Existing law requires social media companies to provide a mechanism for certain users to
report suspected child sexual abuse material and requires those companies to review reports
and take specified action within specified timeframes.

This bill would amend those provisions to require the reporting mechanism be clear and
conspicuous, remove the requirement that the reporting user be depicted in the material, add
a requirement that depicted individuals be shown as minors, require that reports be reviewed
via hash matching or, if no hash match exists and the material is not otherwise blocked, by a
natural person, require platforms to contact reporters within 36 hours and provide status
updates every 7 days, require a final determination within 30 days (or 60 days if
circumstances prevent faster action), establish civil penalties up to $250,000 per day for
nonfunctional reporting mechanisms, and provide for statutory damages for depicted
individuals of up to $250,000 per violation (reduced to $125,000 if material was blocked
before complaint filing, or $75,000 if platform participates in NCMEC's Take It Down
service).

This bill would also require social media platforms to submit to twice-yearly independent
third-party audits of designs and algorithms, with audit reports made public (with trade
secret redactions) within 90 days, as a compliance defense against claims of knowingly
facilitating commercial sexual exploitation.

THE PEOPLE OF THE STATE OF CALIFORNIA DO ENACT AS FOLLOWS:

SECTION 1. Section 3273.65 of the Civil Code is amended to read:

3273.65. For purposes of this article, the following definitions apply:

(a) "Child sexual abuse material" means child pornography, as defined in Section 311.4 of
the Penal Code, or obscene matter, as defined in Section 311 of the Penal Code, that depicts
a minor engaging in or simulating sexual conduct, as defined in subdivision (d) of Section
311.4 of the Penal Code, whether real or digitally created.

(b) "Clear and conspicuous" means that the mechanism for reporting is easily accessible and
visible to users, and is not obscured by dark patterns or design elements that discourage use.

(c) "Depicted individual" means an individual shown as a minor in child sexual abuse
material.

(d) "Reporting user" means a natural person who makes a report through the reporting
mechanism established pursuant to this article.

(e) "Social media company" has the same meaning as set forth in Section 22675 of the
Business and Professions Code, but does not include a nonprofit organization.

(f) "Social media platform" has the same meaning as set forth in Section 22675 of the
Business and Professions Code, but does not include a platform that provides end-to-end
encrypted communications or a nonprofit platform.

SECTION 2. Section 3273.66 of the Civil Code is amended to read:

3273.66. A social media platform shall do all of the following:

(a) Provide a clear and conspicuous mechanism for California residents to report material
that the reporting user reasonably believes is child sexual abuse material displayed or
accessible on the platform.

(b) Collect contact information from the reporting user via a method chosen by the reporting
user that is outside the direct control of the platform, and review reports through a hash-
matching process or, if there is not an established or known hash match for the reported
material and the material is not otherwise blocked, by a natural person.

(c) Contact the reporting user through the reporting user's chosen contact method within
36 hours of receiving the report, confirming receipt and providing information about the
review process.

(d) Permanently block access to instances of reported material on the platform if there is a
reasonable basis to believe the material constitutes child sexual abuse material and basic
identifying information permits its location.

(e) Provide written confirmation of receipt to the reporting user within 36 hours.

(f) Provide status updates to the reporting user every 7 days from the date of initial
confirmation until a final determination is made.

(g) Issue a final determination to the reporting user stating whether the reported material
was determined to be child sexual abuse material and blocked, was determined not to be
child sexual abuse material, or could not be found displayed on the platform. The final
determination shall be issued within 30 days of receipt of the report, or within 60 days if
circumstances beyond the reasonable control of the platform prevent a final determination
within 30 days, provided the platform notifies the reporting user of the delay within 48 hours.

SECTION 3. Section 3273.67 of the Civil Code is amended to read:

3273.67. (a) A social media platform that fails to maintain a functional reporting mechanism
as required by Section 3273.66 shall be liable for a civil penalty of up to two hundred fifty
thousand dollars ($250,000) for each day the reporting mechanism is unavailable or
nonfunctional. Civil penalties may be recovered by the Attorney General, a district attorney,
a city attorney, or a county counsel. The court may grant injunctive relief requiring immediate
restoration of the reporting mechanism.

For purposes of this subdivision, a reporting mechanism shall be deemed unavailable or
nonfunctional if any of the following applies:
(1) The reporting mechanism is inaccessible due to technical failures or its removal from the
platform.
(2) The reporting mechanism does not permit reporting in the manner required by
Section 3273.66.
(3) The platform fails to provide confirmation of receipt within the timeframes specified
in Section 3273.66.

(b) A depicted individual or a reporting user who is the depicted individual may bring a
civil action against a social media platform that violates subdivision (b), (c), (d), or (g) of
Section 3273.66. A prevailing plaintiff shall be entitled to:
(1) Actual damages sustained by the depicted individual as a result of the violation.
(2) Statutory damages in an amount up to two hundred fifty thousand dollars ($250,000) per
violation, or up to one hundred twenty-five thousand dollars ($125,000) if the platform
blocked the reported material before the filing of the complaint, or up to seventy-five
thousand dollars ($75,000) if the platform meets the requirements of the NCMEC Take It
Down service and the material was reported through that service before the filing of the
complaint.
(3) Reasonable attorney's fees and costs of litigation.

In determining the amount of statutory damages, the court shall consider the willfulness of
the violation, the severity of harm to the depicted individual, and any prior violations.

(c) A depicted individual who is not a reporting user may bring a civil action against a social
media platform that violates subdivision (d) of Section 3273.66.

(d) Failure to comply with the requirements of subdivision (b), (c), or (d) of Section 3273.66
within 60 days of receipt of the report shall create a rebuttable presumption that the depicted
individual is entitled to statutory damages under subdivision (b).

SECTION 4. Section 3345.1 of the Civil Code is amended to read:

3345.1. (a) A social media platform shall not knowingly facilitate, aid, or abet the
commercial sexual exploitation of a minor or a nonminor dependent, as those terms are
defined in Section 11165.1 of the Penal Code.

(b) For purposes of this section, a social media platform shall be deemed to have knowledge
of commercial sexual exploitation if material meeting specified criteria has been reported
through the platform's reporting mechanism for four consecutive months.

(c) A social media platform that violates subdivision (a) shall be liable for statutory damages
in an amount between one million dollars ($1,000,000) and four million dollars ($4,000,000)
per act of commercial sexual exploitation.

(d) It shall be an affirmative defense to liability under this section if the platform:
(1) Submits to twice-yearly third-party audits by independent auditors with expertise in
content moderation.
(2) Takes action within 30 days to mitigate risks identified in audits.
(3) Notifies its board of directors or equivalent governing body with a copy of each audit.
(4) Makes audit reports publicly available (with trade secret redactions) within 90 days of
completion.

(e) For purposes of this section, "facilitate, aid, or abet" means deploying systems or
features that substantially cause minors to become victims of commercial sexual exploitation.

(f) "Commercial sexual exploitation" includes violations of Sections 236.1, 266h, 266i,
266j, and 647(b) of the Penal Code.

SECTION 5. Severability.

If any provision of this act or its application to any person or circumstance is held invalid,
that invalidity shall not affect other provisions or applications that can be given effect without
the invalid provision or application.
