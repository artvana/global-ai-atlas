## Nevada Assembly Bill 406 (2025)
### Prohibition on Artificial Intelligence in Mental and Behavioral Healthcare Settings

**83rd Nevada Legislature, 2025 Regular Session**
**Enacted:** June 5, 2025 | **Effective:** July 1, 2025

---

## Overview

Nevada Assembly Bill 406 (2025) enacts the first broad US state-level prohibition on artificial intelligence systems providing — or substituting for — licensed professional mental and behavioral healthcare services. The law bars AI platforms and chatbots from performing clinical therapy, counseling, psychological assessment, or psychiatric services without a licensed human mental health professional actively overseeing and accountable for the care. It also restricts licensed professionals from using AI tools in ways that replace, rather than support, their clinical judgment.

AB 406 represents a significant departure from most state AI legislation, which focuses on disclosure and transparency rather than outright prohibition. Nevada's law reflects growing concern that consumer-facing AI therapy and mental health chatbot applications — particularly those marketed as substitutes for professional care — present unacceptable risks of harm to vulnerable populations.

---

## Legislative Background

The bill was introduced in response to the proliferation of AI-powered mental health applications and chatbots marketed to consumers as alternatives to professional therapy. Several high-profile incidents involving AI mental health applications — including cases where AI chatbots failed to recognize suicidal ideation or provided harmful advice — prompted Nevada legislators to act.

Unlike disclosure-based AI mental health laws (such as California's mental health AI disclosure requirements), Nevada AB 406 takes a prohibition-first approach: AI cannot practice mental healthcare regardless of any disclosure to the consumer.

## Scope and Covered Services

### Section 1 — Definitions

**Artificial Intelligence System** — Any machine-based system that uses algorithms, machine learning, neural networks, or similar computational approaches to generate outputs including recommendations, diagnoses, predictions, or therapeutic responses, in the context of mental or behavioral healthcare.

**Mental or Behavioral Healthcare Services** — Services including but not limited to:
- Individual, group, couples, or family psychotherapy or counseling;
- Psychological assessment, testing, or diagnosis;
- Psychiatric evaluation, medication management, or treatment;
- Substance use disorder counseling or treatment;
- Crisis intervention and suicide risk assessment;
- Applied behavior analysis or similar behavioral therapy.

**Licensed Mental Health Professional** — Includes licensed clinical social workers (LCSWs), licensed professional counselors (LPCs), licensed marriage and family therapists (LMFTs), licensed psychologists, licensed psychiatrists, and other individuals licensed under Nevada Revised Statutes to provide mental or behavioral healthcare.

**AI Mental Health Platform** — Any software application, chatbot, digital platform, or automated system that uses artificial intelligence to engage users in therapeutic dialogue, provide mental health advice, conduct assessments, or simulate the functions of a licensed mental health professional.

## Core Prohibitions

### Section 2 — Prohibition on AI Practice of Mental Healthcare

1. No AI mental health platform may:
   - Represent itself as a therapist, counselor, psychologist, or psychiatrist, or use any similar title suggesting licensure;
   - Conduct or purport to conduct psychological assessments, diagnoses, or clinical evaluations;
   - Provide therapeutic interventions as the primary or sole provider of care without a licensed professional's active oversight;
   - Conduct or respond to crisis intervention or suicide risk screening without immediately connecting the individual to a licensed professional or emergency services;
   - Bill or accept payment for mental healthcare services as if it were a licensed provider.

2. AI mental health platforms operating in Nevada must:
   - Clearly disclose at the outset of each interaction that the user is communicating with an AI system, not a licensed human professional;
   - Provide clear and accessible information about how to access human licensed mental health services;
   - Immediately refer users expressing suicidal ideation, self-harm, or mental health emergencies to licensed professionals or emergency services.

### Section 3 — Restrictions on Licensed Professionals

1. A licensed mental health professional who uses AI tools in a clinical setting:
   - May not delegate clinical judgment, diagnostic decision-making, or therapeutic intervention decisions to an AI system;
   - Must retain ultimate professional responsibility for all care decisions, regardless of AI input;
   - Must disclose to patients, in writing at the outset of treatment, any AI tools used in the delivery of care.

2. A licensed professional who violates this section may be subject to disciplinary action by the applicable licensing board, including suspension or revocation of licensure.

---

## Disclosure Requirements

### Section 4 — Mandatory Consumer Disclosure

All AI mental health platforms operating in Nevada, whether or not providing clinical services, must:

1. Display a prominent, clear, and conspicuous disclosure at all points of user entry stating:
   *"This is an artificial intelligence system. It is not a licensed mental health professional and cannot provide mental healthcare, diagnosis, or therapy."*

2. Provide links to the Nevada Division of Public and Behavioral Health crisis resources and the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline at each interaction session.

3. Refrain from any marketing, advertising, or representation — whether direct or implied — suggesting that the AI platform is equivalent to or a substitute for professional licensed mental health care.

---

## Exemptions

The following are not prohibited under AB 406:

1. **Clinical Decision Support Tools** — AI systems used as clinical decision support tools by licensed professionals, where the professional retains ultimate decision-making authority and the AI does not interact directly with patients in a therapeutic capacity.

2. **Administrative and Scheduling Functions** — AI used for appointment scheduling, billing, insurance verification, or administrative tasks in mental health practices.

3. **Psychoeducation and Information** — AI platforms that provide general mental health information, psychoeducation, or wellness content without conducting therapeutic interactions or clinical assessments.

4. **Research Contexts** — AI systems used in IRB-approved clinical research, with appropriate informed consent.

5. **Symptom Checkers** — General health AI tools that screen for symptoms and recommend professional consultation without providing ongoing therapy or clinical assessment.

---

## Enforcement

### Section 5 — Licensing Board Jurisdiction

The following boards have authority to enforce AB 406 with respect to licensees:
- Nevada State Board of Psychological Examiners;
- Nevada State Board of Examiners for Marriage and Family Therapists and Clinical Professional Counselors;
- Nevada State Board of Medical Examiners (for psychiatrists);
- Nevada State Board of Nursing (for psychiatric nurse practitioners).

Each board may investigate complaints, conduct hearings, and impose disciplinary sanctions including:
- License suspension or revocation;
- Probation with conditions;
- Civil fines up to **$10,000 per violation**.

### Section 6 — Attorney General Enforcement

The Nevada Attorney General may bring civil enforcement actions against non-licensed entities (including AI platform operators) that violate the Act's prohibitions, and may seek:
- Civil penalties up to **$10,000 per violation**;
- Injunctive relief;
- Consumer restitution.

---

## Relationship to Existing Nevada Law

AB 406 supplements Nevada's existing mental health licensing framework under NRS Chapter 641 (Psychology), NRS Chapter 641A (Marriage and Family Therapy), and NRS Chapter 641B (Social Work). It does not repeal or modify existing licensing requirements but adds specific restrictions on the use of AI in licensed settings.

The Act also does not limit any applicable federal law, including HIPAA privacy requirements for AI tools processing protected health information in healthcare contexts.

## Significance

Nevada AB 406 is notable as the first broad US state law to prohibit AI from practicing professional mental and behavioral healthcare services. While several states have enacted disclosure requirements for AI mental health tools, Nevada goes further by treating unauthorized AI clinical practice as a per se violation subject to civil penalty and professional licensing sanctions. This positions Nevada alongside a small number of jurisdictions worldwide that have adopted prohibition-based — rather than transparency-based — approaches to high-stakes AI deployment in vulnerable service contexts.

The law reflects broader legislative concern about direct-to-consumer AI therapy applications, which have grown rapidly in the market but operate in a regulatory grey zone that pre-existing professional licensing law did not anticipate.
