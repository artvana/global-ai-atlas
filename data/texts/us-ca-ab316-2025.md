# California Assembly Bill 316 — Artificial Intelligence Autonomous Harm Defense Limitation

**Assembly Bill No. 316**

*California — October 13, 2025*

---

## Key Provisions

### Purpose and Scope

AB 316 addresses the use of "autonomous AI" as a liability shield in civil litigation. The bill modifies California civil law to prevent AI developers, modifiers, and deployers from evading legal responsibility by claiming that an AI system caused harm autonomously, independent of human involvement.

### Prohibition on the Autonomous AI Defense

In any civil action where a plaintiff alleges harm caused by an AI system, the defendant — whether a developer, modifier, or deployer of the AI system — may not assert as a complete defense that the AI system acted autonomously or without human involvement in causing the alleged harm. This eliminates the argument that the harm was caused entirely by the AI, not by the humans who built, modified, or deployed it.

### Preserved Defenses

The bill expressly preserves all other civil defenses available to defendants, including:
- Causation (challenging whether the AI system actually caused the plaintiff's harm)
- Foreseeability (arguing the harm was not a foreseeable consequence of the AI's use)
- Assumption of risk
- Comparative negligence
- Any other applicable defense under California civil law

### Policy Rationale

The Legislature found that allowing defendants to claim complete immunity because an AI system acted "autonomously" would undermine accountability for the design, training, modification, and deployment choices made by human actors. The bill does not create strict liability; it simply forecloses one specific defense strategy.

### Effective Date

The act takes effect January 1, 2026.
