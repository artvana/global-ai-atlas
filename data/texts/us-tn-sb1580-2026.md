# Tennessee SB 1580 — Prohibition on Healthcare AI Chatbots Claiming Professional Status

**Enacted April 1, 2026 | Amends T.C.A. Title 63 (Professions of the Healing Arts)**

---

## Overview

Tennessee SB 1580 prohibits healthcare-related AI chatbots and automated systems from representing themselves as licensed healthcare professionals, and requires clear disclosures when AI systems provide health-related advice, diagnoses, or treatment recommendations. The Act amends Tennessee's existing healthcare professional licensing laws to address AI impersonation of licensed practitioners.

---

## Definitions

**"Healthcare AI chatbot"** means any automated conversational system, virtual assistant, or artificial intelligence application that:
(a) interacts with users through natural language interfaces, whether voice or text;
(b) provides, generates, or facilitates information about health conditions, symptoms, medications, diagnoses, or treatments; and
(c) is deployed by or on behalf of a healthcare entity, or is made publicly available to individuals seeking healthcare information.

**"Licensed healthcare professional"** has the meaning as defined in T.C.A. § 63-1-102 and includes physicians, nurses, pharmacists, mental health professionals, and all other licensed healing arts practitioners.

**"Healthcare entity"** means any hospital, clinic, pharmacy, insurance company, telehealth provider, or other organization providing healthcare services or benefits management in Tennessee.

---

## T.C.A. § 63-1-150 — Prohibition on AI Impersonation of Healthcare Professionals

**(a) Prohibition.** No healthcare AI chatbot may:
(1) Represent that it is a licensed healthcare professional or that its outputs constitute the professional judgment of a licensed healthcare professional;
(2) Use a human name together with a professional title (such as "Dr.," "R.N.," "Pharm.D.," or similar) in a manner that would lead a reasonable person to believe they are communicating with a human licensed professional;
(3) Represent that a diagnosis, treatment recommendation, or drug dosage advice generated by the AI system has been personally reviewed or approved by a specific licensed healthcare professional, unless that professional has in fact reviewed and approved the specific output;
(4) Fail to disclose, at the beginning of each interaction, that the user is communicating with an artificial intelligence system.

**(b) Required AI disclosure.** Every healthcare AI chatbot must disclose at the beginning of each user interaction, clearly and conspicuously: **"You are interacting with an artificial intelligence system, not a licensed healthcare professional. AI-generated health information is not a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment."**

The disclosure must:
- Appear in plainly legible text before any health-related content is provided
- Be repeated if the session is interrupted and resumed
- Be accessible to users with disabilities in accessible formats

---

## T.C.A. § 63-1-151 — Scope of Permissible AI Healthcare Assistance

**(a) Permitted uses.** Nothing in this Act prohibits:
(1) AI systems that provide general wellness information clearly labeled as not constituting medical advice
(2) AI-assisted clinical decision support tools that are clearly identified as AI and that route recommendations through supervising licensed professionals before delivery to patients
(3) Administrative AI systems for scheduling, billing, or insurance verification
(4) AI systems used for research or educational purposes

**(b) Professional oversight.** AI systems that assist licensed healthcare professionals in clinical decision-making must:
(1) Make clear to the patient or consumer that AI is involved in the process
(2) Ensure that a licensed professional reviews and takes responsibility for any diagnosis, prescription, or treatment plan before it is communicated to a patient

---

## T.C.A. § 63-1-152 — Enforcement

**(a) Civil penalties.** The Tennessee Department of Health may assess civil penalties against any healthcare entity or operator of a healthcare AI chatbot that violates this Act:
- First violation: civil penalty up to $5,000
- Second violation: civil penalty up to $25,000
- Third or subsequent violations: civil penalty up to $50,000 per violation

**(b) License consequences.** A licensed healthcare professional who operates a healthcare AI chatbot in violation of this section is subject to disciplinary action by the relevant licensing board, including license suspension or revocation.

**(c) Private right of action.** Any individual who suffers harm as a result of a violation of this Act may bring a civil action for actual damages, injunctive relief, and attorney's fees.

---

## Effective Date

This Act takes effect July 1, 2026. Healthcare entities deploying AI chatbots prior to that date have until July 1, 2026 to bring their systems into compliance.
