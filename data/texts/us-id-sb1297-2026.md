# Conversational AI Safety Act

**Idaho Senate Bill No. 1297, As Amended**
*Sixty-eighth Idaho Legislature, Second Regular Session — 2026*
*Effective: July 1, 2027*

*Amending Title 48, Idaho Code, by adding Chapter 21*

---

## § 48-2101 — Short title

This chapter may be cited as the **"Conversational AI Safety Act."**

---

## § 48-2102 — Definitions

**(1) "Account holder"** — an individual who has or opens an account or profile to use a conversational AI service.

**(2) "Conversational AI service"** — an AI software application, web interface, or computer program accessible to the general public that primarily simulates human conversation through textual, visual, or aural communications.

**Does NOT include:**
- Applications primarily designed and marketed for use by developers or researchers;
- A feature within another application that is not itself a conversational AI service;
- Video game chatbots limited to game-related replies that do not discuss mental health, self-harm, or material harmful to minors;
- Applications designed to provide outputs relating to a narrow and discrete topic;
- Applications primarily designed and marketed for commercial use by business entities (employees, contractors, or clients), whether cloud, on-premises, or hybrid;
- Speaker/voice command interfaces or voice-activated virtual assistants for consumer electronics;
- Applications used by a business solely for internal purposes;
- Applications accessible only to individuals under a commercial/enterprise agreement with the operator; or
- Chatbots used only for customer service, operational purposes, productivity, or analysis of source information, internal research, or technical assistance.

**(3) "Individual"** — a natural person.

**(4) "Minor"** — an individual under circumstances where an operator has **actual knowledge or reasonable certainty** that the individual is under 18 years of age.

**(5) "Minor account holder"** — an account holder who is a minor.

**(6) "Operator"** — a person who makes available a conversational AI service to the public. Does **not** include mobile application stores or search engines solely because they provide access to a conversational AI service.

**(7) "Person"** — a natural person or legal entity.

**(8) "Sexually explicit conduct"** and **"visual depiction"** — same meanings as in 18 U.S.C. § 2256.

---

## § 48-2103 — Operator requirements (General)

**(1) AI disclosure where confusion with a human is possible.** If reasonable persons would be misled to believe they are interacting with a human, the operator shall **clearly and conspicuously disclose** that the service is artificial intelligence.

**(2) Suicide-response protocol.** Operators shall adopt a protocol for responding to user prompts regarding suicidal ideation. The protocol shall include making reasonable efforts to provide a response that refers users to crisis service providers such as a suicide hotline, crisis text line, or other appropriate crisis services.

**(3) No false representations of professional mental health care.** Operators shall not knowingly and intentionally cause or program a conversational AI service to make any representation or statement explicitly indicating that the service is designed to provide professional mental or behavioral health care.

---

## § 48-2104 — Operator requirements — Minors

**(1) Persistent AI disclosure to minor account holders.** Operators shall clearly and conspicuously disclose to minor account holders that they are interacting with artificial intelligence, either:
- **(a)** As a **persistent visible disclaimer**; or
- **(b)** Both: (i) at the beginning of each session; **and** (ii) appearing at least every **3 hours** in a continuous interaction.

**(2) No variable-reward engagement mechanics for minors.** Where an operator knows or has reasonable certainty that an account holder is a minor, the operator shall not provide points or similar rewards at **unpredictable intervals** with the intent to encourage increased engagement.

**(3) Sexual-content safeguards for minors.** For minor account holders, operators shall institute reasonable measures to prevent the service from:
- **(a)** Producing visual material of sexually explicit conduct;
- **(b)** Generating direct statements that the account holder should engage in sexually explicit conduct; or
- **(c)** Generating statements that sexually objectify the account holder.

**(4) Anti-anthropomorphism safeguards for minors.** For minor account holders, operators shall institute reasonable measures to prevent the service from generating statements that would lead reasonable persons to believe they are interacting with a human, including:
- **(a)** Explicit claims that the service is sentient or human;
- **(b)** Statements that simulate emotional dependence;
- **(c)** Statements that simulate romantic or sexual innuendos; or
- **(d)** Role-playing of adult-minor romantic relationships.

**(5) Privacy and account-management tools.** Operators shall offer tools for account holders — and, for those under 13, their parents or guardians — to manage privacy and account settings. Related tools shall also be offered to parents or guardians of minor account holders 13 and older, as appropriate based on relevant risks.

---

## § 48-2105 — Penalties and enforcement

**(1) Civil penalties.** Operators violating this chapter are subject to injunction and liable for civil penalties of:
- **$1,000 per violation**, not to exceed **$500,000 per operator**, **or**
- **actual damages**,

whichever is greater.

**(2) Attorney General enforcement; no private right of action.** Civil penalties are sought by the Attorney General. This chapter does **not** create a private right of action to enforce its provisions or support a private right of action under any other law.

**(3) No developer liability for third-party operator violations.** This chapter does not create liability for the **developer of an AI model** for violations by a conversational AI system made available to the public by a third-party operator.

*Effective: July 1, 2027*
