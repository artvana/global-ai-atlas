                                             ACT

To create the "Media and Information Literacy Act of Puerto Rico" with the
    purpose of guaranteeing that the student body of the public education system develops
    media and digital literacy competencies where the responsible use of technology
    and artificial intelligence (AI) is fostered, and to strengthen citizen
    resilience in the face of disinformation and digital manipulation; and for other
    related purposes.

                              STATEMENT OF MOTIVES

     The expansion of digital media and artificial intelligence has radically
transformed the way in which society accesses, produces and shares information. In
this environment of immediacy and viralization, citizens are faced with a constant flow
of false or manipulated information that compromises the collective capacity to make
informed decisions and participate responsibly in democratic life.

     The phenomenon of digital disinformation constitutes one of the greatest challenges of
our era. Online platforms have democratized the creation of content,
but have also fostered the proliferation of erroneous information, conspiracy
theories and manipulation campaigns that spread with unprecedented
rapidity. Recommendation algorithms create "filter bubbles" that reinforce
preexisting beliefs and limit exposure to diverse ideas.
                                           2


     Children and youth are especially vulnerable to this informational crisis.
They spend most of their time in digital environments, where the majority lack
critical tools to distinguish between truthful and deceptive information. Their stage of
cognitive and emotional development makes them more susceptible to the effects of
media manipulation, affecting their well-being, self-esteem and trust in
institutions. This gap between the technical skills to use technology and the
critical understanding of the media ecosystem represents a real risk for their
intellectual and civic development.

     In Puerto Rico, the experience of recent years has shown how
informational challenges can be amplified in moments of crisis. Following events such as
hurricane María, the earthquakes and the COVID-19 pandemic, the dissemination of
false information impacted public trust and collective well-being. Puerto Rican youth,
exposed to a growing volume of digital content, need critical skills to
distinguish between truthful and deceptive information, regardless of the context in which
they study or live. Promoting media literacy in all schools ensures
equitable opportunities for each student to be able to be formed as an informed,
reflective and resilient citizen.

     Jurisdictions such as California have recognized this urgency through the
approval of Act AB 873 (2023) and its expansion in 2024, which integrate
media and digital literacy in the K-12 school curriculum. These initiatives
recognize that media literacy is not a complement, but rather an essential
competency for the citizenship of the 21st Century. Countries such as Finland, Sweden, and Canada have
adopted similar strategies with proven results in democratic resilience and
critical thinking.

     This Act is based on Article II, Section 5 of the Constitution of Puerto
Rico, which guarantees the right to an education that promotes the integral development of
the person and respect for human rights. Likewise, it harmonizes with Act 85-
2018, as amended, better known as the "Educational Reform Act of Puerto
                                                 3


    Rico", which promotes pedagogical innovation, school autonomy, and the formation of
    critical and responsible citizens.

         In view of the magnitude of the contemporary informational challenge, Puerto Rico must
    respond in an educational, structured and sustained manner. It is not enough to warn about the
    dangers of disinformation: it is imperative to train students capable of analyzing
    sources, identifying biases, verifying facts and exercising their freedom of expression with ethics and
    responsibility.

         Therefore, this Legislative Assembly establishes a legal framework to integrate
    media and digital literacy as an essential tool of critical thinking,
    autonomous learning and democratic participation. With this, the aim is to
    strengthen individual and collective resilience in the face of informational manipulation,
    protect the integral well-being of children and youth, and ensure that the next
    generations of Puerto Ricans grow up prepared to participate, with criterion and
    commitment, in the digital ecosystem that defines the modern world.

    BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY OF PUERTO RICO:

1          Article 1.- Title

2          This Act shall be known as the "Media and Information Literacy Act

3   of Puerto Rico".

4          Article 2.- Declaration of Purposes

5          This Act has as its fundamental purposes:

6          a)   To foster in the student body the skills necessary to analyze and use with

7   responsibility the information and digital technology.

8          b) To protect children and youth from the effects of disinformation,

9   media manipulation and harmful digital content.
                                                  4


 1          c) To strengthen the capacity of the public educational system to form

 2   critical, informed and responsible citizens in the digital environment.

 3          Article 3.- Definitions

 4          For the purposes of this Act, the following terms shall have the meaning

 5   indicated below:

 6          a) Media and information literacy: Set of knowledge,

 7   skills and dispositions that allow access, analysis, critical evaluation, creation, and

 8   sharing of messages and information in multiple formats (printed, audiovisual and

 9   digital) in a responsible, ethical and effective manner.

10          b) Manipulated content: Material such as photos, videos or messages, that has been

11   altered, edited out of context or generated with programs or artificial intelligence

12   (such as so-called deepfakes) that appears to be real but is not, with the purpose of

13   distorting reality or deceiving the public.

14          c) Disinformation: False, misleading or partial information disseminated with the

15   deliberate intention of manipulating perceptions, influencing behaviors, generating

16   confusion.

17          d) Artificial Intelligence (AI): Computational systems that use algorithms

18   to process information and perform tasks that simulate human intelligence.

19          e) Curricular framework: Structure that defines the contents and essential

20   competencies of the official curriculum of the Department of Education.

21          f) Microcredential: Educational certification that accredits specific skills in

22   media and digital literacy.
                                                  5


 1          g) Algorithmic bias: situation in which a digital or artificial intelligence

 2   system shows information or makes decisions in a partial manner, favoring some

 3   ideas or groups more than others. This may occur because the data used to

 4   program the system contains errors or reflects social prejudices.

 5          h) Information verification (fact-checking): Systematic process of

 6   corroboration of factual statements through the analysis of primary sources,

 7   documentary evidence, objective data and recognized research methods.

 8          Article 4.- Public Policy

 9          The Government of Puerto Rico declares as a priority public policy the

10   systematic integration of media and information literacy as a

11   transversal and permanent component of the curriculum of the public education system.

12   This policy recognizes that in the era of digital information, the capacity to critically

13   analyze media content, identify disinformation, understand the

14   functioning of emerging technologies, and participate responsibly in digital

15   spaces constitutes a fundamental and indispensable competency for the full

16   exercise of democratic citizenship, integral personal development, and preparation

17   for the workplace of the 21st Century.

18          This public policy seeks to ensure that each student develops the competencies

19   necessary to analyze information, identify disinformation, understand the

20   functioning of modern technologies, and participate responsibly in

21   digital spaces.

22          Article 5.- Curricular Integration
                                                   6


 1          a) The Department of Education of Puerto Rico (DEPR) shall transversally

 2   incorporate media and information literacy competencies in the

 3   curricular frameworks of all subjects at the elementary, intermediate and superior level, with particular

 4   emphasis on the subjects of Spanish, Social Studies, Sciences and Mathematics,

 5   beginning within a term not exceeding two (2) academic years.

 6          b) The media and information literacy competencies that

 7   shall be integrated into the curriculum shall include, but not be limited to:

 8          1. Critical evaluation of sources and verification of information.

 9          2. Identification of disinformation and manipulation, whether false information,

10             deepfakes, manipulated images, sensationalist headlines (clickbait) and other

11             forms of media manipulation.

12          3. Basic understanding of the functioning of algorithms and artificial intelligence.

13          4. Verification of facts and corroboration, application of fact-

14             checking methodologies, use of digital verification tools, reverse search of

15             images, consultation of multiple reliable sources, and triangulation of

16             information to confirm veracity.

17          5. Responsible production of digital content, including factual

18             accuracy, adequate citation of sources, and responsibility in the dissemination of

19             information.

20          6. Respect for privacy, intellectual property and digital rights.

21          7. Development of healthy habits in the use of technology.
                                                  7


 1          c) The DEPR shall actively promote interdisciplinary projects and practical

 2   activities where the student body applies these skills in real life. These activities

 3   shall include, but not be limited to:

 4          1. Comparative analysis of news coverage on current events from

 5               different media sources.

 6          2. Collaborative verification of viral statements on social media using

 7               fact-checking tools.

 8          3. Creation of community educational campaigns on identification of

 9               disinformation.

10          4. Production of responsible and verified multimedia informative content.

11          5. Simulations of debates and discussions on ethical dilemmas related to

12               technology and media.

13          6. Investigations on the impact of algorithms on the formation of public

14               opinion.

15          Article 6.- Teacher Training and Professional Development

16          a)   The DEPR, in collaboration with universities, public and

17   private accredited institutions, specialized organizations, and experts in educational technology,

18   shall develop a continuous teacher training program in media and digital literacy

19   that allows the teaching staff to constantly update their knowledge

20   in the face of the accelerated evolution of the digital system.

21          b) This program shall include virtual and in-person training modules, practical

22   workshops, professional learning communities and self-training resources. The
                                                8


 1   Certifications or microcredentials obtained shall count as hours of professional

 2   development.

 3          c)   Initial priority shall be given to teachers of Spanish, Social Studies,

 4   Librarians and Specialists in Educational Technology, progressively expanding the

 5   training to all the teaching personnel.

 6          Article 7.- Evaluation, Monitoring and Reports

 7          The DEPR shall prepare and submit a detailed annual report to the Legislative

 8   Assembly of Puerto Rico, that includes:

 9          a) The progress of curricular implementation.

10          b) The number of teachers who have completed training in media

11   and digital literacy.

12          c) Status of curricular implementation at each educational level.

13          d) The schools that have integrated the program.

14          e) Results of evaluations and recommendations for improvement.

15          f) Identification of challenges, obstacles and barriers encountered during

16   implementation.

17          g) Specific and substantiated recommendations to improve the effectiveness

18   of the program, including additional resource needs, curricular adjustments or

19   legislative changes.

20          Article 8.- Regulations

21          The Secretary of the Department of Education of Puerto Rico shall adopt,

22   promulgate and publish, within a term not greater than one hundred and eighty (180) days
                                                    9


 1   from the date of approval of this Act, the regulations, administrative norms,

 2   and technical guides necessary for the effective implementation of all the provisions

 3   contained in this Act.

 4           Article 9.- Severability Clause

 5           If any article, section, paragraph, sentence, clause or part of this Act is

 6   declared unconstitutional, null or invalid by a court with competent jurisdiction,

 7   such determination shall not affect, prejudice or invalidate the rest of this Act, the

 8   effect of which shall be limited to the article, section, paragraph, sentence, clause or part thereof so

 9   declared unconstitutional, null or invalid.

10           Article 10.- Implementation

11           The initial implementation of this Act shall be carried out through the optimization

12   of human, technological, financial and infrastructure resources existing in the

13   Department of Education, maximizing the use of low-cost or free virtual platforms,

14   open educational resources, collaborative alliances with universities

15   and non-profit organizations, and training in hybrid and virtual modalities

16   that reduce operational costs.

17           The DEPR may coordinate with the Office of Management and Budget and the

18   Department of the Treasury the identification of funds for the implementation of this

19   Act.

20          The Department shall complete its full implementation within a term not greater

21   than two (2) academic years from its promulgation, allowing for a gradual,
                                            10


1   reflective and quality implementation that ensures effectiveness and

2   sustainability.

3        Article 11.- Effective Date

4        This Act shall take effect immediately after its approval.
