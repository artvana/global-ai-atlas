# Decreto No. 64/020 — Reglamentación de decisiones individuales automatizadas — Uruguay
**Decreto del Poder Ejecutivo No. 64/020**
*Uruguay — March 5, 2020*

---

## Overview

Uruguay's Executive Decree No. 64/020 of March 5, 2020, regulates individual automated decision-making within the framework of Law 18.331 on Personal Data Protection. This decree is one of Latin America's most advanced AI-specific data protection rules, adopted prior to any comprehensive AI legislation in the region. It establishes specific requirements for organizations that make decisions based solely on automated processing — including profiling — when those decisions significantly affect individuals. The decree is inspired by Article 22 of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and represents a meaningful step toward algorithmic accountability in Uruguay. The URCDP (Unidad Reguladora y de Control de Datos Personales) oversees compliance.

---

## Key Provisions

### Scope of Automated Decision-Making Regulation
- Applies to any decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, that produces legal effects concerning a data subject or similarly significantly affects them
- Covers decisions in areas including credit assessment, employment, healthcare prioritization, insurance pricing, and similar consequential domains
- Applies to both public and private sector organizations

### Right to Human Intervention
- Individuals subject to significant automated decisions have the right to request meaningful human intervention in the decision-making process
- The human review must be substantive — not a mere formality — and must involve a competent person who can genuinely influence the outcome
- Organizations must establish a procedure for receiving and processing such requests within a reasonable timeframe

### Right to Explanation
- Data subjects have the right to receive an explanation of the logic, criteria, and main factors that influenced an automated decision affecting them
- Explanations must be intelligible and meaningful — not merely a reference to the existence of automated processing
- Controllers must be able to demonstrate the factors underlying specific decisions

### Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs)
- Organizations conducting high-risk automated decision-making must carry out DPIAs before implementing or significantly changing their systems
- DPIA must assess risks to data subjects' rights and freedoms and identify measures to mitigate those risks
- DPIAs may be required to be submitted to URCDP for prior consultation in certain circumstances

### Prohibited Automated Decisions
- Automated decisions based solely on sensitive personal data categories (health, political opinions, biometric data, etc.) are prohibited unless specific exceptions apply (explicit consent, vital interests, or specific legislative authorization)

### Transparency Obligations
- Organizations must proactively inform data subjects when they are subject to automated decision-making
- Information must be provided at the time of data collection or at the time of the decision
- Privacy notices must specifically disclose automated decision-making practices

### Accountability and Documentation
- Organizations must maintain records of automated decision-making systems and their logic
- Records must be made available to URCDP upon request
- Controllers must implement appropriate technical safeguards to prevent discriminatory or arbitrary outcomes

## Enforcement & Penalties

- Enforcement through URCDP under the framework of Law 18.331
- URCDP may investigate complaints from individuals who believe their rights under the decree have been violated
- Administrative sanctions including formal warnings, compliance orders, and fines under Law 18.331
- Data subjects retain habeas data judicial remedy for enforcement of rights
- Organizations that fail to provide human review or explanation may be subject to enforcement action
