# Kingdom of Bahrain — AI Ethics Principles

**Information & eGovernment Authority (iGA), Kingdom of Bahrain**

*Adopted: July 2025 (formal adoption announced July 27, 2025)*

*Note: Bahrain formally adopted the GCC Guiding Manual on AI Ethics (November 2023) as its national AI ethics framework, rather than publishing a separate Bahrain-specific document. This record documents Bahrain's adoption and the principles applied. For the full text of the GCC AI Ethics Manual, see the record `gcc-gcc-aiethics-2023.md`. The DGA (dga.gov.bh) AI Ethics Principles page was inaccessible at the time of this record's creation.*

---

## Overview

Under directives from the Minister of Interior, the Kingdom of Bahrain launched its national AI policy framework and formally adopted the GCC Guiding Manual on AI Ethics on July 27, 2025. This adoption establishes the GCC AI ethics principles as binding guidance for government entities in Bahrain.

The adoption was announced alongside the launch of Bahrain's **General Policy for the Use of Artificial Intelligence** (Version 1.0, May 2025), published by the Information & eGovernment Authority (iGA). For the full text of that policy, see the record `bh-bh-aigenpolicy-2025.md`.

---

## Core AI Ethics Principles (GCC Manual, as Adopted by Bahrain)

Bahrain's adoption of the GCC Guiding Manual on AI Ethics establishes the following foundational principles:

### 1. Preserving Human Autonomy

AI systems must be designed and deployed in ways that respect and support human decision-making authority. AI should augment human capabilities, not replace meaningful human agency in decisions affecting individuals' rights and wellbeing.

### 2. Ensuring System Safety and Reliability

All AI applications must be safe, reliable, and resilient. Systems should be tested and validated before deployment, operated within their design parameters, and monitored for safety throughout their operational life.

### 3. Promoting Justice and Non-Discrimination

AI systems must treat all individuals fairly and equitably, without discrimination based on race, religion, gender, or other non-objective factors. AI applications must be designed to prevent and mitigate bias.

### 4. Protecting Privacy and Data Security

AI systems must comply with applicable data protection laws, including Bahrain's Personal Data Protection Law. Personal and sensitive data must be protected through appropriate technical and organisational controls, and data minimisation principles must be applied.

## Relationship to Bahrain's Broader AI Policy Framework

Bahrain's adoption of the GCC AI Ethics Manual operates alongside:

- **General Policy for the Use of Artificial Intelligence** (iGA, May 2025) — the national operational policy governing government entities' use of AI, organised around four pillars: (1) legal compliance, (2) AI use and adoption, (3) public education and awareness, and (4) local and international cooperation;
- **Personal Data Protection Law** (Bahrain, 2018) — binding data protection obligations applicable to AI systems processing personal data; and
- **GCC regional frameworks** — Bahrain participates in GCC-level AI governance coordination.

---

## Target Audience

The AI ethics framework applies to:
- Government officials and decision-makers;
- Academics and researchers;
- Citizens and residents interacting with government AI systems; and
- Training organisations developing AI capabilities in Bahrain.
