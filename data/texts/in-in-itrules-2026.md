## Summary and Analysis

### Purpose and Scope

The Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Amendment Rules, 2026 — colloquially referred to as the "IT Rules Amendment 2026" or the "AI/SGI Labeling Rules" — were notified by the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) of the Government of India on 10 February 2026, amending the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021 (the "IT Rules 2021"). These amendments represent India's most targeted regulatory response to date to the proliferation of AI-generated content and synthetic media, and reflect the government's concerns about the risks of deepfakes, AI-generated disinformation, and the erosion of trust in authentic content in India's vast social media environment.

The 2026 amendments focus specifically on obligations for **Significant Social Media Intermediaries (SSMIs)** and **AI platform providers** in relation to: mandatory labeling of AI-generated content including text, images, audio, and video; disclosure requirements for synthetic media; and technical standards for content provenance. The amendments do not constitute a comprehensive AI governance law — that is addressed through India's broader AI policy framework and sector-specific guidance — but instead target the specific and acute problem of undisclosed AI-generated content circulating through large digital platforms.

**Significant Social Media Intermediaries (SSMIs)** are defined under the IT Rules 2021 as intermediaries that primarily enable online interaction between users and allow users to create, upload, and share information, and that have more than 50 lakh (5 million) registered users in India. Major platforms covered include social media networks, video sharing platforms, messaging applications (to the extent they carry content publicly), and AI-powered content generation platforms that allow users to create and share content.

The 2026 amendments also apply to providers of **Generative AI (GenAI) services** and **Synthetic Media tools** that are made available to users in India, whether or not those providers are established in India. This extraterritorial application mirrors the approach taken in IT Rules 2021 for intermediary obligations generally.

The policy rationale for the 2026 amendments is grounded in the serious risks that undisclosed AI-generated content poses to the Indian democratic process, social cohesion, journalistic integrity, and individual reputation. The rapid proliferation of AI-generated "deepfake" videos and audio impersonating politicians, public figures, and private individuals — particularly in the lead-up to and during elections — and the mass generation of AI-produced disinformation content through social media platforms, motivated the government to act through targeted intermediary obligations rather than waiting for comprehensive AI legislation.

### Key Definitions Introduced by the 2026 Amendments

**AI-generated content** means any content — including text, images, video, audio, or combinations thereof — created substantially by an artificial intelligence system, including large language models, image generation models, voice synthesis systems, and video generation systems.

**Synthetic media** means any audio or video content that has been digitally manipulated, generated, or fabricated using AI techniques to depict a real person saying or doing something they did not say or do, or to create a realistic-appearing depiction of a non-existent person or event. Synthetic media includes but is not limited to "deepfake" content.

**AI labeling** means a visible, prominent, and machine-readable disclosure embedded in or attached to AI-generated content that informs users that the content was created or significantly altered by an artificial intelligence system.

**Content provenance** means the verifiable record of a piece of content's origin, creation process, and modification history, enabling platforms and users to assess whether content was created by a human, an AI system, or a combination.

**Generative AI service** means any online platform or service that enables users to generate or significantly alter content using AI, including text-to-image services, text-to-video services, AI voice cloning services, AI-powered writing assistants, and similar capabilities.

**Significant social media intermediary (SSMI)** retains its definition from IT Rules 2021 — an intermediary primarily enabling user interactions with more than 50 lakh registered users in India.

### Mandatory Labeling Obligations for SSMIs

**AI Content Labeling Requirement.** SSMIs are required to implement technical mechanisms to detect and label AI-generated content uploaded or shared by users on their platforms. Specifically:

- Where an SSMI has knowledge or reasonable grounds to believe that content posted on its platform was generated by an AI system, the SSMI must ensure that the content bears a visible and clear AI label informing viewers that the content was AI-generated.
- Where an AI-generated content posting tool is built into the SSMI's own platform (such as an integrated text-to-image generator), the SSMI must automatically and mandatorily apply an AI label to any content produced by that tool at the time of creation.
- SSMIs must implement automated detection mechanisms to identify AI-generated content, including through content provenance technologies such as digital watermarks, cryptographic signatures, and metadata analysis, and must apply labels where detection is triggered.

**Synthetic Media — Enhanced Labeling.** For synthetic media specifically, SSMIs must apply prominent and unmissable labels — not merely metadata labels but visible on-screen disclosures — indicating that the content depicts an AI-generated or AI-manipulated representation of a person or event. Labels for synthetic media must:

- Be displayed in English and in the primary regional language of the platform's interface;
- Appear before the content begins playing (in the case of video) or in a prominent position adjacent to the content (in the case of images);
- Be resistant to cropping or editing — meaning they must not disappear when the content is downloaded, screen-captured, or shared.

**User Disclosure at Upload.** SSMIs are required to update their terms of service and upload interfaces to: (a) require users to declare, at the point of uploading or sharing content, whether the content was generated or significantly altered by AI; and (b) apply an AI label automatically where a user declares AI generation. Self-declaration obligations do not replace the SSMI's own detection and labeling obligations but supplement them.

### Obligations for Generative AI Service Providers

**Mandatory Watermarking.** Providers of generative AI services that operate in India or that are accessible to users in India must implement technical content provenance mechanisms — specifically, invisible cryptographic watermarks or equivalent techniques — in all AI-generated content produced by their services. These watermarks must be detectable by SSMIs' automated detection systems, enabling downstream labeling.

**Disclosure at Point of Creation.** Generative AI service providers must display a clear disclosure to users at the point of content generation, informing them that: (a) the content they are creating is AI-generated; (b) they must disclose this when sharing the content on social media or other public platforms; and (c) certain uses of AI-generated content — particularly for political messaging, electoral purposes, or representation of real individuals without consent — may be subject to additional legal requirements.

**Records Retention.** Generative AI service providers must maintain logs of AI-generated content creation requests — specifically the fact and time of generation, the user identifier, and the hash of the generated content — for a period of six months, to enable investigation of complaints involving AI-generated content. These logs must be made available to law enforcement or MeitY upon receipt of a lawful order.

**Prohibition on Generating Certain Content.** Generative AI service providers must implement safeguards preventing their systems from generating: non-consensual synthetic media depicting real identifiable individuals in sexual or defamatory contexts; synthetic media that impersonates electoral candidates in a misleading manner during electoral periods; and content that misrepresents official government statements or communications using AI-generated voices or images of government officials.

### Synthetic Media and Deepfakes — Specific Provisions

The 2026 amendments include dedicated provisions targeting the most harmful category of AI-generated content — deepfakes and non-consensual synthetic media — which received significant public attention following multiple high-profile incidents in India involving AI-generated videos of politicians and Bollywood actors.

**Prohibition on Non-Consensual Deepfakes.** SSMIs must take down deepfake content — specifically synthetic media depicting an identifiable real person — within 24 hours of receiving a complaint from the depicted individual or a report from an authorized government entity, where the content was created without the depicted person's consent and the depicted person has not consented to its creation and distribution. For content involving public officials or depicting criminal activity, the takedown timeline is reduced to 6 hours.

**Dedicated Grievance Mechanism for Deepfakes.** SSMIs must establish a dedicated grievance channel for complaints about AI-generated content and synthetic media, separate from general content moderation grievance mechanisms. The dedicated grievance officer responsible for AI/synthetic media complaints must be identifiable and contactable, and must acknowledge complaints within 24 hours.

**Disclosure Requirements for AI-Generated Political Advertisements.** Advertisers who use AI-generated content in political advertising on SSMIs must prominently disclose, in the advertisement itself, that the content is AI-generated. SSMIs must verify and enforce this disclosure requirement for political advertisements.

### Technical Standards and Interoperability

The 2026 amendments task MeitY with working with the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) and industry bodies to develop standardized technical specifications for:

- AI content watermarking formats compatible with the Content Authenticity Initiative (CAI) and C2PA (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity) standards;
- Machine-readable AI content labels that can be recognized across platforms;
- Application programming interfaces (APIs) that SSMIs must expose to enable authorized fact-checking organizations to query the AI-generated status of content.

SSMIs and generative AI service providers are required to adopt these technical standards within 12 months of their publication by MeitY.

### Transparency Reporting

SSMIs covered by the 2026 amendments must include in their monthly transparency reports (required under IT Rules 2021) specific data on:

- The volume of AI-generated content detected and labeled on the platform;
- The volume of synthetic media content removed or labeled;
- The number of complaints received specifically about AI-generated or synthetic media content and the disposition of those complaints;
- The detection accuracy rate of AI content detection mechanisms and any improvements implemented.

### Enforcement

The 2026 amendments are enforced through the existing IT Rules 2021 enforcement framework, under which MeitY has the authority to: issue directions to SSMIs requiring compliance; require SSMIs to remove non-compliant content; impose monetary penalties; and — in cases of persistent non-compliance — initiate action under the Information Technology Act 2000 that could include blocking of the platform.

Non-compliance by SSMIs with labeling and takedown obligations under the 2026 amendments may result in: loss of the safe harbor protection under Section 79 of the IT Act (which protects intermediaries from liability for user-generated content, provided they comply with applicable rules); monetary penalties under IT Act provisions; and show cause notices and orders from MeitY.

Penalties for generative AI service providers that fail to implement required watermarking, maintain required logs, or comply with prohibited content requirements may include orders blocking access to the service in India pending compliance.

MeitY has indicated that enforcement will initially be supervisory and compliance-focused, with escalation to punitive measures for persistent or egregious violations. MeitY's dedicated Cyber and Information Security Division oversees monitoring of SSMI compliance with the 2026 amendments and operates a coordinated complaint management system for reports of unlabeled AI content and deepfakes.

### Context and Significance

The 2026 IT Rules amendments are a direct regulatory response to the global surge in AI-generated content and the specific Indian experience of deepfake-related harms. They are notable for targeting the intermediary layer — SSMIs and generative AI platforms — rather than end users, reflecting the regulatory judgment that scalable solutions to AI content labeling can only be achieved through platform-level obligations. The amendments are also notable for adopting a technology-neutral approach to labeling, permitting SSMIs to use watermarking, metadata, or other technically equivalent mechanisms to fulfill their labeling obligations, rather than mandating a specific technology.

The 2026 amendments should be read alongside India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 (DPDPA), the IT Act 2000, and the evolving draft National Framework for Responsible and Trusted AI (NFRTAI), which together form the broader legal environment governing AI in India. India has signaled that more comprehensive AI governance legislation is forthcoming, and the 2026 amendments represent a targeted, interim measure addressing the most urgent visible harm from AI — the proliferation of undisclosed synthetic media.
