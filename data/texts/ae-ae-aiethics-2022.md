1. OVERVIEW
================================================================================

1.1 Introduction
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AI's rapid advancement and innovation potential across a range of fields is incredibly
exciting. Yet a thorough and open discussion around AI ethics, and the principles
organizations using this technology must consider, is needed.

This document — AI Ethics: Principles and Guidelines — intends to meet this need to
balance these two central considerations of AI. It is designed to offer detailed guidance
to help AI actors adhere to eight principles of AI ethics.

We will make AI systems that are:
1. Explainable
2. Privacy preserving
3. Transparent
4. Sustainable and environmentally friendly
5. Accountable
6. Human centered
7. Fair
8. Robust, safe and secure

The guidelines are non-binding, and are drafted as a collaborative multi-stakeholder
effort, with full awareness of organizations' needs to innovate and protect their
intellectual property. This is a collaborative process in which all stakeholders are
invited to be part of an ongoing dialogue. We would like to see the AI Ethics Guidelines
evolve into a universal, practical and applicable framework informing ethical
requirements for AI design and use. The eventual goal is to reach widespread agreement
and adoption of commonly agreed policies to inform the ethical use of AI nationally and
around the world.


1.2 Scope
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This document gives non-mandatory guidelines for achieving the ethical design and
deployment of AI systems in both the public and private sectors. AI already surrounds
us, but some applications are more visible and sensitive than others.

This document is applicable only to those AI systems which make or inform 'significant
decisions' — that is, those decisions which have the potential for significant impact
either on individuals or on society as a whole. They also apply to 'critical decisions',
which are a subset of significant decisions and are of especially critical nature.

This document guides the further developments of sector specific requirements and
principles, hence every entity within its sector can further amend, use, or complement
its current frameworks to properly suit its context and the need of affected stakeholders.

It is a living document and will undergo further future reviews and enhancements.


1.3 Strategic Alignment
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This document comes as a fulfillment of the goals and aspirations set in the UAE National
AI Strategy and the international Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). The UAE has
long been at the forefront of sustainable development. These shared global milestones
have created global movement and adoption in several disciplines and sectors. As an
early adopter, the UAE has established a national committee on SDGs with a long-term
realization of the UAE SDG agenda 2030. These goals are further engrained and
reflected in the AI Ethics Principles under the topics of fairness, inclusiveness, equality,
human benefit, and rights.

Additionally, the AI Office is currently developing additional policies to be taken into
consideration along with this document:

AI Seal:
The UAE AI Seal brand (UAI) will be used to attract talent and business from across the
globe to come to the UAE to test and develop AI. This includes a UAI mark recognizing
high quality, ethical AI companies. It would reward safe, efficient, verified AI technology
with a 'UAI Seal of Approval'. The UAE AI Seal brand is currently under development
by the AI Office.

AI Procurement Guidelines:
The AI Procurement Guidelines will be used to guide Federal Government entities in
the procurement of AI systems. They will list the general principles, proposed mechanisms
for procurement and review of AI products between the federal government and vendors.
The policy considers R&D for specific challenges that do not currently have an AI
solution product that is market-ready. Based on global standards for AI Procurement,
the policy aims to incentivize AI adoption within government entities and promote AI
R&D within the private sector for specified government challenges. The procurement
policy is currently under development by the AI Office.


1.4 Responsibility
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The AI Office will not be responsible for any misuse of the AI Ethics Principles and
Guidelines. The user bears all the consequences of their use.

2. DEFINITIONS
================================================================================

2.1 AI Developer Organization
An organization which does any of the following:
a. determines the purpose of an AI system;
b. designs an AI system;
c. builds an AI system, or;
d. performs technical maintenance or tuning on an AI system.

Note: The definition applies regardless of whether the organization is the ultimate user
of the system, or whether they sell it on or give it away.

2.2 AI Operator Organization
An organization which does any of the following:
a. uses AI systems in operations, backroom processes or decision-making;
b. uses an AI system to provide a service to an AI subject;
c. is a business owner of an AI system;
d. procures and treats data for use in an AI system; or
e. evaluates the use case for an AI system and decides whether to proceed.

Note: It is possible for organizations to be both an AI developer organization and an AI
operator organization.

2.3 Artificial Intelligence (also AI)
The capability of a functional unit to perform functions that are generally associated with
human intelligence such as reasoning, learning and self-improvement. (Consistent with
ISO/IEC 2382:2015)

2.4 Artificially Intelligent System (also AI system)
A product, service, process or decision-making methodology whose operation or outcome
is materially influenced by artificially intelligent functional units.

Notes:
1. It is not necessary for a system's outcome to be solely determined by artificially
   intelligent functional units in order for the system to be defined as an artificially
   intelligent system.
2. A particular feature of AI systems is that they learn behavior and rules not explicitly
   programmed in.

2.5 Bias (of a system)
Inclination or prejudice for or against one person or group, especially in a way considered
to be unfair.

2.6 Critical Decision
An individually significant decision which is deemed to either have a very large impact
on an individual or to have especially high stakes. These can be especially sensitive,
have the potential to cause high loss or damage, be societally significant, or set an
important precedent.

2.7 Functional Unit
Entity of hardware or software, or both, capable of accomplishing a specified purpose.
(From ISO/IEC 2382:2015)

2.8 Individually Significant Decision
A decision which has the potential for significant impact on at least one individual's
circumstances, behavior or choices, or has legal or similarly significant effects on him
or her.

2.9 Non-Operational Bias (of a system)
Bias that is either:
a. not a design feature; or
b. not important in achieving the stated purpose of the system.

2.10 Set of Significant-at-Scale Decisions
A set of decisions made by the same system or organization which, when taken in
aggregate, have significant impact on society as a whole or groups within it.

2.11 Significant Decision
A decision which is either individually significant or is part of a set of significant-at-scale
decisions.

2.12 Subject of an Artificially Intelligent System (also AI subject)
A natural person who is any of the following:
a. an end-user of an AI system;
b. directly affected by the operation of or outcomes of an AI system; or
c. a recipient of a service or recommendation provided by an AI system.


3. PRINCIPLES AND GUIDELINES
================================================================================

3.1 FAIRNESS (Principle 1)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1.1 Principle: We will make AI systems fair

- Data ingested should, where possible, be accurate and representative of the affected
  population.
- Algorithms should avoid non-operational bias.
- Steps should be taken to mitigate and disclose the biases inherent in datasets.
- Significant decisions should be provably fair.
- All personnel involved in the development, deployment and use of AI systems have a
  role and responsibility to operationalize AI fairness and should be educated accordingly.

3.1.2 Guidelines

3.1.2.1 Benefits of AI systems should be available and accessible to all
- Organizations should document their own definition of fairness for the context the AI
  system will be implemented in.
- Identify and document demographic groups that may be adversely impacted and
  mitigate risk where possible.
- Consider formal procedures such as Discrimination Impact Assessments pre-release
  and regularly thereafter.
- Encourage a diverse and inclusive AI ecosystem.

3.1.2.2 Consideration should be given to whether data ingested is accurate and
representative of the affected population
- Undertake reasonable data exploration and/or testing to identify potentially prejudicial
  decision-making tendencies.
- Evaluate all datasets to assess inclusiveness of identified demographic groups and
  collect data to close any gaps.
- Refrain from training AI systems on data not likely to be representative of the affected
  AI subjects.

3.1.2.3 Consideration should be given to whether decision-making processes
introduce bias
- When subjecting different groups to different decision-making processes, consider
  whether this will lead to non-operational bias.
- When evaluating fairness, consider whether AI subjects in the same circumstances
  receive equal treatment.

3.1.2.4 Significant decisions informed by AI should be fair and accessible across
user groups; diversity in development and deployment processes should be considered

3.1.2.5 Consideration should be given to the effect of diversity on the development
and deployment processes
- Seek to include people from diverse demographic backgrounds across the full lifecycle.
- Consult AI subjects in a representative manner during design, development and
  deployment.


3.2 ACCOUNTABLE AI (Principle 2)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.2.1 Principle: We will make AI systems accountable

- Accountability for outcomes lies not with the system itself but is apportioned between
  those who design, develop and deploy it.
- Developers should make efforts to mitigate risks inherent in the systems they design.
- AI systems should have built-in appeals procedures whereby users can challenge
  significant decisions.
- AI systems should be developed by diverse teams which include experts in the area in
  which the system will be deployed.
- AI systems should be subject to external audit and decision quality assurance.

3.2.2 Guidelines

3.2.2.1 Accountability for outcomes should not lie with the system itself
- Accountability for loss or damages should not be attributed to the system itself.
- Consider designating individuals responsible for investigating and rectifying cause of
  loss or damage from AI systems.

3.2.2.2 Positive efforts should be made to identify and mitigate significant risks
- Use only AI systems backed by evidence-based academic or industrial research.
- Identify the likely impact of incorrect automated decisions on AI subjects; consider
  mitigating measures.
- Conduct internal risk assessments or ethics frameworks to identify risks.
- Maintain data accuracy over time including completeness, timely updates, and context
  suitability.
- Tune AI models periodically to cater for changes to data and/or models over time.
- Subject AI systems informing significant decisions to quality checks.

3.2.2.3 AI systems informing critical decisions should be subject to appropriate
external audit
- External auditing should ensure meaningful standards of transparency and
  accountability.
- In the case of civic-interest critical decisions, public release of audit results should
  be considered.
- Life and death decisions should be supported by further validation via a human
  operator.
- Facilitate traceability and auditability, particularly in critical contexts.

3.2.2.4 AI subjects should be able to challenge significant automated decisions
and opt out
- Provide procedures by which affected AI subjects can challenge a specific decision.
- Make AI subjects aware of these procedures and design them to be user-friendly.
- Consider employing human case evaluators to review challenges.
- Consider "crowd challenge" mechanisms whereby a critical number of complaints
  triggers an investigation.

3.2.2.5 AI systems informing significant decisions should not make value
judgements without prior consent
- AI systems should not unreasonably restrict available options or attempt to influence
  value judgements without explicit consent.

3.2.2.6 AI systems informing significant decisions should be developed by diverse
teams with appropriate backgrounds
- Involve experts with backgrounds in social science, policy, or subjects covering
  broader societal impact.
- Include consultation with experts in the field in which the system will be deployed.

3.2.2.7 AI operators should understand AI systems sufficiently to assess suitability
- For critical decisions, avoid AI systems that cannot be subjected to meaningful
  standards of accountability and transparency.
- AI developers should notify customers of use cases for which the system has been
  designed and those for which it is not suitable.


3.3 TRANSPARENT AI (Principle 3)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.3.1 Principle: We will make AI systems transparent

- Developers should build systems whose failures can be traced and diagnosed.
- People should be told when significant decisions about them are being made by AI.
- Within the limits of privacy and preservation of intellectual property, those who deploy
  AI systems should be transparent about the data and algorithms they use.
- Responsible disclosures should be provided in a timely manner with reasonable
  justifications for AI system outcomes.

3.3.2 Guidelines

3.3.2.1 Traceability should be considered for significant decisions, especially those
that have the potential to result in loss, harm or damage
- Build in traceability (ability to trace key factors leading to a specific decision) for
  AI systems informing significant decisions.
- Document: provenance of training data; model design and algorithms; changes to
  codebase and authorship.
- Where possible, build in a means by which the "decision journey" can be logged.
- Ensure harms caused through AI systems are investigated and redressed.

3.3.2.2 People should be informed of the extent of their interaction with AI systems
- Inform AI subjects when a significant decision affecting them has been made by an
  AI system.
- If an AI system can convincingly impersonate a human being, it should identify itself
  as an AI system before doing so.


3.4 EXPLAINABLE AI (Principle 4)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.4.1 Principle: We will make AI systems as explainable as technically possible

- Decisions and methodologies of AI systems which have a significant effect on
  individuals should be explainable to them, to the extent permitted by available
  technology.
- It should be possible to ascertain the key factors leading to any specific decision that
  could have a significant effect on an individual.
- Channels through which people can request such explanations should be provided.

3.4.2 Guidelines

3.4.2.1 AI operators could consider providing affected AI subjects with a high-level
explanation of how their AI system works
- Inform affected AI subjects in understandable, non-technical language of: the data
  ingested; types of algorithms employed; categories into which people can be placed;
  the most important features driving decision outcomes; a comprehensive list of feature
  engineering and models considered.
- For non-sensitive public sector use cases, consider making source code available
  publicly or upon request.
- Maintain documentation providing elaboration and clarification on how the algorithm
  works, including decision-making flow charts and the appeal process.
- Individuals should have the ability to contest and seek effective redress.

3.4.2.2 AI operators should consider providing affected AI subjects with a means to
request explanations for specific significant decisions
- Provide a means by which people affected by a significant decision can access the
  reasoning behind that decision.
- Where such explainability is not possible, consider counterfactual reasoning or listing
  the most heavily weighted factors contributing to the decision.
- Such explanations should be easily accessible, free of charge, and user-friendly.


3.5 ROBUST, SAFE AND SECURE AI (Principle 5)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.5.1 Principles: AI systems will be technically robust

- AI systems should be technically robust with a preventative approach to risks.
- AI Developers should ensure AI systems will not cause any unintentional harm and
  adverse impacts.
- AI systems should be resilient to attacks and security threats such as data poisoning
  and model leakage.
- AI systems should have safeguards that enable a fallback plan in case of problems.
- AI system results should be reproducible.

3.5.2 Guidelines

3.5.2.1 AI Operators should conduct ongoing vulnerability assessments, including
verification of system behavior in unexpected situations and for dual-use cases
- Put measures in place to ensure integrity and resilience against potential attacks.
- Verify how AI systems behave in unexpected situations and environments.
- Take preventative measures against possible system dual-use.

3.5.2.2 AI operators should define a suitable fallback plan and test it
- Develop a plan to measure and assess potential safety risks from accidental or
  malicious misuse.
- Define thresholds for acceptable system results and governance procedures.
- Consider insurance policy to mitigate risks arising from potential damages.

3.5.2.3 AI Operators should assure end users of the system's reliability through
documentation
- Design AI systems with an approach to continuously monitor whether the system
  meets goals, purposes and intended application.
- Ensure results are reproducible through clear process and documentation.
- Publish documentation to assure system robustness to end users.

3.5.2.4 AI systems will be safe, secure and controllable by humans
- Safety and security of people will be of paramount concern in the design of any
  AI system.
- AI systems should be verifiably secure and controllable throughout their operational
  lifetime.
- The continued security and privacy of users should be considered when
  decommissioning AI systems.
- Systems that may directly impact people's lives in a significant way should receive
  commensurate care in their designs.
- Such systems should be able to be overridden or their decisions reversed by
  designated individuals.

3.5.2.5 AI systems should not be able to autonomously hurt, destroy or deceive
humans
- AI systems should be built to serve and inform, and not to deceive and manipulate.
- Nations should collaborate to avoid an arms race in lethal autonomous weapons;
  such weapons should be tightly controlled.
- Active cooperation should be pursued to avoid corner-cutting on safety standards.


3.6 HUMAN CENTERED AI (Principle 6)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.6.1 Principles: We will give AI systems human values and make them beneficial
to society

- Government will support the research of the beneficial use of AI.
- Stakeholders throughout society should be involved in the development of AI and
  its governance.
- Design AI systems to adopt, learn and follow the norms and values of the community
  they serve.
- Systematic analyses that examine the ethics of designing affective systems to nudge
  human beings prior to deployment are needed.
- Decisions related to lethal force, life and death should not be delegated to AI systems.
- Rules and standards should be adopted to ensure effective human control over those
  decisions.

3.6.2 Guidelines

3.6.2.1 AI should be developed to align with human values, contribute to human
flourishing and benefit society
- Society should be consulted in a representative fashion to inform the development
  of AI.
- Stakeholders throughout society should be involved in the development of AI and
  its governance.
- Stakeholders should proactively engage in responsible stewardship of trustworthy AI.
- Organizations should prioritize having all stakeholders learn about wellbeing metrics
  as a potential determinant of how they create, deploy, market and monitor AI.

3.6.2.2 Systematic analyses examining the ethics of affective systems designed to
nudge human beings are needed prior to deployment
- Nudging in AI systems should have an opt-in policy with explicit consent.
- When appropriate, affective systems should accurately distinguish between users,
  including detecting characteristics such as whether the user is an adult or a child.
  Additional protections must be put in place for vulnerable populations.
- AI systems with nudging strategies must be carefully evaluated, monitored, and
  controlled.

3.6.2.3 Humanity should retain the power to govern itself and make the final
decision, with AI in an assisting role
- Decisions related to lethal force, life and death should not be delegated to AI systems.
- Responsible parties should be trained to detect the influence of AI and ineffective
  mitigation techniques.
- In the most extreme cases it should always be possible to shut down harmful AI
  systems.
- Users should be able to make informed autonomous decisions regarding AI systems.

3.6.2.4 AI systems should be designed to respect the rule of law, human rights, and
societal values
- AI developers should design AI systems to adopt, learn and follow the norms and
  values of the community they serve.
- Identify the norms of the specific community in which AI systems are to be deployed.
- The norm identification process must document the similarities and differences between
  the norms that humans apply to other humans and the norms they apply to AI systems.
- Where needed, human rights impact assessments and human rights due diligence
  should be considered.
- Mechanisms should be put in place to receive external feedback regarding AI systems
  that potentially infringe on fundamental rights.


3.7 SUSTAINABLE AND ENVIRONMENTALLY FRIENDLY AI (Principle 7)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.7.1 Principle: We will promote sustainable and environmentally friendly AI

- AI systems should be used to benefit all human beings, including future generations.
  The environment is fundamental to this.
- Throughout the AI lifecycle, implementations should only be carried out on the basis
  of a full understanding and acknowledgement of implications for sustainability and
  environment.
- An AI system's development, deployment and use should be assessed via critical
  examination of resource usage and energy consumption.
- Mechanisms to measure environmental impact due to type of energy use and processing
  power provided by data centers should be established.

3.7.2 Guidelines

3.7.2.1 Throughout the AI lifecycle, implementations should always be carried out
after full understanding and acknowledgement of AI implications on sustainability
and environment
- AI model development and commissioning should balance technical performance with
  energy consumption and environmental impact.
- Estimate and understand sustainability and environmental impact across the AI
  lifecycle (energy consumption costs associated with model training; CO2 emissions
  and cloud compute costs associated with deployment and running of the system).
- Make carbon impacts a core consideration alongside functional and business
  requirements.
- Consider smaller models — shrinking model size and using fewer compute cycles.
- Consider carbon awareness — adjusting operational parameters to dynamically select
  the best time and location for energy use from the grid.


3.8 PRIVACY PRESERVING AI (Principle 8)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.8.1 Principle: We will respect people's privacy

- AI systems should respect privacy and use the minimum intrusion necessary.
- AI systems should uphold high standards of data governance and security, protecting
  personal information.
- Surveillance or other AI-driven technologies should not be deployed to the extent of
  violating internationally and/or UAE's accepted standards of privacy and human dignity
  and people rights.
- Privacy by design should be embedded in AI systems and where possible AI algorithms
  should have adequate privacy impact assessments.
- Adequate data protection frameworks and governance mechanisms should be established
  in a multi-stakeholder approach and ensured throughout the lifecycle of AI systems.
- AI developers and operators should strive to balance privacy requirements, individual
  rights, innovation growth and society benefits.

3.8.2 Guidelines

3.8.2.1 Establish mechanism for users to flag issues related to privacy or data
protection

3.8.2.2 Establish an oversight mechanism for data collection, storage and
processing and use across your organization
- Review the system for proper consent logging and ability of users to revoke permission.
- Assess who can access data and under which conditions.
- Assign specific responsibilities and roles for Data Protection Officers.
- AI systems must guarantee privacy and data protection throughout a system's entire
  lifecycle.
- Ensure data collected about individuals will not be used to unlawfully or unfairly
  discriminate against them.
- Use measures to enhance privacy such as encryption, anonymization and aggregation.
- In any organization handling individuals' data, data protocols governing data access
  should be in place in line with national privacy legislation.
- Algorithmic systems require adequate privacy impact assessments, which also include
  societal and ethical considerations and an innovative use of the privacy by design
  approach.
- Establish data policies to ensure full security for personal data and sensitive data,
  including biometric, genetic, health data; racial, ethnic origin; religion; political opinion.
- Promote mechanisms such as open repositories for publicly funded data, source code
  and data trusts to support safe, fair, legal and ethical sharing of data.
- Promote and facilitate the use of quality and robust datasets for training, development
  and use of AI systems, and exercise vigilance in overseeing their collection and use.
