# Executive Order 13960 — Promoting the Use of Trustworthy Artificial Intelligence in the Federal Government

**Executive Order No. 13960**

*Signed: December 3, 2020 | Published: 85 Fed. Reg. 78,939 (December 8, 2020)*

*President Donald J. Trump — First Term*

---

## Overview

Executive Order 13960 directed all federal agencies to design, develop, acquire, and use artificial intelligence in a manner consistent with nine enumerated principles of "trustworthy AI." The order was aimed at fostering public trust in government use of AI, ensuring accountability and transparency, and positioning the federal government as a model for responsible AI adoption. It was issued in the final weeks of Trump's first term alongside the **National AI Initiative Act of 2020** (enacted January 1, 2021) and complemented the earlier **Executive Order 13859** (February 2019), which had established the American AI Initiative and directed federal investment in AI research and development.

---

## The Nine Principles of Trustworthy AI

EO 13960 required that federal agency AI applications conform to nine principles:

### 1. Lawful
Agencies shall design, develop, acquire, and use AI in a manner that is consistent with the Constitution, applicable federal statutes, and all other applicable laws and policies, and that exhibits due respect for the Nation's values.

### 2. Purposeful and Performance-Driven
Agencies shall seek opportunities for designing, developing, acquiring, and using AI where the benefits of doing so significantly outweigh the risks, and the risks can be assessed and managed.

### 3. Accurate, Reliable, and Effective
Agencies shall ensure that their application of AI is consistent with the use cases for which that AI was trained, that the AI performs as intended, and that the AI's outputs are accurate, reliable, and effective.

### 4. Safe, Secure, and Resilient
Agencies shall ensure the safety, security, and resiliency of their AI applications, including resilience against systematic vulnerabilities, adversarial manipulation, and other malicious exploitation.

### 5. Understandable
Agencies shall ensure that the operations and outcomes of their AI applications are sufficiently understandable by subject matter experts, users, and other relevant stakeholders, as appropriate for the context of use.

### 6. Responsible and Traceable
Agencies shall ensure that human roles and responsibilities are clearly defined, understood, and appropriately assigned for all phases of the AI lifecycle. Relevant inputs and outputs of AI applications should be well documented and traceable.

### 7. Regularly Monitored
Agencies shall ensure that their AI applications are regularly tested against the nine principles to ensure continued conformance throughout the AI's operational lifecycle, including monitoring for performance degradation, bias, and security vulnerabilities.

### 8. Transparent
Agencies must be transparent with appropriate stakeholders — including Congress, oversight bodies, and the public — about the use of AI, consistent with the need to protect national security, law enforcement sensitive information, and other applicable protections.

### 9. Accountable
Agencies shall be accountable for ensuring proper safeguards are in place for the use and function of AI. Accountable officials should be identified for each AI application.

## Implementation Requirements

### Agency Consistency Plans
Within 60 days of the order, the head of each agency with AI use cases was required to:
- Designate a senior official responsible for coordinating the agency's implementation
- Develop a plan (an "agency AI inventory and consistency plan") documenting how existing agency AI use cases conform to the nine principles
- Submit the plan to the Office of Management and Budget (OMB)

### NIST Role
The order directed the **National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)** to develop further standards, guidelines, and associated methods and tools to promote the nine principles, consistent with NIST's broader AI standards and trustworthy AI work.

### OMB Oversight
OMB was responsible for coordinating agency compliance, reviewing consistency plans, and issuing supplemental guidance on implementation.

---

## Scope and Limitations

The order applied exclusively to **federal agencies** and their AI systems. It did not:
- Impose obligations on private sector AI developers or deployers
- Create individual rights for members of the public
- Establish a penalty regime or enforcement mechanism against agencies
- Define "artificial intelligence" in a highly specific technical manner (it used a broad functional definition)
- Prohibit any category of AI application

---

## Current Status (as of May 2026)

EO 13960 was a **first-term Trump executive order**. It was not revoked by the Biden administration (Biden's EO 14148 targeted Biden's own early orders; EO 14110 (2023) built on and complemented rather than revoked EO 13960). The Trump administration's January 2025 rescission orders (EO 14148) targeted Biden-era orders and did not revoke EO 13960. The order therefore remains nominally in force as of May 2026, though its practical effect has been substantially overlaid by subsequent OMB memoranda, NIST guidance, and the second Trump administration's own AI governance framework.

---

## Historical Significance

EO 13960 was the first executive order to codify government-wide AI principles specifically governing federal agency *use* of AI. It represented a counterpart to EO 13859's focus on AI *leadership and development*: where EO 13859 directed federal investment in AI R&D, EO 13960 addressed the governance of AI already being deployed inside the government. The nine principles influenced subsequent AI governance frameworks internationally and were cited in the development of the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0, January 2023).
