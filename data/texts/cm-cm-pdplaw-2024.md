Law No. 2024/017 of 23 December 2024 on Personal Data Protection in Cameroon
(Loi n° 2024/017 du 23 décembre 2024 relative à la protection des données à caractère personnel)
CAMEROON | 2024

Source: https://pollicy.medium.com/key-features-of-law-2024-017-on-personal-data-protection-in-cameroon-a5f3d7571ce3

Comprehensive data protection statute enacted by the National Assembly of Cameroon and signed into law on 23 December 2024. The law makes Cameroon the 38th African country to adopt comprehensive data protection legislation. It is set to enter into full force in June 2026 following an 18-month implementation period.

Key Provisions (based on publicly reported analysis):

1. Scope and Application
   The law applies to the processing of personal data by:
   - Public and private entities established in Cameroon
   - Any entity processing personal data of Cameroonian residents, regardless of where the entity is located
   - Both automated and manual processing of personal data in organized filing systems

2. Legal Bases for Processing
   Processing of personal data is lawful where based on:
   - Consent of the data subject
   - Performance of a contract
   - Compliance with a legal obligation
   - Protection of vital interests
   - Performance of a task in the public interest
   - Legitimate interests of the controller or a third party

3. Rights of Data Subjects
   The law grants individuals the following rights:
   - Right of access to their personal data
   - Right to rectification of inaccurate data
   - Right to erasure (right to be forgotten)
   - Right to data portability
   - Right to object to processing
   - Right not to be subject to solely automated decision-making with significant effects

4. Automated Decision-Making and AI (Article relevance)
   The law includes explicit provisions on automated decision-making, including profiling. Data subjects have the right not to be subject to decisions based solely on automated processing, including profiling, which produce legal or similarly significant effects. Controllers must implement appropriate safeguards including the right to obtain human intervention, to express one's point of view, and to contest the decision.

5. Data Protection Authority
   The law establishes a Personal Data Protection Authority (Commission de Protection des Données Personnelles — CPDP) as an independent administrative authority responsible for:
   - Receiving and processing complaints
   - Conducting investigations and audits
   - Issuing guidance and recommendations
   - Imposing administrative sanctions

6. Special Categories of Data
   Heightened protection is provided for sensitive data including racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, health data, biometric data, genetic data, and data relating to sexual orientation.

7. Cross-Border Data Transfers
   Personal data may be transferred to third countries only where the recipient country provides an adequate level of protection, or where appropriate safeguards are in place.

8. Data Protection Officers
   Certain controllers and processors are required to designate a Data Protection Officer (DPO) responsible for ensuring compliance.

9. Sanctions
   Administrative fines and criminal penalties apply to violations. The law empowers the CPDP to impose significant administrative fines.

10. Implementation Timeline
    The law entered into force on 23 December 2024 with an 18-month grace period for compliance, meaning full enforcement begins in June 2026.
