# Lithuania — Amendments to the Law on Communications Designating AI Act Supervisory Authorities

**Amendments to the Law on Electronic Communications of the Republic of Lithuania**
*Lithuania — Adopted and in force 14 January 2025*

---

## Overview

Lithuania's parliament (Seimas) enacted amendments to the Law on Electronic Communications (Elektroninių ryšių įstatymas) on 14 January 2025, designating national supervisory authorities for the EU AI Act ahead of the February 2025 deadline for member states to establish such authorities. Lithuania was among the first EU member states to complete this formal designation.

The amendments establish the regulatory structure for Lithuanian implementation of the EU AI Act, distributing supervisory competences across two existing institutions rather than creating a new dedicated body. Lithuania also launched the EU's first national AI regulatory sandbox pilot program in parallel with these legislative changes, allocating €3 million for its operation.

## Key Provisions

### Designation of Supervisory Authorities

**Lithuanian Innovation Agency as Notifying Authority.** The Innovation Agency (Inovacijų agentūra) is designated as Lithuania's notifying authority for conformity assessment bodies under the EU AI Act. In this capacity, the Agency is responsible for: assessing, designating, and monitoring conformity assessment bodies (notified bodies) that audit high-risk AI systems; notifying the European Commission of designated bodies; and withdrawing designations where bodies fail to meet requirements.

**Communications Regulatory Authority (RRT) as Market Surveillance Authority.** The Communications Regulatory Authority (Ryšių reguliavimo tarnyba — RRT) is designated as Lithuania's primary market surveillance authority for the EU AI Act. RRT's new AI-related competences include: monitoring compliance of AI systems placed on the Lithuanian market; investigating complaints and non-compliance reports; issuing corrective orders; coordinating with the EU AI Office; and participating in the European AI Board.

### Coordination Mechanism

The amendments establish a coordination protocol between the Innovation Agency and RRT for handling AI Act cases that span both notified body oversight and market surveillance functions. A joint working group is established to manage inter-agency coordination and harmonize enforcement approaches.

### Regulatory Sandbox Program

Concurrently with the legislative designation, Lithuania launched a national AI regulatory sandbox pilot program. Key features:
- €3 million initial budget allocation (2025–2027)
- Sandbox operated by the Innovation Agency in coordination with RRT
- Designed to allow developers of high-risk AI systems to test compliance with EU AI Act requirements in a supervised environment
- Lithuania described this as the first EU member state AI regulatory sandbox pilot at the national level
- Applications open to Lithuanian-registered AI developers and international companies with Lithuanian operations
- Sandbox agreements provide temporary waivers of selected administrative obligations during testing periods

### Scope of Market Surveillance

The amendments clarify RRT's sector-specific jurisdiction: RRT covers AI systems in electronic communications, digital services, and general-purpose applications. Sector-specific authorities (financial, healthcare, transport) retain jurisdiction over AI systems in their respective domains. A sectoral coordination mechanism is established for multi-sector AI systems.

### Relationship to EU AI Act Obligations

The designation aligns with the EU AI Act's requirement under Article 70 for member states to designate one or more national competent authorities. Lithuania's choice to distribute competences between the Innovation Agency (notifying) and RRT (market surveillance) reflects the functional distinction the EU AI Act draws between conformity assessment oversight and deployment-stage monitoring.
