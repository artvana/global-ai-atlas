Newsletter on Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning in Banking

Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS)
Newsletter No. 27
16 March 2022

Bank for International Settlements, Basel, Switzerland

Note: This newsletter "does not constitute new supervisory guidance or expectations." It describes the Basel Committee's ongoing internal analysis of AI and ML in banking supervision.

OVERVIEW

Banks are increasingly exploring opportunities for using artificial intelligence (AI), including machine learning (ML), in their operations. While these technologies offer significant opportunities for efficiency and risk management, they also present risks that require careful examination by regulators and supervisors.

The Basel Committee issued this newsletter detailing its internal discussions on AI/ML in banking. The publication status is "Current" and falls under the topic of Fintech.

KEY OPPORTUNITIES OF AI/ML IN BANKING

AI/ML technologies offer potential benefits including:
- Operational efficiency improvements
- Enhanced risk management capabilities
- More accurate credit risk assessment
- Advanced fraud detection
- Regulatory compliance automation
- Improved customer service through personalization
- Better prediction of market and credit events

IDENTIFIED CHALLENGES AND RISKS

The Committee identifies several key supervisory concerns:

Model Complexity: AI/ML models are often more complex than traditional models, creating management difficulties. Banks seek "transparency in model design, operation, and interpretability" aligned with banking activity risks.

Accountability: Despite outsourcing model development to third parties, banks maintain full accountability requirements for AI/ML decisions. Third-party development does not transfer regulatory responsibility.

Cybersecurity Risks: Multiple cyber risk points emerge from:
- Large datasets required for AI/ML training
- Third-party interconnectivity
- Cloud technologies and platforms
- Data transmission and storage

Data Governance Challenges: Ensuring data quality, relevance, security, and confidentiality presents significant challenges. Data used in AI/ML must meet high standards of:
- Quality and accuracy
- Relevance to the intended use case
- Security against unauthorized access
- Confidentiality protection

Bias and Ethical Risks: AI/ML models may reflect training data biases and produce unethical outcomes if not properly managed. Systems can perpetuate historical discrimination in lending, risk assessment, and other banking functions.

Explainability: Many AI/ML models, particularly deep learning systems, function as "black boxes" where decision-making processes are opaque. This creates challenges for:
- Internal governance and audit
- Regulatory examination
- Customer rights and recourse

Governance Gaps: Banks require appropriately skilled personnel including model developers, validators, users, and independent auditors to manage AI/ML risks effectively. Many institutions lack sufficient expertise.

SUPERVISORY FOCUS AREAS

The Committee will examine three priority areas:

1. Model outcome explainability and interpretability: How can supervisors assess and require adequate explanations of AI/ML model decisions? What standards of explainability are appropriate for different banking functions?

2. Governance structures and accountability: What governance frameworks are appropriate for AI/ML in banking? How should accountability be structured when AI makes or significantly influences decisions affecting customers or financial stability?

3. Implications for individual bank resilience and broader financial stability: How do AI/ML systems affect individual banks' operational resilience? What are the systemic implications if many banks use similar AI/ML approaches or rely on the same third-party providers?

BROADER CONTEXT

The Basel Committee's work on AI/ML is part of broader international efforts to understand and appropriately regulate technology in banking. The Committee has been engaged with the issue since at least 2018 and continues to monitor developments globally.

Key areas of international coordination include:
- Cross-border supervisory cooperation on AI/ML oversight
- Common approaches to model risk management
- Standards for AI/ML governance and accountability
- Assessment of systemic risks from AI concentration

NEXT STEPS

The Committee plans to continue its exploration of AI/ML issues in banking, with particular focus on:
- Developing supervisory guidance on explainability requirements
- Clarifying governance and accountability expectations
- Assessing systemic implications of widespread AI/ML adoption
- Examining the supervisory use of AI/ML (SupTech)
