# FTC Policy Statement on Artificial Intelligence, Automated Decision-Making, and the FTC's Authority

*Federal Trade Commission — November 2023*

*Note: The FTC issued several interrelated policy statements, enforcement advisories, and reports on AI in 2023. This file consolidates the key FTC enforcement positions on AI from the following primary sources: (1) the FTC's November 2023 policy statement on biometric information and AI surveillance; (2) FTC enforcement actions and advisory statements on AI deception and unfairness under Section 5 of the FTC Act; (3) the FTC's report "Generative AI Raises Competition Concerns" (June 2023); and (4) enforcement policy positions on AI-related violations of the FTC Act, the Equal Credit Opportunity Act, the Fair Credit Reporting Act, and Section 5.*

---

## I. Introduction and Background

The Federal Trade Commission exercises jurisdiction over unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce under Section 5 of the FTC Act (15 U.S.C. § 45). The FTC also enforces the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), the Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA), the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA), the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), and other consumer protection statutes. These authorities apply fully to AI-enabled products, services, and practices.

Artificial intelligence and algorithmic decision-making systems are increasingly used by businesses to make or influence decisions affecting consumers — including decisions about credit, employment, housing, insurance, healthcare, and access to products and services. The FTC has made clear that it will use all available enforcement tools to protect consumers from AI-related harms, including unfair or deceptive practices, unlawful discrimination, and privacy violations.

The FTC's AI-related enforcement positions reflect longstanding legal principles applied to new technology. The fact that a decision is made or influenced by an AI system does not insulate a business from liability under the FTC Act or other applicable statutes.

## II. Unfair or Deceptive AI Practices Under Section 5 of the FTC Act

### A. Deceptive AI Claims

Under Section 5 of the FTC Act, it is unlawful to make false or misleading representations to consumers in connection with the sale or marketing of AI-enabled products and services. The FTC has identified the following categories of deceptive AI claims as enforcement priorities:

**1. False or Unsubstantiated Claims About AI Capabilities**

Businesses must have competent and reliable evidence to support claims about the performance, accuracy, or capabilities of their AI systems. Claims that AI systems are highly accurate, unbiased, or superior to human performance are material representations that must be substantiated. The FTC will take action against:

- Exaggerated accuracy claims for AI-powered products (e.g., claims that an AI hiring tool eliminates bias when no testing has been performed);
- Claims that AI systems are "objective" or "neutral" when they may reflect historical biases in training data;
- Claims about AI healthcare products that are not supported by clinical evidence;
- Misleading claims about how AI systems work or what data they use.

**2. Deceptive Use of AI to Impersonate Humans**

The FTC considers it a deceptive practice for businesses to use AI to impersonate human beings in consumer interactions without adequate disclosure. This includes:

- AI-powered chatbots deployed in customer service contexts that are designed to appear human;
- AI-generated voice calls or messages that impersonate human representatives;
- AI-generated content (reviews, endorsements, social media posts) that creates a false impression of authenticity.

Businesses using AI in consumer interactions should clearly and prominently disclose when consumers are interacting with an AI system.

**3. Deceptive AI-Generated Endorsements and Reviews**

The FTC's Endorsement Guides (16 C.F.R. Part 255) apply to AI-generated content. Businesses may not use AI to generate fake consumer reviews, fabricate celebrity endorsements, or create false impressions of widespread consumer satisfaction. Using AI to manufacture the appearance of consumer or expert approval is deceptive under Section 5.

**4. Hidden AI Decision-Making**

Where AI is used to make or substantially influence decisions that materially affect consumers, and where the use of AI is not disclosed or is actively concealed, this may constitute a deceptive practice under Section 5.

### B. Unfair AI Practices

A practice is unfair under Section 5 if it causes or is likely to cause substantial injury to consumers, the injury is not reasonably avoidable, and the benefits to consumers or competition do not outweigh the harm. The FTC has identified the following AI-related practices as potentially unfair:

**1. AI-Enabled Surveillance and Biometric Data Collection**

The FTC's November 2023 Policy Statement on biometric information and surveillance identified the following AI-related practices as potentially unfair:

- Using AI-powered facial recognition or other biometric identification systems to covertly identify or track consumers;
- Using AI to infer sensitive characteristics (race, religion, political affiliation, health status, sexual orientation) from biometric data without meaningful consumer notice or consent;
- Deploying AI-powered emotion recognition, gait analysis, or other biometric AI systems in employment, retail, or other consumer contexts without adequate disclosure;
- Using AI-generated biometric analyses to make consequential decisions (employment, access to services, insurance) without adequate validation.

The FTC warned that the collection, use, and monetization of biometric information—including through AI systems—raises substantial consumer protection concerns and may constitute an unfair practice where the potential for harm is high and consumers lack meaningful ability to avoid the practice.

**2. AI-Enabled Discrimination**

The use of AI systems that produce discriminatory outcomes may be both a deceptive practice (where capabilities are misrepresented) and an unfair practice under Section 5. The FTC has taken the position that:

- An AI system that produces discriminatory outcomes for consumers in protected classes may constitute an unfair practice regardless of whether the developer or deployer intended discrimination;
- The FTC Act's prohibition on unfair practices supplements, and does not displace, specific anti-discrimination statutes such as ECOA, the Fair Housing Act, and Title VII;
- Businesses cannot avoid liability under the FTC Act by attributing discriminatory outcomes to an AI system that they deploy.

**3. Manipulative and Deceptive AI Design ("Dark Patterns")**

The FTC has identified AI-powered dark patterns as an enforcement priority, including:

- Using AI to personalize and optimize manipulative interface designs that exploit consumer psychology to override autonomous decision-making;
- AI-driven price discrimination or dynamic pricing that targets consumers based on characteristics associated with vulnerability;
- AI systems that present false urgency, social proof, or scarcity to drive consumer decisions;
- AI-powered subscription enrollment and cancellation systems designed to trap consumers.

**4. AI-Enabled Data Exploitation**

The FTC considers the following AI-related data practices as potentially unfair:

- Repurposing data collected for one purpose to train AI systems without adequate disclosure or consumer consent;
- Using AI to infer sensitive personal information from non-sensitive data and using those inferences to target or harm consumers;
- Monetizing AI-derived inferences about consumers in ways that materially depart from consumers' reasonable expectations.

## III. AI and the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)

The FCRA applies to "consumer reporting agencies" (CRAs) that compile and sell consumer reports, and to users of consumer reports. AI-based scoring, decisioning, and profiling systems may trigger FCRA obligations.

### A. AI-Powered Systems as Consumer Reports

An AI-generated risk score, creditworthiness assessment, or other consumer evaluation may constitute a "consumer report" under the FCRA if it:

- Relates to a consumer's creditworthiness, credit standing, credit capacity, character, general reputation, personal characteristics, or mode of living; and
- Is used or expected to be used to establish eligibility for credit, insurance, employment, or other FCRA-covered purposes.

Companies that sell AI-powered scores or assessments bearing on consumer creditworthiness, insurance eligibility, employment, or housing may be operating as CRAs subject to the full obligations of the FCRA, including obligations related to accuracy, disputes, and permissible purpose.

### B. Adverse Action Obligations

Users of AI systems that constitute consumer reports must comply with FCRA adverse action notification requirements. When an adverse action is taken (denial of credit, insurance, employment, or housing) based in whole or in part on an AI-generated assessment, the user must:

- Provide the consumer with a written notice of the adverse action;
- Identify the consumer reporting agency that provided the report;
- Include a statement of the consumer's right to obtain a free copy of the report and to dispute inaccurate information.

The FTC has emphasized that AI-generated scores and profiles do not exempt creditors, employers, or insurers from FCRA adverse action obligations.

### C. Accuracy Requirements

CRAs that use AI to generate consumer assessments have an obligation under FCRA Section 607 to follow reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy of the information in consumer reports. This includes:

- Validating AI models for accuracy on an ongoing basis;
- Addressing known biases in AI-generated assessments that could lead to inaccurate consumer evaluations;
- Implementing dispute resolution procedures adequate to address AI-specific error modes.

## IV. AI and Regulatory Enforcement Priorities

### A. Enforcement Coordination

The FTC coordinates its AI enforcement activities with other federal agencies, including:

- **DOJ Civil Rights Division** on AI-related discrimination in employment, housing, and credit;
- **CFPB** on AI in consumer financial services, including credit scoring and adverse action;
- **EEOC** on AI-related employment discrimination;
- **HHS Office for Civil Rights** on AI in healthcare.

The FTC's AI enforcement work is part of a broader whole-of-government approach to AI accountability.

### B. Commercial Surveillance Rulemaking

The FTC's Commercial Surveillance and Data Security rulemaking proceeding, initiated in 2022, addresses the use of AI in commercial surveillance practices. Key issues under consideration include:

- Requirements for notice and consent when AI is used to collect, process, or monetize consumer data;
- Prohibitions on unfair data practices enabled by AI;
- Data security requirements for AI systems processing sensitive consumer data;
- Restrictions on discriminatory uses of AI in commercial contexts.

### C. AI and Section 5 Enforcement History

The FTC has brought enforcement actions against AI-related deceptive and unfair practices in multiple contexts:

**Employment AI (HireVue, 2021):** The FTC accepted a settlement from HireVue, a video interviewing software company that used AI to assess job applicants, following concerns about misleading claims regarding the validity and bias-reduction capabilities of its AI system.

**AI-Powered Health Claims:** The FTC has taken action against companies making unsupported claims that AI-powered products can diagnose diseases, predict health risks, or treat medical conditions.

**Synthetic and AI-Generated Content:** The FTC has warned that the use of AI to generate fake reviews, fabricate testimonials, or create false impressions of consumer endorsement constitutes a deceptive practice.

**AI and Children's Privacy:** The FTC has warned that AI systems that collect data from children without parental consent violate COPPA, and that using children's data to train AI models implicates COPPA obligations.

## V. FTC Report: Generative AI Raises Competition Concerns (June 2023)

In June 2023, the FTC issued a report examining competition concerns raised by generative AI. Key findings include:

### A. Market Concentration Risks

The generative AI market exhibits structural characteristics that may lead to problematic concentration, including:

- **Compute concentration**: The high capital requirements for training large foundation models may entrench incumbents and raise barriers to entry;
- **Data moats**: Companies with large proprietary datasets may have durable competitive advantages in AI development;
- **Vertical integration risks**: Large technology platforms that control AI infrastructure, foundation models, distribution platforms, and AI applications may have incentives and ability to foreclose competition;
- **Acquisition of AI startups**: Acquisitions of nascent AI companies by dominant platforms may eliminate competitive threats before they develop.

### B. FTC Enforcement Tools for AI Competition

The FTC indicated it would use the following tools to protect competition in AI markets:

- Section 7 of the Clayton Act (mergers that substantially lessen competition), applied to acquisitions in AI markets;
- Section 5 of the FTC Act (unfair methods of competition), applied to exclusionary practices in AI ecosystems;
- Compulsory access and interoperability remedies where concentration in AI infrastructure threatens downstream competition.

### C. Generative AI and Deception Risks at Scale

The FTC expressed concern about the use of generative AI to produce deceptive content at scale, including:

- AI-generated fake reviews and testimonials deployed at unprecedented scale;
- AI-powered impersonation of businesses, government agencies, and public figures in fraud schemes;
- AI-generated phishing, scam communications, and social engineering content;
- AI-generated synthetic media (deepfakes) used to defraud consumers and damage reputations.

## VI. AI-Specific Guidance for Businesses

Based on its enforcement positions, the FTC has provided the following guidance for businesses developing or deploying AI:

### A. Pre-Deployment Obligations

- **Substantiate capability claims**: Before marketing AI products with specific capability claims (accuracy, bias-reduction, etc.), businesses must have competent and reliable evidence substantiating those claims.
- **Conduct bias testing**: AI systems used to make or influence decisions affecting consumers should be tested for discriminatory bias before deployment.
- **Assess privacy implications**: The collection and use of consumer data in AI systems should be reviewed for compliance with FTC privacy principles and applicable privacy statutes.
- **Review for FCRA applicability**: Businesses should assess whether their AI-generated consumer assessments constitute consumer reports subject to FCRA.

### B. Disclosure Obligations

- Clearly and conspicuously disclose when consumers are interacting with an AI system.
- Disclose when AI is used to make or substantially influence decisions that materially affect consumers.
- Do not imply human involvement in AI-generated interactions.
- Disclose material limitations of AI systems, including known error rates and potential for bias.

### C. Ongoing Monitoring Obligations

- Monitor AI systems for performance degradation, emerging bias, and unanticipated harms after deployment.
- Establish consumer feedback channels for reporting AI-related harms or errors.
- Periodically re-validate AI models used in consequential decisions.
- Maintain records sufficient to audit AI-related decisions and respond to consumer disputes.

### D. Accountability Structures

- Designate responsibility for AI governance and FTC compliance within the organization.
- Ensure that legal, compliance, and privacy teams are involved in AI product development and deployment decisions.
- Maintain documentation adequate to demonstrate compliance with FTC Act obligations related to AI.
- Implement contractual accountability measures when deploying third-party AI systems.

## VII. FTC Act Section 5 Enforcement Principles Applied to AI

### A. The "Reason to Know" Standard

The FTC has stated that businesses have a responsibility to understand how their AI systems work and what outcomes they produce. A business cannot avoid FTC Act liability by claiming ignorance of how its AI system operates. Where a business has reason to know that its AI system may produce harmful outcomes—including discriminatory outcomes, inaccurate assessments, or manipulative behaviors—it has an obligation to investigate and address those risks.

### B. Proximate Cause and Third-Party AI

A business that deploys a third-party AI system and presents that system's outputs to consumers takes responsibility for those outputs under the FTC Act. The fact that the AI was developed by a vendor does not absolve the deploying business of liability for unfair or deceptive practices resulting from the AI's operation.

### C. Remedies

FTC enforcement actions related to AI may result in:

- Injunctive relief prohibiting specific AI practices;
- Mandatory algorithmic audits and bias assessments;
- Deletion of AI models trained on unlawfully collected data ("algorithmic disgorgement");
- Civil penalties where applicable statutory frameworks permit (e.g., FCRA, COPPA, Rule violations);
- Monetary equitable relief under Section 13(b) to compensate harmed consumers.

---

## VIII. Relation to Other Federal AI Initiatives

The FTC's AI enforcement positions are coordinated with and complement:

- **White House Executive Order on AI Safety and Security (October 2023)**: The EO directed federal agencies, including the FTC, to take actions to protect consumers from AI-related harms.
- **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (2023)**: The FTC has encouraged businesses to use the NIST AI RMF as a risk management tool, while noting that NIST guidance does not establish a safe harbor from FTC enforcement.
- **Biden Administration Blueprint for an AI Bill of Rights (2022)**: The FTC endorsed the Blueprint's principles on algorithmic discrimination, data privacy, and transparency.
- **Congressional AI Legislation**: The FTC has expressed support for legislation clarifying AI regulatory authority and establishing consumer rights with respect to AI.
