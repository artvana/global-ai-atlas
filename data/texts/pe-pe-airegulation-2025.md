# Supreme Decree No. 115-2025-PCM — Implementing Regulations for Law No. 31814, Law Promoting the Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence in Peru

**Issued by:** Presidency of the Council of Ministers of Peru (PCM)  
**Date of Issue:** September 9, 2025  
**Published in:** Official Gazette El Peruano, September 10, 2025  
**Legal Basis:** Law No. 31814 of July 20, 2023  

---

## Preamble

That Law No. 31814, the Law Promoting the Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence in Peru, was promulgated on July 20, 2023, and established the foundational principles and framework for the governance of artificial intelligence in Peru;

That Article 26 of Law No. 31814 mandated the Executive Branch to issue implementing regulations within 180 calendar days of promulgation;

That the National Secretariat of Digital Government of the Presidency of the Council of Ministers, in coordination with relevant ministries and following a public consultation process, has developed these Regulations to operationalize the requirements of Law No. 31814;

In exercise of the powers conferred by Article 118, numeral 8 of the Political Constitution of Peru and the Organic Law of the Executive Branch;

**THE PRESIDENT OF THE REPUBLIC DECREES:**

---

## Title I: General Provisions

### Article 1: Purpose

These Regulations establish the procedures, technical requirements, institutional arrangements, and sanctions framework for the implementation of Law No. 31814, the Law Promoting the Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence in Peru.

### Article 2: Scope

These Regulations apply to:

(a) Natural persons and legal entities, whether public or private, established or operating in Peru that provide, deploy, or use AI systems;

(b) Foreign providers of AI systems that are used in Peru or affect the rights of persons in Peru;

(c) State entities at all levels of government that use AI systems in their functions and the provision of public services.

Micro-enterprises as defined in Law No. 28015 and its amendments are subject to simplified compliance procedures as set out in Chapter VII of these Regulations.

### Article 3: Competent Authority

The National Secretariat of Digital Government (SNGD) of the Presidency of the Council of Ministers is designated as the lead competent authority for the implementation, supervision, and enforcement of these Regulations and Law No. 31814, in coordination with sectoral regulators.

The SNGD shall:

(a) Maintain the National Registry of High-Risk AI Systems;

(b) Issue administrative guidelines, technical standards, and compliance tools;

(c) Conduct supervision and investigation of potential violations in areas not subject to sectoral regulation;

(d) Coordinate with sectoral regulators on AI governance in regulated sectors;

(e) Promote AI literacy, public education, and international cooperation on AI governance.

---

## Title II: Classification of AI Systems

### Article 4: Risk Classification Framework

AI systems are classified into the following risk categories for the purposes of these Regulations:

(a) **Prohibited AI systems**: Systems whose use is absolutely prohibited under Article 8 of Law No. 31814;

(b) **High-risk AI systems**: Systems that present a significant risk to the fundamental rights, safety, or well-being of persons, as defined in Article 5 and Annex I of these Regulations;

(c) **Limited-risk AI systems**: Systems that present limited risks, primarily related to transparency obligations, as defined in Article 6;

(d) **Minimal-risk AI systems**: All other AI systems that do not fall into the above categories.

### Article 5: High-Risk AI System Categories

An AI system is classified as high-risk if it is:

(a) Intended to be used as a safety component of critical infrastructure, including energy, water, and transportation systems, or is the critical infrastructure itself;

(b) Used in education or vocational training to determine access to educational programs, assess students, or make consequential decisions about academic progress;

(c) Used in employment and workforce management decisions, including recruitment screening, evaluation of candidates, decisions on promotions, assignment of tasks, or monitoring and evaluation of workers;

(d) Used in the administration or determination of access to essential private and public services, including social benefits, health insurance, credit and loans, and housing;

(e) Used by or on behalf of law enforcement authorities to assess the risk of individuals committing crimes, to carry out polygraph testing or similar tools, to evaluate the reliability of evidence, to assess the risk of reoffending, or to profile persons;

(f) Used in immigration, asylum, and border control management, including risk assessment of persons, verification of identity, examination of asylum applications, or decisions on entry;

(g) Used in the administration of justice, including assisting judicial authorities in researching and interpreting facts and law and in applying legal norms to specific cases;

(h) Used in democratic processes, including electoral systems and campaign targeting;

(i) Used to perform real-time or post-hoc biometric identification, categorization, or analysis of natural persons.

### Article 6: Limited-Risk AI Systems

AI systems that interact with natural persons are classified as limited risk if they are not high-risk but have a non-negligible risk of deceiving users or affecting their decisions. Limited-risk AI systems shall be subject to transparency requirements, as specified in Article 20 of these Regulations.

### Article 7: Review and Update of Risk Categories

The SNGD may update the list of high-risk AI system categories and subcategories in Annex I by administrative resolution, following public consultation, to reflect technological and market developments and emerging risks. Updates shall be published in the Official Gazette.

## Title III: Requirements for High-Risk AI Systems

### Article 8: Pre-Deployment Conformity Assessment

Before placing a high-risk AI system on the market or into service in Peru, AI providers shall conduct a conformity assessment to verify compliance with the requirements of these Regulations. The conformity assessment shall be documented and shall cover:

(a) Verification that the AI system meets the applicable technical requirements set out in Article 9;

(b) A risk assessment identifying foreseeable risks to users and affected persons and the measures implemented to mitigate such risks;

(c) Verification that the system's training data meets applicable data governance requirements;

(d) Testing results demonstrating the system's accuracy, robustness, and performance across relevant subgroups and contexts.

Providers shall retain conformity assessment documentation for ten years after the system is placed on the market and shall make it available to the SNGD upon request.

### Article 9: Technical Requirements for High-Risk AI Systems

High-risk AI systems shall meet the following technical requirements:

**Data and Data Governance:**

(a) Training, validation, and testing data shall be subject to data governance practices covering data collection, data quality, and data relevance;

(b) Providers shall take all reasonable measures to ensure that training datasets are free from significant errors and bias, and shall document data collection methodologies and quality assurance procedures;

(c) Where personal data is processed, providers shall comply with Law No. 29733 and its implementing regulations.

**Technical Documentation:**

(d) Providers shall maintain technical documentation that enables the competent authority to assess compliance, including a general description of the system, its intended purpose, the design specifications, the training methodology, performance metrics, and known limitations.

**Transparency and Provision of Information:**

(e) High-risk AI systems shall be designed to enable deployers to implement transparency measures toward affected persons, including the provision of explanations for automated decisions.

**Human Oversight:**

(f) High-risk AI systems shall be designed to enable effective human oversight, including the ability to monitor the system's operation, understand its outputs, and intervene when necessary;

(g) Providers shall specify the human oversight measures appropriate for the system in its technical documentation.

**Accuracy, Robustness, and Cybersecurity:**

(h) High-risk AI systems shall achieve levels of accuracy, robustness, and resilience appropriate to their intended purpose and risk level, as specified in applicable technical standards;

(i) Providers shall implement cybersecurity measures to protect high-risk AI systems against unauthorized access and adversarial manipulation.

**Post-Market Monitoring:**

(j) Providers of high-risk AI systems shall implement post-market monitoring plans to collect and analyze data on system performance and to identify and address risks that emerge after deployment.

### Article 10: Registration of High-Risk AI Systems

Providers of high-risk AI systems intended for use in Peru shall register their systems with the SNGD's National Registry of High-Risk AI Systems before the systems are placed on the market or into service.

Registration shall include:

(a) The provider's name and contact information;

(b) A description of the AI system, its intended purpose, and intended deployers;

(c) The risk category and the basis for the provider's classification;

(d) A summary of the conformity assessment results;

(e) A declaration of conformity signed by the provider's authorized representative.

The SNGD shall maintain the National Registry of High-Risk AI Systems and shall publish a public-facing version of the Registry, excluding commercially sensitive information.

### Article 11: Obligations of Deployers of High-Risk AI Systems

Deployers of high-risk AI systems in Peru shall:

(a) Verify that the AI system is registered in the National Registry of High-Risk AI Systems;

(b) Use the AI system only for the purposes and under the conditions specified by the provider;

(c) Implement the human oversight measures specified by the provider and appropriate to the deployment context;

(d) Monitor the AI system's performance and promptly report serious incidents to the SNGD, using the incident reporting mechanism established under Article 24;

(e) Inform affected persons that they are subject to AI-assisted decisions and provide access to explanations upon request;

(f) Maintain records of the AI systems they deploy and make them available to the SNGD upon request.

## Title IV: Rights of Affected Persons

### Article 12: Right to Information in Practice

AI deployers shall implement the right to information established in Article 13 of Law No. 31814 by:

(a) Clearly disclosing in their communications, interfaces, and service agreements that AI systems are used and for what purpose;

(b) Providing accessible notices or labels when users interact with AI systems, including chatbots and virtual assistants;

(c) Publishing plain-language descriptions of their AI systems on their official websites.

### Article 13: Right to Explanation

AI deployers in high-risk use cases shall implement the right to explanation established in Article 14 of Law No. 31814 by:

(a) Establishing a mechanism for affected persons to request explanations of AI-assisted decisions, accessible through the deployer's standard customer service channels or a dedicated channel;

(b) Providing explanations in plain language that describe the main factors that influenced the decision;

(c) Providing explanations within fifteen (15) calendar days of the request.

### Article 14: Right to Human Review

AI deployers in high-risk use cases shall implement the right to human review established in Article 14 of Law No. 31814 by:

(a) Ensuring that a qualified human with the authority to modify or reverse the AI-assisted decision is available to review cases upon request;

(b) Informing affected persons of the availability of human review and the procedure for requesting it;

(c) Completing human reviews within thirty (30) calendar days, or within a shorter period where required by applicable procedural rules.

### Article 15: Complaints Mechanism

The SNGD shall establish a national complaints mechanism enabling individuals to report violations of their rights under Law No. 31814 and these Regulations. The mechanism shall be accessible through digital and non-digital channels.

Sectoral regulators shall establish sector-specific complaints mechanisms for AI violations within their respective sectors.

## Title V: AI in Public Administration

### Article 16: Inventory of Government AI Systems

State entities at the national level shall, within ninety (90) days of the entry into force of these Regulations, submit to the SNGD an inventory of the AI systems they currently use or are in the process of deploying, classified by risk level in accordance with Article 4 of these Regulations.

Regional and local governments shall submit inventories within one hundred and eighty (180) days.

The SNGD shall consolidate the inventories into a public Government AI Register.

### Article 17: AI Impact Assessments for State Entities

State entities shall conduct an AI impact assessment before deploying any new high-risk AI system. The AI impact assessment shall evaluate:

(a) The purpose and necessity of the AI system and whether less privacy-intrusive or less risky alternatives could achieve the same objective;

(b) Potential impacts on fundamental rights, including the right to non-discrimination, the right to privacy, the right to due process, and the right to access public services;

(c) Data governance measures, including compliance with personal data protection requirements;

(d) Human oversight arrangements;

(e) The measures to be taken to mitigate identified risks.

AI impact assessments shall be reviewed and updated at least every two years or when material changes are made to the AI system.

### Article 18: Transparency Obligations of State Entities

In implementing Article 11 of Law No. 31814, state entities shall publish the following information in their Government AI Register entries, updated annually:

(a) The name and purpose of each AI system;

(b) The decisions the AI system informs and the level of human oversight;

(c) A plain-language explanation of how the AI system works;

(d) The categories of data used;

(e) The supplier of the AI system, if applicable;

(f) Contact information for requesting explanations or human review.

## Title VI: Supervision and Enforcement

### Article 19: Supervision Activities

The SNGD and sectoral regulators shall, within their respective competencies, conduct the following supervisory activities:

(a) Reviews of the National Registry of High-Risk AI Systems and Government AI Register;

(b) Audits and inspections of AI providers and deployers;

(c) Investigation of complaints and incident reports;

(d) Market surveillance to identify non-compliant AI systems.

The SNGD may request that AI providers and deployers provide technical documentation, testing results, and any other information necessary for supervisory purposes.

### Article 20: Serious Incident Reporting

Deployers of high-risk AI systems shall report to the SNGD any serious incident — defined as an incident that results or could result in significant harm to the health, safety, or fundamental rights of affected persons — within seventy-two (72) hours of becoming aware of the incident.

The SNGD shall maintain a confidential serious incident register and shall publish anonymized aggregated data on serious incidents annually.

### Article 21: Corrective Measures

Where the SNGD or a sectoral regulator identifies a violation of Law No. 31814 or these Regulations, the authority may order:

(a) Immediate suspension of the use of an AI system pending compliance;

(b) Mandatory modification or withdrawal of an AI system;

(c) Recall or deactivation of a non-compliant AI system that has been deployed;

(d) Notification to affected persons.

## Title VII: Administrative Sanctions

### Article 22: Infringement Categories and Sanctions

Violations of Law No. 31814 and these Regulations are classified as follows:

**Very Serious Violations** — subject to fines of between 50 UIT and 500 UIT (Unidades Impositivas Tributarias), or up to 5% of the global annual turnover of the infringing entity, whichever is greater:

(a) Use of prohibited AI systems as specified in Article 8 of Law No. 31814;

(b) Placing a high-risk AI system on the market without completing the required conformity assessment;

(c) Deliberate obstruction of supervisory activities;

(d) Failure to comply with orders issued under Article 21 of these Regulations.

**Serious Violations** — subject to fines of between 10 UIT and 50 UIT:

(a) Failure to register a high-risk AI system in the National Registry;

(b) Failure to implement required human oversight measures for high-risk AI systems;

(c) Failure to comply with transparency obligations toward affected persons;

(d) Failure to report a serious incident within the required timeframe.

**Minor Violations** — subject to fines of up to 10 UIT:

(a) Incomplete or inaccurate registration of AI systems;

(b) Failure to respond to requests for information or explanations within prescribed timeframes;

(c) Failure to maintain required technical documentation.

### Article 23: Aggravating and Mitigating Factors

In determining the applicable sanction, the following aggravating factors shall be considered:

(a) The severity and breadth of harm caused;

(b) Whether the violation was intentional;

(c) Whether the violator has prior violations;

(d) Whether the violation affected vulnerable populations.

Mitigating factors include prompt corrective action, cooperation with supervisory authorities, and prior compliance history.

### Article 24: Simplified Compliance for Micro-Enterprises

Micro-enterprises as defined in applicable law shall be subject to simplified compliance procedures under these Regulations, including:

(a) Simplified self-assessment procedures in place of full conformity assessments;

(b) Reduced documentation requirements;

(c) A twelve-month grace period from the entry into force of these Regulations to achieve compliance.

---

## Title VIII: Final Provisions

### Article 25: Entry into Force

These Regulations enter into force thirty (30) days after publication in the Official Gazette, El Peruano, with the following exceptions:

(a) The requirements for high-risk AI systems set out in Title III enter into force one hundred and eighty (180) days after publication;

(b) The registration requirements for State entities set out in Article 16 enter into force ninety (90) days after publication.

### Article 26: Regulatory Updates

The SNGD shall review these Regulations every two years and shall propose amendments as necessary to reflect technological developments, emerging risks, and developments in international AI governance.
