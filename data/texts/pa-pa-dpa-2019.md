# Ley No. 81 de 2019 sobre Protección de Datos Personales — Panama
**Ley No. 81 de 26 de marzo de 2019**
*Panama — March 26, 2019 (effective March 29, 2021 following two-year implementation period)*

---

## Overview

Panama's Law No. 81 of March 26, 2019, on Personal Data Protection is the country's first comprehensive personal data protection law. Enacted on March 26, 2019, it entered into force two years later on March 29, 2021, following an implementation period to allow organizations to prepare for compliance. Executive Decree No. 285 of 2021 provides the detailed implementing regulations. The law designates the Autoridad Nacional para la Innovación Gubernamental (AIG), through its data protection unit (APDATOS), as the supervisory authority responsible for overseeing compliance, receiving complaints, and enforcing the law. The law applies to all processing of personal data in Panama, covering both public and private sectors.

---

## Key Provisions

### APDATOS within AIG as Supervisory Authority
- Autoridad Nacional para la Innovación Gubernamental (AIG) designated as supervisory body
- APDATOS unit within AIG exercises data protection functions
- Maintains a registry of personal data databases
- Investigates complaints from data subjects
- Conducts ex officio investigations and audits
- Issues compliance orders and administrative sanctions
- Issues guidance and technical standards for data protection

### Seven Core Data Protection Principles
- Lawfulness: processing must have a legal basis
- Purpose: purposes must be specific, explicit, and legitimate
- Consent: must be free, informed, and unambiguous where consent is the lawful basis
- Proportionality: data limited to what is necessary for the specified purpose
- Quality: data must be accurate and kept current
- Security: appropriate technical and organizational measures
- Responsibility: controllers accountable for compliance

### Lawful Processing Bases
- Consent of the data subject
- Performance of a contract to which the data subject is party
- Compliance with a legal obligation
- Protection of vital interests
- Legitimate interests of the controller or third party

### Sensitive Personal Data Special Categories
- Categories requiring explicit consent or specific legal authority:
  - Health and medical data
  - Genetic and biometric data
  - Political opinions
  - Religious or philosophical beliefs
  - Racial or ethnic origin
  - Trade union membership
  - Criminal record

### Data Subject Rights (ARCO)
- Right of Access to personal data held by any controller
- Right of Rectification of inaccurate or incomplete data
- Right of Cancellation of data no longer necessary or processed unlawfully
- Right of Opposition to processing on legitimate grounds

### Mandatory Database Registration
- Data controllers must register personal databases with APDATOS before commencing processing
- Registration discloses data categories, purposes, security measures, and transfer recipients
- Sensitive data databases subject to heightened registration requirements

### Cross-Border Data Transfers
- Transfers outside Panama restricted unless recipient jurisdiction provides adequate protection or specific safeguards are implemented (contractual clauses, consent)
- APDATOS may authorize transfers through approved mechanisms

### Security Obligations
- Controllers must implement technical and organizational measures appropriate to the risk
- Breach notification obligations to APDATOS and affected data subjects

### Breach Notification
- Mandatory notification to APDATOS of significant personal data breaches
- Notification to affected data subjects where breach poses significant risk to their rights

## Enforcement & Penalties

- APDATOS may issue compliance orders, formal warnings, and corrective measures
- Administrative fines: up to USD 50,000 for serious violations
- Criminal penalties for the most serious offenses under Panama's criminal code
- Civil liability: data subjects may seek compensation through Panamanian courts
- Processing suspension as an enforcement remedy
