# South Carolina Senate Bill 28 / Act No. 57 — Obscene Visual Representations of Child Sexual Abuse

**South Carolina 126th General Assembly, 2025–2026 Session**
**Enacted:** May 22, 2025 | **Effective:** May 27, 2025

**Citation**
*South Carolina — Act No. 57, May 22, 2025*

---

## Overview

South Carolina SB 28 (Act No. 57) creates the criminal offense of obscene visual representations of child sexual abuse, expressly including AI-generated CSAM (child sexual abuse material) and morphed images in which real or AI-generated child imagery is combined with explicit content. The Act adds this offense to the state's sex offender registry requirements and imposes felony-level criminal penalties. It covers creation, distribution, and possession with intent to distribute AI-generated child sexual abuse material. The law took effect five days after enactment given the urgency of protecting children from this emerging harm.

---

## Key Provisions

### Criminal Offense — Obscene Visual Representations of Child Sexual Abuse
- It is a felony offense to **create, distribute, transmit, or possess with intent to distribute** obscene visual representations of child sexual abuse, including:
  - **AI-generated CSAM** — synthetically produced images or video depicting minors in sexually explicit situations created entirely through artificial intelligence or generative models without involvement of any real child;
  - **Morphed images** — content in which a real child's image (photograph, video, or likeness) is combined with or altered by AI-generated explicit content;
  - **Digitally altered material** — any digitally manipulated visual content that depicts a minor engaged in sexually explicit conduct, regardless of whether the source material involved a real child.

### Covered Conduct
- Creation (including AI generation) of such material;
- Distribution, sharing, transmission, or publication;
- Possession with intent to distribute.

### Penalties
- **Felony offense** with a maximum penalty of **up to 10 years imprisonment** per count.
- Applies to each discrete act of creation or distribution.

### Sex Offender Registry
- Conviction under this Act triggers mandatory registration as a **sex offender** under South Carolina's Sex Offender Registry Act (S.C. Code § 23-3-400 et seq.).
- Registration requirements apply regardless of whether the offense involved a real child, recognizing the severity of AI-generated CSAM.

### Key Legal Principle
- The Act expressly applies even where **no real child was involved** in the creation of the content, closing the legal gap that existed under laws that required proof of a real child victim.
- This reflects legislative determination that AI-generated CSAM causes cognizable harm through normalization of child sexual abuse and the risk of use in grooming real children.

---

## Relationship to Federal Law

South Carolina SB 28 supplements federal law (18 U.S.C. § 1466A — PROTECT Act) which already criminalizes obscene visual representations of child sexual abuse, including drawings, computer-generated images, and other material that does not depict real children. The state law provides a parallel state-level criminal framework with state-level prosecution authority and sex offender registration consequences.

---

## Enforcement

- Criminal prosecution by South Carolina county solicitors and the Attorney General's Office.
- Sex offender registration administered by the South Carolina Law Enforcement Division (SLED).

## Significance

SB 28 is notable for its rapid enactment (effective five days after signing) and its express inclusion of purely AI-generated CSAM — content with no real child victim — within the criminal prohibition. This approach addresses a critical enforcement gap as generative AI models become increasingly capable of producing photorealistic synthetic imagery. South Carolina joins a growing number of states enacting dedicated AI-CSAM legislation, including Wyoming (HB 102, 2025) and federal law under the PROTECT Act.
