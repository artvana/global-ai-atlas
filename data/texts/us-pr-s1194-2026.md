                     GOVERNMENT OF PUERTO RICO
20th Legislative Assembly                                                  3rd Regular Session

                      SENATE OF PUERTO RICO

                               S. B. 1194
                                     April 10, 2026
                        Introduced by Mr. Morales Rodríguez
                                    Referred to the Committee

                                            ACT

To amend Article 268 of Act No. 146-2012, as amended, known
     as the "Penal Code of Puerto Rico," for the purpose of including as criminal conduct
     the use of artificial intelligence systems in the fabrication of false allegations that
     induce criminal investigations.

                                STATEMENT OF MOTIVES


The Puerto Rican legal system has historically recognized the cardinal importance
of truthfulness in investigative and judicial processes as a cornerstone
of the Rule of Law. The administration of justice rests, to a great extent, on
the presumption that the statements, allegations, and complaints presented before the
authorities respond to true facts or, at least, to an honest belief in their
veracity. When said presumption is broken through deliberate falsehood, not
only is a particular proceeding affected, but the very integrity of the
judicial system is undermined.


The crime of false declaration or allegation about a crime, criminalized in Article 268 of the
Penal Code of Puerto Rico, responds precisely to the need to protect the
investigative function of the State against malicious or fraudulent complaints. However,
                                              2


the accelerated development of digital technologies, particularly artificial
intelligence systems, has introduced new modalities of deception that were not
contemplated at the time of the original drafting of said provision.


In recent years, the use of artificial intelligence tools, including systems for
the generation of synthetic images, audio, and video, commonly known as
"deepfakes," has allowed for the creation of completely fabricated evidence with a high
degree of realism. These technologies have been used to simulate non-existent
events, manipulate statements of individuals, and construct false narratives with
the appearance of authenticity, which represents a significant risk for the
investigative and judicial processes.


Comparative experience has evidenced the consequences of this phenomenon. In the
federal sphere, the case United States v. Guy Wesley Reffitt, NO. 1:21- cr-00032 (D.D.C. 2022).
highlighted a contemporary legal reality: digital evidence, although admissible,
is susceptible to alteration, editing, or manipulation. This vulnerability acquires greater
relevance in the current context, where advanced technologies, such as artificial
intelligence, allow for the creation or modification of digital content with a high degree
of realism, which represents a significant challenge for the judicial function and the
determination of the truth.


These developments have been accompanied by concerns expressed in decisions
such as Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc., 509 U.S. 579 (1993), which establish the
need for reliability in scientific evidence, a principle that acquires renewed
relevance in the face of the proliferation of technologies capable of distorting reality.
Likewise, in Napue v. Illinois, 360 U.S. 264 (1959), the Supreme Court of the United
States reaffirmed that the presentation of false information in a judicial process attempts
against the due process of law, underscoring that the truth constitutes an
indispensable element for justice.
                                             3



In this context, it is imperative to recognize that the use of artificial intelligence to
fabricate false facts with the purpose of activating the investigative machinery of the State
constitutes an aggravated form of deception. Unlike traditional falsehood, these
technologies allow for the creation of parallel realities with a level of sophistication that
makes their detection difficult, exponentially increasing the potential for institutional damage.


Lying before the judicial or security function is not a trivial or merely
individual act, it constitutes a direct affront to public order, to the due process of law, and to
public trust in institutions. Deliberate falsehood, particularly
when instrumentalized through advanced technologies, distorts the search for
the truth, diverts public resources, exposes innocent persons to investigations
unjustified, and compromises the legitimacy of the justice system. In doctrinal
terms, this implies a violation of the principle of procedural good faith and the duty
of veracity that governs all action before the State.


Therefore, this Legislative Assembly recognizes the urgent need to bring
the criminal legal system in line with contemporary technological realities, expressly
incorporating within the criminal sphere those conducts in which artificial
intelligence systems are used to create, alter, or simulate false facts that induce
the authorities to initiate criminal investigations. This amendment not only strengthens the
protection of the judicial function, but reaffirms the commitment of the State with the
truth as an essential foundation of justice.


Consequently, by means of the present legislation, Article 268 of the Penal Code
of Puerto Rico is amended, for the purpose of expressly criminalizing the improper use of
artificial intelligence in the fabrication of false allegations of crime, and recognizing said
conduct as a particularly harmful modality that warrants a clear and forceful penal
response.

     BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY OF PUERTO RICO:

 1          Section 1.— Article 268 of Act No. 146-2012, as

 2   amended, is hereby amended to read as follows:

 3          "Article 268. — False declaration or allegation about a crime.

 4          Any person who, by means of a complaint, request, information, tip,

 5   regardless of whether it is anonymous or under a false name, directed to persons or

 6   officials with authority by Law to conduct investigations of a criminal nature,

 7   declares or alleges falsely while having knowledge of its falsehood, that a

 8   crime has been committed, that thus provokes the initiation of an investigation aimed at clarifying it,

 9   shall incur a misdemeanor.

10          Likewise, any person shall incur a misdemeanor who, through the use of artificial

11   intelligence systems, advanced technological tools, or any digital means, creates,

12   alters, simulates, or fabricates a situation, evidence, or false fact with the intention of presenting it to the

13   authorities, provoking a criminal investigation based on said falsehood.

14          If the act falsely alleged is one that constitutes a felony, the person

15   shall incur a felony and shall be sanctioned with a fixed term of imprisonment of

16   three (3) years.

17          When the conduct described in this article is carried out through the use of artificial

18   intelligence or analogous technologies, the court may consider said use as an aggravating

19   circumstance at the time of imposing the corresponding fine."

20          Section 2. — Effectiveness

21          This Act shall take effect immediately upon its approval.
