GOVERNMENT OF PUERTO RICO

20th Legislative Assembly                                                       2nd Regular Session

SENATE OF PUERTO RICO
S. B. 760
October 3, 2025
Introduced by Ms. Álvarez Conde and Mr. Toledo López
Co-authors Messrs. Colón La Santa, González López; Ms. Pérez Soto; Mr. Reyes Berríos; Ms. Román Rodríguez; Messrs. Rosa Ramos and Santiago Rivera
Referred to the Committee on the Judiciary

ACT

To amend Article 4 of Act 21-2021, as amended, known as the "Puerto Rico Pornographic Revenge Act," for the purpose of criminalizing the disclosure and publication of any explicit material of an intimate or sexual nature produced by Artificial Intelligence; and for other related purposes.

STATEMENT OF MOTIVES
Act 21-2021, as amended, known as the "Puerto Rico Pornographic Revenge Act," establishes as public policy the condemnation of the unauthorized disclosure of explicit intimate or sexual material as a violation of the right to privacy, dignity, honor, and reputation. Likewise, it seeks to prevent conducts, which are revenge porn or sextortion: that is when someone discloses, threatens to disclose, or extorts with that type of intimate content without consent. To the extent that technology advances, new methods and tools, such as Artificial Intelligence (AI), are being used to create false and harmful content. Given the advance of these technologies, it is urgent to amend the legislation of Puerto Rico to specify that the creation and distribution of images and videos through Artificial Intelligence without the consent of the persons involved are also part of the criminalized conduct.
      In the United States, cases have been documented in which Artificial Intelligence has been used to create false sexual videos, known as deepfakes, of celebrities and political figures. Actresses such as Scarlett Johansson and Gal Gadot have denounced the circulation of digitally generated intimate material without their consent, while, in the political sphere, manipulated images and videos of figures such as Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, Donald Trump, and other leaders have been fabricated for purposes of discredit. These incidents have generated a debate on the ethical, legal, and privacy risks associated with the use of AI and the need for stricter regulations to protect victims.
             In addition, this phenomenon is exacerbated by the use of Artificial Intelligence tools that allow for the creation of falsified videos, which are not only difficult to detect, but also have a devastating impact on the affected persons.
        The use of Artificial Intelligence in the creation of false content has opened a new dimension in the realm of pornographic revenge. Currently there are digital pages that use Artificial Intelligence with the purpose of sexualizing images and/or videos. This is alarming when knowing that one of these pages experienced over 3 million visits in a single month, as reported by Graphika, a company that studies the flow of users on various social platforms.
    Although the modality of pornographic revenge through AI is considered novel and in continuous development, cities such as San Francisco have acted urgently in seeking to stop said problem. Using legal resources adapted to this problem, in 2024 they achieved the closure of 10 pages of image manipulation with AI. One of the companies was imposed a civil penalty in the sum of one hundred thousand dollars ($100,000). Likewise, on May 19, 2025, Public Law No. 119-12, known as the Take it Down Act, was approved in the United States with the purpose of prohibiting the dissemination of intimate publications, real or generated by AI. This establishes the penalties such as imprisonment, fines, and restitution for those who violate said law.
In response to these problems, it is imperative that Puerto Rico adopt more robust legislation adapted to technological advances. The world has realized that Artificial Intelligence is a great tool, but in malicious hands it can cause much harm. The protection of the privacy and dignity of persons is a priority, even in an increasingly complex digital world. No one should be subjected to humiliation, abuse, and the invasion of their privacy, regardless of the advance of technology. It is an opportunity to build a future where justice, empathy, and the protection of privacy are always at the forefront, ensuring that no Puerto Rican feels unprotected in a digital world that continues evolving. Given that this Act is the one that establishes the public policy on this subject, the unauthorized disclosure of explicit intimate or sexual material, it becomes imperative to adapt to the times and protect everyone, that the Artificial Intelligence used for these criminal purposes be included.
BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY OF PUERTO RICO:
Section 1.- Article 4 of Act 21-2021, as amended, known as the "Puerto Rico Pornographic Revenge Act," is hereby amended to read as follows:
"Article 4.- Criminal Conduct; Penalties
Any person who, without authorization of the victim, deliberately, knowingly, or recklessly impairs the privacy thereof, disseminates, discloses, reveals, or transfers to a third party or third parties explicit material of the victim, whether real or fabricated, manipulated, simulated, or altered through the use of Artificial Intelligence or other technological, electronic, or digital means, by any type of communication, including electronic communications or using electronic or cybernetic communication media, shall incur a felony and shall be sanctioned with a fixed term of three (3) years of imprisonment. If any of the mitigating circumstances established in the Penal Code of Puerto Rico or in the Rules of Criminal Procedure are present, the penalty may be reduced to one (1) year of imprisonment. Any person who threatens the victim with disseminating, disclosing, revealing, or transferring to a third party or third parties explicit material of the victim, whether real or fabricated, manipulated, simulated, or altered through the use of Artificial Intelligence or other technological, electronic, or digital means, by any type of communication, including electronic communications or using electronic or cybernetic communication media, shall incur a misdemeanor.
If the conduct described in the previous paragraph is carried out to extort or to obtain any type of profit, the person who extorts or profits shall incur a felony that shall be sanctioned with a fixed term of eight (8) years of imprisonment. If the convicted person is a recidivist in this modality, the Court shall order their inscription in the Registry of Persons Convicted of Sexual Crimes and Abuse Against Minors as Type I Sexual Offender.
For the purposes of this Article, the fact that a person sends or exchanges an image, audio, video, or any other material through the use of any electronic device through any means, does not mean a waiver of the reasonable expectation of privacy and intimacy of the victim. The provisions of this Article include that material that has been falsified, modified, or altered for the same purposes.
Excluded from this Article are interactive informational services, systems, or software providers that supply, activate, or enable the access of multiple users to a server equipment, including a system that provides access to the Internet, for the content placed by another person.
It is provided that the criminal conduct described in this Article shall prescribe in ten (10) years.
Section 2.- This Act shall take effect immediately upon its approval.
