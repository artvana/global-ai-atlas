# Federal Act on Data Protection (Data Protection Act, FADP)

**SR 235.1**

*English is not an official language of the Swiss Confederation. This translation is provided for information purposes only and has no legal force.*

**of 25 September 2020 (Last amended on 7 July 2025)**

---

## Chapter 1: Purpose, Scope of Application and Federal Supervisory Authority

### Art. 1 Purpose

This Act has the purpose of protecting the personality and fundamental rights of natural persons whose personal data is processed.

### Art. 2 Personal and material scope of application

¹ This Act applies to the processing of personal data of natural persons by:

- a. private persons;
- b. federal bodies.

² It does not apply to:

- a. personal data being processed by a natural person exclusively for personal use;
- b. personal data being processed by the Federal Assembly and parliamentary committees as part of their deliberations;
- c. personal data being processed by institutional beneficiaries under Article 2 paragraph 1 of the Host State Act of 22 June 2007 who enjoy immunity from jurisdiction in Switzerland.

³ The applicable procedural law regulates the processing of personal data and the data subject's rights in court proceedings and in proceedings governed by federal procedural regulations. This Act applies to first instance administrative proceedings.

⁴ The public registers for private legal transactions, and in particular the access to these registers and the data subject's rights, shall be regulated by the specific provisions of the applicable federal law. If the specific provisions do not contain any rules, this Act applies.

### Art. 3 Territorial scope of application

¹ This Act applies to circumstances that have an effect in Switzerland, even if they were initiated abroad.

### Art. 4 Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner

¹ The Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC) supervises the application of the federal data protection regulations.

² The following are exempted from supervision by the FDPIC:

- a. the Federal Assembly;
- b. the Federal Council;
- c. the federal courts;
- d. the Office of the Attorney General of Switzerland in relation to processing personal data as part of criminal proceedings;
- e. federal authorities in relation to processing personal data in terms of a judicial activity or proceedings for international mutual assistance in criminal matters.

## Chapter 2: General Provisions

### Section 1: Definitions and Principles

### Art. 5 Definitions

In this Act:

- a. **personal data** means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person;
- b. **data subject** means a natural person whose personal data is processed;
- c. **sensitive personal data** means:
  1. data relating to religious, philosophical, political or trade union-related views or activities,
  2. data relating to health, the private sphere or affiliation to a race or ethnicity,
  3. genetic data,
  4. biometric data that uniquely identifies a natural person,
  5. data relating to administrative and criminal proceedings or sanctions,
  6. data relating to social assistance measures;
- d. **processing** means any handling of personal data, irrespective of the means and procedures used, in particular the collection, storage, keeping, use, modification, disclosure, archiving, deletion or destruction of data;
- e. **disclosure** means transmitting personal data or making such data accessible;
- f. **profiling** means any form of automated processing of personal data consisting of the use of personal data to evaluate certain personal aspects relating to a natural person, in particular to analyse or predict aspects concerning that natural person's performance at work, economic situation, health, personal preferences, interests, reliability, behaviour, location or movements;
- g. **high-risk profiling** means profiling that poses a high risk to the data subject's personality or fundamental rights by matching data that allow an assessment to be made of essential aspects of the personality of a natural person;
- h. **breach of data security** means a breach of security that leads to the accidental or unlawful loss, deletion, destruction or modification or unauthorised disclosure or access to personal data;
- i. **federal body** means an authority or service of the Confederation or a person entrusted to carry out public tasks on behalf of the Confederation;
- j. **controller** means a private person who or federal body which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purpose and the means of processing personal data;
- k. **processor** means a private person or federal body that processes personal data on behalf of the controller.

### Art. 6 Principles

¹ Personal data must be processed lawfully.

² The processing must be carried out in good faith and be proportionate.

³ Personal data may only be collected for a specific purpose that the data subject can recognise; personal data may only be further processed in a manner that is compatible with this purpose.

⁴ They shall be destroyed or anonymised as soon as they are no longer required for the purpose of processing.

⁵ Any person who processes personal data must satisfy themselves that the data are accurate. They must take all appropriate measures to correct, delete or destroy data that are incorrect or incomplete insofar as the purpose for which they are collected or processed is concerned.

⁶ If the consent of the data subject is required, such consent is only valid if given voluntarily for one or more specific instances of processing based on appropriate information.

⁷ The consent must be explicitly given for:

- a. processing sensitive personal data;
- b. high-risk profiling by a private person; or
- c. profiling by a federal body.

### Art. 7 Data protection by design and data protection by default

¹ The controller is obliged to arrange the data processing in technical and organisational terms so that the data protection regulations, and in particular the principles under Article 6, are respected. It shall take account of this from the planning stage.

² The technical and organisational measures must in particular be appropriate with regard to the state of the art, the nature and the extent of the data processing and the risk that the processing poses to the data subject's personality or fundamental rights.

³ The controller is obliged to ensure by means of suitable default settings that the processing of personal data is limited to the minimum required for the purpose intended, unless the data subject specifies otherwise.

### Art. 8 Data security

¹ The controller and the processor shall guarantee a level of data security appropriate to the risk by taking suitable technical and organisational measures.

² The measures must make it possible to avoid breaches of data security.

³ The Federal Council shall issue provisions on the minimum requirements for data security.

### Art. 9 Processing by processors

¹ The processing of personal data may be assigned by contract or by the legislation to a processor if:

- a. the data is processed only in the manner in which the controller itself is permitted to do it; and
- b. no statutory or contractual duty of confidentiality prohibits assignment.

² The controller must satisfy itself in particular that the processor is able to guarantee data security.

³ The processor may only assign processing to a third party with prior approval from the controller.

### Art. 10 Data protection officer

¹ Private controllers may appoint a data protection officer.

² The data protection officer is the contact point for the data subjects and for the authorities responsible for data protection in Switzerland, with tasks including training and advising the private controller and supporting application of data protection regulations.

³ Private controllers may invoke the exception in Article 23 paragraph 4 if the data protection officer exercises functions independently, has required expertise, and the controller publishes the officer's contact details and notifies the FDPIC.

### Art. 11 Code of conduct

Professional, industry and trade associations and federal bodies may submit codes of conduct to the FDPIC, who shall state and publish opinions on them.

### Art. 12 Record of processing activities

¹ The controller and the processor shall each maintain a record of their processing activities.

² The controller's record shall as a minimum contain: identity of the controller, purpose of processing, description of categories of data subjects and personal data, categories of recipients, retention period or criteria for determining it, general description of data security measures, and cross-border transfer details.

³ The processor's record shall contain information on identity of the processor and controller, categories of processing carried out, and the security and transfer information.

⁴ Federal bodies shall notify the FDPIC of their records of processing activities.

### Art. 13 Certification

The manufacturers of data processing systems or programs and controllers and processors may have their systems, products and services evaluated by recognised independent certification bodies.

### Section 2: Data Processing by Private Controllers with Registered Office or Domicile Abroad

### Art. 14 Representative

¹ Private controllers with registered office or domicile abroad shall appoint a representative in Switzerland if they process personal data of persons in Switzerland and the processing is: connected with offering goods/services or monitoring behaviour in Switzerland; on a large scale; carried out regularly; and poses a high risk to the data subjects.

² The representative shall serve as contact point for data subjects and the FDPIC, and the controller shall publish the representative's name and address.

### Art. 15 Duties of the representative

The representative shall maintain a record of the controller's processing activities and, on request, provide the FDPIC with information from the record and provide data subjects with information on how to exercise their rights.

### Section 3: Cross-Border Disclosure of Personal Data

### Art. 16 Principles

¹ Personal data may be disclosed abroad if the Federal Council has decided that the legislation of the State or international body guarantees an adequate level of protection.

² In the absence of such a decision, personal data may be disclosed abroad only if an appropriate level of data protection is guaranteed by: an international treaty; data protection clauses in an agreement notified to the FDPIC; specific guarantees by the competent federal body; standard data protection clauses approved by the FDPIC; or binding corporate rules approved by the FDPIC or an adequate-protection authority.

### Art. 17 Exceptions

Personal data may be disclosed abroad in derogation from Article 16 in the following cases: explicit consent of the data subject; necessity for the conclusion or performance of a contract; necessity to safeguard an overriding public interest or to establish legal rights; necessity to protect life or physical integrity; data made generally accessible by the data subject; or data from a statutory public register.

## Chapter 3: Duties of the Controller and of the Processor

### Art. 19 Duty to provide information when collecting personal data

¹ The controller shall inform the data subject in an appropriate manner when collecting personal data; this duty applies even if data is not collected directly from the data subject.

² As a minimum, the controller shall provide: controller's identity and contact details; purpose of processing; and recipients or categories of recipients.

³ If data is not collected from the data subject, the controller shall also inform them of the categories of processed personal data.

⁴ For cross-border transfers, the controller shall also inform the data subject of the destination state and applicable guarantees or exceptions.

⁵ Where data is not collected from the data subject, the controller shall provide information within one month of receiving the data, or at time of disclosure if earlier.

### Art. 20 Exceptions from the duty to provide information and restrictions

The duty to provide information ceases to apply if: the data subject already has the information; processing is required by law; the controller is required by law to preserve confidentiality; or the requirements of Article 27 (media) are satisfied.

The duty also ceases to apply when data is not collected from the data subject if: providing information is impossible; requires disproportionate effort; is required by overriding third-party interests; or would defeat the purpose of processing.

### Art. 21 Duty to provide information in the case of an automated individual decision

¹ The controller shall inform the data subject about any decision based exclusively on automated processing that has a legal consequence for or considerable adverse effect on the data subject.

² The controller shall allow the data subject on request to express their point of view, and the data subject may request review of the automated decision by a natural person.

³ Paragraphs 1 and 2 do not apply if: the automated decision is directly connected with a contract and the data subject's request is granted; or the data subject has explicitly consented to automated decision-making.

### Art. 22 Data protection impact assessment

¹ If processing is likely to result in a high risk to the data subject's personality or fundamental rights, the controller shall carry out a data protection impact assessment beforehand.

² A high risk arises in particular in the case of large-scale processing of sensitive personal data, or systematic large-scale monitoring of public areas.

³ The assessment shall include: description of planned processing; evaluation of risks to personality and fundamental rights; and description of protective measures.

⁴ Private controllers are exempt if required by law to process the data.

⁵ A private controller may dispense with the assessment if using a certified system or complying with a qualifying code of conduct.

### Art. 23 Consultation of the FDPIC

¹ If the data protection impact assessment indicates that planned processing will still pose a high risk despite planned measures, the controller shall seek the FDPIC's opinion beforehand.

² The FDPIC shall inform the controller within two months of any objections (extendable by one month for complex processing).

³ If the FDPIC objects, he or she shall propose suitable measures.

⁴ A private controller may dispense with FDPIC consultation if it has consulted an appointed data protection officer under Article 10.

### Art. 24 Notifications of data security breaches

¹ The controller shall notify the FDPIC of any breach of data security likely to lead to a high risk to the data subject's personality or fundamental rights as quickly as possible.

² The notification shall specify: the nature of the breach, its consequences, and measures taken or planned.

³ The processor shall notify the controller of any breach as quickly as possible.

⁴ The controller shall inform the data subject if required for their protection or if the FDPIC requests.

## Chapter 4: Rights of the Data Subject

### Art. 25 Right to information

¹ Any person may request information from the controller on whether personal data relating to them is being processed.

² The data subject is entitled to: controller identity and contact details; the processed personal data; purpose of processing; retention period; source of the data; whether automated individual decisions have been taken and the logic behind them; and information about recipients and cross-border transfers.

⁵ No one may waive their right to information in advance.

⁶ The controller must provide information free of charge.

⁷ The information shall in general be provided within 30 days.

### Art. 26 Limitations on the right to information

The controller may refuse, restrict or delay the provision of information if: a formal law so provides; required to safeguard overriding third-party interests; or the request is obviously unjustified or frivolous. Further limitations apply where the controller's own overriding interests require it and no disclosure to third parties is intended, or where the controller is a federal body and public security interests or ongoing proceedings require restriction.

### Art. 27 Limitation on the right to information for the media

If personal data are processed exclusively for publication in the editorial section of a periodically published medium, the controller may refuse, restrict or delay information if it would reveal sources, allow access to drafts, or compromise freedom of public opinion formation.

### Art. 28 Right to data portability

¹ Any person may request the controller to deliver personal data they have disclosed to it in a conventional electronic format if the controller carries out automated processing and the data are processed with the data subject's consent or in direct connection with a contract.

² The data subject may also request transfer of their personal data to another controller where these requirements are met and no disproportionate effort is required.

³ Delivery or transfer shall be free of charge.

### Art. 29 Restrictions on the right to data portability

The controller may refuse, restrict or delay delivery or transfer for the same reasons as Article 26 and must give reasons for such refusal.

## Chapter 5: Special Provisions on Data Processing by Private Persons

### Art. 30 Breaches of personality rights

¹ Any person who processes personal data must not unlawfully breach the data subjects' personality rights.

² A breach arises in particular if: data are processed contrary to the principles of Articles 6 and 8; data are processed contrary to the data subject's express wishes; or sensitive personal data are disclosed to third parties.

### Art. 31 Grounds for justification

A breach of personality rights is unlawful unless justified by: consent of the data subject; an overriding private or public interest; or the law. Overriding private interests exist in particular for: contract-related processing; competitive purposes without third-party disclosure; creditworthiness checks meeting specified conditions; journalistic processing; anonymised research, planning, or statistics; and processing of public figures' public-activity data.

### Art. 32 Legal rights

¹ The data subject may request correction of incorrect personal data unless prohibited by statute or the data are processed for public-interest archiving.

² Actions to protect the personality may include requests to prohibit specific processing, prohibit disclosure to third parties, or delete/destroy personal data.

## Chapter 6: Special Provisions on Data Processing by Federal Bodies

### Art. 34 Legal basis

¹ Federal bodies may only process personal data if there is a statutory basis.

² A statutory basis in a formal law is required for: processing sensitive personal data; profiling; or data processing likely to cause a serious violation of fundamental rights.

### Art. 36 Disclosure of personal data

Federal bodies may only disclose personal data with a statutory basis, with specified exceptions including: data subject consent; necessity to protect life or physical integrity; official public information with overriding public interest; and surname, first name, address and date of birth on request.

### Art. 39 Data processing for purposes not related to specific persons

Federal bodies may process personal data for research, planning or statistics provided: data are anonymised as soon as purpose permits; sensitive data are only disclosed in non-identifiable form; recipients may only transmit data with the federal body's consent; and results are published in non-identifiable form.

## Chapter 7: Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner

### Art. 43 Election and status

¹ The United Federal Assembly shall elect the head of the FDPIC (the Commissioner).

⁴ The Commissioner shall exercise duties independently, without seeking or accepting instructions from any authority or third party.

### Art. 49 Investigation

¹ The FDPIC shall open an investigation into a federal body or private person ex officio or in response to a report if there are sufficient indications that a data processing activity could violate data protection regulations.

### Art. 50 Powers

If a federal body or private person fails to fulfil duties to cooperate, the FDPIC may order: access to information, documents, records and personal data; access to premises and installations; questioning of witnesses; and expert appraisals.

### Art. 51 Administrative measures

¹ If data protection regulations have been violated, the FDPIC may order that processing be modified, suspended or terminated, wholly or in part, and that personal data be deleted or destroyed.

² The FDPIC may delay or prohibit cross-border disclosure.

³ The FDPIC may in particular order the controller or processor to: provide information about cross-border transfer safeguards; implement data protection by design/default and security measures; inform data subjects; conduct a data protection impact assessment; consult the FDPIC; notify data security breaches; and provide information to data subjects on request.

## Chapter 8: Criminal Provisions

### Art. 60 Violation of the duty to provide information, the right to information and the duty to cooperate

On complaint, a fine not exceeding 250,000 francs shall be imposed on private persons who wilfully provide false or incomplete information in violation of information duties, fail to provide information to data subjects, or provide false information to the FDPIC or fail to cooperate in an investigation.

### Art. 61 Violation of duties of care

On complaint, a fine not exceeding 250,000 francs shall be imposed on private persons who wilfully: disclose personal data abroad in violation of cross-border transfer rules; assign processing to a processor without meeting the processor requirements; or fail to comply with Federal Council minimum data security requirements.

### Art. 62 Violation of the professional duty of confidentiality

Any person who, while practising their profession, acquires knowledge of secret personal data but wilfully discloses such data shall on complaint be liable to a fine not exceeding 250,000 francs.

### Art. 63 Disregard of decisions

Any private person who wilfully fails to comply with a ruling issued by the FDPIC or a decision of the appeal courts shall be liable to a fine not exceeding 250,000 francs.

### Art. 65 Jurisdiction

¹ The prosecution and adjudication of criminal acts is a matter for the cantons.

² The FDPIC may file a complaint with the competent prosecution authority and exercise the rights of a private claimant.

### Art. 66 Statute of limitations for prosecution

Prosecution is subject to a statute of limitations of five years.

---

## Chapter 10: Final Provisions

### Art. 69 Transitional provisions relating to ongoing instances of processing

Articles 7, 22 and 23 do not apply to data processing that began before this Act comes into force, provided the purpose of processing remains unchanged and no new data are collected.

### Art. 74 Referendum and commencement

Commencement date: 1 September 2023
