# FTC Final Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials

**16 CFR Part 465 | 89 Fed. Reg. 60064 (Aug. 14, 2024)**
*Federal Trade Commission (United States) — Effective October 21, 2024*

---

## Overview

The Federal Trade Commission's Final Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465) was published August 14, 2024, and became effective October 21, 2024. The rule codifies longstanding FTC guidance into binding, enforceable regulation and explicitly addresses the emerging threat of AI-generated fake reviews. It prohibits a range of deceptive practices involving consumer reviews and testimonials, including the purchase, creation, and dissemination of fake reviews — including those generated by artificial intelligence — and the suppression of genuine negative reviews.

The rule creates civil penalty liability of up to $51,744 per violation for companies that violate its provisions, making it a significant enforcement tool against AI-enabled manipulation of online reputation systems. It is the first binding federal rule to explicitly prohibit AI-generated fake consumer reviews.

## Key Provisions

### Prohibition on Fake Consumer Reviews and Testimonials

- Prohibits businesses from creating, buying, or disseminating consumer reviews or testimonials that they know or should know are fake.
- Explicitly covers AI-generated fake reviews: a business that uses AI tools to generate fake positive reviews of its own products or fake negative reviews of competitors' products violates the rule.
- Prohibits any dissemination of fake reviews regardless of the platform on which they appear (e-commerce platforms, app stores, social media, review aggregators, etc.).

### Prohibition on Buying Positive Reviews

- A company may not offer compensation or other incentives in exchange for positive consumer reviews without clear and conspicuous disclosure of that material connection.
- Covers payments in cash, gift cards, free products, discounts, or any other consideration.

### Insider Reviews and Company-Controlled Review Sites

- Prohibits companies from publishing reviews written by company insiders (officers, employees, agents) without a clear and conspicuous disclosure of the relationship.
- Prohibits operating a website that purports to offer independent opinions about a business if the site is controlled by the business being reviewed.

### Review Suppression

- Prohibits businesses from using legal threats, unjustified legal action, or other intimidation to prevent or remove legitimate negative reviews.
- Prohibits misrepresenting that a review platform displays all reviews when negative reviews have been selectively suppressed.
- Prohibits using review gating — the practice of asking customers about their experience before inviting only satisfied customers to leave reviews.

### Misuse of Social Media Influence

- Prohibits the purchase of fake social media indicators such as followers, likes, or views that are generated by bots or undisclosed paid actors.
- Prohibits the dissemination of fake social proof indicators in connection with a commercial offering.

### Enforcement and Penalties

- Violations subject companies to civil penalties of up to $51,744 per violation.
- Each fake review or testimonial constitutes a separate violation; each day of ongoing violation may be treated as a separate violation.
- FTC may bring enforcement actions; the rule does not create a private right of action.
- Rule supplements existing FTC Act Section 5 authority over unfair or deceptive acts and practices.

### Safe Harbors and Exemptions

- Genuine reviews from customers who received compensation (e.g., free product for testing), provided the material connection is clearly and conspicuously disclosed, are not prohibited.
- Reviews from independent experts or journalists who received products for review purposes, with disclosure, are permitted.
