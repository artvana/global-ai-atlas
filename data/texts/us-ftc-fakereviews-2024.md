# Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials

**Federal Trade Commission**

*16 CFR Part 465 — 89 Fed. Reg. 60064 (Aug. 14, 2024) | FR Doc. 2024-18528, published Aug. 22, 2024*

*Effective: October 21, 2024*

---

## AGENCY

Federal Trade Commission (FTC).

## ACTION

Final rule.

## SUMMARY

The Federal Trade Commission (Commission or FTC) issues this final rule prohibiting certain unfair or deceptive acts or practices involving consumer reviews and testimonials. The rule bans fake or false reviews and testimonials, certain incentivized reviews, insider reviews and testimonials without disclosure, company-controlled review sites that are misrepresented as independent, review suppression using legal threats or selective display, and fake indicators of social media influence. The rule authorizes civil penalties of up to $51,744 per violation (adjustable for inflation).

---

## PART 465 — RULE ON THE USE OF CONSUMER REVIEWS AND TESTIMONIALS

### Authority

15 U.S.C. 41–58.

---

## § 465.1 Definitions

As used in this part:

**(a) Business** means any entity engaged in selling products or services for profit, including any individual, partnership, corporation, association, governmental entity, or other organization that sells products or services.

**(b) Celebrity testimonial** means advertising in the form of a statement by a celebrity that appears in a communication that contains or implies an endorsement, including by use of the celebrity's name, signature, likeness, or other identifying personal characteristics.

**(c) Clear and conspicuous** means that a required disclosure is easily noticeable (i.e., difficult to miss) and easily understandable by ordinary consumers. A disclosure meets this standard only if it:

(1) Is presented in a way that is consistent with the communication medium in which the review or testimonial appears (e.g., visual in visual communications, audible in audio communications);

(2) Stands out from accompanying text or other visual elements so that it is easily noticed;

(3) Is in a readable font size and color that contrasts with the background;

(4) Is understandable to ordinary consumers, including the target audience of the review or testimonial;

(5) Is placed where consumers will likely notice it given the format and context; and

(6) Does not contradict or obscure the required disclosure.

**(d) Consumer review** means an evaluation submitted by a consumer, or by someone purporting to be a consumer, that reflects the individual's opinions or experiences and that is published or displayed on a website or platform dedicated to publishing or displaying such evaluations, including any associated rating.

**(e) Consumer review hosting** means providing a technological platform, including any website, application, or similar interface, for the purpose of enabling consumers to post and access consumer reviews.

**(f) Consumer testimonial** means promotional messaging that reflects the purported opinions, beliefs, findings, or experiences of a consumer (or someone purporting to be a consumer) with respect to a product, service, or business.

**(g) Fake indicators of social media influence** means metrics such as followers, subscribers, views, likes, shares, reposts, or similar measures of audience engagement or popularity on a social media platform that were generated by bots, fake accounts, or hijacked accounts, or were otherwise acquired through deceptive means, and that misrepresent the actual popularity or influence of a person or entity.

**(h) Immediate relative** means a spouse, parent, sibling, or child.

**(i) Manager** means any individual who directs the work of other employees and who has authority to hire or fire employees, or whose suggestions and recommendations regarding the hiring, firing, advancement, promotion, or any other change of status of other employees are given particular weight.

**(j) Officer** means a president, vice president, secretary, treasurer, principal financial officer, comptroller, or person performing similar functions with respect to any organization.

**(k) Purchase a consumer review** means to provide compensation, directly or indirectly, to a person in exchange for the person's writing, creating, or submitting a consumer review.

**(l) Reviewer** means a person who has written or created a consumer review.

**(m) Testimonialist** means a person who provides a consumer or celebrity testimonial.

**(n) Unfounded or groundless legal threat** means a legal threat that the threatening party knows lacks merit or that a reasonable person in the same situation would know lacks merit.

## § 465.2 Fake or False Consumer Reviews, Consumer Testimonials, or Celebrity Testimonials

**(a) Creating or selling.** It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for a business to write, create, or sell a consumer review, consumer testimonial, or celebrity testimonial that materially misrepresents that:

(1) The reviewer or testimonialist exists;

(2) The reviewer or testimonialist used or had actual experience with the product, service, or business being reviewed; or

(3) The review or testimonial reflects the honest opinions or experiences of the reviewer or testimonialist.

**(b) Purchasing or disseminating.** It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for a business to purchase a consumer review, or to disseminate a consumer or celebrity testimonial, that the business knew or should have known materially misrepresents that:

(1) The reviewer or testimonialist exists;

(2) The reviewer or testimonialist used or had actual experience with the product, service, or business; or

(3) The review or testimonial reflects the honest opinions or experiences of the reviewer or testimonialist.

**(c) Procuring from company insiders.** It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for a business to procure from its officers, managers, employees, agents, or their immediate relatives a consumer review for posting on a third-party consumer review platform or any other platform when the business knew or should have known the review materially misrepresents that:

(1) The reviewer exists as a member of the general public;

(2) The reviewer used or had actual experience with the product, service, or business; or

(3) The review reflects the honest opinions or experiences of the reviewer.

**(d) Exceptions.** Subsections (b) and (c) do not apply to:

(1) Consumer reviews arising from a business's solicitation addressed generally to purchasers of its products or services, provided that the solicitation does not condition the incentive on writing a positive review; or

(2) Consumer reviews appearing through the normal operations of a consumer review hosting service that the business uses to display consumer reviews.

## § 465.4 Buying Positive or Negative Consumer Reviews

It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for a business to provide compensation or other incentives (monetary or non-monetary) to any person contingent on the person writing a consumer review that expresses a particular sentiment, whether positive or negative, including:

**(a)** Expressly conditioning compensation or other incentives on writing a positive review or a review expressing a particular sentiment; or

**(b)** Implicitly doing so through context or the nature of the incentive offered.

This section applies to consumer reviews only; it does not apply to consumer testimonials. A disclosure of any incentive does not cure a violation of this section if the review is conditioned on expressing a particular sentiment.

---

## § 465.5 Insider Consumer Reviews and Consumer Testimonials

It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for a business to:

**(a)** Disseminate a consumer or celebrity testimonial provided by an officer, manager, employee, or agent of the business, or by the immediate relative of any such person, without clear and conspicuous disclosure of the material relationship between the testimonialist and the business;

**(b)** Require, request, or instruct an officer, manager, employee, or agent of the business, or the immediate relative of any such person, to write or create a consumer review without clearly and conspicuously disclosing the reviewer's material relationship to the business; or

**(c)** Write or create a consumer review or consumer testimonial about the business or its products or services by an officer or manager without clearly and conspicuously disclosing that the reviewer is an officer or manager of the business.

---

## § 465.6 Company-Controlled Review Websites or Entities

It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for a business to materially misrepresent, expressly or by implication, that a website, organization, or entity that the business controls, owns, or operates provides independent reviews or opinions about a category of businesses, products, or services that includes the business or any of its products or services.

---

## § 465.7 Review Suppression

It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for a business or any person acting on its behalf to:

**(a)** Use an unfounded or groundless legal threat, a physical threat, intimidation, or a false public accusation to:

(1) Prevent the writing or creation of a consumer review; or

(2) Cause the removal of a consumer review or any portion of a consumer review;

**(b)** Knowingly make a false public accusation that a consumer review is fake or fraudulent in order to cause its removal; or

**(c)** Materially misrepresent that consumer reviews displayed in a portion of a website or platform dedicated to receiving and displaying consumer reviews represent most or all reviews submitted, when reviews have been suppressed based on their ratings or negative sentiment.

This section does not prohibit a business from removing reviews that are fake, fraudulent, or violate applicable terms of service, provided the business applies such criteria consistently without regard to whether the review is positive or negative.

---

## § 465.8 Misuse of Fake Indicators of Social Media Influence

It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for any person to sell, distribute, purchase, or otherwise procure fake indicators of social media influence that the person knew or should have known to be fake and that can be used, or are used, by individuals or businesses to materially misrepresent their influence or importance for a commercial purpose.

---

## § 465.9 Severability

The provisions of this part are separate and severable from one another. If any provision is stayed or determined to be invalid, it is the Commission's intention that the remaining provisions continue in effect.

---

## Enforcement and Penalties

Violations of this rule constitute unfair or deceptive acts or practices under Section 5 of the FTC Act (15 U.S.C. 45). Pursuant to Section 18(d)(3) of the FTC Act (15 U.S.C. 57a(d)(3)), any person who violates a rule promulgated under Section 18 is subject to a civil penalty of not more than $51,744 per violation (as adjusted for inflation pursuant to the Federal Civil Penalties Inflation Adjustment Act of 1990, as amended). Each day of a continuing violation constitutes a separate violation.

There is no private right of action under this rule. Enforcement is exclusively by the Federal Trade Commission.
