GOVERNMENT OF PUERTO RICO
SENATE OF PUERTO RICO
Senate Bill No. 903
January 9, 2026
Submitted by Mrs. Soto Aguilú
Referred to the Committee on Innovation, Reform and Appointments
ACT
To create the "Fair Access to Shows and Events Act" (AJEE Act); to amend Article 5 of Act No. 182-1996, as amended, known as the "Public Show Promoter Act"; to establish tiered penalties; to promote total transparency in the setting and disclosure of ticket prices for public events, including in-person, virtual or hybrid events; to prohibit additional charges not authorized by law such as service fees, facility fees, promoter fees, electronic processing fees, call center fees or any other accessory cost; to regulate the responsible use of artificial intelligence in determining dynamic pricing, seat selection and fraud prevention; to strengthen the duties of promoters and ticket sales platform providers; to guarantee comprehensive and automatic refunds in cases of cancellation; to empower the Department of Consumer Affairs to audit algorithms, impose sanctions and adopt innovative regulations; and to protect consumers, especially vulnerable groups such as persons with disabilities and minors, through equitable practices and cutting-edge technologies.
STATEMENT OF MOTIVES
Without a doubt, Puerto Rico is the main stage of the Caribbean when it comes to important presentations and shows. In recent years, particularly since 2024, Puerto Ricans have faced an exponential increase in the costs of access to cultural and entertainment events, exacerbated by post-pandemic recovery, global inflation, and the increasing use of artificial intelligence (AI) in pricing.
One of the main complaints that has been confirmed is the lack of transparency at the moment of purchasing tickets, since it turns out to be a considerably high amount compared to the prices that are advertised. To the base ticket prices are added hidden charges such as the Digital Service Charge, Facility Sustainability Charge and Biometric Verification Charge, which raise the final cost by an average of 20-30%, according to market studies conducted in 2025. These charges, plus the 11.5% IVU (sales tax), disproportionately impact low-income families and young people, who constitute a significant portion of attendees at these events.
At the end of 2025, new scenarios arose, that is, controversies such as the use of AI-driven dynamic pricing on international platforms generated mass complaints, with prices that doubled in minutes without transparency. Locally, the rise of hybrid events amplified problems related to broadcast or "streaming" charges, especially affecting Puerto Rico, an Island where music is promoted on a large scale and which attracts numerous international artists, who carry out massive tours that generate a significant economic and cultural impact.
This vibrant musical scene, which positions the Island as a Caribbean entertainment hub, makes consumer protection even more urgent, as consumers deserve fair practices to enjoy these shows without being victims of abuses in ticket sales. The issue does not only affect us; at the federal level, the Junk Fees Prevention Act of 2024 and FTC rules of 2025 require full price disclosure and combat hidden fees. States such as California integrated blockchain for transparent tracking, reducing fraud.
AI offers benefits such as demand prediction and bot detection, but without regulation it can generate opacity in dynamic pricing or algorithmic manipulation. It is imperative to update the legal framework to incorporate cutting-edge protections: transparency in the use of AI, integration of blockchain and fair practices, prioritizing accessibility over hidden gains, thereby safeguarding the right of consumers in a territory where music and artistic events are pillars of cultural identity and economic development.
BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY OF PUERTO RICO:
Article 1. – Title.
This Act shall be called the "Fair Access to Shows and Events Act" (AJEE Act).
Article 2. – Declaration of Public Policy.
It is declared the public policy of the Government of Puerto Rico to foster an equitable and transparent ecosystem in the marketing of tickets for public entertainment activities, encompassing in-person, digital or mixed modalities. This includes: 1) the mandatory integration of all operating expenses in the initial value of the ticket, suppressing supplementary fees not established by legal rule; 2) complete and automated refunds in suspension situations; 3) immediate and exhaustive disclosure of total values and available stock through online interfaces; 4) the ethical and controlled use of technological innovations such as machine learning systems and blockchains to favor the buyer, with mandatory notification if machine learning is applied in adjustments of variable values or distribution of locations; 5) the restriction of computerized or machine learning processes that distort the choice of locations, simulate fictitious shortages, or create individualized values without clarity.
Article 3. – Definitions.
 a) Promoter: Individual or entity, local or foreign, that holds a promoter's license issued by the Office of Services to the Promoter of Public Shows of the Department of the Treasury, in charge of promoting or coordinating a public entertainment event, involving the selection of the site, contractual agreements, and managerial and dissemination aspects.
b) Ticket Sales Platform Provider: Organization accredited by the Office of Services to the Promoter of Public Shows of the Department of the Treasury to distribute physical, digital or NFT-format tickets, with responsibility for incorporating technological safeguards against deception.
c) Public Show: Any activity with paid admission, whether live, remote or combined, including recitals, stage performances, athletic competitions, forums, or live broadcasts. Non-profit initiatives of community, religious, partisan, educational or governmental groups are excepted, as well as commercial expositions or workshops for experts.
d) Technological Charge: Any surcharge for online authentication, live broadcast or use of applications, prohibited if not incorporated in the initial value of the ticket.
e) Artificial Intelligence in Ticket Sales: Application of algorithmic routines or machine learning models to establish variable values, assign positions, or estimate requirements, the implementation of which must be prominently notified to the buyer.
f) Base Price: The initial amount established by the promoter that encompasses all expenses inherent in the holding of the event, without subsequent additions.
g) Hybrid Event: Activity that combines in-person and virtual elements, allowing remote participation through technology.
Article 4. – Duties and Obligations of the Platform Providers.
Every provider shall:
a) Hold a current license from the Office of Services to the Promoter of Public Shows of the Department of the Treasury.
b) Display an interactive diagram of locations with the final total value (including IVU) and its exhaustive breakdown from the start of the selection process, being able to incorporate augmented reality where appropriate. It is prohibited to disguise or postpone the disclosure of total values.
c) Present in real time the precise availability of positions. The use of systems that emulate non-existent transactions or alter the perception of stock through non-transparent machine learning is prohibited.
d) Notify the refund guidelines clearly through push alerts on mobile applications. If machine learning is used for values or distribution, it must be specified in a visible and accessible manner.
e) Ensure universal inclusion, with adapted alternatives for persons with disabilities.
f) Submit detailed marketing reports to the Office of Services to the Promoter of Public Shows of the Department of the Treasury.
g) Adopt blockchains for the secure tracking of tickets and machine learning tools only for positive purposes such as identification of scams, not to generate lack of clarity.
Article 5. – Right to Comprehensive Refund in Case of Cancellation.
In the event of suspension or postponement of a public entertainment activity, every buyer shall have the right to the automatic and complete return of the amount paid, encompassing initial value, IVU and any related expense. The refund to credit cards must be executed within a maximum term of 72 hours.
Article 6. – Article 5 of Act No. 182-1996, as amended, known as the "Public Show Promoter Act," is hereby amended to read as follows:
"Article 5. — Obligation of the promoter.
In the event of postponement or suspension of the artistic event, whether by fortuitous event or by any other reason, the promoter shall be under the obligation to refund the amount of the tickets to the consumers who have acquired them within a period of 15 days following the date of suspension.
Within the period of fifteen (15) days, previously mentioned, the promoter shall provide the consumer with a minimum of seven (7) days for him to be able to request the refund. They must be available during eight (8) working hours including Saturday, in such a way that it facilitates the consumer's request for the refund without his having to be absent from his employment.
Likewise, he shall be obligated to [give notices] issue notifications through the press, social platforms, websites, text messages, electronic mail, radio or television of the suspension of the artistic event. Provided, that he shall give notice during reasonable hours in the aforementioned media of the dates, hours and place where the money will be refunded.
Any suspension or modification of the date or time of the event must be communicated immediately to the public, with a minimum of forty-eight (48) hours in advance of the event, except for force majeure. If the promoter used any digital interface for the marketing and sale of tickets for which the buyer registered his electronic mail, the notification must be sent to the registered mail address. Likewise, he shall inform the Office of Services to the Promoter of Public Shows of the Department of the Treasury about the suspension within the indicated period.
The promoter shall also be obligated to inform the public clearly of what the event to be offered will consist. In the case of a musical event, he must inform the public of the possibility that the performer uses backing tracks or performs lip-sync in one or more of his performances.
The promoter shall be responsible for sharing with the ticket sales platform provider the following information for its disclosure at the time of marketing: a) total positions or spaces available for the event, b) refund guidelines in case of suspension of the event, c) layout of positions with the total values of each area, together with the breakdown of the components by ticket value, fees and taxes, and d) any other data that is required to be disclosed by legal rule."
Article 7. – Prohibition of Accessory Charges.
It is prohibited to apply any supplementary fee to the initial value established by the promoter. All operational, technological or machine learning system-derived expenses must be incorporated from the beginning in said initial value.
This expressly includes the prohibition of service charges, facility charges ("facility fees"), charges for the promoter ("promoter fees"), digital operations processing charges, call center charges, biometric authentication charges, artificial intelligence processing charges, venue sustainability charges, digital verification charges, live broadcast charges ("streaming access"), as well as any other fee, surcharge or addition to the price of a ticket for public entertainment activities that is not set by legal provision.
The Department of Consumer Affairs shall conduct annual reviews, encompassing the scrutiny of machine learning routines to ensure clarity and fairness.
Article 8. – Penalties.
The Department of Consumer Affairs may impose tiered fines between $5,000 and $50,000 per infraction, considering the severity and recidivism. Violations related to opaque or ambiguous use of artificial intelligence or lack of transparency shall be sanctioned with greater severity. In cases of recidivism, the revocation of licenses shall proceed.
Article 9. – Powers of the Department of Consumer Affairs.
 DACO is empowered to adopt regulations within ninety (90) days, including: 1) a mandatory mobile application for the filing of complaints; 2) detailed guides on the ethical and transparent use of AI in the ticket sales industry; and 3) periodic algorithmic audit protocols.
Article 10. - Severability Clause.
If any provision of this Act is declared unconstitutional, null or invalid by a competent court, said declaration shall not affect the other provisions of this Act, which shall continue in full force and effect.
Article 11.- Effective Date.
This Act shall take effect ninety (90) days after its approval.
