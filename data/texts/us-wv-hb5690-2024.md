# West Virginia House Bill 5690 — West Virginia Task Force on Artificial Intelligence Act

**West Virginia 86th Legislature, 2024 Regular Session**
**Enacted:** April 22, 2024 | **Effective:** April 22, 2024

**Citation**
*West Virginia — April 22, 2024*

---

## Overview

West Virginia HB 5690 creates the West Virginia Task Force on Artificial Intelligence within the Governor's Office of Technology. The Task Force is a multi-stakeholder body composed of state officials, industry representatives, and academic experts. It is charged with defining artificial intelligence for state purposes, determining appropriate oversight responsibilities, identifying best practices for government AI use, and recommending legislative and regulatory frameworks for West Virginia. The Task Force is required to submit a report to the Legislature by July 1, 2025. This is a study and advisory measure; it does not impose obligations on industry or create regulatory requirements.

---

## Key Provisions

### Creation of the Task Force
- The **West Virginia Task Force on Artificial Intelligence** is established within the Governor's Office of Technology (or equivalent executive office).
- The Task Force is a permanent advisory body subject to a reporting obligation.

### Membership Composition
- Members include:
  - State government officials — representatives from relevant state agencies (technology, education, economic development, workforce, public safety);
  - Industry representatives — technology companies, AI developers, and relevant private sector stakeholders operating in West Virginia;
  - Academic experts — faculty from West Virginia University, Marshall University, and other state institutions with expertise in AI, computer science, or related fields;
  - Legislative liaisons — members of the West Virginia Legislature, appointed by the Senate President and House Speaker.

### Duties and Charge
The Task Force is tasked with:

1. **Defining AI for state purposes** — Establishing a working definition of "artificial intelligence" and related terms for use in West Virginia law, regulation, and policy.

2. **Oversight mapping** — Determining which state agencies and offices have existing authority over AI systems and identifying gaps in current oversight capacity.

3. **Best practices for government AI** — Identifying best practices for the responsible use of AI tools by state agencies, including guidance on procurement, transparency, bias mitigation, and accountability.

4. **Legislative and regulatory recommendations** — Developing and submitting recommended legislative changes or new legislation governing AI use in West Virginia, covering sectors such as:
   - Employment and hiring;
   - Healthcare and insurance;
   - Criminal justice and public safety;
   - Education;
   - Consumer protection.

5. **Economic development** — Assessing opportunities to position West Virginia as an AI-friendly environment for business investment while ensuring appropriate consumer protections.

### Report Requirement
- The Task Force must submit a **written report** to the Governor and the West Virginia Legislature by **July 1, 2025**.
- The report must include findings, conclusions, and specific legislative and regulatory recommendations.

---

## Scope and Nature

- **Advisory only** — The Task Force has no regulatory authority and imposes no obligations on private industry.
- **Non-binding** — Recommendations require subsequent legislative or executive action to take effect.
- **Government-facing** — While the Task Force may study industry AI use, its primary scope is state government AI policy.

## Significance

HB 5690 represents West Virginia's initial formal step toward developing a state AI governance framework. By establishing a task force rather than enacting substantive AI regulation, West Virginia follows the approach taken by many states (including Mississippi, North Carolina, and others) of studying AI policy before committing to legislative solutions. The Task Force model allows for multi-stakeholder input and avoids premature regulation of a rapidly evolving technology sector, while ensuring that state government is positioned to respond as AI adoption grows.
