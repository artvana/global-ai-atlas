## Summary (verify for accuracy)

European Commission legislative proposal (November 2025) to amend the EU AI Act as part of the Digital Omnibus package. Key changes: defer Annex III high-risk AI system obligations from August 2026 to December 2027; narrow AI literacy obligations for deployers; expand SME and research exemptions; add legitimate interest legal basis for personal data use in AI training. In trilogue as of May 2026 — second trilogue failed to reach agreement; third trilogue scheduled 13 May 2026.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Defers Annex III high-risk AI compliance to December 2027
- Narrows AI literacy requirements to staff directly interacting with high-risk AI
- Expands SME and micro-enterprise exemptions from conformity assessment obligations
- Adds legitimate interest legal basis for AI training data use

## Overview

The European Commission introduced the Digital Omnibus package on 19 November 2025 as part of a broader effort to reduce regulatory compliance burdens across the digital sector and support EU competitiveness in technology. The package comprises two linked proposals: this regulation amending the EU AI Act, and a separate regulation amending other digital laws (GDPR, NIS2, DORA, Data Act, and related instruments).

The AI Act amendment proposal targets several provisions of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 that industry groups and member states had identified as creating disproportionate compliance burdens, particularly for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and academic research institutions. The proposal does not alter the EU AI Act's core risk-tier architecture, prohibited practices list, or GPAI model obligations.
