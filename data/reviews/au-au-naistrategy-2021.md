## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Australia's AI Action Plan 2021 is a high-level national strategy organised around three pillars — getting AI foundations right (data, infrastructure, trust), growing Australia's AI capability (research, business adoption, international engagement), and using AI for the greater good (health, environment, regional communities). It reinforces Australia's voluntary AI Ethics Principles (2019) and commits the federal government to applying them in all AI procurement and development, while encouraging (but not requiring) private sector adoption. The Action Plan has been substantially superseded by the Voluntary AI Safety Standard (September 2024) and the forthcoming mandatory high-risk AI regime.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Government agencies must apply Australia's AI Ethics Principles in all AI procurement and development (voluntary/internal policy)
- CSIRO Data61 to establish and operate the National AI Centre (NAIC) as national coordination hub
- Digital Transformation Agency to coordinate whole-of-government AI adoption strategy
- Government to release high-value public datasets for AI research through data.gov.au
- DISER to invest in AI research through ARC and CSIRO and to support SME AI adoption through advisory programs
- Australia to actively participate in OECD AI Policy Observatory and GPAI

## Overview

Australia's Artificial Intelligence Action Plan 2021 is the Australian Government's national strategy for maximising the economic and social benefits of AI while ensuring its responsible and ethical use. Published by the Department of Industry, Science, Energy and Resources (DISER, now DISR — Department of Industry, Science and Resources) in June 2021, it replaced the earlier "AI Technology Roadmap" consultations and established a government-wide framework for AI investment, adoption, and governance.

The Action Plan is a policy document, not legislation. It sets out priorities and government commitments rather than legally binding obligations on the private sector. Its primary audiences are government agencies (which are expected to lead by example in responsible AI adoption), Australian businesses (particularly SMEs), and the research community.
