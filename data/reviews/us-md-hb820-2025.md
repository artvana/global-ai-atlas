## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Regulates AI use in health insurance utilization management, requiring coverage decisions to be based on individual patient clinical data (not solely on group-level statistical data) and mandating licensed clinician review and sign-off before any AI-informed denial. Insurers must disclose to both providers and patients when AI is used in coverage or medical necessity determinations.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Utilization review decisions must be based on individual patient's specific clinical data, not solely group-level statistical data
- Licensed clinician must review and approve or deny coverage decisions; AI output may not substitute for clinician judgment
- Insurers must disclose to treating providers when AI is used in utilization review
- Insurers must disclose to patients when AI is used in coverage or medical necessity determinations affecting their care

## Law Text (excerpt)

```
# Maryland House Bill 820 — Health Insurance AI in Utilization Review Act

**House Bill No. 820**

*Maryland — May 1, 2025*

---

## Key Provisions

### Purpose and Scope

HB 820 regulates the use of artificial intelligence and algorithms in health insurance utilization management (utilization review) decisions, prohibiting AI from replacing licensed clinician judgment in medical necessity determinations and requiring disclosure to patients and providers when AI is used.

### Individual Clinical Data Requirement

Utilization review decisions must be based on the individual patient's specific clinical data. Insurers may not base coverage or medical necessity determinations solely on group-level statistical data, population averages, or algorithmic outputs that do not account for the individual patient's actual clinical presentation and circumstances.

### Clinician Review Requirement (Human-in-the-Loop)

AI systems may not replace the judgment of a licensed clinician in medical necessity determinations. A licensed clinician must review and approve or deny coverage decisions; algorithmic outputs may inform but not substitute for that clinician review and sign-off.
```
