## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Canada’s proposed federal AI governance statute, Part 3 of Bill C-27 (Digital Charter Implementation Act, 2022). Would have created obligations for developers and deployers of ‘high-impact AI systems’ to assess and mitigate risks of harm and biased output, maintain records, and publish plain-language descriptions. A new Artificial Intelligence and Data Commissioner would have overseen compliance with powers to audit AI systems and impose administrative penalties. Prohibited processing personal data obtained unlawfully to train AI, deepfake intimate images, and deployment of AI systems known to cause serious harm. Also proposed GPAI-specific obligations (added in 2023 amendments) addressing general-purpose AI systems. Died January 6, 2025 when Parliament prorogued following PM Trudeau’s resignation announcement.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Assess whether AI system is a high-impact system and identify foreseeable risks of harm or biased output
- Implement and document measures to mitigate identified risks of harm and biased output
- Make available plain-language public description of high-impact AI system and its uses
- Notify Minister of serious risks and cease operation if serious risk cannot be mitigated
- Maintain records of risk assessments and mitigation measures for regulatory review
- Developers of GPAI must maintain technical documentation and publish training data summaries (2023 amendments)
- Prohibition on processing personal data obtained unlawfully to train AI systems
- Prohibition on deploying AI systems known or recklessly likely to cause serious harm
- Prohibition on generating non-consensual synthetic intimate images (deepfakes)

# Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA) — Canada Bill C-27

## Overview

The **Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA)** was Part 3 of **Bill C-27**, the *Digital Charter Implementation Act, 2022*, introduced by Minister of Innovation François-Philippe Champagne in the House of Commons on June 16, 2022. It was Canada's proposed comprehensive federal AI governance framework — the first of its kind at the federal level in Canada — and would have been the most significant AI law in North America had it passed.

Bill C-27 had three parts:
- **Part 1**: *Consumer Privacy Protection Act (CPPA)* — replacing PIPEDA with a modernized private-sector privacy law.
- **Part 2**: *Personal Information and Data Protection Tribunal Act* — creating a new enforcement tribunal.
- **Part 3**: *Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA)* — the AI-specific component.

AIDA died when Parliament was prorogued on **January 6, 2025**, after Prime Minister Justin Trudeau announced his intention to resign as Liberal Party leader. The bill had been in extended committee study and was never brought to a final vote. It was never enacted.
