## Summary (verify for accuracy)

European Commission proposal (November 2025) amending six EU digital regulations simultaneously: narrows GDPR personal data identifiability standard; extends breach notification from 72 to 96 hours; integrates cookie consent into GDPR framework; clarifies legitimate interest basis for AI training data; amends NIS2, Data Act, DORA, CER, and eIDAS 2.0. Part of the Digital Omnibus package alongside the AI Act amendment proposal.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Narrows personal data definition identifiability standard (GDPR amendment)
- Extends breach notification window to 96 hours (GDPR Art. 33 amendment)
- Moves cookie consent framework into GDPR (ePrivacy simplification)
- Clarifies legitimate interest for AI training data (GDPR Recital)
- Harmonizes NIS2 significant incident definition across member states
- Clarifies DORA application to AI-based financial tools

## Overview

The Digital Omnibus digital law amendment proposal is the companion instrument to the AI Act amendment proposal, forming part of the European Commission's November 2025 Digital Omnibus package. This proposal amends six major EU digital regulations simultaneously in a single omnibus legislative act, a legislative technique designed to reduce the complexity and cost of sequential reforms.

The proposal does not establish a new regulatory framework; it is a horizontal amendment instrument modifying: the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, Regulation (EU) 2016/679); the NIS2 Directive (Directive (EU) 2022/2555); the Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854); the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA, Regulation (EU) 2022/2554); the Critical Entities Resilience (CER) Directive; and the European Digital Identity (eIDAS 2.0) Regulation.
