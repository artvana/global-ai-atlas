## Summary (verify for accuracy)

The Digital Services Act establishes a tiered regulatory framework for online intermediary services in the EU, imposing the heaviest obligations — including annual algorithmic audits, systemic risk assessments, and researcher data access — on Very Large Online Platforms and Very Large Online Search Engines with ≥45 million EU users. All online platforms using recommender systems must disclose their main parameters and offer at least one non-profiling recommendation option. The European Commission has direct enforcement jurisdiction over VLOPs/VLOSEs with penalties up to 6% of global turnover.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Art. 27: All online platforms using recommender systems must disclose main parameters in plain language and offer at least one non-profiling option
- Art. 38: VLOPs/VLOSEs must make the non-profiling recommender option prominently available at the point of recommendation
- Art. 34: VLOPs/VLOSEs must conduct at least annual systemic risk assessments covering algorithmic amplification effects
- Art. 35: VLOPs/VLOSEs must implement proportionate risk mitigation measures addressing identified systemic risks
- Art. 37: VLOPs/VLOSEs must submit to at least annual independent algorithmic audits and publish audit reports
- Art. 40: VLOPs/VLOSEs must provide vetted researchers with access to data necessary for systemic risk research
- Art. 26: All online platforms must label advertisements and disclose targeting parameters to recipients
- Art. 39: VLOPs/VLOSEs must maintain a publicly searchable advertising repository

## Overview

The Digital Services Act (DSA) is a horizontal EU regulation that establishes a comprehensive framework governing online intermediary services — including hosting providers, online platforms, and very large online platforms (VLOPs) and very large online search engines (VLOSEs). Its central aim is to ensure that the digital environment remains safe, predictable, and trustworthy. The DSA repeals and replaces the relevant liability provisions of the 2000 E-Commerce Directive (2000/31/EC).

The DSA applies to all providers of intermediary services that offer services to recipients located in the EU, regardless of the provider's place of establishment.
