## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Uruguay's primary data protection law and the first outside Europe to receive EU adequacy recognition (2012). Establishes the URCDP as supervisory authority, provides seven data protection principles, ARCO rights (access, rectification, cancellation, opposition), habeas data judicial action, mandatory database registration, sensitive data categories, and cross-border transfer restrictions.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Register all personal data databases with URCDP
- Comply with seven data protection principles
- Implement lawful processing basis for all personal data processing
- Protect sensitive data categories with explicit consent or legal authority
- Provide data subject ARCO rights (access, rectification, cancellation, opposition)
- Implement appropriate security measures
- Restrict cross-border transfers to countries with adequate protection

## Overview

Uruguay's Law 18.331 of August 11, 2008, on Personal Data Protection and the Habeas Data Action is the country's primary data protection law and one of the most internationally recognized data protection frameworks in Latin America. Uruguay received adequacy recognition from the European Commission in 2012 under EU Directive 95/46/EC — the first and only Latin American country to achieve this status as of 2026. This recognition significantly facilitates data flows between Uruguay and EU member states. The law establishes the Unidad Reguladora y de Control de Datos Personales (URCDP) as the supervisory authority and provides both administrative remedies through URCDP and judicial remedies through the habeas data action.
