## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Jordan's first comprehensive data protection law establishes the Personal Data Protection Council as an independent supervisory authority and creates a consent-based framework covering all organizations processing data of Jordanians and residents. Key provisions include core processing principles, sensitive data categories (health, genetic, biometric, political, religious, criminal, financial), full data subject rights including portability, DPO requirements for large-scale or sensitive processing, mandatory breach notification, and cross-border transfer restrictions. Implementing regulations were still in development as of 2026.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Establish a valid lawful basis before processing personal data of Jordanians or residents
- Apply heightened protections for sensitive data categories including biometric, health, financial, and criminal data
- Respond to data subject access, correction, deletion, objection, and portability requests within regulatory timeframes
- Notify PDPC and affected individuals of personal data breaches without undue delay
- Appoint a DPO and register with PDPC for large-scale or sensitive data processing activities

## Overview / Background

Law No. 24 of 2023 on the Protection of Personal Data (the Jordan PDPL) is Jordan's first comprehensive personal data protection statute. Enacted on September 24, 2023, and entering into force on the same date, the PDPL establishes the legal framework for the protection of personal data in the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan and creates an independent supervisory authority — the Personal Data Protection Council (PDPC) — to oversee and enforce compliance.

The Jordan PDPL was developed in the context of Jordan's e-government modernization agenda and reflects the Kingdom's engagement with international data protection norms including the OECD Privacy Guidelines, the Arab League's data protection model law, and elements of the EU GDPR. Jordan's law shares structural similarities with Bahrain's PDPL 2018 and Oman's PDPL 2022, reflecting a broader convergence in GCC and Levant data protection frameworks.
