## Summary (verify for accuracy)

The UAE's first comprehensive federal data protection law establishes personal data rights including protections for automated processing. Article 14 provides data subjects the right to human review of decisions with legal/significant effects produced solely by automated means. Modeled on GDPR with lighter enforcement.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Lawful basis required for all personal data processing including AI training
- Data subject right to contest decisions made solely by automated means (Article 14)
- Data breach notification within 72 hours
- DPO appointment required for high-risk processing
- Cross-border transfer controls

## Summary and Analysis

### Purpose and Scope

Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on the Protection of Personal Data (the "UAE PDPL" or "the Law") represents the United Arab Emirates' first comprehensive, federal-level data protection statute. Issued on 26 September 2021 and entering into force on 2 January 2022, with an implementing regulation published in 2022, the Law was designed to bring the UAE into alignment with international data protection standards — most notably the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) — while reflecting the UAE's particular economic model and federal governance structure.

The Law applies to the processing of personal data of natural persons residing within the UAE, whether the processing is carried out by public or private entities established in the UAE or by entities outside the UAE that process data of UAE residents. Critically, the Law explicitly excludes two of the UAE's most significant financial free zones: the Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) and the Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC), each of which maintains its own independent data protection regime.
