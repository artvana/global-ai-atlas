## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Qatar's personal data protection law establishing rights and obligations for personal data processing. Applied to AI systems through NCSA guidance. Requires consent for processing personal data, provides access and rectification rights, and classifies sensitive data (health, biometric, financial) with heightened protections. Penalties include both fines and imprisonment for serious violations.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Obtain consent for collection and processing of personal data
- Provide data subjects with access, rectification, and deletion rights
- Apply heightened protections to sensitive data including biometrics and health data
- Register personal data processing operations with NCSA
- Implement technical and organisational measures for data security

### Purpose and Scope

Qatar Law No. 13 of 2016 on the Protection of Personal Data Privacy (the "PDPPL" or "the Law") is Qatar's primary data protection statute, establishing a general framework governing the collection, processing, storage, disclosure, and transfer of personal data in the State of Qatar. Enacted in November 2016, the Law reflects Qatar's commitment, as part of its National Vision 2030 digital development agenda, to establishing a modern regulatory infrastructure for the digital economy while protecting citizens' and residents' privacy rights. The Law predates the GDPR (effective May 2018) and was drafted with reference to earlier Council of Europe and OECD frameworks, though subsequent guidance from the implementing authority has sought to align its application with more recent international standards.
