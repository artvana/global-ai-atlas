## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Virginia’s proposed comprehensive AI consumer protection statute, closely modeled on Colorado SB 24-205 (Colorado AI Act). Would have required developers and deployers of ‘high-risk AI’ systems used in consequential decisions (employment, housing, credit, healthcare, education, insurance) to conduct algorithmic impact assessments, notify consumers of AI use, provide human review rights, and use reasonable care to prevent algorithmic discrimination. Passed both chambers of the Virginia General Assembly in early 2025. Vetoed by Governor Glenn Youngkin in April 2025 on grounds of regulatory burden and innovation concerns.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Deployers must use reasonable care to prevent algorithmic discrimination in consequential decisions
- Complete documented algorithmic impact assessment before deployment and annually thereafter
- Notify consumers when high-risk AI is used in a consequential decision
- Provide opportunity for human review and appeal of AI-assisted consequential decisions
- Developers must provide deployers with documentation on intended uses, limitations, and performance data
- Maintain impact assessment records for at least three years

# High-Risk Artificial Intelligence Developer and Deployer Act (Virginia SB 1090)

## Overview

Virginia Senate Bill 1090, introduced by Senator Saddam Salim in the 2025 legislative session, would have enacted the **High-Risk Artificial Intelligence Developer and Deployer Act** — Virginia's first comprehensive AI governance statute. The bill passed both chambers of the Virginia General Assembly and was enrolled to the Governor. Governor Glenn Youngkin vetoed the bill in April 2025, citing concerns about regulatory burden on technology companies and innovation.

SB 1090 was closely modeled on **Colorado SB 24-205** (the Colorado AI Act, enacted May 2024), applying a risk-based consumer protection framework to AI developers and deployers operating in Virginia. It would have been the second US state comprehensive AI governance law after Colorado.

The bill applied to:
- **Developers** of high-risk AI systems that are offered to Virginia consumers or used to make consequential decisions affecting Virginia consumers.
