## Summary (verify for accuracy)

The UK Government's March 2023 White Paper sets out a deliberately non-statutory, sector-led approach to AI governance — explicitly diverging from the EU AI Act model — based on five cross-sector principles (safety, transparency, fairness, accountability, and contestability) to be implemented by existing UK sector regulators within their statutory mandates. Rather than creating a new AI regulator or binding obligations, the White Paper delegates implementation to bodies including the FCA, Ofcom, ICO, CMA, and MHRA. The February 2024 consultation response confirmed this architecture while introducing planned 'backstop' legislative powers for Ministers to direct regulators if they fail to implement the principles.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Principle 1 (Safety, Security, Robustness): Sector regulators should address AI system testing, monitoring, and secure failure modes
- Principle 2 (Transparency and Explainability): Sector regulators should require appropriate transparency about AI functioning, proportionate to risk of harm
- Principle 3 (Fairness): Sector regulators should ensure AI does not discriminate unlawfully under the Equality Act 2010
- Principle 4 (Accountability and Governance): Sector regulators should require clear accountability lines for AI systems and their outcomes
- Principle 5 (Contestability and Redress): Sector regulators should ensure affected individuals can contest AI decisions and seek redress
- DSIT: Publish AI regulatory landscape monitoring and coordinate cross-sector regulatory consistency
- Sector regulators: Report AI regulatory activities to DSIT; update guidance to address AI within existing statutory remit

## Overview

The UK Government's White Paper "A Pro-Innovation Approach to AI Regulation" (CP 815), published by the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT) on 29 March 2023, sets out the UK's post-Brexit strategy for governing artificial intelligence. It represents a deliberate and explicit rejection of the EU's binding, centralised, and sector-agnostic regulatory model in favour of a flexible, sector-led, and initially non-statutory approach.

The White Paper was accompanied by a public consultation (closed 21 June 2023). The government's response to the consultation, published in February 2024, confirmed the general architecture while committing to monitoring powers for the central AI Safety Institute (AISI) and a future "backstop" legislative power if regulators fail to act.
