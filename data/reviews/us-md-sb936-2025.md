## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Maryland SB 936 (2025), with House companion HB 1331, would have required developers and deployers of high-risk AI systems to use reasonable care to protect consumers from algorithmic discrimination. Developers would have been required to document intended uses, limitations, and risks; deployers would have been required to implement risk management policies, conduct pre-deployment impact assessments, and notify consumers when high-risk AI is used in consequential decisions affecting them. The Attorney General would have enforced the act. Both bills died in committee during the 2025 regular session without reaching a floor vote. Note: sometimes cited in secondary sources under the bill numbers SB 1050/HB 1457, which do not correspond to AI legislation in the 2025 Maryland session.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Developers of high-risk AI must document intended uses, known limitations, and foreseeable risks of algorithmic discrimination
- Deployers must implement written risk management policies before deploying high-risk AI
- Deployers must conduct impact assessments prior to deployment of high-risk AI
- Consumers must be notified when high-risk AI is used in consequential decisions affecting them
- Consumers have the right to correct personal data and appeal adverse AI-driven decisions
- Attorney General may enforce; no private right of action

## Legislative Status

SB 936 and its House companion HB 1331 were introduced in the Maryland General Assembly's 2025 regular session. SB 936 had a hearing before the Senate Finance Committee on February 27, 2025, but did not advance to a floor vote. HB 1331 similarly failed to cross the legislative deadline. Both bills died in committee at the close of the 2025 session (Sine Die, April 2025). Neither bill was signed or vetoed.
