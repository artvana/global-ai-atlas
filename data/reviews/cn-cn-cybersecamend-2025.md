## Summary (verify for accuracy)

First major amendment to China's Cybersecurity Law since its 2017 enactment. Adds explicit AI safety provisions: AI systems deployed in critical infrastructure and key sectors must meet cybersecurity standards, AI providers must conduct security assessments, and entities using AI must implement AI ethics standards and monitoring. Increases maximum fines tenfold (from RMB 1M to RMB 10M) and adds personal liability for responsible persons.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- AI in critical infrastructure must meet CAC cybersecurity standards
- Conduct AI security assessments before deployment in key sectors
- Implement AI ethics standards and ongoing monitoring
- Report AI-related cybersecurity incidents to authorities
- Designated responsible persons subject to personal liability for violations

**Adopted at the 13th Session of the Standing Committee of the 14th National People's Congress | October 28, 2025**

## Overview

The 2025 amendments to the Cybersecurity Law of the People's Republic of China (first enacted 2017) significantly update China's foundational cybersecurity framework to address AI systems, critical information infrastructure, data security, and new categories of network products and services. The amendments incorporate and systematize provisions from previously scattered administrative regulations (notably the Data Security Law 2021, the PIPL 2021, and the AI Governance Regulations 2023–2025) into the unified cybersecurity framework.
