## Summary (verify for accuracy)

The FTC's 2023 Policy Statement on AI articulates the Commission's approach to AI enforcement under existing FTC Act authority. It asserts that unfair or deceptive AI practices, discriminatory algorithmic decisions, unfair data collection for AI training, and deceptive AI claims all fall within the FTC's existing Section 5 jurisdiction. The statement covers generative AI, algorithmic pricing, biometric data, and synthetic media.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Truthful claims about AI capabilities required
- Discriminatory algorithmic outcomes may violate FTC Act Section 5
- Unfair data collection for AI training prohibited
- Algorithmic pricing coordination subject to antitrust scrutiny

*Federal Trade Commission — November 2023*

*Note: The FTC issued several interrelated policy statements, enforcement advisories, and reports on AI in 2023. This file consolidates the key FTC enforcement positions on AI from the following primary sources: (1) the FTC's November 2023 policy statement on biometric information and AI surveillance; (2) FTC enforcement actions and advisory statements on AI deception and unfairness under Section 5 of the FTC Act; (3) the FTC's report "Generative AI Raises Competition Concerns" (June 2023); and (4) enforcement policy positions on AI-related violations of the FTC Act, the Equal Credit Opportunity Act, the Fair Credit Reporting Act, and Section 5.*

The Federal Trade Commission exercises jurisdiction over unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce under Section 5 of the FTC Act (15 U.S.C. § 45).
