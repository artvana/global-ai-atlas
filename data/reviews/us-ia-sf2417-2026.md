## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Requires operators of conversational AI services to disclose AI identity to users (with persistent disclosure requirements for minor users), prohibit AI from impersonating licensed mental health professionals, and adopt suicide and crisis response protocols. Modeled closely on Nebraska's LB 525 and Idaho's SB 1297.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Operators must disclose AI identity at outset of conversational interactions
- Persistent AI identity disclosure for minor users
- AI systems may not impersonate licensed mental health professionals
- Must adopt suicide and crisis response protocols including referral to crisis hotlines
- Must not use manipulative engagement techniques targeting minors

## Law Text (excerpt)

# Iowa Senate File 2417 — Conversational Artificial Intelligence Safety Act

**Senate File 2417**

*Iowa — April 16, 2026*

---

## Key Provisions

### Purpose and Scope

SF 2417 establishes safety and disclosure requirements for operators of conversational AI services, with particular protections for minor users. The act is closely modeled on Nebraska's Conversational AI Safety Act (LB 525) and Idaho SB 1297.

### AI Identity Disclosure

Operators of conversational AI services must:
- Disclose to users at the outset of an interaction that they are communicating with an AI system, not a human
- Implement persistent disclosure mechanisms for minor users so that the AI identity is continuously apparent during the interaction
- Not deny being an AI when sincerely asked by a user

### Prohibition on Mental Health Professional Impersonation

The bill prohibits conversational AI systems from impersonating licensed mental health professionals. Operators may not allow or design their AI systems to present themselves as psychiatrists, psychologists, licensed counselors, therapists, or other licensed mental health practitioners.
