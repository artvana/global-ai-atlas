## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Amends New York's post-mortem right of publicity law to remove the 'reasonable observer' deception standard and eliminate the disclaimer carve-out that previously permitted unauthorized use of deceased performer digital replicas with sufficient warnings. Extends protection to sound recordings and live musical performances, in addition to audiovisual works.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Unauthorized use of deceased performer digital replicas actionable without needing to show deception of a reasonable observer
- Disclaimer carve-out eliminated — disclaimers alone no longer authorize unauthorized digital replica use
- Protection extended to sound recordings and live musical performances in addition to audiovisual works

## Key Provisions

### Purpose and Scope

SB 8391 amends New York's post-mortem right of publicity law as it applies to digital replicas and AI-generated likenesses of deceased performers, significantly strengthening protections by closing two major loopholes.

### Removal of the "Reasonable Observer" Standard

Prior law required that a digital replica of a deceased performer be realistic enough to deceive a reasonable observer in order to be actionable. SB 8391 removes this standard, meaning that digital replicas need not be indistinguishable from the real person to be covered by the law.
