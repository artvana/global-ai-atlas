## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Canada's Treasury Board directive requires federal government institutions to assess and document the impact of automated decision systems before deploying them. Uses a tiered impact level system (I-IV) where higher-impact systems require peer review, algorithmic impact assessments, human review options, and explanations to affected parties. Level IV systems (highest impact) require ministerial approval. One of the first binding automated-decision governance rules for a national government.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Complete Algorithmic Impact Assessment before deploying automated decision systems
- Apply tiered obligations based on impact level (I-IV)
- Provide human review option for Level II+ systems
- Explain automated decisions to affected parties on request
- Obtain ministerial approval for Level IV (highest impact) systems
- Log all automated decisions for audit purposes

## Law Text (excerpt)

```
# Directive on Automated Decision-Making

The Government of Canada is increasingly looking to use artificial intelligence to make or support administrative decisions to improve service delivery. The government is committed to using artificial intelligence in a manner that is compatible with core principles of administrative law such as transparency, accountability, legality, and procedural fairness to ensure that decision-making processes are fair and unbiased. Understanding that this technology is changing rapidly, this directive will continue to evolve to ensure that it remains relevant.

Date modified: 2025-06-24
```
