## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Algeria's comprehensive personal data protection law establishing the ANPDP as an independent supervisory authority. Provides lawful processing bases, extensive sensitive data categories (health, genetic, biometric, political, religious, criminal), data subject rights, mandatory registration and authorization requirements, cross-border transfer restrictions, and criminal penalties.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Declare standard processing activities to ANPDP or obtain prior authorization for high-risk processing
- Implement lawful processing basis for all personal data processing
- Protect sensitive data categories with explicit consent or specific legal authority
- Provide data subject rights (information, access, rectification, opposition, deletion)
- Implement appropriate technical and organizational security measures
- Notify ANPDP of significant personal data breaches
- Restrict cross-border transfers to countries with adequate protection or ANPDP approval

## Overview

Algeria's Law 18-07 of June 10, 2018, is the country's comprehensive personal data protection statute, establishing a full data protection regime for the processing of personal data of Algerian residents. The law establishes the Autorité Nationale de Protection des Données à Caractère Personnel (ANPDP) as the independent supervisory authority. Drawing on the French civil law tradition and aligned with international best practices, the law applies to all processing of personal data carried out in Algeria or relating to Algerian residents, covering both public and private sectors. It represents Algeria's first comprehensive data protection legislation and was developed within the broader context of North Africa's digital governance reforms.
