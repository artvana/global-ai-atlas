## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Nigeria's first Act-level data protection law supersedes the 2019 NDPR regulation and establishes the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC). Section 36 requires disclosure and human review rights for automated decisions with significant effects, directly applicable to AI systems. GDPR-aligned principles apply across all sectors.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Legal basis required for all personal data processing (6 lawful bases)
- Section 36: automated decision-making with significant effects requires disclosure and human review right
- Data breach notification within 72 hours of discovery
- Data protection impact assessments for high-risk processing
- DPO appointment for large-scale or high-risk processing

## Summary and Analysis

### Purpose and Scope

The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (Act No. 4 of 2023, "NDPA" or "the Act") was signed into law on 12 June 2023 by President Bola Ahmed Tinubu, making it Nigeria's first comprehensive, primary legislation dedicated to the protection of personal data. Prior to the NDPA, Nigeria's data protection framework rested primarily on the Nigeria Data Protection Regulation 2019 (NDPR), a subsidiary instrument issued by the National Information Technology Development Agency (NITDA) under the NITDA Act. While the NDPR was landmark in establishing data protection principles and enforcement mechanisms, its status as a subsidiary regulation raised questions about its constitutional robustness and limited the enforcement penalties available. The NDPA corrects these limitations by establishing a comprehensive statutory framework backed by primary legislation.
