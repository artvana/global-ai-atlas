## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Australia's Voluntary AI Safety Standard articulates ten guardrails for responsible AI — covering accountability, transparency, human oversight, risk assessment, security, testing, privacy, data quality, explainability, and fairness/non-discrimination — published as a voluntary precursor to a proposed mandatory regime for high-risk AI. Organizations adopting the standard receive guidance for compliance with existing consumer protection, privacy, and anti-discrimination law. As of May 2026, the mandatory regime had not yet been enacted.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Establish governance structures with clear accountability for AI systems throughout their lifecycle
- Conduct structured risk assessments before deploying AI systems proportionate to potential impact
- Implement human oversight and control mechanisms enabling review and override of AI decisions
- Test AI systems before deployment and conduct ongoing evaluation including bias assessments
- Comply with Privacy Act 1988 Australian Privacy Principles for AI systems processing personal data

## Overview / Background

The Australian Voluntary AI Safety Standard was published by the Australian Department of Industry, Science and Resources on September 5, 2024, as part of the government's Safe and Responsible AI in Australia consultation process. It articulates ten voluntary guardrails for the responsible development and deployment of AI systems, intended to apply to organizations across all sectors.

The standard was developed in response to public consultation (conducted in 2023-2024) on Australia's approach to AI governance. It serves as a bridge between Australia's existing legal framework — which covers AI-related harms through existing consumer protection, privacy, anti-discrimination, and product liability laws — and a future proposed mandatory regime for high-risk AI systems. As of May 2026, the mandatory regime had not yet been enacted; the voluntary standard represents the primary operational guidance available to Australian organizations.
