## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Chile's comprehensive overhaul of personal data law replaces the 1999 Law 19.628 and aligns with GDPR standards. Article 8 bis grants data subjects the right to explanation and human review for automated decisions with significant effects. Creates an independent enforcement agency. Major obligations take effect December 2026.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Article 8 bis: right to explanation and human review for automated decisions with significant effects
- Legal basis required for processing (including legitimate interest)
- DPIAs required for high-risk processing
- Mandatory data breach notification
- DPO requirements for certain entities
- Cross-border transfer restrictions

## Summary and Analysis

### Purpose and Scope

Ley 21.719, published in the Diario Oficial on 13 December 2024, represents the most comprehensive reform of Chile's personal data protection framework since the enactment of Law 19.628 in 1999. The 2024 Law substantially amends Law 19.628 while introducing new institutional architecture, expanded individual rights, an AI-specific automated decision-making chapter, and a meaningful enforcement regime. It positions Chile as the first Latin American country with a fully modernized, GDPR-compatible data protection law, making Chile an attractive hub for data-processing enterprises seeking a robust and internationally recognized legal framework in the region.
