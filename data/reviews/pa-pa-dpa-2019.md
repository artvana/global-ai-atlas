## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Panama's first comprehensive personal data protection law establishing APDATOS within AIG as the supervisory authority. Provides seven data protection principles, consent-based processing, sensitive data categories, ARCO data subject rights, mandatory database registration, cross-border transfer restrictions, security obligations, breach notification, and administrative fines up to USD 50,000.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Register personal databases with APDATOS before commencing processing
- Implement seven data protection principles
- Obtain consent or other lawful basis for processing
- Protect sensitive data categories with explicit consent or legal authority
- Provide data subject ARCO rights
- Implement appropriate security measures
- Notify APDATOS and data subjects of significant breaches
- Restrict cross-border transfers to countries with adequate protection

## Overview

Panama's Law No. 81 of March 26, 2019, on Personal Data Protection is the country's first comprehensive personal data protection law. Enacted on March 26, 2019, it entered into force two years later on March 29, 2021, following an implementation period to allow organizations to prepare for compliance. Executive Decree No. 285 of 2021 provides the detailed implementing regulations. The law designates the Autoridad Nacional para la Innovación Gubernamental (AIG), through its data protection unit (APDATOS), as the supervisory authority responsible for overseeing compliance, receiving complaints, and enforcing the law. The law applies to all processing of personal data in Panama, covering both public and private sectors.
