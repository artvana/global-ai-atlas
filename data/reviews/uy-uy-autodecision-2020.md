## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Uruguay's regulation on automated individual decision-making, requiring meaningful human intervention for decisions significantly affecting individuals based solely on automated processing. Provides rights to human review, explanation of algorithmic logic, and DPIAs for high-risk automated processing. One of Latin America's most advanced AI-specific data protection rules.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Provide meaningful human intervention for significant automated decisions upon request
- Provide intelligible explanation of logic and criteria underlying automated decisions
- Conduct DPIAs for high-risk automated decision-making systems
- Inform data subjects when they are subject to automated decision-making
- Prohibit automated decisions based solely on sensitive data categories unless exceptions apply
- Maintain records of automated decision-making systems and their logic
- Implement safeguards to prevent discriminatory or arbitrary outcomes

## Overview

Uruguay's Executive Decree No. 64/020 of March 5, 2020, regulates individual automated decision-making within the framework of Law 18.331 on Personal Data Protection. This decree is one of Latin America's most advanced AI-specific data protection rules, adopted prior to any comprehensive AI legislation in the region. It establishes specific requirements for organizations that make decisions based solely on automated processing — including profiling — when those decisions significantly affect individuals. The decree is inspired by Article 22 of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and represents a meaningful step toward algorithmic accountability in Uruguay. The URCDP (Unidad Reguladora y de Control de Datos Personales) oversees compliance.
