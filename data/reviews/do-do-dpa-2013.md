## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Dominican Republic's primary data protection law covering personal data in public and private databases. INDOTEL serves as supervisory authority. Provides data subject ARCO rights, habeas data judicial remedy, lawful processing requirements, mandatory database registration, sensitive data protections, and criminal penalties up to 2 years imprisonment. Particular focus on credit bureau and financial data.

## Overview

The Dominican Republic's Law No. 172-13 on Comprehensive Protection of Personal Data Contained in Public and Private Databases is the country's primary personal data protection law, enacted on December 15, 2013, and effective from January 15, 2014. The law designates the Instituto Dominicano de las Telecomunicaciones (INDOTEL) — the national telecommunications regulator — as the supervisory authority for personal data protection in addition to its existing telecom mandate. The law covers all personal data held in public and private databases, with initial emphasis on credit bureau and financial data. It provides both administrative remedies through INDOTEL and a judicial habeas data remedy for individuals. The law applies to both public and private sector data processing in the Dominican Republic.
