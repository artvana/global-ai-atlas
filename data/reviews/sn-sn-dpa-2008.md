## Summary (verify for accuracy)

One of West Africa's earliest and most comprehensive personal data protection laws. Establishes the CDP as an independent supervisory authority and serves as an ECOWAS reference framework. Provides five data protection principles, mandatory CDP declaration or authorization, sensitive data categories, data subject rights (information, access, rectification, opposition), cross-border transfer restrictions, and criminal penalties.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Declare processing activities to CDP or obtain prior authorization for sensitive/high-risk processing
- Comply with five data protection principles
- Protect sensitive data categories with explicit consent or legal authority
- Provide data subject rights (information, access, rectification, opposition)
- Implement technical and organizational security measures
- Maintain data confidentiality among staff
- Restrict cross-border transfers to countries with adequate protection

## Overview

Senegal's Law 2008-12 of January 25, 2008, on the protection of personal data is one of West Africa's earliest and most comprehensive personal data protection statutes. Enacted in 2008, the law establishes the Commission de Protection des Données Personnelles (CDP) as an independent administrative authority. Modeled partly on the EU Directive 95/46/EC and the French data protection tradition, the law has served as a reference point for ECOWAS (Economic Community of West African States) member states developing their own frameworks. It applies to all processing of personal data on Senegalese territory, covering both automated and partly automated processing as well as certain manual filing systems.
