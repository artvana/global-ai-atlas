## Summary (verify for accuracy)

Italy's Law 132/2025 is the first comprehensive national AI law enacted by an EU member state, comprising 28 articles across six chapters. It creates sector-specific binding rules for healthcare, employment, justice, and intellectual property, establishes a new criminal offense for unlawful AI-generated deepfake dissemination (1–5 years imprisonment), and sets up a €1 billion state AI investment fund.

## Key Obligations (verify completeness and accuracy)

- Patients must be informed when AI is used in their healthcare; no AI-based discrimination in healthcare access
- Employers must inform employees when AI systems are used in work processes
- AI assistance permitted in judiciary but legal interpretation reserved exclusively to magistrates
- Criminal offense: unlawful production/dissemination of AI-altered content without consent (1–5 years imprisonment)
- AI-generated works protectable only where substantial human creative contribution exists

## Provisions and Delegations to the Government Regarding Artificial Intelligence

**Italian Republic · Effective: 1 November 2025 · Reference: 25G00143**

---

## Chapter I — Principles and Objectives

### Article 1 — Purpose and Scope of Application

This law sets out principles regarding research, experimentation, development, adoption, and application of artificial intelligence systems and models. It promotes correct, transparent, and responsible use, in an anthropocentric dimension, of artificial intelligence. It guarantees supervision of economic and social risks and of the impact on fundamental rights of artificial intelligence. The provisions of this law are interpreted and applied in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the EU AI Act).

### Article 2 — Definitions

a) **artificial intelligence system**: the system defined in Article 3(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689;

b) **data**: any digital representation of acts, facts, or information, also in the form of sound, visual, or audiovisual recording;

c) **artificial intelligence models**: the models defined in Article 3(63) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.
